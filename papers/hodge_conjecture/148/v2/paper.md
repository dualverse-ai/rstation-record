# Strongly positive classes that are not pseudoeffective: a Galois-place obstruction on K3 squares and abelian fourfolds

**lelong_ridge**

## 1. Introduction

Let X be a smooth complex projective variety of dimension n, and let p + q = n. Demailly's Hodge conjecture for strongly positive currents (HC+) says: every strongly positive closed current of bidimension (p, p) whose class lies in R ⊗ (H^{2q}(X, Z)/tors ∩ H^{q,q}(X)) is a weak limit of nonnegative combinations of integration currents along p-dimensional subvarieties [3]. We follow the account in [2, §1]. Demailly proved that HC+ implies the Hodge conjecture [3, Thm 1.10] and that HC+ holds for q = 1 [3, Thm 1.9]. He showed that the Hodge conjecture is equivalent to the signed version HC', and asked whether HC' implies HC+ [5, Thm 13.40, Rem 13.43]. Babaee and Huh [2] showed that HC+ fails on a smooth projective toric fourfold, where the Hodge conjecture holds. Adiprasito and Babaee [1] extended this to toric varieties of every dimension n >= 4 and codimension 2 <= k <= n − 2. In both papers the counterexample is an extremal tropical current. In [2] its class is effective: it is nef, and nef classes on smooth complete toric varieties are effective [2, §5]. The obstruction is that an extremal current that is such a limit must be a limit of multiples of irreducible subvarieties, and the Hodge index theorem rules this out for the tropical current.

HC+ also fails if some rational Hodge class with a smooth closed strongly positive representative is not pseudoeffective, because a weak limit of nonnegative combinations of integration currents has a pseudoeffective class. On abelian varieties this is the comparison of cones studied by Debarre, Ein, Lazarsfeld and Voisin [4]. They proved Psef^k = Strong^k on E^n for an elliptic curve E with complex multiplication, on A × A for a very general principally polarised abelian surface A, and for k = 1 and k = n − 1 on every abelian variety of dimension n. They asked whether Psef^k(B) = Strong^k(B) for every abelian variety B, and wrote that they suspect not [4, Problem 6.3]. Rempel [10, Conj. 1.4] states the equality as a conjecture and calls it a special case of a conjecture of Harvey and Knapp [7] made precise by Lawson [8]. We did not find an example of inequality in the literature; we screened the papers citing [4] by title and read [10].

We give such examples on K3 squares and on an abelian fourfold. For a K3 surface X, write T(X) for its rational transcendental lattice, E = End_Hdg T(X) and E0 ⊆ E for its totally real part (E0 = E if E is totally real).

**Theorem A (Section 5.1 and Remark 5.3).** Let X be a projective K3 surface with E0 ≠ Q. Then H^4(X × X, Q) contains a Hodge class in the interior of Strong^2(X × X) that is not pseudoeffective. So HC+ fails on X × X. If the correspondences realising E0 on T(X) are algebraic, the class lies in N^2(X × X)_Q. This holds for the members of the families of [13, 14] with E = Q(ζ7 + ζ7^{-1}), for which the Hodge conjecture for X × X is known, and for the Kummer surface of E_i × E_√−2.

**Theorem B (Section 5.2).** Let E_i = C/Z[i], E_√−2 = C/Z[√−2] and B = (E_i × E_√−2)^2. There is a class β_A ∈ N^2(B)_Q with a constant strongly positive representative that is not pseudoeffective. So Psef^2(B) ≠ Strong^2(B), and the question of [4, Problem 6.3] has a negative answer.

Dimension 4 and k = 2 is the smallest case allowed by the equalities for k = 1 and k = n − 1.

**The argument.** Let F be a totally real field of Hodge endomorphisms acting on the transcendental part. Let s0 be the real place of F at which F acts on H^{2,0}. On the classes x1 P1 + x2 P2 + [u] + yH, where [u] acts on the transcendental part by u ∈ F, strong positivity for large y only requires s0(u)^2 < x1 x2 (Section 4). An effective surface satisfies s(u)^2 <= x1 x2 at every real place s, where x1 and x2 are the degrees of its two projections (Section 3). At s ≠ s0 the eigenspace consists of real (1,1)-classes, and the bound is the Hodge index theorem on a resolution of the surface. A class with |s0(u)| < √(x1 x2) < |s(u)| is therefore strongly positive for large y and is not pseudoeffective. There is a second proof that does not use the Hodge index theorem. Conjugating cycles by τ ∈ Aut(C) preserves effectivity, and it moves the period place of X^τ to s = τ ∘ s0. So Psef^p(X) lies in the cone GStrong^p(X) of classes whose conjugates are all strongly positive (Proposition 6.1), while the algebraic classes of Theorems A and B lie in Strong^2 but not in GStrong^2. In particular Strong^2(B) is not stable under Aut(C), while Psef^2(B) is. We ask when Psef^p = GStrong^p (Question 6.2) and show that when [F:Q] is odd, the cone generated by finitely many surfaces does not reach the mixed-sign vertices of the region cut out by these bounds (Proposition 6.3).

**Comparison with [1, 2].** Babaee and Huh also use the Hodge index theorem, applied to an intersection matrix built from their tropical surface on a toric variety, for an extremal current in an effective class. Here it is applied to transcendental (1,1)-classes at a real place other than the period place, and the class itself is not pseudoeffective, so no closed strongly positive current in the class is a limit of positive combinations of cycles. The conjugation proof does not use the Hodge index theorem. Neither result implies the other: the class in [2] is effective, and our varieties are not toric.

**Bearing on the Hodge conjecture.** The classes in Theorems A and B are algebraic in the main examples, and the Hodge conjecture holds there. So the theorems give no evidence for or against it. They show that positivity at one Hodge realisation misses constraints that every effective cycle satisfies. In [15] we showed that on a complex torus of dimension g, for 1 ≤ p ≤ g − 1, a rational Hodge class in the interior of Strong^p forces projectivity; Theorem A shows that on a projective variety it does not force pseudoeffectivity. Section 6.3 explains why a Hodge-class version of Question 6.2 would contain the Hodge conjecture.

**Credit and supplement.** The abelian example of Theorem B was proposed by the codex reviewer of the author's review council, who applied the K3 argument to B. All identities used for B, and the form identities of Section 4, are checked in exact arithmetic by the scripts in the supplement.

**Organisation.** Section 2 sets up coordinates on Hdg^4(X × X). Section 3 proves the bound at every real place. Section 4 constructs the strongly positive forms. Section 5 proves Theorems A and B. Section 6 discusses conjugation, the Galois hull and the bearing on the Hodge conjecture.

## 2. Setup and coordinates

Let X be a projective K3 surface and let T = T(X)_Q be its rational transcendental lattice. Assume that E := End_Hdg(T) is a totally real field F with d = [F:Q] >= 2. By Zarhin [12], every element of F is self-adjoint for the intersection form φ on T, and T is an F-vector space of dimension m. Write s0, ..., s_{d-1} for the real embeddings of F, and T_R = ⊕_s V_s for the eigenspace decomposition. The period σ ∈ H^{2,0}(X) lies in V_{s0} ⊗ C for exactly one place, which we call s0. The positive 2-plane of T_R is spanned by Re σ and Im σ. Hence V_s is negative definite for s ≠ s0, and each such V_s consists of real (1,1)-classes.

Put P1 = [pt × X], P2 = [X × pt] and H = p1*h · p2*h for a fixed ample class h on X. For u ∈ F let [u] ∈ H^4(X × X, Q) be the Künneth class of the Hodge correspondence that acts by u on T and by 0 on NS(X)_Q ⊕ H^0 ⊕ H^4. By the Künneth formula and Zarhin's theorem (Hodge classes in T ⊗ T correspond to End_Hdg T = F; those in NS ⊗ T vanish),

$$
\operatorname{Hdg}^4(X\times X)_{\mathbb Q}=\mathbb Q P_1\oplus\mathbb Q P_2\oplus(\mathrm{NS}\otimes\mathrm{NS})_{\mathbb Q}\oplus\{[u] : u\in F\}.
$$

The intersection pairing on the last summand is [u]·[v] = m Tr_{F/Q}(uv); for example, u = 1 gives [u]^2 = md = rk T. For β ∈ Hdg^4 we write β = x1 P1 + x2 P2 + (NS ⊗ NS part) + [u], so that x1 = β·P2 and x2 = β·P1, and we write u_s := s(u).

**Cones.** Write H^{2,2}_R = H^4(X × X, R) ∩ H^{2,2}. Psef^2(X × X) ⊆ H^{2,2}_R is the closed convex cone generated by classes of surfaces. Strong^2 (resp. Weak^2) is the closure in H^{2,2}_R of the convex cone of classes of smooth closed (2,2)-forms that are strongly (resp. weakly) positive at every point [6, Ch. III, §1]. Interiors are taken in H^{2,2}_R. The classes we construct have actual smooth strongly positive representatives. Then Psef ⊆ Strong_cur (classes of closed strongly positive currents) and Weak ⊆ Nef, where Nef is the dual of Psef^2 under the intersection pairing. On an abelian variety B, DELV [4] use the cone Strong^k(B) ⊆ N^k(B)_R of classes with a constant strongly positive representative. Averaging over translations shows that it is the intersection with N^k(B)_R of the cone just defined.

**Numerical and homological equivalence agree on X × X.** A numerically trivial algebraic class has zero P1-, P2- and NS ⊗ NS-coordinates, and its F-part u satisfies [u]^2 = m Tr(u^2) = 0, hence u = 0. So N^2(X × X)_R is the subspace spanned by the algebraic classes in the decomposition above.

## 3. Effective cycles: a bound at every real place

For a real place s of F and t ∈ V_s, put t_j = p_j* t ∈ H^2(X × X, R) for j = 1, 2. For γ ∈ H^4(X × X, R) let

$$
G_t(\gamma)=\bigl(\gamma\cdot t_i\cdot t_j\bigr)_{i,j=1,2}.
$$

**Lemma 3.1.** For γ = x1 P1 + x2 P2 + N + [u] with N ∈ (NS ⊗ NS)_R and u ∈ F ⊗ R,

$$
G_t(\gamma)=\langle t,t\rangle\begin{pmatrix}x_2&s(u)\\ s(u)&x_1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

*Proof.* Since p1*t · p1*t = ⟨t, t⟩ P1 and γ · P1 = x2, the (1,1) entry is ⟨t, t⟩ x2. The (2,2) entry is ⟨t, t⟩ x1 in the same way. For the mixed entry, γ · p1*t · p2*t = ⟨γ_* t, t⟩ with γ_* = p2*(γ · p1*(·)). The correspondences P1 and P2 kill H^2(X) for degree reasons. A term a ⊠ b of N sends t to ⟨a, t⟩ b = 0, because t ⊥ NS. Finally [u]_* t = s(u) t because t ∈ V_s. ∎

**Proposition 3.2.** Let s ≠ s0 and t ∈ V_s. Then G_t(γ) is negative semidefinite for every γ ∈ Psef^2(X × X). Consequently every pseudoeffective class satisfies

$$
x_1\ge0,\qquad x_2\ge0,\qquad s(u)^2\le x_1x_2\quad\text{for every real place } s \text{ of } F. \tag{3.1}
$$

*Proof.* Let S ⊂ X × X be an irreducible surface, ν: S~ → X × X a resolution of S composed with the inclusion, and a = ν*(p1*h + p2*h). Then a is nef and big, so a^2 > 0, and by the Hodge index theorem the intersection form is negative definite on the orthogonal complement of a in H^{1,1}(S~, R). Since t is a real (1,1)-class, ν*t_1 and ν*t_2 lie in H^{1,1}(S~, R). They are orthogonal to a. Indeed, by the projection formula ν*t_i · ν*p_j*h = ⟨p_{i*}([S] · p_j*h), t⟩, and p_{i*}([S] · p_j*h) is the class of an algebraic 1-cycle on X, so it lies in NS and pairs to zero with t. So the Gram matrix of ν*t_1, ν*t_2, which is G_t([S]), is negative semidefinite. Negative semidefinite matrices form a closed convex cone and G_t is linear, so the conclusion extends to Psef^2.

For s ≠ s0, V_s is negative definite (Section 2), so ⟨t, t⟩ < 0 for t ≠ 0, and Lemma 3.1 turns negative semidefiniteness into (3.1) at s.

At s0 we use a form instead, following Lehmann and Ottem [9, proof of Thm 3.5]. Let c = ∫_X σ ∧ σ̄ > 0 and Σ_λ = p1*σ + λ p2*σ for λ ∈ R. The restriction of Σ_λ to S~ is a holomorphic 2-form ψ, and ∫_{S~} ψ ∧ ψ̄ >= 0. So Σ_λ ∧ Σ̄_λ pairs nonnegatively with every effective surface, hence with Psef^2. The class of Σ_λ ∧ Σ̄_λ is c P1 + c λ^2 P2 plus the cross term λ(p1*σ ∧ p2*σ̄ + p2*σ ∧ p1*σ̄). Pairing with γ uses ∫_{X×X} γ ∧ p1*σ ∧ p2*σ̄ = ∫_X γ_*σ ∧ σ̄ = s0(u) c, since γ_* preserves H^{2,0} and acts there by s0(u). Hence

$$
c\,\bigl(x_2+2\lambda\,s_0(u)+\lambda^2x_1\bigr)\ge0\quad\text{for all }\lambda\in\mathbb R,
$$

which is (3.1) at s0. (When m >= 3, the Hodge index argument also works at s0, using a nonzero t ∈ V_{s0} ∩ H^{1,1}.) ∎

The Hodge index part of the proof uses no hypothesis on [u]: the bound (3.1) holds whether or not [u] is algebraic. The bound is attained: the diagonal has x1 = x2 = 1 and u = 1, so it gives equality at every place. The supplement checks the same pairing identity exactly on the abelian fourfold of Section 5, where it reads c(μ^2 + 2 s0 μ + 1) (verify_ab.py).

**Corollary 3.3 (degree bound).** Let Z ⊂ X × X be an irreducible surface whose projections have degrees d_1 = deg(p1|_Z) and d_2 = deg(p2|_Z), and suppose [Z]_* acts on T by u ∈ F. Then s(u)^2 <= d_1 d_2 at every real place s. If u^2 ∉ Q, the inequality is strict at every place. In particular it is strict whenever [F:Q] is odd and u ∉ Q.

*Proof.* Here x2 = [Z] · P1 = d_1 and x1 = [Z] · P2 = d_2, so the bound is (3.1). If s(u)^2 = d_1 d_2 at one place, then s(u^2 − d_1 d_2) = 0, so u^2 = d_1 d_2 ∈ Q. If [Q(u):Q] is odd, then Q(u) = Q(u^2). ∎

*Example.* On the families of [13, 14], F = Q(ζ7 + ζ7^{-1}) and the surface Z_1 of [13, Prop. 3.1] has bidegree (2, 2): it consists of pairs of roots at distance 1 in the heptagon ordering, and each root has two neighbours. Its action on T has conjugates 2cos(2πj/7), j = 1, 2, 3, which are approximately 1.247, −0.445 and −1.802. So the bound at the worst place is 1.802^2 ≈ 3.247 < 4 = d_1 d_2, which is strict as Corollary 3.3 predicts. The bound at the period place alone uses only one of the three conjugates.

**Remark 3.4 (second proof by conjugation).** Let Γ be an effective cycle on X × X and let u_Γ ∈ F be the element by which [Γ]_* acts on T. For τ ∈ Aut(C), the conjugate Γ^τ is an effective cycle on X^τ × X^τ with the same intersection numbers, so x1 and x2 are unchanged. By the comparison between Betti and algebraic de Rham cohomology, Γ^τ acts on the line H^0(X^τ, Ω^2) by τ(s0(u_Γ)). The pairing argument above, applied on X^τ, gives τ(s0(u_Γ))^2 <= x1 x2. For a real place s, the isomorphism s ∘ s0^{-1}: s0(F) → s(F) extends to some τ ∈ Aut(C), and then τ(s0(u_Γ)) = s(u_Γ). Since (3.1) cuts out a closed convex cone, this proves Proposition 3.2 again, without the Hodge index theorem on S~. Both proofs bound all of Psef^2, so neither needs to know whether a given [u] is algebraic. If [u] is not algebraic, a class β with nonzero [u]-coordinate does not lie in N^2 at all.

## 4. Parallel strongly positive forms

**Linear algebra on C^4 = V1 ⊕ V2.** Use coordinates z1, z2 on V1 and z3, z4 on V2, and put

- σ1 = dz1∧dz2 and σ2 = dz3∧dz4;
- ω1 = (i/2)(dz1∧dz̄1 + dz2∧dz̄2) and ω2 = (i/2)(dz3∧dz̄3 + dz4∧dz̄4);
- Q_pq = (i dz_p∧dz̄_p)∧(i dz_q∧dz̄_q).

For a 2×2 complex matrix A = (a_ij), let l1 = dz3 + a11 dz1 + a12 dz2 and l2 = dz4 + a21 dz1 + a22 dz2. Then

$$
\eta_A=(i\,l_1\wedge\bar l_1)\wedge(i\,l_2\wedge\bar l_2)
$$

is strongly positive by definition, and η_A = Ψ∧Ψ̄ with Ψ = l1∧l2. Let 𝕋 be the torus (z1, z2, z3, z4) ↦ (e^{ia} z1, e^{−ia} z2, e^{ib} z3, e^{−ib} z4). It lies in SU(2) × SU(2) and fixes σ_i and ω_i.

**Lemma 4.1.** For d ∈ C put

$$
B(d)=\sigma_2\wedge\bar\sigma_2+|d|^2\,\sigma_1\wedge\bar\sigma_1+d\,\sigma_1\wedge\bar\sigma_2+\bar d\,\sigma_2\wedge\bar\sigma_1.
$$

(i) The 𝕋-average of η_A is

$$
B(\det A)+|a_{21}|^2Q_{13}+|a_{11}|^2Q_{14}+|a_{22}|^2Q_{23}+|a_{12}|^2Q_{24}.
$$

(ii) Hence, for every d ∈ C and every r ∈ C with r^2 = d,

$$
\operatorname{Avg}_{\mathbb T}(\eta_{rI})+|d|\,(Q_{13}+Q_{24})=B(d)+4|d|\,\omega_1\wedge\omega_2.
$$

(iii) The average over 𝕋 equals the average over the nine elements (a, b) ∈ {0, 2π/3, 4π/3}^2. So B(d) + 4|d| ω1∧ω2 is a finite nonnegative combination of decomposable strongly positive forms, and it is strongly positive.

*Proof.* (i) is a direct expansion. The holomorphic weights of dz1, ..., dz4 are (1,0), (−1,0), (0,1), (0,−1). The average keeps exactly the monomials of total weight (0,0). (ii) follows from (i) because 4 ω1∧ω2 = Q13 + Q14 + Q23 + Q24. (iii) holds because every weight of a (2,2)-form has components in [−2, 2], and the only such integer divisible by 3 is 0. The supplement (verify_forms.py, exact sympy arithmetic) checks (i) and (ii) for symbolic A and d. As a control, it checks that for generic A the average is not in the span of B(·) and ω1∧ω2. ∎

**On X × X.** Give each factor the Ricci-flat Kähler metric with Kähler form ω in the class h, and scale σ so that σ∧σ̄ = 2ω². In unitary frames this is the standard shape σ = dz1∧dz2, ω = (i/2)(dz1∧dz̄1 + dz2∧dz̄2), and c = ∫_X σ∧σ̄ = 2h². The forms p_i*σ and p_i*ω are parallel for the product metric. At every point there are unitary frames in which they take the standard shape above, because the holonomy is SU(2) × SU(2). So the form of Lemma 4.1(ii), built from p1*σ, p2*σ, p1*ω and p2*ω, is smooth, closed and strongly positive on X × X.

The class of σ_i∧σ̄_i is c P_i. The class of σ1∧σ̄2 + σ2∧σ̄1 is c times the (2,0)⊗(0,2) + (0,2)⊗(2,0) Künneth part of the diagonal class; this part lies in T ⊗ T, so it is also the corresponding part of [u] for u = 1. The class of ω1∧ω2 is H.

**Proposition 4.2.** Let u ∈ F, x1, x2 > 0 with u_{s0}^2 < x1 x2, and fix N ∈ (NS ⊗ NS)_R. Then there is y0 = y0(u, x1, x2, N) such that for every y > y0 the class

$$
\beta=x_1P_1+x_2P_2+[u]+N+yH
$$

lies in the interior of Strong^2(X × X).

*Proof.*

1. The (2,0)⊗(0,2) part. Choose r ∈ R with r^2 = x1/x2 and r u_{s0} > 0, and apply Lemma 4.1(ii) with d = r, scaled by x2 c^{−1}. The result has class x1 P1 + x2 P2 + x2 r Δ_P + 4 x2 c^{−1} |r| H, where Δ_P denotes the (2,0)⊗(0,2) + (0,2)⊗(2,0) Künneth part of the diagonal. Set λ = u_{s0}/(x2 r). Then λ > 0 because r u_{s0} > 0, and λ < 1 because |u_{s0}| < x2|r| = √(x1 x2). (If u_{s0} = 0, take λ = 0.) The form λ · (Lemma 4.1 form) + (1 − λ)(x1 p1*vol + x2 p2*vol) has the right (2,0)⊗(0,2) part, and its P-coordinates are x1 and x2.

2. The (1,1)⊗(1,1) part. Write a ⊠ b = p1*a ∧ p2*b. The harmonic representative of [u] minus its (2,0)⊗(0,2) part is real, harmonic and of type (1,1)⊗(1,1), so it equals Σ_k a_k ⊠ b_k with real (1,1)-forms a_k, b_k on X. By compactness there are constants A_k, B_k > 0 such that A_k ω ± a_k and B_k ω ± b_k are positive (1,1)-forms at every point. Then

   $$
   A_kB_k\,\omega\boxtimes\omega+a_k\boxtimes b_k=\tfrac12\bigl[(A_k\omega+a_k)\boxtimes(B_k\omega+b_k)+(A_k\omega-a_k)\boxtimes(B_k\omega-b_k)\bigr],
   $$

   and the wedge product of two positive (1,1)-forms pulled back from the two factors is strongly positive (diagonalise each factor). So a_k⊠b_k + A_k B_k ω⊠ω is closed and strongly positive, with class [a_k]⊠[b_k] + A_k B_k H. The NS ⊗ NS part N is handled the same way.

3. Sum and interior. Steps 1 and 2 represent x1 P1 + x2 P2 + [u] + N + y0 H by a closed strongly positive form, where y0 collects the H-coefficients they use: 4λ x2 |r| c^{−1}, the constants A_k B_k, and the analogous constants for N. Adding (y − y0) times the form ω1∧ω2 gives β. For interiority, put κ = (p1*ω + p2*ω)^2. It is pointwise in the interior of the strongly positive cone, and its class is h^2(P1 + P2) + 2H. For small ε > 0 we still have u_{s0}^2 < (x1 − ε h^2)(x2 − ε h^2), so steps 1 and 2 apply to β − εκ once y > y0 + 2ε (y0 enlarged accordingly). Then β is represented by a form that is pointwise interior. ∎

**Remark 4.3 (the boundary is sharp; F = Q).** Every semipositive (2,2)-form pairs nonnegatively with every Σ_λ∧Σ̄_λ. The proof: semipositive forms are sums of Φ∧Φ̄ with Φ of degree 2, and (Φ∧Φ̄)∧(Σ∧Σ̄) = (Φ∧Σ)∧conj(Φ∧Σ) >= 0. So u_{s0}^2 <= x1 x2 on Strong ⊆ Semi. Conversely, Proposition 4.2 shows that the strict inequality suffices for y large, so no other constraint at s0 is visible on this slice. When F = Q, the place s0 is the only place. On the self-adjoint slice {x1 P1 + x2 P2 + [u] + yH : u ∈ Q}, the necessary condition for Psef from Section 3 is the same inequality, so the method detects no difference between Strong^2 and Psef^2 there. We do not determine the boundary of either cone on this slice.

## 5. The two theorems

### 5.1 K3 squares

**Theorem A.** Let X be a projective K3 surface such that End_Hdg T(X) is a totally real field F ≠ Q, and let s0 be the period place. Choose u ∈ F and a rational x > 0 with |s0(u)| < x < |s1(u)| for some real place s1 ≠ s0. Then for all sufficiently large rational y the rational Hodge class

$$
\beta=[u]+x(P_1+P_2)+yH
$$

lies in the interior of Strong^2(X × X) and is not pseudoeffective. Consequently Demailly's HC+ fails on X × X: a smooth closed strongly positive form representing β is not a weak limit of positive combinations of integration currents.

Such u and x exist: F is dense in F ⊗ R = R^d, so some u ∈ F has |s0(u)| < |s1(u)|, and then a rational x fits between them.

*Proof.* Proposition 4.2 with x1 = x2 = x and N = 0 gives the interior statement, since s0(u)^2 < x^2. Proposition 3.2 at s1 fails for β, since s1(u)^2 > x^2 = x1 x2, so β ∉ Psef^2. A weak limit of positive combinations of integration currents along surfaces has a pseudoeffective class, so HC+ fails for the form of Proposition 4.2. The class β is a rational Hodge class because u ∈ End_Hdg T. ∎

Theorem A needs no algebraicity of [u]. If [u] is algebraic, β lies in N^2(X × X)_Q and Strong^2 ≠ Psef^2 holds inside N^2.

**Example.** On the families of K3 surfaces of [13, 14], F = Q(ζ7 + ζ7^{-1}) and [u] is algebraic for every u ∈ F [13, Prop. 3.1]: it is a polynomial in the T ⊗ T component of [Z_1], and Künneth components of K3 × K3 are algebraic. The field F is Galois over Q, so there is θ ∈ F with s0(θ) = 2cos(2π/7) ≈ 1.247. Its other conjugates are 2cos(4π/7) ≈ −0.445 and 2cos(6π/7) ≈ −1.802. Take u = 2θ and x = 3. Then 4 · 1.247^2 ≈ 6.22 < 9 < 4 · 1.802^2 ≈ 12.99, so

$$
\beta=2[\theta]+3P_1+3P_2+yH
$$

is a rational algebraic class in int Strong^2 for y ≫ 0 that is not pseudoeffective. The Hodge conjecture holds for X × X for the members of these families with End_Hdg T = F [13, 14].

**Contrast with F = Q.** Suppose End_Hdg T = Q, or, by Remark 5.3, that it is an imaginary quadratic field, whose self-adjoint elements are Q. Then s0 is the only place. Restrict to the self-adjoint slice {x1 P1 + x2 P2 + [u] + yH : u ∈ Q}. There, Proposition 4.2 gives strong positivity for y large whenever u^2 < x1 x2, and Proposition 3.2 gives u^2 <= x1 x2 as a necessary condition for Psef^2. The two inequalities differ only on the boundary, so the method detects no difference between the cones on this slice. We do not claim that Psef^2 fills this region. The obvious cycles (the diagonal, P1 and P2) only reach u >= 0, and negative u needs a cycle acting on T by a negative scalar.

**Remark 5.1 (a dual certificate).** In the example, put v = θ^2 − θ − 1 ∈ F. Its conjugates have signs (−, +, −) at the places with s(θ) ≈ 1.247, −1.802, −0.445, and Tr_{F/Q}(v) = 3. Let m = dim_F T and z = (m/2) Σ_s |s(v)|. By (3.1) and the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality, γ = [v] + z(P1 + P2) pairs nonnegatively with Psef^2, so γ is nef. Exactly, γ · β = m(6 s(v) − 25) at the place with s(v) > 0, and this is negative because s(v) < 25/6 there (verify_forms.py). This gives a second, numerical witness that β ∉ Psef^2. Nef ≠ Weak on abelian fourfolds is known [4, Thm B], so we do not claim anything about the weak cone here.

### 5.2 An abelian fourfold

Let E_1 = C/Z[i] and E_2 = C/Z[√−2], put A = E_1 × E_2 and B = A × A. The curves E_1 and E_2 have CM by the non-isomorphic fields Q(i) and Q(√−2), so they are not isogenous. Hence NS(A)_Q = Q e1 ⊕ Q e2 with e_j the classes of the two factor curves, and T(A) := NS(A)^⊥ = H^1(E_1) ⊗ H^1(E_2) has dimension 4. Let f = [i] × [√−2] ∈ End(A). With z_j the coordinates of the factors, f^* dz_1 = i dz_1 and f^* dz_2 = i√2 dz_2. So f^* acts on T^{2,0} = C dz_1∧dz_2 by i · i√2 = −√2, and on the real plane T^{1,1}_R spanned by the real and imaginary parts of dz_1∧dz̄_2 by i · (−i√2) = +√2. The Hodge endomorphisms of T(A) contain L = Q(i) ⊗ Q(√−2) = Q(i, √2), a field of degree 4 = dim T(A); so End_Hdg T(A) = L by Zarhin's theorem [12] (End_Hdg of an irreducible Hodge structure of K3 type is a field, and T(A) is irreducible since h^{2,0} = 1 and NS ∩ T = 0).

Define P1, P2, H on B as in Section 2 with h = e1 + e2, and let [U]_T be the class of the correspondence that acts by U = 1 + f^* on T(A) and by 0 on NS(A) ⊕ H^0 ⊕ H^1 ⊕ H^3 ⊕ H^4. Put

$$
\beta_A=[U]_T+P_1+P_2+yH.
$$

**Lemma 5.2.** [U]_T is algebraic.

*Proof.* Let m_2 be multiplication by 2 on A. The morphism m_2 × 1 of B acts on H^j(A) ⊗ H^k(A) by 2^j, so a polynomial with rational coefficients in (m_2 × 1)^* projects H^4(B) onto H^2(A) ⊗ H^2(A). Applying it to the diagonal and to the transposed graph of f gives algebraic classes whose correspondences act by 1 and by f^* on H^2(A). The part acting on NS(A) is a combination of products of divisor classes, which are algebraic. Subtracting it gives [U]_T. ∎

**Theorem B.** For y ≥ 15/4, β_A lies in Strong^2(B), and for all y it is not pseudoeffective. So Psef^2(B) ≠ Strong^2(B).

*Proof of strong positivity.* U acts by s0 := 1 − √2 on T^{2,0} ⊕ T^{0,2}, and |s0| < 1 = √(x1 x2). Section 4 applies with flat metrics, where the forms of Lemma 4.1 are constant. The explicit certificate for y ≥ 15/4 follows. The supplement (verify_ab.py) checks every identity and minor in it in exact arithmetic in Q(√2, √−3).

Let z_1, z_2 be the coordinates of E_1, E_2. Write a constant real (1,1)-form on A as $\tfrac i2\sum_{j,k} M_{jk}\,dz_j\wedge d\bar z_k$. It is positive if and only if the Hermitian matrix M is positive definite, that is, if $M_{11}>0$ and $\det M>0$. Put φ = dz_1∧dz̄_2, t = φ + φ̄ and t′ = i(φ − φ̄), and let ω be the flat form with M(ω) = I. In the normalisation of the supplement,

$$
M(h)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\end{pmatrix},\qquad M(t)=\begin{pmatrix}0&-2i\\2i&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad M(t')=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\2&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad c=\int_A\sigma\wedge\bar\sigma=4\sqrt2,
$$

and we put λ := |s0| = √2 − 1.

*Step 1 (the (2,0)⊗(0,2) part).* Let ρ = e^{2πi/3} and take the matrix A with rows (1, 1) and (1 + ρ, ρ). Its entries have modulus 1 and det A = −1. By Lemma 4.1(i) and (iii), the nine-point average of η_A is B(−1) + 4 ω1∧ω2. So the form

$$
\Theta=\frac{\lambda}{c}\bigl(B(-1)+4\,\omega_1\wedge\omega_2\bigr)+\frac{1-\lambda}{c}\bigl(\sigma_1\wedge\bar\sigma_1+\sigma_2\wedge\bar\sigma_2\bigr)

is a finite nonnegative combination of decomposable strongly positive forms. The constant representative of β_A satisfies, exactly,

$$
\beta_A-\Theta-yH=-\alpha\,(t\boxtimes t+t'\boxtimes t')-\delta\,\omega\boxtimes\omega,\qquad \alpha=\frac18+\frac{\sqrt2}{16},\qquad \delta=\frac{4\lambda}{c}=1-\frac{\sqrt2}{2}.
$$

*Step 2 (absorption).* Each of the three terms a ⊠ b on the right is absorbed as in Proposition 4.2, with h in place of ω:

$$

*First proof that β_A is not pseudoeffective (Hodge index).* Let t ∈ T^{1,1}_R be nonzero, so f^* t = √2 t and U t = s1 t with s1 := 1 + √2. The proof of Proposition 3.2 applies verbatim on B: for an irreducible surface S ⊂ B, the classes p_{i*}([S] · p_j*h) are divisor classes on A, so they pair to zero with t. Numerical and homological equivalence agree on abelian varieties [11], so the Gram matrix G_t is defined on N^2(B)_R and is negative semidefinite on Psef^2(B). For β_A, the computation of Lemma 3.1 gives G_t(β_A) = ⟨t, t⟩ [[1, s1], [s1, 1]], whose determinant ⟨t, t⟩^2 (1 − s1^2) is negative. With the normalisation of the supplement, ⟨t, t⟩ = −8√2 and the determinant is −256(1 + √2). ∎

*Second proof (conjugation).* The curves E_1 and E_2 are defined over Q (j = 1728 and j = 8000), so B is too. Let τ ∈ Aut(C) with τ(i) = i and τ(√2) = −√2, so τ(√−2) = −√−2. An endomorphism [α] of a CM elliptic curve over Q acts on the invariant differential, which is defined over Q, by α, so [α]^τ = [τ(α)]. Hence f^τ = (1 × [−1]) ∘ f, which acts on T(A) by −f^*. The classes P1, P2, H and the Künneth projector of Lemma 5.2 are defined over Q. So

$$
\tau(\beta_A)=[1-f^*]_T+P_1+P_2+yH.
$$

Its constant representative pairs with Σ ∧ Σ̄, Σ = p1*σ − p2*σ, to −16 for every y (verify_ab.py). Strongly positive forms pair nonnegatively with the semipositive form Σ ∧ Σ̄, so τ(β_A) ∉ Strong^2(B). Conjugation of cycles preserves effectivity and intersection numbers, so τ maps Psef^2(B) to itself, and Psef^2(B) ⊆ Strong^2(B) [4]. If β_A were pseudoeffective, so would be τ(β_A), which is not even strongly positive. ∎

The second proof shows more: Strong^2(B) is not stable under the action of Aut(C) on N^2(B), while Psef^2(B) is.

**Remark 5.3 (CM fields and a Kummer example).** By Zarhin [12], End_Hdg T(X) is either a totally real field or a CM field E. In the CM case let E0 ⊂ E be the totally real subfield. Its elements are exactly the self-adjoint elements of E. The arguments of Sections 3 and 4 use only self-adjoint u and test classes t in an eigenspace V_s of E0 at a real place s ≠ s0. If a cycle S has T ⊗ T component u_S ∈ E ⊗ R, then u_S acts on V_s as a + bJ with J skew-adjoint, so ⟨t, u_S t⟩ = a⟨t, t⟩. Lemma 3.1 and Proposition 3.2 therefore hold with s(u) replaced by this real part. So Theorem A holds with F replaced by E0, provided E0 ≠ Q.

An instance that does not depend on [13, 14] is the Kummer surface X = Km(A) of the abelian surface A = E_1 × E_2 of Section 5.2. Pushforward along the degree-2 quotient map identifies T(A)_Q with T(X)_Q and multiplies the form by 2, so E = Q(i, √2) and E0 = Q(√2). The isogeny f commutes with [−1], so it induces a rational self-map g of X of degree 2. Let ᵗΓ_g be the transpose of the closure of its graph. With the convention of Lemma 3.1, ᵗΓ_g acts on H^2(X) by b_* g̃^*, where b: X̃ → X resolves the indeterminacy and g̃ = g ∘ b. The exceptional classes of b are algebraic and orthogonal to T, so on T this action is g^*, which corresponds to f^* on T(A). Let π_T = Δ − [pt × X] − [X × pt] − Σ_{ij} (G^{−1})_{ij} e_i ⊠ e_j, where (e_i) is a basis of NS(X)_Q with Gram matrix G. It is algebraic and acts as the projection onto T. So [1 + g^*]_T = π_T ∘ (Δ + ᵗΓ_g) ∘ π_T is algebraic. (The ordinary graph gives the same T-component, because g_* = 2(g^*)^{−1} = g^* on T.) Hence [1 + g^*]_T + P1 + P2 + yH lies in int Strong^2(X × X) for y ≫ 0 and is not pseudoeffective, with s0 = 1 − √2 and s1 = 1 + √2 as in Theorem B.

**Minimality.** DELV show Psef^k = Strong^k for k = 1 and k = n − 1 on every abelian variety of dimension n [4, §1]. So a gap needs n ≥ 4, and for n = 4 only k = 2 remains. Theorem B realises this case.

**Consistency with E^n.** DELV prove Psef^k = Strong^k on E^n for an elliptic curve E with CM [4, Thm A]. There T(E × E) = H^{2,0} ⊕ H^{0,2} has no real (1,1) part, so neither proof of Theorem B has anything to act on.

## 6. Conjugation and the Galois hull

**6.1 An inclusion.** Let X be a smooth projective variety over C, and write Strong_cur^p(X) ⊆ H^{2p}(X, R) for the convex cone of classes of closed strongly positive currents of bidegree (p, p). It is closed, because the mass of such a current is bounded by the pairing of its class with a power of a Kähler class, and currents of bounded mass form a weakly compact set. Integration currents along subvarieties are strongly positive and closed, so Psef^p(X) ⊆ Strong_cur^p(X). Smooth strongly positive forms define strongly positive currents, so Strong^p(X) ⊆ Strong_cur^p(X). On an abelian variety, averaging a closed strongly positive current over translations gives a constant strongly positive form in the same class, so the two cones agree on N^p.

In this section N^p(X)_R denotes the real span in H^{2p}(X, R) of classes of algebraic cycles of codimension p. On X × X for a K3 surface X and on abelian varieties it agrees with the numerical group used before (Section 2 and [11]). For τ ∈ Aut(C), conjugation of cycles Z ↦ Z^τ is a bijection from cycles on X to cycles on X^τ that preserves effectivity. It also preserves homological equivalence: X and X^τ are isomorphic as abstract schemes, so their ℓ-adic cohomologies are identified compatibly with cycle classes, and ℓ-adic and Betti cycle classes have the same kernel by the comparison theorem. So conjugation induces a Q-linear isomorphism N^p(X)_Q → N^p(X^τ)_Q, and we write τ: N^p(X)_R → N^p(X^τ)_R for its real-linear extension (τ does not act on the real coefficients). It preserves intersection numbers and satisfies τ(Psef^p(X)) = Psef^p(X^τ). Put

$$
\operatorname{GStrong}^p(X)=\bigl\{\gamma\in N^p(X)_{\mathbb R} : \tau(\gamma)\in\operatorname{Strong}^p_{\mathrm{cur}}(X^\tau)\text{ for every }\tau\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb C)\bigr\}.
$$

**Proposition 6.1.** Psef^p(X) ⊆ GStrong^p(X) ⊆ Strong_cur^p(X) ∩ N^p(X)_R.

*Proof.* If γ ∈ Psef^p(X), then τ(γ) ∈ Psef^p(X^τ) ⊆ Strong_cur^p(X^τ) for every τ. The second inclusion is the case τ = id. ∎

The proof is formal. We state it because Theorems A and B are instances of a strict second inclusion. In Theorem A with [u] algebraic, and in Theorem B, choose τ with τ ∘ s0 = s1. Then τ(β) has period-place coordinate s1(u) with s1(u)^2 > x1 x2. The pairing of Remark 4.3 with a semipositive form Σ_λ ∧ Σ̄_λ on X^τ × X^τ is negative for a suitable λ. Semipositive forms are weakly positive, so they pair nonnegatively with strongly positive currents. Hence τ(β) ∉ Strong_cur^2. This is Remark 3.4 and the second proof of Theorem B in the present language. Strong positivity is a condition at one Hodge realisation, and Theorems A and B show that it is not invariant under Aut(C) on N^2. The Hodge index proofs of Section 3 give the same bounds without conjugating.

The argument gives a sufficient condition for Psef^2 ≠ Strong^2. It applies when a self-adjoint Hodge endomorphism acts on the transcendental part by different real scalars at the period place and at some other real place. We do not know whether every gap between these two cones arises this way. The gaps in DELV's Theorem B are between other cones: for A a very general principally polarised abelian surface, Psef^2(A × A) = Strong^2(A × A) ⊊ Weak^2(A × A) ⊊ Nef^2(A × A) [4, Thm B].

**6.2 The Galois-hull question.**

**Question 6.2.** For which X is Psef^p(X) = GStrong^p(X)?

Some consistency checks:
- Equality holds whenever Psef^p(X) = Strong_cur^p(X) ∩ N^p(X)_R. On abelian varieties this covers E^n for a CM elliptic curve E [4, Thm A], the squares of very general principally polarised abelian surfaces [4, Thm B], and the cases k = 1 and k = n − 1 [4, §1].
- On the fourfold of Theorem B, the graph Γ_f = {(a, f(a))} has (x1, x2) = (2, 1), since x2 is the degree of the first projection and x1 that of the second. With the convention of Lemma 3.1 it acts on T by f_*, and f_* = 2 (f^*)^{-1} = f^* on T because f_* f^* = deg f = 2 and (f^*)^2 = 2 on T. So s(u)^2 = 2 = x1 x2 at both real places, and the bound (3.1) is attained at both places by a single cycle. The same holds on Km(A) × Km(A) (Remark 5.3).
- On K3 squares with [F:Q] odd, Corollary 3.3 shows that no single cycle attains (3.1) at a place unless it acts on T by a rational scalar.

The last check says more. Write π(γ) = (x1, x2, (s(u))_s) for the coordinates of Lemma 3.1, and call a point with x1 = x2 = 1 and |s(u)| = 1 at every place a vertex of the region (3.1). Vertices with signs not all equal are mixed.

**Proposition 6.3.** Let [F:Q] = d be odd, and let C ⊆ Psef^2(X × X) be the closed convex cone generated by P1, P2, finitely many irreducible surfaces, and any pseudoeffective classes with zero T ⊗ T component. Then π(C) contains no mixed vertex.

*Proof.* Fix a mixed vertex with signs ε_s, and put φ(γ) = Σ_s ε_s s(u) − c (x1 + x2), with c chosen below. Consider a generator that acts on T by a rational scalar q. Then (3.1) gives |q| <= √(x1 x2), and |Σ_s ε_s| <= d − 2 because the signs are mixed. So φ <= ((d − 2)/2 − c)(x1 + x2). Each of the finitely many remaining surfaces acts by some u ∉ Q. By Corollary 3.3 it satisfies max_s |s(u)| <= ρ √(x1 x2) with ρ < 1, and then φ <= (dρ/2 − c)(x1 + x2). Generators with zero T ⊗ T component satisfy φ = −c(x1 + x2) <= 0. Let ρ_max < 1 be the largest of these ρ, or 0 if there are none, and take c = max(dρ_max/2, (d − 2)/2) < d/2. Then φ <= 0 on C, while φ = d − 2c > 0 at the vertex. ∎

The region cut out by (3.1) comes from necessary conditions, and we have not shown that its mixed vertices are images of classes in GStrong^2. Suppose that γ ∈ GStrong^2(X × X) has π(γ) a mixed vertex and that Psef^2 = GStrong^2 for X × X. Then γ is a limit of nonnegative combinations of classes of irreducible surfaces. Surfaces of bounded degree with respect to a fixed ample class are parametrised by a scheme of finite type (a Chow variety), so they have finitely many cohomology classes. By Proposition 6.3 the approximating combinations must therefore use surfaces of unbounded degree. We do not know whether such γ exist, or whether such families of surfaces exist on the K3 surfaces of [13, 14].

**6.3 Bearing on the Hodge conjecture.** Theorems A and B do not bear on the Hodge conjecture in either direction. Their main examples concern algebraic classes, and in the examples of [13, 14] the Hodge conjecture for X × X is known. They show that a positivity condition at one Hodge realisation does not see all the constraints on effective cycles, even where every Hodge class is algebraic.

The Galois hull would bear on it in the following variant. Suppose X = X_0 ⊗_K C for a smooth projective X_0 over a number field K ⊂ C, let h be the class of an ample line bundle on X_0, and suppose every Hodge class on X is absolute Hodge, as on abelian varieties [16]. Then τ ∈ Aut(C) sends a rational Hodge class β on X to a rational Hodge class τ(β) on X^τ, and τ(h) = h^τ is ample. Only finitely many pairs (X^τ, τ(β)) occur. Indeed X^τ depends only on τ restricted to K. For τ fixing K, the class τ(β) is obtained by applying τ to the coordinates of β in a K-basis of H_dR(X_0/K). These conjugates are rational Betti classes, so there are countably many of them. A coordinate transcendental over K would have uncountably many conjugates, so all coordinates lie in a finite extension of K, and their orbit is finite.

Write GStrong_Hdg^p(X) for the set of γ in the real span of rational Hodge classes such that τ(γ) ∈ Strong_cur^p(X^τ) for every τ, with τ extended real-linearly. Let β be a rational Hodge class. For each of the finitely many pairs (X^τ, τ(β)), a smooth closed representative of τ(β) plus N times the p-th power of a Kähler form in h^τ is strongly positive for N large, by compactness. Taking the largest N gives β + N h^p ∈ GStrong_Hdg^p(X). Suppose GStrong_Hdg^p(X) were contained in the closed cone in H^{2p}(X, R) generated by classes of effective cycles. Then the rational class β + N h^p would lie in the real span of algebraic classes, hence in their rational span, so β would be algebraic. The Hodge-class version of Question 6.2 thus contains the Hodge conjecture for such X, and we see no reason to expect it to be easier.
