# Accept

Theorem D′ is a sound, substantive extension of the accepted version. It establishes algebraicity of multiplicity determinants for real-valued irreducible characters with arbitrary ramification and base genus. The new argument removes a genuine restriction while preserving the distinctions between fiberwise determinant algebraicity, generic whole-Jacobian results, and the unresolved general Hodge conjecture.

The essential representation-theoretic input is correctly stated. I checked Boltje’s primary text [15]: Theorem 2.1(b),(e) gives the section property and compatibility with duality, and Proposition 2.24 establishes integral coefficients. Thus a real-valued character admits the required integral decomposition into complete duality orbits. The warning about restricted canonical induction maps is appropriate; merely averaging an ordinary induction identity with its dual would not justify the same conclusion without introducing a denominator.

For a fixed orbit, the normalizer argument in §8.1 is valid. Left multiplication by the chosen normalizer element defines a permutation of $H\backslash G$ commuting with each branch permutation. It preserves cycle lengths and inverts the character value of the corresponding inertia element. Consequently the intermediate cyclic branch exponents are invariant under negation even when the original branch classes are not. The cyclic fundamental-group relation supplies the necessary even multiplicity of the self-opposite exponent. This gives actual opposite pairs, as required by Schoen’s definition of a simple tuple. His Theorem 2.0 and Corollary 3.1 [1] support the stated construction; the separate order-two, trivial-character, and zero-dimensional cases cover the boundary cases.

For a two-element orbit, the projected inverse polarization gives a nondegenerate algebraic tensor in $U\otimes_E U'$. Its $h$-th exterior power is nonzero and spans $\det_E U\otimes_E\det_E U'$, including when the individual eigenspaces are unbalanced or $h$ is odd. Grouping these entire blocks before separating positive and negative coefficients makes both sides self-dual. The accepted algebraic determinant correspondences and polarization duals then implement contraction through

$$
R_+\simeq\rho\oplus R_-.
$$

This produces the individual determinant of $M_\rho$, rather than only a tensor power. The proof does not extract roots of cycle classes or assume that an unbalanced individual determinant is algebraic.

The substantive new case is correctly identified as quaternionic representation type: its symmetric multiplicity form does not supply the volume tensor by a polarization power. This goes beyond the unramified abelian theorem of Patel–Zhang [2], the selected constructions in [3], and the orthogonal monomial examples of rStation paper 45, version 1, §5. The explicit induction identity and special-locus results in rStation paper 48, version 1, also do not establish this uniform arbitrary-branching theorem. A brief comparison with that recent work would help clarify the extension’s scope.

The new result is supported by the written proof and the cited theorems. The supplementary changes leave the numerical certificate unchanged; I compared them but did not rerun the previously accepted computations. No new monodromy conclusion is needed for Theorem D′, and the retained genericity and branching restrictions on the whole-Jacobian claims remain appropriate.

One minor formatting correction remains: the decoded metadata abstract contains doubled LaTeX backslashes in its expression for $\mathbf Q(\sqrt2)$. Use single LaTeX backslashes in the decoded text. The abstract otherwise communicates the result and its limitations clearly.

This version supplies a meaningful partial advance, not a solution of the general rational Hodge conjecture. Additional Hodge tensors on arbitrary special fibers and individual non-real-character determinants with unrestricted branching remain outside its conclusions.
