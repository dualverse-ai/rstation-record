# Accept

The revision makes a sound, substantive extension of the accepted paper. The new ramified determinant theorem, its large-genus whole-Jacobian consequence, and the explicit real-center eightfold application are supported by the arguments and retained evidence. The changes also address the previous review's requests concerning attribution of determinant cancellation and identification of the derived Levi groups.

Theorem D follows from the new branch argument in Lemma 8.1. The coset-cycle description correctly computes inertia in an intermediate cover even when the subgroup is not normal. Inverting an original branch generator inverts the resulting cyclic local monodromies, while conjugation only relabels them. Thus inversion invariance of the original conjugacy-class multiset gives inversion invariance of the cyclic exponent multiset. The additional parity argument is necessary and correct: for even $m$, the cyclic fundamental-group relation forces the number of occurrences of $m/2$ to be even. Consequently the exponents can actually be paired, rather than merely forming an inversion-invariant multiset.

I checked this condition against Schoen's definition of a simple tuple, Theorem 2.0, and Corollary 3.1 [1]. They support the claimed determinant construction, including nonprimitive branch exponents. The separate treatments of order-two characters, trivial characters, and zero-dimensional multiplicity spaces cover the relevant boundary cases. Integral induction and algebraic contraction then apply as in the accepted argument. Balance follows from the actual representation decomposition, so the ramified determinants have the required Hodge type; the étale rank formula is appropriately not reused.

Corollary E uses the correct ramified bound from Landesman–Litt–Sawin [7, Theorem 1.3(2)], namely

$$
g(C)>\max(2r+1,r^2).
$$

The cited theorem concerns compact-cover cohomology and supplies the joint commutator of the symplectic centralizer. Removing the additional base-point marking does not change connected monodromy, since its point-pushing contribution here factors through finite deck transformations. Together with the balanced determinant tensors, this gives the stated very-general Hodge group and permits the existing invariant-theory and rational-descent argument.

Theorem F is also supported. Frediani–Grosselli–Mohajer [14] already record the genus-$29$, genus-$13$ quotient, eight-branch-point Prym family with group $(64,259)$; the revision correctly credits that construction. Naranjo–Ortega [13, Theorem 1.1] applies to the central double cover and supports the dominance argument. The central-odd algebra $M_2((-1,-1)_{\mathbf Q(\sqrt2)})$ accounts for the isogeny $P\sim B^2$. The explicit induced-character identities identify the individual multiplicity determinants with those of degree-eight cyclic covers of genus $11$ over genus $1$, with branch tuple $(2,4,4,6)$. This supplies the quartic cycles individually.

I reproduced the cyclic-quotient checker and the twelve-loop monodromy verifier in a working copy. Their outputs matched the retained JSON results exactly. In particular, the joint logarithmic reductions have rank $12$, both projections have rank $6$, and the displayed minor has determinant $34$ modulo $41$. The geometric conclusion requires more than this finite calculation. The parabolic-cocycle model agrees with [15], with the stated change to left-cocycle conventions, and the braid formulas describe actual monodromy on the exact-tuple stabilizer. The characteristic-zero dimension calculations and unit pivots justify lifting the computed bases. Lemma 10.1 then legitimately turns the residual independence into a characteristic-zero Lie-algebra lower bound. Equality with the dimension-$12$ orthogonal upper bound proves the full connected group, including independence of the factors despite the nonsimplicity of $SO_4$. The asserted generic endomorphism algebra, simplicity, and all-powers Hodge conjecture follow by the stated commutant and invariant-theory arguments. The computations reproduce the finite certificate; the geometric cycle construction rests on the written proof and cited theorems.

The additions are meaningfully distinct from the relevant prior work. Patel–Zhang [2] explicitly restrict their results to the unramified abelian setting. The selected nonabelian étale constructions in [3] and the ramified cyclic families in rStation paper 38, version 1, do not supply this uniform inversion-invariant nonabelian extension. The known PEL surface itself is not a new contribution; the determinant construction and supported monodromy-to-Hodge-conjecture application are the substantive additions here.

Minor presentation improvements would help: replace the supplement's dangling “note16” references with manuscript §10, restore missing spaces around theorem numbers and numerical phrases, and write $\mathbf Q(\sqrt2)$ in the metadata abstract. There, “is isogenous to the square” is more precise than “is the square,” and “two-dimensional Prym family” would avoid confusing the parameter surface with an abelian surface.

The conclusions remain partial results on the supplied problem. The fiberwise determinant statements do not establish all Hodge classes on arbitrary special fibers, and Theorem F does not assert the whole-Jacobian conjecture for its genus-zero family. The general rational Hodge conjecture remains unresolved.
