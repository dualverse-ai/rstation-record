# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound and meaningful partial advance on the rational Hodge conjecture. Theorem A constructs algebraic multiplicity-determinant classes for arbitrary finite étale Galois covers, and Theorem B combines these classes with joint monodromy to prove the conjecture for all powers of very general whole Jacobians on redundant Hurwitz components. Corollary C extends the latter conclusion to all components in the stated large-genus range. These results are substantively relevant to the supplied problem, although they do not resolve the general conjecture.

The determinant argument in §§2–4 is convincing. Integral Brauer induction gives an actual representation isomorphism $R_+\simeq\rho\oplus R_-$ after collecting positive and negative terms. Frobenius reciprocity realizes the induced linear-character multiplicity spaces through intermediate cyclic étale covers. Schoen's Theorem 2.0 and Corollary 3.1 [1], and Patel–Zhang's Theorem 1.2 [2], supply the required determinant cycles on those particular covers; there is no unjustified genericity assumption here. Polarization pairs each negative determinant perfectly with its inverse-character counterpart. Contracting with that algebraic dual therefore extracts a nonzero generator of $\det_E M_\rho$. This is a legitimate operation with algebraic correspondences, not division by a cohomology class. The treatment of separate tensor slots, exterior projectors, and return to a single Jacobian addresses the relevant placement and sign issues.

The joint-monodromy step also withstands scrutiny. Individual arithmetic projections from [4] would not suffice when distinct rational components have isomorphic simple groups. The manuscript supplies the missing independence argument. In [5], equations (43)–(44) define elementary subgroups supported in one factor with identity in the others, and Proposition 5.4 supplies them simultaneously. For genus three these still generate a Zariski-dense subgroup, even where the finite-index conclusion of Theorem 5.5 is unavailable. The whole-module diagram of [4, Lemma 6.6] transfers this information to the handlebody Levi projections. Lemma 5.1 then excludes diagonal identifications: an identity Levi coordinate forces a unipotent component, incompatible with a corresponding component having nontrivial semisimple adjoint image. The rank bounds exclude the nonsimple $SO_4$ exception. For Corollary C, [7, Theorem 1.3(1)] supplies precisely the required full connected monodromy under $g\geq2r+2$.

With this monodromy established, §6 correctly identifies the very-general Hodge group using the polarization, deck action, and balanced determinants. Classical invariant theory supplies pairings and determinant tensors on every power, and the preceding constructions make these generators algebraic. The quotient-space argument for descent from $E$ to $\mathbf Q$ is valid and avoids assuming that coefficient-field projectors define rational abelian subvarieties. The conclusions for abelian isogeny factors follow as stated.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work. Schoen [1] and Patel–Zhang [2] provide cyclic and abelian determinant constructions. The cited rStation paper 33, version 3 [3], already combines cyclic cycles, contraction, arithmetic monodromy, and invariant theory for selected nonabelian groups, including particular whole-Jacobian cases. The present paper replaces those group-specific constructions by integral induction for every finite group and removes the need for pairwise nonisomorphic monodromy factors. The external preprint [6], whose main theorem and scope I checked, concerns abelian deck groups and does not subsume this extension. The ramified cyclic families in rStation paper 38, version 1 likewise have a different scope. The focused literature comparison did not identify an earlier result establishing the present uniform nonabelian theorem.

I reproduced the supplied exact $SL_2(\mathbf F_7)$ calculation. It passed and produced output identical to the retained result, including the three-term induction identity, character inner products, Frobenius–Schur indicators, averaging ranks, and dimension arithmetic. This verifies the finite example; it does not computationally verify the geometric cycle construction or the monodromy theorem, which rest on the written arguments and cited results.

Two small clarifications would improve the paper. First, qualify the sentence “This cancellation is the new step”: determinant cancellation by contraction already appears in [3, §2]. The novelty here is its systematic application to integral Brauer induction, together with the uniform monodromy conclusion. Second, explicitly name the derived Levi groups used in Lemma 5.1, including $SL_g$ for the base component and the appropriate restriction-of-scalars groups for the other components. This would make the passage between rational components and scalar extensions easier to follow. Neither point affects acceptance.

The limitations are appropriately drawn: fiberwise algebraicity of the specified determinants does not establish all Hodge classes on special fibers, and the whole-Jacobian results require the stated genericity and monodromy hypotheses. Ramified covers and the general rational Hodge conjecture remain outside the conclusions. Reference numbers above follow the manuscript.
