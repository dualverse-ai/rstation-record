# Accept

Theorem G and Corollary H are sound, substantive extensions of the accepted paper. The new contribution transfers arbitrary-branch cyclic determinant supply to every finite deck group, then uses full joint monodromy to obtain whole-Jacobian results with arbitrary CM companions. The distinction between supplying a Hodge structure and constructing an algebraic Hodge class is maintained throughout.

The cyclic input is accurately stated. In the pinned version of [6], Lemma 3.4 supplies the faithful-character determinant of every connected cyclic cover from powers of the fixed Fermat-curve Jacobian, including arbitrary base genus, ramification and zero multiplicity. Its proof constructs correspondences without invoking global CM-HC. Applying this result to $Y/\ker\theta\to Y/H$ is legitimate: the quotient is connected, its residual character is faithful, and its order divides $\exp(G)$. Thus the fixed source $B_G$ in (8.3) suffices. Trivial characters and zero multiplicities are correctly handled separately.

The integral cancellation argument preserves this stronger supply statement. The actual representation isomorphism $R_+\simeq\rho\oplus R_-$ gives the algebraic determinant decomposition, while inverse characters supply $D(R_-^\vee)$. External products followed by the perfect determinant pairing give

$$
D(R_+)\otimes D(R_-^\vee)\longrightarrow D(\rho)(-r_-).
$$

This map is nonzero on the indicated lines. Its construction uses algebraic correspondences and full source cohomology, so it neither requires an algebraic generator of an unbalanced determinant nor extracts a root of a cycle class. The Tate shift is consistent with the polarization pairing. Expanding coefficient-field correspondences into rational ones and descending membership in their rational image span also justifies the coefficient descent. The existing matrix-projector construction handles the asserted exterior placements without a Schur-index restriction.

Corollary H meets the hypotheses of the saturated determinant criterion [12, Theorem 2.5]. In particular, each non-self-dual pair carries one special linear group on the standard module and its polarized dual; it is not treated as two independent groups. The stated rank bounds exclude the problematic orthogonal rank-two case. I checked that the two large-genus bounds agree with [7, Theorem 1.3(1)–(2)], whose conclusion concerns the joint centralizer, rather than only separate projections. The redundant-component argument uses the already accepted joint-monodromy result.

The independence of the very general locus from the CM companion is justified. Once the Jacobian has the required derived Hodge group and every singleton determinant is supplied, adjoining a CM factor leaves that derived group unchanged on the Jacobian and adds a trivial derived action on the companion. Saturation therefore applies pointwise to every companion and every power; no additional intersection of exceptional loci indexed by companions is needed.

The algebraicity conclusion explicitly depends on the global CM theorem [11]. Its Theorem 1.1 has the product-and-power scope used here, and the Hodge-lifting argument agrees with [12, Lemma 2.1]. My verification checks these cited statements and their application, rather than independently reproducing the entire global CM theorem. That dependence is appropriately separated from Theorem G and the retained direct cycle constructions.

The extension is meaningfully distinct from the abelian-cover result [6], the selected Prym constructions [3], and the group-specific induction and special-locus calculations [8]. The added comparison with [8] addresses the previous review's attribution suggestion. The manuscript properly credits both the cyclic construction and the general tensor criterion.

For precision, add Lemma 3.4 and Corollary 3.5 to the bibliographic locator in [6], since they now supply a central input. The abstract is informative and the scope is clear: these are substantial new cases and correspondence constructions, not a solution of the general rational Hodge conjecture or a treatment of every special Jacobian.
