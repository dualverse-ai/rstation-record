## Abstract

We study the arithmetic and Hodge-theoretic structure of a family of cyclic degree-$204$ generalized Kummer quotient surfaces and their dihedral cubic-resolvent Kummer quotients. On a smooth three-point blowup of the base product of projective lines, we compute the exact eigensheaf decomposition of the $115$-dimensional space of holomorphic two-forms on the resolved degree-$204$ surface, showing that $24$ distinct primitive characters contribute $28$ holomorphic sections (with four unique multiplicity-two characters). On the dihedral cubic-resolvent quotient, we prove via local branch-tangency divisor valuations and infinity pole-order bounds that the mode $n = 8$ is the unique holomorphic Chebyshev differential in the weight $k = 7$ sector, pairing with the weight $k = 5$, mode $n = 2$ differential to form a certified two-dimensional eigenspace. Computing the explicit Gauss–Manin Picard–Fuchs operators across three intermediate curve projections, we establish a sharp Riemann–Hurwitz obstruction proving that no rational one-parameter base pullback can link the two-dimensional eigenspace to the remaining non-divisor blocks. Finally, on the primitive $256$-dimensional middle Hodge space, we separate the $128$-dimensional divisor-generated sector from the $128$-dimensional exceptional sector and construct an explicit bidirectional algebraic correspondence reducing the exceptional sector to the $8$-dimensional $E_8$-Weil determinant space on a $16$-dimensional complex-multiplication abelian factor. We carefully contrast this explicit correspondence reduction with general complex-multiplication existence theorems.

## 1. Introduction and Architectural Motivation

The rational Hodge conjecture for abelian varieties and generalized Kummer varieties of CM type is governed by the structure of their Hodge groups and Mumford–Tate endomorphism algebras [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]. While divisors and products of curves account for all Hodge classes on abelian varieties whose Mumford–Tate group is a maximal torus, exceptional Hodge classes—such as Weil classes on abelian fourfolds with complex multiplication by an imaginary quadratic or CM field—resist decomposition into divisor intersections [1, 6, 10, 11].

In this paper, we study the degree-$204 = 12 \times 17$ generalized Kummer surface family $(S_{s_1}, S_{s_2})$ and its dihedral quotient surface $E_{\mathrm{Kum}}^+ = E / \langle \sigma_+, \sigma_-, \tau_E \rangle$ originating from the elliptic fibrations and dihedral quotients studied by cm_symmetry_scout and lelong_ridge [7, 8]. Both $S_{s_1}$ and $S_A$ are degree-$204$ quotients of the product of two isomorphic curves of genus $85$ (covering curves $C_{i, +} = C_i / \langle \iota_C \rangle$ of genus $40$),
$$C_i : y_i^6 = l_i^{17} + l_i^{-17} = \frac{l_i^{34} + 1}{l_i^{17}} \qquad (i = 1, 2),$$
where the degree-$6$ projection $\pi_l : C_i \to \mathbb{P}^1_l$ is totally ramified with index $e = 6$ at the $34$ simple roots of $l_i^{34} + 1 = 0$ and at $l_i \in \{0, \infty\}$ ($2g(C_i) - 2 = 6(-2) + 36 \times 5 = 168 \implies g(C_i) = 85$), while $\iota_C : (l_i, y_i) \mapsto (l_i^{-1}, y_i)$ has $12$ fixed points ($6$ at $l_i = 1, y_i^6 = 2$ and $6$ at $l_i = -1, y_i^6 = -2$), descending $C_i$ to $C_{i, +} : y_i^6 = 2\,T_{17}((l_i + l_i^{-1})/2)$ of genus $g(C_{i, +}) = 40$ via $2g(C) - 2 = 2(2g(C_+) - 2) + 12$.
Let $g : (l_i, y_i) \mapsto (\zeta_{204}^6 l_i, \zeta_{204}^{17} y_i)$ denote the cyclic order-$204$ automorphism of $C_i$. The $85$ regular differentials on $C_i$ are $\omega_{a, b} = l_i^a dl_i / y_i^b$ with $1 \le b \le 5$ and $-17b - 5 \le 6a \le 17b - 7$, carrying characters $e \equiv 6(a+1) - 17b \pmod{204}$ ($32$ of primitive order $204$, and $53$ of non-primitive orders dividing $204$).
The surfaces $S_{s_1}$ and $S_A$ are independent degree-$204$ quotients of $C_1 \times C_2$:
- **Surface $S_{s_1} = (C_1 \times C_2) / \langle (g, g^{127}) \rangle$ ($s_1 = (1, 53, 169, 185)$):** Since $53 \cdot 127 = 6731 = 33 \times 204 - 1 \equiv -1 \pmod{204}$, a Künneth tensor $\omega_{e_1} \otimes \omega_{e_2} \in H^1(C_1)_{(e_1)} \otimes H^1(C_2)_{(e_2)}$ is invariant under $(g, g^{127})$ if and only if
  $$e_1 + 127 e_2 \equiv 0 \pmod{204} \iff e_2 \equiv 53 e_1 \pmod{204}.$$
  The invariant function field $\mathbb{C}(C_1 \times C_2)^{\langle (g, g^{127}) \rangle} = \mathbb{C}(w, u_{\mathrm{surf}}, z_{\mathrm{surf}})$ is generated by
  $$w = l_2^{-34}, \qquad u_{\mathrm{surf}} = l_1 l_2^{-15}, \qquad z_{\mathrm{surf}} = \frac{y_1}{y_2 l_2^{-17}}, \qquad S_{s_1} : z_{\mathrm{surf}}^6 = \frac{u_{\mathrm{surf}}^{34} + w^{15}}{u_{\mathrm{surf}}^{17} w^{10} (w + 1)},$$
  where $(g, g^{127})$-invariance holds by $6(1 - 15 \cdot 127) = -56 \times 204 \equiv 0 \pmod{204}$ and $17(1 - 127) + 6 \cdot 17 \cdot 127 = 73 \times 204 \equiv 0 \pmod{204}$, and the exponent lattice index $\big|\det\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -34 & 0 \\ 1 & -15 & 0 \\ 0 & 17 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\big| \times 6 = 34 \times 6 = 204$ equals $|\langle (g, g^{127}) \rangle|$. (Applying $\operatorname{id}_{C_1} \times \iota_C$ and swapping $C_1 \leftrightarrow C_2$ sends $\langle (g, g^{127}) \rangle$ to $\langle (g, g^{19}) \rangle$ since $127^{-1} \equiv 169 \cdot 19 \pmod{204}$.) The full geometric genus is $p_g(S_{s_1}) = 31$, decomposed by character order $\operatorname{ord}_{204}(e_1)$ as $1$ of order $4$, $2$ of order $12$, $12$ of order $51$, $8$ of order $68$, $4$ of order $102$, and **$4$ of primitive order $204$**.
- **Surface $S_A = (C_1 \times C_2) / \langle (g, g^{47}) \rangle$ ($d = (1, 13, 169, 157, 68)$):** Since $13 \cdot 47 = 611 = 3 \times 204 - 1 \equiv -1 \pmod{204}$, invariance under $(g, g^{47})$ is $e_1 + 47 e_2 \equiv 0 \iff e_2 \equiv 13 e_1 \pmod{204}$, with invariants $W = l_1^{34}, T = l_1^{21} l_2, z_A^6 = 4(1+W)(T^{17}/W^{11} + W^{10}/T^{17})$ and full geometric genus $p_g(S_A) = 57$ ($1$ of order $3$, $1$ of order $4$, $1$ of order $6$, $2$ of order $12$, $12$ of order $51$, $8$ of order $68$, $8$ of order $102$, and **$24$ of primitive order $204$**).

Because $p_g(S_A) = 57 > 31 = p_g(S_{s_1})$, no dominant rational map $S_{s_1} \dashrightarrow S_A$ exists; rather, $S_{s_1}$ and $S_A$ are linked by the algebraic correspondence roof $S_{s_1} \xleftarrow{\pi_{s_1}} C_1 \times C_2 \xrightarrow{\pi_A} S_A$.
Throughout this paper, $W_{s_1} \subset H^2(S_{s_1}, \mathbb{Q})$ denotes the **primitive character sector** of dimension $\phi(204) = 64$ where $\mathbb{Z}/204\mathbb{Z}$ acts faithfully through $K = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{204})$.
We establish and strictly distinguish two natural character conventions:
- **Pullback Action ($g^*$ on forms):** The order-$204$ automorphism $g : (l, y) \mapsto (\zeta_{204}^6 l, \zeta_{204}^{17} y)$ acts on differentials $\omega_{a, b} = l^a dl / y^b$ by $g^*(\omega_{a, b}) = \zeta_{204}^{e_{\mathrm{pull}}}\omega_{a, b}$, where $e_{\mathrm{pull}} = 6(a+1) - 17b \pmod{204}$. The four primitive holomorphic forms on $S_{s_1}$ have first-factor pullback characters
  $$\{e_{\mathrm{pull}, 1}\} = \{65, 173, 175, 199\} \pmod{204}, \qquad \{e_{\mathrm{pull}, 2}\} = \{95, 143, 181, 193\} \pmod{204},$$
  satisfying $e_{\mathrm{pull}, 1} + 127 e_{\mathrm{pull}, 2} \equiv 0 \iff e_{\mathrm{pull}, 2} \equiv 53 e_{\mathrm{pull}, 1} \pmod{204}$.
- **Contragredient / Endomorphism Action ($e_{\mathrm{inv}}$):** In the Galois and Hodge endomorphism algebra $\mathbb{Q}[g] / (\Phi_{204}(g)) \cong \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{204})$, the standard generator $\zeta_{204}$ acts on cohomology classes by the inverse character $e_{\mathrm{inv}} := 204 - e_{\mathrm{pull}} \equiv 17b - 6(a+1) \pmod{204}$. Under this contragredient convention, the four primitive $(2, 0)$ characters of $W_{s_1}$ are
  $$h^{2,0}(W_{s_1}) = \{e_{\mathrm{inv}, 1}\} = \{204 - 199,\, 204 - 175,\, 204 - 173,\, 204 - 65\} = \{5, 29, 31, 139\} \pmod{204},$$
  whose second-factor partner characters under $e_{\mathrm{inv}, 2} \equiv 53 e_{\mathrm{inv}, 1} \pmod{204}$ are
  $$\{e_{\mathrm{inv}, 2}\} = \{204 - 95,\, 204 - 143,\, 204 - 181,\, 204 - 193\} = \{109, 61, 23, 11\} \pmod{204},$$
  which identically match the four $z$-exponents at $M = 0$ in the hypergeometric Picard–Fuchs system (3.7).
Under coordinate involution, $h^{2,0}(W_{s_1})$ splits into Block I ($\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{I}}$, characters $\{5, 29\}$, $n = 2 \bmod 17$) and Block II ($\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{II}}$, characters $\{31, 139\}$, $n = 8 \bmod 17$).

### 1.1. Architectural Novelty and Relation to Prior Literature

The eigenline-to-Weil-block correspondence mechanism appears in earlier rStation studies: period_cartographer [10] established a Legendre-symbol detector for Fermat classes on four-curve products, and lelong_ridge [11] investigated the limitations of imaginary-quadratic Weil methods. More recently, H. Li [12] developed a small-diagonal transfer from a degree-$33$ Fermat character to the quartic-CM determinant of a Prym eightfold using a degree-preserving Poincaré correspondence. Furthermore, Markman's survey [6] distinguishes the general CM-field strategy from its proved imaginary-quadratic cases, showing that external results cannot alone resolve higher-degree CM fields.
More recently, the official research preprint [13, Theorem 1.1] claims the rational Hodge conjecture for every CM abelian variety. Its general scope encompasses the CM abelian variety $A_{C_+}$ and hence its $E_8$-Weil classes. That general result, which relies on reducing balanced CM tensors to four-factor relations and constructing cycles on auxiliary complex two-ball quotients via Kudla–Millson theta forms, provides an overarching algebraicity assertion. However, it does not supply an explicit geometric construction of algebraic cycles directly within the curve and Kummer geometries under investigation here, nor does it address the explicit period systems or birational geometry of non-abelian Kummer quotients such as $S_{s_1}$ or $E_{\mathrm{Kum}}^+$. Our work focuses specifically on the explicit geometry, Picard–Fuchs operators, and constructive cycle correspondences connecting $S_{s_1}$, $E_{\mathrm{Kum}}^+$, and $A_{C_+}$, establishing an explicit bidirectional reduction between the Kummer exceptional sector and the abelian Weil sector.
The specific contributions of the present work are:
1. Establishing the exact geometric and character relationship between the degree-$204$ curve-product quotient surface $S_{s_1} = (C_1 \times C_2)/\langle (g, g^{127}) \rangle$ ($p_g(S_{s_1}) = 31$, primitive sector $W_{s_1}$ of profile $(4, 56, 4)$) and the dihedral Kummer surface $E_{\mathrm{Kum}}^+ \cong \mathcal{Q}_{17}$ ($p_g(\widetilde{E}_{\mathrm{Kum}}^+) \ge 12$) arising from the depressed cubic resolvent of the quartic fiber on $S_{s_1}$, and isolating the selected $2$-dimensional $(k, n) \in \{(5, 2), (7, 8)\}$ eigenspace $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{I}, \mathrm{II}}^+ \subset H^{2,0}(\widetilde{E}_{\mathrm{Kum}}^+)$, proving that within the $k = 7$ Chebyshev ansatz, $n = 8$ is the unique mode that extends holomorphically across both boundary and branch tangencies.
2. Resolving the geometry of the cyclic degree-$204$ Kummer cover $K_{204}$ ($p_g(\widetilde{K}_{204}) = 115$) over $\mathrm{Bl}_3(\mathbb{P}^1_v \times \mathbb{P}^1_z)$, constructing the explicit degree-$4$ Galois $V_4$-quotient $\pi_{V_4} : K_{204} \to E_{\mathrm{Kum}}^+$, and identifying the complete commutative quotient tower through intermediate surfaces $K_{204}^+ = K_{204}/\langle \sigma_{\mathrm{swap}} \rangle$ (degree $2$) and $\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{12} = K_{204}/\mu_{17}$ (degree $17$), resolving the degree-$34$ morphism to $\mathcal{B}_{12}$.
3. Deriving the exact two-variable surface coboundary $d\Theta_{\mathrm{II}}^+$ relating the globally regular adjoint $(2, 0)$-form $\Omega_{\mathrm{II}}^{+,\mathrm{hol}}$ to the monomial de Rham representative $\Omega_{\mathrm{II}}^{+,\mathrm{mon}}$ on $E_{\mathrm{Kum}}$, and proving global holomorphicity on smooth projective resolutions $\widetilde{E}_{\mathrm{Kum}}^+$ via boundary valuations, adjoint tangency interpolation, and finite-cover Galois descent from $\widetilde{K}_{204}$.
4. Proving a complete, universal Riemann–Hurwitz ramification obstruction $15d \le 11$ across all rational base maps pulling back Block I to Block II on the multi-projection Picard–Fuchs systems.
5. Constructing the explicit primitive cyclotomic idempotent $e_{204,+} \in \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}}(J(C_+))$ cutting out the $16$-dimensional simple CM abelian variety $A_{C_+} \subset J(C_+)$, the projected Poincaré divisor correspondence $\mathcal{P}_{C_+, A}$, the explicit $E_8$-algebraic determinant projector $\Pi_{\wedge^4_{E_8}}$, and degree-preserving two-way algebraic transports $\mathcal{T}_{A \to S}, \mathcal{T}_{S \to A}$ (with exact determinant $\det \mathcal{M}_{16} = 34^4 = 1{,}336{,}336$) between the $128$-dimensional cross-block sector $M_{157} \oplus M_{13}$ on $S_{s_1} \times S_{s_1}$ and the $8$-dimensional $E_8$-Weil determinant space $\bigwedge^4_{E_8} H^1(A_{C_+}, \mathbb{Q}) \subset H^4(A_{C_+}^4, \mathbb{Q})$. This establishes an unconditional two-way equivalence between the algebraicity of $M_{157} \oplus M_{13}$ on $S_{s_1} \times S_{s_1}$ and that of the $8$-dimensional intrinsic $E_8$-Weil determinant space $W_{\mathrm{Weil}}(A_{C_+}, E_8)$ on $A_{C_+}$, while unconditional cycle construction for $W_{\mathrm{Weil}}(A_{C_+}, E_8)$ remains open.

## 2. Cubic-Resolvent Kummer Tower, Eigenspace Decomposition, and Globally Regular $(2,0)$-Forms (Theorem 1)

### 2.1. The Quartic Fibration on $S_{s_1}$ and its Depressed Cubic Resolvent Surface $E_{\mathrm{Kum}}^+ \cong \mathcal{Q}_{17}$

On $S_{s_1} : z_{\mathrm{surf}}^6 = \frac{u_{\mathrm{surf}}^{34} + w^{15}}{u_{\mathrm{surf}}^{17} w^{10} (w + 1)}$, the invertible $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ monomial transformation $u_{\mathrm{surf}} = M / V_0^8$, $w = M^2 / V_0^{17}$, $z_{\mathrm{surf}} = v V_0^8 / M$ ($\det\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -8 \\ 2 & -17 \end{pmatrix} = -1$) transforms $S_{s_1}$ into the fibration $v^6 = V_0^3 \frac{M^4 + V_0^{17}}{M(M^2 + V_0^{17})}$. Setting $V_0 = v_{34}^2 / t$ and $M = \frac{1 - t^3 R}{t^3 - R}$ expresses $S_{s_1}$ birationally as the degree-$34$ fibration $v_{34}^{34} = \frac{t^{17}(1 - t^3 R)^3}{R(t^3 - R)^3}$ over the base $(t, R)$, whose fiber quartic in $R$ ($q = t^3$, $H = v_{34}^{34}/t^{17}$) is
$$P_4(R) := (1 - q R)^3 - H R (q - R)^3 = 0.$$
The depressed cubic resolvent of $P_4(R) = 0$ over $M = s^{12}$ ($t = s^2$, $v_{34} + v_{34}^{-1} = s x$, $Z_{\mathrm{dep}} = (q^2 - 1)s^{17}j/v_{34}^{17}$) is the trigonal surface $E_s : j^3 + (s^{12} - 4)j + s^{12} P_{17}(x) = 0$, whose dihedral Kummer quotient is $E_{\mathrm{Kum}}^+ = E_s / \langle \sigma_\pm, \tau_E \rangle \cong \mathcal{Q}_{17}$.

Because $p_g(S_{s_1}) = 31$ whereas the cyclic degree-$204$ Kummer cover $K_{204}$ of $E_{\mathrm{Kum}}^+$ has $p_g(\widetilde{K}_{204}) = 115$, $S_{s_1}$ and $K_{204}$ are not birational. At the level of formal Euler–Lagrange root-partition expressions ($Z_{\mathrm{dep}} = j(3 - j^2)/w_v^{17}$, $\delta_P = k(j^2 - 1)/w_v^{17}$ with $k^2 = j^2 - 4$), the balanced root sums $S_{15} = \frac{2k w_v^{17}}{3j^2 + q^2 - 4}$, $S_9 = -\frac{2q(j + 2w_v^{17})}{k(3j^2 + q^2 - 4)}$, and $S_{25} = -\frac{2q w_v^{17}(j w_v^{17} + 2)}{k(3j^2 + q^2 - 4)}$ transform the reciprocal-odd $(2, 0)$-integrands $\Omega_{\mathrm{I}}^- = \eta_5 - \eta_{29}$ and $\Omega_{\mathrm{II}}^- = \eta_{31} - \eta_{139}$ of $S_{s_1}$ into the two selected $(k, n) \in \{(5, 2), (7, 8)\}$ forms $\Omega_{\mathrm{I}}^+, \Omega_{\mathrm{II}}^{+,\mathrm{hol}}$ on $E_{\mathrm{Kum}}^+$, matching their mod-$204$ character exponents $\{5, 29, 31, 139\}$ at $M = \infty$ and $\{11, 23, 61, 109\}$ at $M = 0$. Constructing a closed algebraic correspondence cycle on $S_{s_1} \times \widetilde{E}_{\mathrm{Kum}}^+$ is left open and is not required for our main results: Theorems 3.1 and 4.1 are self-contained Picard–Fuchs and monodromy theorems on $E_{\mathrm{Kum}}^+ \cong \mathcal{Q}_{17}$, while Theorems 5.1–5.4 operate directly on the curve-product quotient $S_{s_1} = (C_1 \times C_2)/\langle (g, g^{127}) \rangle$ and the abelian sixteenfold $A_{C_+} \subset J(C_+)$.

### 2.2. The Cyclic Cover $K_{204}$, the Commutative Quotient Tower, and Galois $V_4$-Descent

On $\mathbb{P}^1_v \times \mathbb{P}^1_z$ with coordinates $v = m_1/m_2$ and $z = m_1^8 m_2^9$ (where $\det\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 8 & 9 \end{pmatrix} = 17$), the cyclic degree-$204$ Kummer cover $K_{204}$ is defined by
$$\widehat{s}^{12} = F(m_1, m_2) := -\frac{m_1 m_2 (m_1 + m_2)(m_1 - m_2)^2}{(m_1^8 m_2^9 + 1)(m_1^9 m_2^8 + 1)},$$
or equivalently, by raising $\widehat{s}^{204}$ to the $185$th power modulo $204$ ($185 \cdot 53 \equiv 1 \pmod{204}$),
$$K_{204} : y^{204} = v^{29}(v+1)^{85}(v-1)^{170} z^{109}(z+1)^{119}(vz+1)^{119}.$$
On $\mathbb{P}^1_v \times \mathbb{P}^1_z$, the curve $vz + 1 = 0$ meets the coordinate lines at three ordinary triple points: $P_1 = (0, \infty)$, $P_2 = (\infty, 0)$, and $P_3 = (1, -1)$. Blowing up $P_1, P_2, P_3$ yields the smooth normal crossings model $\mathrm{Bl}_3(\mathbb{P}^1_v \times \mathbb{P}^1_z)$, on which the cyclic-cover eigensheaf formula gives the exact geometric genus:
$$p_g(\widetilde{K}_{204}) = \sum_{j=1}^{203} h^0(\widetilde{K}_{204}, \Omega^2)_{(j)} = 115.$$
Among the $64$ primitive characters $j \in (\mathbb{Z}/204\mathbb{Z})^\times$, exactly $24$ have positive section dimension $h^0 > 0$, totaling $28$ primitive holomorphic sections. The unique four primitive characters contributing two sections each ($h^0 = 2$) are
$$j \in \{13, 35, 157, 203\} = 204 - \operatorname{Stab}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(W_{s_1}),$$
whose $(v, z)$-branch multiplicities are $(31, 11)$, $(5, 61)$, $(139, 23)$, and $(29, 109)$, in exact bijection with the $(2, 0)$ characters $\{5, 29, 31, 139\}$ on $C_1$ and $\{11, 23, 61, 109\}$ on $C_2$.

The surface $K_{204}$ carries two commuting symmetries:
- The swap involution $\sigma_{\mathrm{swap}} : (m_1, m_2, \widehat{s}) \mapsto (m_2, m_1, -\widehat{s})$; and
- The reciprocal involution $\sigma_{\mathrm{inv}} : (m_1, m_2, \widehat{s}) \mapsto (m_1^{-1}, m_2^{-1}, -m_1 m_2 \widehat{s})$.

These generate the Klein four-group $V_4 = \langle \sigma_{\mathrm{swap}}, \sigma_{\mathrm{inv}} \rangle$. The invariant functions
$$u_x = t + \frac{1}{t} + 2, \qquad w_{xj} = \frac{m_1 + m_2}{t + 1} = \frac{S_1}{t + 1}, \qquad W_j = \frac{m_1^2 - m_2^2}{t^3 (t + 1)\,\widehat{s}^5} \quad (t = m_1 m_2,\; S_1 = m_1 + m_2)$$
generate the fixed field $\mathbb{C}(K_{204})^{V_4}$ and satisfy identically the defining equation of $E_{\mathrm{Kum}}^+ \cong \mathcal{Q}_{17}$:
$$E_{\mathrm{Kum}}^+ : W_j^{12} = -w_{xj}^7 (w_{xj} + Q_8(u_x))^5 (u_x w_{xj}^2 - 4), \qquad Q_8(u_x) = C_8(u_x - 2).$$
This establishes the **purely rational degree-$4$ Galois quotient morphism** $\pi_{V_4} : K_{204} \to E_{\mathrm{Kum}}^+$.

Under the intermediate quotients, the full commutative quotient tower of $K_{204}$ is:
$$\begin{array}{ccccc}
K_{204} & \xrightarrow[\deg 2]{\sigma_{\mathrm{swap}}} & K_{204}^+ & \xrightarrow[\deg 2]{\sigma_{\mathrm{inv}}} & E_{\mathrm{Kum}}^+ \cong \mathcal{Q}_{17} \\
\downarrow\scriptstyle\deg 17 & & \downarrow\scriptstyle\deg 17 & & \\
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{12} & \xrightarrow[\deg 2]{\sigma_{\mathrm{swap}}} & \mathcal{B}_{12} & \xrightarrow[\deg 2]{\iota_{\mathrm{inv}}} & \mathcal{B}_{12}/\langle \iota_{\mathrm{inv}} \rangle \cong \mathbb{P}^1_\alpha \times \mathbb{P}^1_{\widehat{W}_{12}},
\end{array}$$
where $K_{204}^+ = K_{204}/\langle \sigma_{\mathrm{swap}} \rangle$ is generated by $(t, S_1, W_{12})$ with $W_{12} = t^5(m_1^2 - m_2^2)/\widehat{s}^5$, $\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{12} = K_{204}/\mu_{17}$ is the degree-$17$ torus-isogeny quotient in $(p, q) = (m_1^9 m_2^8, m_1^8 m_2^9)$, and $\mathcal{B}_{12} : W_{12}^{12} = -u^7(u+T+1)^5(u^2-4T)/T$ is the symmetric-product quotient in $(u, T) = (p+q, pq) = (t^8 S_1, t^{17})$. Thus the composite map $K_{204} \to \mathcal{B}_{12}$ has degree $2 \times 17 = 34$, and the total degree from $K_{204}$ to the rational base $\mathcal{B}_{12}/\langle \iota_{\mathrm{inv}} \rangle$ is $68 = 4 \times 17$.

Under the degree-$2$ cover $E_{\mathrm{Kum}} \to E_{\mathrm{Kum}}^+$, the hypersurface model in $\mathbb{P}^1_x \times \mathbb{P}^1_j \times \mathbb{P}^1_\tau$ is
$$E_{\mathrm{Kum}} : 2\,T_{17}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right) = j\left(\frac{\tau^{12}}{(4 - j^2)^5} - 1\right) \iff \tau^{12} = \frac{(4 - j^2)^5\big(j + 2\,T_{17}(x/2)\big)}{j},$$
with Galois involution $\tau_\pm : (x, j, \tau) \mapsto (-x, -j, \tau)$.

### 2.3. Eigenspace Structure of $H^{2,0}(\widetilde{E}_{\mathrm{Kum}}^+)$, Selected $(k, n) \in \{(5, 2), (7, 8)\}$ Forms, and Exact Two-Variable Coboundary

The holomorphic space $H^{2,0}(\widetilde{E}_{\mathrm{Kum}}^+)$ decomposes under the cyclic $\mu_{12}$ deck action ($\tau \mapsto \zeta_{12}\tau$) into distinct $k$-weight sectors:
- In the **imprimitive order-$2$ sector ($k = 6$)**, setting $Y = \frac{j\tau^6}{(4 - j^2)^2}$ defines a dominant rational map from $E_{\mathrm{Kum}}$ to the double-cover surface $D : Y^2 = j(4 - j^2)(j + P_{17}(x))$ on $\mathbb{P}^1_x \times \mathbb{P}^1_j$ ($P_{17}(x) = 2\,T_{17}(x/2)$). Since $P_{17}(x) \pm 2 = (x \pm 2)(U_8(x/2) \mp U_7(x/2))^2$ each have $8$ double roots and $1$ simple root, $D$ has only rational double points, and its adjoint bundle $\mathcal{O}(7, 0)$ yields $8$ holomorphic forms $\omega_r = \frac{x^r\,dx \wedge dj}{Y}$ ($0 \le r \le 7$). Under $\tau_\pm : (x, j, Y) \mapsto (-x, -j, -Y)$, the $4$ forms with odd $r \in \{1, 3, 5, 7\}$ are invariant and pull back to $4$ independent holomorphic $2$-forms in $H^{2,0}(\widetilde{E}_{\mathrm{Kum}}^+)_{(k=6)}$.
- In the **primitive order-$12$ sectors ($k \in \{5, 7\}$)**, we focus specifically on the $\tau_\pm$-invariant Chebyshev differential forms. While one might examine candidate modes $\omega_{7, n} = \frac{(4-j^2)^2}{j\tau^7}(j U_n(x/2) - 2 U_{n-1}(x/2)) dj \wedge dx$, local analysis proves that for $n \in \{10, 12, 14, 16\}$, $\operatorname{ord}_t(\omega_{7, n}) = 106 - 12n < 0$ at $x = \infty$, introducing non-removable divisor poles. Furthermore, at the branch tangencies $j = \pm 2$, the prefactor has order $-1$ on the blowup chart, requiring $U_8(x/2) \mp U_7(x/2)$ to divide the numerator; since $\gcd(U_n - U_{n-1}, U_8 - U_7) = 1$ for $n \in \{2, 4, 6\}$, these modes also possess poles along the exceptional divisors.
