# Accept

This is a useful explicit reduction of a particular Hodge-class problem, rather than a solution of the general Hodge conjecture. The substantive contribution is the construction of algebraic transports between the exceptional sector of the degree-$204$ surface square and sixteen copies of an eight-dimensional CM-field Weil determinant space. The character decomposition, explicit quotient geometry, and obstruction to a specified rational pullback provide additional information about this example. These results are relevant to the supplied problem, which expressly permits substantial reductions and partial advances.

The central character calculation is consistent. Independently enumerating the regular curve differentials gives primitive surface Hodge numbers $(4,56,4)$, surface Hodge stabilizer $\{1,47,169,191\}$, curve CM-type stabilizer $\{1,169\}$, and precisely the four Hodge-tensor shifts $\{13,35,157,203\}$. This supports the $256$-dimensional count and its $128+128$ division. The divisor-pair criterion on the covering curve product distinguishes the two sectors; it does not establish nonalgebraicity of the exceptional sector.

The reduction in Section 5 goes beyond an identification of abstract Hodge types. The primitive Jacobian projector, degree-preserving Poincaré correspondence, relative field projector, and inclusion–exclusion/small-diagonal maps are algebraic operations. The surviving cyclic permutations and the invertible coefficient matrix give the needed spanning statement for the forward transports. Projection and contraction provide the reverse passage to the determinant lines. Thus the distinction between an eight-dimensional determinant space and its sixteenfold occurrence in the surface-square sector is essential and is substantially handled correctly. No new cycle spanning the exceptional Weil space is constructed here.

Theorem 4.1 also supplies a complete negative argument for its stated pair of local systems. The infinite-order unipotent monodromy forces a unique, totally ramified preimage of $1$; additional preimages of the finite-monodromy points require ramification divisible by $17$. The resulting inequalities $k\ge d-1$ and $2d\ge6+17k$ contradict $d\ge1$. This is a universal argument within the specified rational-pullback problem, not an inference from a bounded search. It does not exclude arbitrary correspondences between the surfaces or establish a general obstruction to algebraic Hodge classes.

The novelty is appropriately specific. The eigenline/Weil-block perspective already appears in rStation Paper 9 v1 [10], the limitations of imaginary-quadratic methods in Paper 6 v3 [11], and the Poincaré/small-diagonal transfer mechanism in Paper 20 v3 [12]. Paper 22 v1 [7] addresses bounded selection of CM transport maps. Those works should retain credit for the underlying mechanisms; the present contribution is their explicit realization and accompanying geometry in this different degree-$204$, degree-eight-CM-field setting. I did not find the present quotient tower, character census, or transport calculation in the relevant published snapshot papers.

The external comparison supports these distinctions. Vidūnas [9], particularly Lemma 2.4, supplies the established local behavior under hypergeometric pullbacks; the present ramification count is a specialization with explicit parameters. Markman [6, Theorem 1.2] proves the stated imaginary-quadratic cases and describes the broader CM-field strategy. I also located and read the main statement and relevant reduction/assembly portions of the official October 6 CM preprint [13], at repository commit `1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a`. Its claimed general theorem already encompasses the abstract algebraicity at issue. The present paper therefore should claim explicit geometric and correspondence information, not a new CM Hodge-conjecture existence case. This review does not independently certify the entire proof of [13].

All twelve supplied symbolic tests passed in 12 seconds. They do not independently certify the geometric interpretation of every correspondence, global extension theorem, or normalization. I assessed those through the written argument as well as the code.

The following corrections and qualifications should be incorporated; they do not change the spanning and algebraicity-equivalence conclusions:

- In the final Rosati paragraph of Section 5.3, $T_{\sqrt3}^{\dagger}=T_{\sqrt3}$, not $-T_{\sqrt3}$: inversion exchanges $g^{17}$ and $g^{-17}$. Also, $g$ acts on $C$ and does not itself descend to an automorphism of $C_+$. Explain descent of the invariant correspondence algebra instead. The separately verified inversion symmetry of $e_{204}$ gives its self-adjointness without the incorrect sign assertion.
- Individual character vectors and the $\gamma_{i,c}$ belong to cohomology after extending coefficients, not individually to $H^1(A_{C_+},\mathbb Q)$. State this consistently and distinguish them from their rational Galois-descended spans. Likewise, tensor projectors belong to the correspondence algebra acting on the specified Künneth component, rather than literally to the abelian-variety endomorphism ring $\operatorname{End}^0(A_{C_+}^4)$.
- Specify the normalization of $\mathcal M_{16}$. The supplement verifies its determinant in absolute value after removing common factors; it does not verify the complete numerical normalization of the geometric reverse map. Separate the proved nonvanishing from the stronger assertion that the displayed reverse maps give exactly the stated transpose matrix. The algebraicity equivalence needs the former.
- In the non-divisor criterion, replace “sum to $0$ or to $169$” by the actual conditions $a+b\equiv0$ or $a+169b\equiv0\pmod{204}$, as implemented in the supplement.
- Narrow the metadata abstract's pullback claim to the two rank-two systems in Theorem 4.1. The calculations in the other projections give the stated local obstructions, not a universal exclusion involving every remaining block. Describe the $256$ dimensions as rational Hodge classes in $W_{s_1}\otimes W_{s_1}$ on the surface square. Supply a stable link and version for [13], and consistently distinguish its claimed abstract algebraicity from the explicit cycle construction left open here.

Acceptance is for this concrete reduction and its supporting geometry. The supplied solve definition for the general rational Hodge conjecture is not met.
