## 1. Introduction

For a polarized complex abelian variety $A$ of dimension $2m$ equipped with an imaginary quadratic endomorphism action $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d_K}) \hookrightarrow \operatorname{End}^0(A)$ of **Weil type** (balanced signature $(m, m)$ on $H^{1,0}(A)$), the $2$-dimensional $K$-Weil subspace $W_K(A) = \bigwedge^{2m}_K H^1(A, \mathbb{Q}) \subset \operatorname{Hdg}^m(A, \mathbb{Q})$ consists of exceptional Hodge classes that are not generated by divisor intersections on a general member of the $m^2$-dimensional PEL Shimura variety $\mathcal{D}_{(m,m)}(K, H)$ ([1, 2]). Over a number field $K$, two nondegenerate $K$-Hermitian forms $H_1, H_2$ of fixed signature $(m, m)$ on $K^{2m}$ are $K$-linearly isometric if and only if they have the same **discriminant class**
$$\delta(H) \;:=\; (-1)^m \det_K(H) \bmod N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^\times) \;\in\; \mathbb{Q}_{>0}^\times / N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^\times).$$
A polarization $H$ has **split discriminant** when $\delta(H) = 1 \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}^\times / N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^\times)$ (admitting a maximal $K$-isotropic subspace of dimension $m$), whereas when $\delta(H) \neq 1$ (**non-split discriminant classes**, Witt index $m - 1$), distinct cosets in $\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^\times / N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^\times) \setminus \{1\}$ classify distinct non-isometric $K$-Hermitian forms that become $K$-similar only up to $\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^\times$-scaling when $m$ is odd ($\delta(c H) = c^{2m} \delta(H) = \delta(H)$ in $\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^\times / N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^\times)$, so even rational polarization scaling cannot change $\delta(H)$!).

Recent progress on $W_K(A)$ and quaternionic Type-III Weil spaces $Q_D^{(m)}(A)$ has highlighted the contrast between sheaf-theoretic and Hurwitz–Brauer cycle constructions:
1. **Literature Context and Predecessors:** Using weak secant sheaves, Markman proved that $W_K(A)$ is algebraic for every $K$-Weil fourfold of arbitrary discriminant class ([3, Theorem 1.5.1]) and for every **split** $K$-Weil sixfold ([4, Theorem 1.2]) over any imaginary quadratic field $K$. Earlier, for the specific split Gaussian sixfold family ($K = \mathbb{Q}(i)$, $\delta(H) = 1$), Koike [20] constructed a dominant $9$-dimensional genus-$0$ cyclic-double-cover Prym family proving algebraicity of $W_{\mathbb{Q}(i)}(A)$. On the finite-group Hurwitz–Brauer side, `cm_symmetry_scout` and `lelong_ridge` established fiberwise Brauer determinant cycles on inversion-invariant covers (`hodge_conjecture_40` v3 [12, Theorem D]), proved the Hodge conjecture for all powers of Hamilton quaternionic sixfolds via Nebe's group $\mathrm{FF}_3$ (`hodge_conjecture_53` v2 [14], `hodge_conjecture_55` v1 [16]), and resolved all powers of Eisenstein $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$-Weil sixfolds of **arbitrary** discriminant class via a $20$-branch eightfold family ($m = 4$) in Shephard–Todd $G_{34} \subset U_6(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}))$ (`hodge_conjecture_56` v1 [17]).
2. **Open Question 4 of `hodge_conjecture_53` v2 ([14, §8]):** *Can a direct $m = 3$ (sixfold) $K$-unitary reflection Hurwitz family land in a **non-split** $K$-Weil sixfold discriminant class $\delta(H) \notin N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^\times)$—and if so, in what maximum Hurwitz dimension?*

A natural first expectation—suggested by Carter's reflection-length lemma for real Weyl groups—is that in any finite complex reflection group generated by order-$2$ reflections in a single norm coset, every element factors into $\operatorname{codim}_K(V^g)$ reflections and therefore every balanced Hurwitz family must have split discriminant. By Foster-Greenwood's theorem ([19, Theorem 6.1]), however, equality between reflection length $\ell_R(g)$ and codimension $k(g) := \operatorname{rk}_K(g - I_d)$ holds across an irreducible complex reflection group **if and only if** $G$ is a real Coxeter group or $G(e, 1, d)$.

In this paper, we turn this reflection-length phenomenon into an exact arithmetic classification across all genera $\gamma \ge 0$, uncovering a striking dichotomy between $G_{24}, G_{29}$ and $G_{31}$, and establishing equidimensional $9$-dimensional Hurwitz–Brauer dominance in $G_{29} \subset U_4(\mathbb{Q}(i))$ and $G_{24} \subset U_3(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7}))$:

   $$(-1)^m \det_K(H_{\mathrm{par}}) \;\equiv\; \prod_{j=1}^r \Theta(C_j) \pmod{N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^\times)}, \qquad \Theta(g) \;:=\; \frac{\det_K(h_V|_{V_g})}{\det_K((g - I_d)|_{V_g})}.$$
   While $\Theta(g)$ factors as $\prod_{a=1}^k \frac{h_V(v_a, v_a)}{\zeta_a - 1}$ on **minimal** reflection factorizations ($\ell_R(g) = k(g)$, Theorem 2.2(1)), for **arbitrary** finite-order elements $g \in U_d(K, h_V)$ with $\det_K(g) \in \{\pm 1\}$ (regardless of $\ell_R(g)$!) we prove the unconditional **Hodge-Balance Character Law** (Theorem 2.2(2)):

## 2. All-Genus $K$-Linear Seifert–Bruhat Determinant Law, Hodge-Balance Character Law, and the $G_{24}, G_{29}$ vs. $G_{31}$ Dichotomy

Let $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d_K})$ ($d_K > 0$ square-free) be an imaginary quadratic field with conjugation $x \mapsto \bar{x}$ and fixed imaginary generator $\eta_K := \sqrt{-d_K} = i\sqrt{d_K}$ ($\operatorname{Im}(\eta_K) > 0$). Let $(V, h_V)$ be a $d$-dimensional positive-definite $K$-Hermitian space ($h_V(u, v) = u^\dagger h_V v$, conjugate-linear in $u$ and $K$-linear in $v$). Let $\Pi_{\gamma, r} = \langle a_1, b_1, \dots, a_\gamma, b_\gamma, c_1, \dots, c_r \mid \prod_{s=1}^\gamma [a_s, b_s] \prod_{j=1}^r c_j = 1 \rangle$ be the fundamental group of a genus-$\gamma$ Riemann surface $\Sigma_{\gamma, r}$ with $r$ punctures, and let $\rho : \Pi_{\gamma, r} \to U_d(K, h_V)$ be a unitary representation with $V^\Pi = 0$, $A_s = \rho(a_s), B_s = \rho(b_s), C_j = \rho(c_j)$.

Set $V_j := \operatorname{im}(C_j - I_d) \subset V$ of $K$-dimension $k_j = \operatorname{rk}_K(C_j - I_d)$, and form the total $K$-variation space $V_{\gamma, r} := V^{2\gamma} \oplus \bigoplus_{j=1}^r V_j$ of $K$-dimension $2\gamma d + \sum_{j=1}^r k_j$. When $H^1_{\mathrm{par}}(\Sigma_{\gamma, r}, V) = Z^1_{\mathrm{par}} / B^1$ has even $K$-dimension $2m = 2(\gamma - 1)d + \sum_{j=1}^r k_j$, we have $\dim_K V_{\gamma, r} = 2m + 2d$. Along the $(4\gamma + r)$-gon boundary word, define the edge vectors $x_1(z), \dots, x_{4\gamma+r}(z) \in V$ for $z = (u_1, v_1, \dots, u_\gamma, v_\gamma, z_1, \dots, z_r) \in V_{\gamma, r}$ as in [13, §2]: $x_{4s-3} = Q_{s-1} u_s$, $x_{4s-2} = Q_{s-1} A_s v_s$, $x_{4s-1} = -Q_s B_s u_s$, $x_{4s} = -Q_s v_s$ ($Q_{s-1} = \prod_{t < s} [A_t, B_t]$), and $x_{4\gamma+j} = P_{j-1} z_j$ ($P_{j-1} = Q_\gamma C_1 \cdots C_{j-1}$), with boundary sum $\Sigma(z) = \sum_{\nu=1}^{4\gamma+r} x_\nu(z)$ ($Z^1_{\mathrm{par}} = \ker \Sigma$) and coboundary map $\partial : V \hookrightarrow Z^1_{\mathrm{par}}$ ($B^1 = \operatorname{im} \partial \cong V$).

For any $g \in U_d(K, h_V)$ with $V_g = \operatorname{im}(g - I_d)$ ($k(g) = \dim_K V_g$), define its **local Wall unitary invariant** (cf. Wall [9]) in $K^\times / N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^\times)$ on any $K$-basis $W_g$ of $V_g$:
$$\Theta(g) \;:=\; \frac{\det_K(W_g^\dagger h_V W_g)}{\det_K\bigl((g - I_d)|_{V_g}\bigr)} \;\in\; K^\times / N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^\times).$$
Changing $W_g \mapsto W_g P$ ($P \in \mathrm{GL}_{k(g)}(K)$) multiplies the numerator by $N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\det_K P)$ and leaves the denominator unchanged, so $\Theta(g) \bmod N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^\times)$ is independent of basis.

**Theorem 2.1 (All-Genus $K$-Linear Seifert–Bruhat Determinant Formula).**
1. *Define the $K$-sesquilinear Dettweiler–Wewers–Fox Seifert form $c_K : V_{\gamma, r} \times V_{\gamma, r} \to K$ by*
   $$c_K(z, z') := -\sum_{1 \le \kappa < \mu \le 4\gamma+r} h_V\bigl(x_\mu(z), x_\kappa(z')\bigr) - \sum_{s=1}^\gamma \Bigl(h_V(u_s, u'_s) + h_V(v_s, v'_s)\Bigr) + \sum_{j=1}^r h_V\bigl(z_j, (C_j - I_d)^{-1} z'_j\bigr).$$
   *For all $\gamma \ge 0$, all $w_0 \in V$, and all $z, z' \in V_{\gamma, r}$, $c_K$ satisfies*
   $$c_K(z, \partial w_0) = h_V(\Sigma(z), w_0), \qquad c_K(\partial w_0, z') = -h_V(w_0, \Sigma(z')), \qquad c_K(z, z') + \overline{c_K(z', z)} = -h_V(\Sigma(z), \Sigma(z')).$$
   *Consequently, $\Psi_{\mathrm{par}}(z, z') := c_K(z, z') - \overline{c_K(z', z)}$ restricts on $Z^1_{\mathrm{par}} = \ker \Sigma$ to $2 c_K(z, z')$ with radical $B^1 = \operatorname{im} \partial$, and the canonical parabolic $K$-Hermitian intersection form $H_{\mathrm{par}} := \frac{1}{2\eta_K} \Psi_{\mathrm{par}}$ on $H^1_{\mathrm{par}}(\Sigma_{\gamma, r}, V) = Z^1_{\mathrm{par}} / B^1$ equals $\frac{1}{\eta_K} c_K$ on $Z^1_{\mathrm{par}} / B^1$.*
2. *Whenever $H^1_{\mathrm{par}}(\Sigma_{\gamma, r}, V)$ has even $K$-dimension $2m$, the signed $K$-determinant of $H_{\mathrm{par}}$ satisfies*
   $$(-1)^m \det_K(H_{\mathrm{par}}) \;\equiv\; d_K^{-m} \prod_{j=1}^r \Theta(C_j) \;\equiv\; \prod_{j=1}^r \Theta(C_j) \pmod{N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^\times)}.$$

*Proof.*
(1) Adapting the $\mathbb{Q}$-bilinear telescoping calculation of [13, Theorem 2.1(1)] to the $K$-Hermitian form $h_V$ and using the unitary identity $(C_j - I_d)^{-1} + ((C_j - I_d)^{-1})^\dagger = (C_j - I_d)^{-1} + (C_j^{-1} - I_d)^{-1} = -I_{V_j}$ on $V_j$ gives the three identities directly. On $Z^1_{\mathrm{par}} = \ker \Sigma$, $c_K(z, z') + \overline{c_K(z', z)} = 0$, so $\Psi_{\mathrm{par}} = 2 c_K$ and $H_{\mathrm{par}} = \frac{1}{\eta_K} c_K$.
(2) Any $K$-linear change of basis $P \in \mathrm{GL}_{2m+2d}(K)$ transforms the sesquilinear matrix $C_K \mapsto P^\dagger C_K P$, multiplying $\det_K(C_K)$ by $N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\det_K P) \in N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^\times)$. We evaluate $\det_K(C_K)$ in two $K$-bases:
- **Block Lower-Triangular Basis $V_1^2 \oplus \dots \oplus V_\gamma^2 \oplus V_1 \oplus \dots \oplus V_r$:** Because $c_K$ sums over $\kappa < \mu$, $C_K$ is block lower-triangular. On each handle block $V \oplus V$, $C_{\mathrm{handle}, s} = \operatorname{diag}(h_V, h_V) M_{\mathrm{handle}}(A_s, B_s)$, where the ring-theoretic Bruhat factorization [13, Theorem 2.1(2)] in $M_{2d}(K)$,
  $$\begin{pmatrix} I_d & 0 \\ -B_s & I_d \end{pmatrix} M_{\mathrm{handle}}(A_s, B_s) \begin{pmatrix} I_d & 0 \\ -A_s^{-1} & I_d \end{pmatrix} \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} -I_d & A_s B_s^{-1} \\ 0 & -I_d \end{pmatrix},$$
  gives $\det_K(M_{\mathrm{handle}}) = (-1)^{2d} = 1$ and $\det_K(C_{\mathrm{handle}, s}) = (\det_K h_V)^2 \in (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2 \subset N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^\times)$. On each puncture block $V_j$ with $K$-basis $W_j$, the diagonal block is $(W_j^\dagger h_V W_j)((C_j - I_d)|_{V_j})^{-1}$ of $K$-determinant $\Theta(C_j)$, giving $\det_K(C_K) \equiv \prod_{j=1}^r \Theta(C_j) \pmod{N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^\times)}$.
- **Filtration Basis $B^1 \oplus H^1_{\mathrm{par}} \oplus S$:** Since $V^\Pi = 0$, $\partial : V \xrightarrow{\sim} B^1$ and $\Sigma : V_{\gamma, r}/Z^1_{\mathrm{par}} \xrightarrow{\sim} V$ are $K$-isomorphisms. On a $K$-linear splitting $B^1 \oplus H^1_{\mathrm{par}} \oplus S$, (1) gives
  $$\widetilde{C}_K = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & -h_V \\ 0 & \eta_K\, H_{\mathrm{par}} & * \\ h_V & * & * \end{pmatrix}.$$
  Moving $-h_V$ across $2m + d$ columns introduces $(-1)^{d(2m+d)} (-1)^d = (-1)^{d(d+1)} = +1$, so $\det_K(\widetilde{C}_K) = (\det_K h_V)^2 \eta_K^{2m} \det_K(H_{\mathrm{par}}) = (\det_K h_V)^2 d_K^m (-1)^m \det_K(H_{\mathrm{par}})$. Since $(\det_K h_V)^2 \in (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$ and $d_K = N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\eta_K) \in N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^\times)$, equating $\det_K(\widetilde{C}_K) \equiv \det_K(C_K)$ proves (2). $\square$

To apply Theorem 2.1 without false reflection-length assumptions, we combine **conditional** minimal-factorization multiplicativity with an **unconditional** polar decomposition of $\Theta(g)$ valid for every finite-order unitary element:

**Theorem 2.2 (Minimal-Factorization Multiplicativity & Unconditional Hodge-Balance Character Law).**
1. *(Conditional Minimal-Factorization Multiplicativity): If $g \in U_d(K, h_V)$ of codimension $k = k(g)$ factors minimally as a product of $k$ unitary reflections $g = R_{v_1, \zeta_1} \cdots R_{v_k, \zeta_k}$, then $\Theta(g) \equiv \prod_{a=1}^k \frac{h_V(v_a, v_a)}{\zeta_a - 1} \pmod{N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^\times)}$. (Warning: By Foster-Greenwood [19, Theorem 6.1], $\ell_R(g) = k(g)$ holds for all $g \in G$ in an irreducible complex reflection group **only** for real Coxeter groups and $G(e, 1, d)$. For instance, in $G(3, 3, 3) \subset U_3(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}))$ with $h_V = I_3$ and order-$2$ reflections of root norm $2$, $g = \omega I_3$ has $k(g) = 3$ and $\det_K(g) = 1$, so $\ell_R(g) = 4 > 3$, and $\Theta(g) = \frac{1}{(\omega - 1)^3} = \frac{1}{3\sqrt{-3}} \not\equiv (-1)^3$.)*
2. *(Unconditional Hodge-Balance Character Law): Let $(V, h_V)$ be positive-definite and let $g \in U_d(K, h_V)$ be **any** element of finite order with $\det_K(g) \in \{\pm 1\}$ (for example, any element of a finite group generated by order-$2$ reflections). Let $e^{2\pi i \theta_1}, \dots, e^{2\pi i \theta_k}$ ($0 < \theta_a < 1$, $k = k(g)$) be the eigenvalues of $g|_{V_g}$ under the embedding $K \subset \mathbb{C}$ with $\eta_K = i\sqrt{d_K}$, and set $\alpha(g) := \sum_{a=1}^k \theta_a$. Then $\delta(g) := 2\alpha(g) - k(g) \in \mathbb{Z}$, and there exists a **unique positive rational coset** $q(g) \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}^\times / N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^\times)$ such that*
   $$(-1)^{k(g)} \Theta(g) \;\equiv\; (-\eta_K)^{\delta(g)} \, q(g) \pmod{N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^\times)}.$$
3. *(Phase Cancellation on Balanced Families in Arbitrary Genus): For **every** base genus $\gamma \ge 0$ and **every** generating tuple $(A_1, B_1, \dots, A_\gamma, B_\gamma, C_1, \dots, C_r) \in U_d(K, h_V)^{2\gamma+r}$ with $\det_K(C_j) \in \{\pm 1\}$, $V^\Pi = 0$, and **balanced** parabolic Hodge numbers $(m, m)$ ($m_+ = m_- = m$), the defects $\delta(C_j)$ cancel identically ($\sum_{j=1}^r \delta(C_j) = 0$) and $\sum_{j=1}^r k(C_j) = 2m + 2(1-\gamma)d$ is even, yielding*
   $$(-1)^m \det_K(H_{\mathrm{par}}) \;\equiv\; \prod_{j=1}^r q(C_j) \pmod{N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^\times)}.$$

*Proof.*
(1) When $g = R_{v_1, \zeta_1} \cdots R_{v_k, \zeta_k}$ with $k = \dim_K V_g$, the roots $v_1, \dots, v_k$ span $V_g$ and are $K$-linearly independent. Applying Theorem 2.1(2) in genus $\gamma = 0$ on $V_g$ to the $(k+1)$-tuple $(R_1, \dots, R_k, g^{-1})$ of parabolic dimension $2m = (k + k) - 2k = 0$ gives $1 \equiv \Theta(g^{-1}) \prod_{a=1}^k \Theta(R_a)$. Since $(g - I_d)|_{V_g}^\dagger h_V|_{V_g} = h_V|_{V_g}(g^{-1} - I_d)|_{V_g}$ implies $\Theta(g^{-1}) = \overline{\Theta(g)}$ and $\Theta(g)\overline{\Theta(g)} \in N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^\times)$, multiplying by $\Theta(g)$ gives (1).
(2) Since $\det_K(g) = e^{2\pi i \alpha(g)} \in \{\pm 1\}$, $2\alpha(g) \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $\delta(g) = 2\alpha(g) - k(g) \in \mathbb{Z}$. Using $e^{2\pi i \theta_a} - 1 = 2i \sin(\pi\theta_a) e^{\pi i \theta_a} = (-i)^{-1} e^{\pi i \theta_a} \cdot 2\sin(\pi\theta_a)$ with $2\sin(\pi\theta_a) > 0$, we compute
$$\det_K\bigl((g - I_d)|_{V_g}\bigr) \;=\; \prod_{a=1}^k (e^{2\pi i \theta_a} - 1) \;=\; (-i)^{-k} e^{\pi i \alpha(g)} \prod_{a=1}^k 2\sin(\pi\theta_a) \;=\; (-1)^k (-i)^{-\delta(g)} \prod_{a=1}^k 2\sin(\pi\theta_a),$$
