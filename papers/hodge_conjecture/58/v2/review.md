# Accept

The revision corrects the earlier review’s substantive local errors and adds useful extensions to the accepted contribution. The corrected reflection lengths, Hodge-number subtraction, monomial-subgroup identification, canonical-extension citation, and attribution to Markman and Koike are appropriate. The new parity identity follows directly from the determinant character and provides a useful consistency check on the reflection-length computations.

The principal additions are scientifically sound. Theorem 2.4’s monomial-cycle calculation supplies an all-rank argument, rather than extrapolating from bounded enumeration. The treatment of $W(G_2)$ correctly distinguishes non-norm local invariants from non-split global discriminants: the long-reflection character is trivial on commutators, so the surface-group relation forces cancellation. Together with the specified reflection-group realizations, this supports the distinction between $G_{31}$ and the other groups generated by order-two reflections.

Theorem 2.5 gives a meaningful geometric consequence of the previously established eight-dimensional period image. In the reducible case, invariance under the Hodge circle makes the invariant complex subspace nondegenerate for the Hermitian form; thus the orthogonal block decomposition and the bound
$$
p_1q_1+(3-p_1)(3-q_1)\leq 6
$$
apply even when a decomposition appears only after extending scalars. In the irreducible case, the weight-one restrictions of Moonen–Zarhin [2, §1.5], also used in [17, §8], give the required dimension bound. Consequently an eight-dimensional image cannot have a proper generic Hodge group. The resulting full special unitary group, together with the already constructed algebraic Weil classes, satisfies the almost-neat hypothesis of Milne [18, Theorem 1.8] and yields the all-powers conclusion for very general fibres. This does not assert the same Hodge group at every special point or settle the general Hodge conjecture.

I reproduced all three modified or newly added tests, including the reflection-length checks, monomial-cycle examples, exhaustive $G_2$ and $G_8$ calculations, and the dimension arithmetic. The standalone certificate also completed successfully, reproducing the six $G_{29}$ volumes and the $G_{31}$ class data, subgroup disjointness, and volume $45{,}147{,}375/16$. These are exact finite computations. The all-rank statement and the Hodge-group classification rely on the written proofs and references; Test 5 checks dimensions of a supplied list, not completeness of that list.

The comparison with [12] and [17], and with the representation-theoretic and all-powers results in [2] and [18], supports presenting these additions as applications and extensions of acknowledged ingredients. The revised distinction between new reflection-group results and alternative proofs of previously known split-sixfold and fourfold algebraicity is substantially clearer.

The following local corrections and scope qualifications should be incorporated. They do not undermine the main balanced-family classification or the generic $G_{31}$ conclusion.

1. **Remove the erroneous $G(6,3,2)$ identification in the proof of Theorem 2.4.** Its order is $6^2\cdot2/3=24$, whereas $W(G_2)$ has order $12$. Moreover, its defining representation contains $\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_6,\zeta_6^2)$, whose trace is $\sqrt{-3}$, so its character field is not contained in $\mathbb Q(i)$. The relevant rational dihedral group is $G(6,6,2)$, already treated correctly.

2. **Correct the extra-endomorphism parenthesis in Theorem 2.5.** The domain of $\mathrm U(1,2)\times\mathrm U(2,1)$ has complex dimension $2+2=4$, not $2$. This remains below both the general bound $6$ and the image dimension $8$, so the conclusion is unaffected. Also distinguish the rational group $\mathrm{SU}(H_{\mathrm{par}})$ from its real form $\mathrm{SU}(3,3)$.

3. **State the determinant restriction explicitly in the $G_8$ consequence.** The supplied enumeration of $q(g)$ covers $\det(g)\in\{\pm1\}$, while the subsequent sentence refers to any balanced family, including branching with determinant $\pm i$. Either retain the demonstrated restriction or supply the additional phase-cancellation argument for those reflections. This issue is separate from the classification of groups generated by order-two reflections.

4. **Specify the normalization in the elementwise uniqueness assertion of Theorem 2.4(2)(a).** A single reflection norm coset need not be the trivial coset. Rescaling the invariant form by $c\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$ changes $q(g)$ by $c^{k(g)}$, so elementwise norm membership requires the displayed normalizations. The balanced-family discriminant is unchanged, since the total codimension is even.

The metadata abstract is informative but dense; shortening the class bookkeeping and retaining the distinction between very general fibres and the full non-split domain would improve readability.
