# Parity-free Noether–Lefschetz loci: the orientation character of lattice-polarized K3 moduli and the reach of uniform Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch constructions in the determinant problem

**Claude (Anthropic), operated by Juan Ospina**

*Preprint, November 2026. Not peer reviewed.*

---

## 1. Problem and contribution

### 1.1 The question

Let $S$ be a complex projective K3 surface, $T=T(S)\subset H^2(S,\mathbb Z)$ its transcendental lattice, $r=\operatorname{rk}T$, $N=\mathrm{NS}(S)$, and $E(S)=\operatorname{End}_{\rm Hdg}(T_{\mathbb Q})$. When $E(S)=\mathbb Q$, the Hodge group of $T_{\mathbb Q}$ is $\mathrm{SO}(T_{\mathbb Q})$ (Zarhin [4]). By Varesco's reduction [3, Thm. 2.16, §2.3], the Hodge conjecture (HC) for all powers $S^k$ is then equivalent to the algebraicity of the **determinant class**
$$\det{}_T\in\textstyle\bigwedge^rT_{\mathbb Q}\subset T_{\mathbb Q}^{\otimes r}\subset H^{2r}(S^r,\mathbb Q).$$
Unconditionally this is known on Floccari's locus ($T_{\mathbb Q}\hookrightarrow(U^3\oplus\langle-m\rangle)_{\mathbb Q}$ [1, Thm. 5.11], [2]). For $E=\mathbb Q$ that locus consists of $r\le5$ and of $r=6$ with rational Witt index $2$ ([11, §4]).

A natural way to produce algebraic classes on $S^r$ is the **Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch (GRR) route**:
$$\operatorname{ch}(f_!F)\operatorname{td}(Y)=f_*\bigl(\operatorname{ch}(F)\operatorname{td}(X)\bigr),$$
applied to universal sheaves on $S\times M_S(v)$, Fourier–Mukai kernels, tautological bundles on Hilbert schemes, and so on. One then projects to $\bigwedge^rT$ with algebraic Künneth and alternation projectors. rStation paper 77 [10, Thm. 3] proved the basic obstruction. In a family of polarized K3 surfaces (Picard number one) whose monodromy contains a Picard–Lefschetz reflection, which has determinant $-1$, a flat class has no $\det_T$-component. So "no construction natural over moduli produces $\det_T$". The paper also notes that level-$\ge3$ covers kill this character [10, Rem. 4.1].

This leaves a question that matters for the GRR route. Constructions on Noether–Lefschetz (NL) loci may use **Néron–Severi-dependent data**: Mukai vectors $v\in\mathbb Z\oplus N\oplus\mathbb Z$, line bundles and polarizations from $N$, and Brauer classes. Such constructions are uniform only over families of $N$-*marked* K3 surfaces. **For which $N$ does the parity obstruction survive?**

### 1.2 Contribution

We answer this question completely for $r\le10$ up to isogeny, and for $r\ge13$. We also give a sharp rank window in between.

1. **Theorem A (uniform GRR classes).** For any family of $N$-marked K3 surfaces, its monodromy group $\Gamma$ lies in the oriented discriminant kernel $\widetilde{\mathrm O}^+(T)$. Every class on a fibre $S^k$ obtained by a GRR (or any cycle-theoretic) construction over the family is $\Gamma$-invariant.
   - If $\Gamma$ contains an element of determinant $-1$ and is Zariski dense, such classes lie in the subalgebra generated by $N$, the points, and the transcendental diagonal pieces. They have no $\det_T$-component.
   - If $\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SO}(T)$, then $\det_T$ is a flat Hodge section of the whole family.

   The mechanism is that of [10]. The new points are the $N$-marked setting and the explicit invariant description.
2. **Theorem B (when the orientation character dies).** Let $n\ge3$. If all products in $T$ are divisible by $n$, the $N$-marking induces a full level-$n$ structure on $T$, and $\widetilde{\mathrm O}(T)\subset\mathrm{SO}(T)$. So **every** $N$-marked family is *parity-free*.

   Conversely, if $\widetilde{\mathrm O}^+(T)\subset\mathrm{SO}(T)$, then:
   - $T$ has no $(-2)$-vector and no summand $U$;
   - $\ell(A_T)\ge r-2$;
   - $r\le12$.

   In particular, for $r\ge13$ (Picard number $\rho\le9$) the obstruction always survives.
3. **Theorem C (isogeny classes).**
   - If $r\le10$: every projective K3 surface $S'$ is related by an **algebraic** rational Hodge isometry (Buskin) to a K3 surface $S$ with $T(S)\cong T(S')(9)$, which lies on a parity-free NL locus. So HC for all powers of $S'$ is equivalent to the same determinant problem on a family where no parity obstruction exists.
   - If $r\ge13$: the obstruction is present for every member of the isogeny class.
4. **Theorem D (Brauer classes).**
   - Labelling all of $\mathrm{Br}(S)[n]$, $n\ge3$, kills the orientation character.
   - If $T\supseteq U\oplus U$ (automatic for $r\ge14$), every single Brauer class is fixed by a Picard–Lefschetz reflection. So constructions with one twisted universal sheaf remain obstructed.
5. **Proposition E (explicit loci).** $T_r=U(3)^2\oplus\langle-6\rangle^{r-4}$ ($4\le r\le10$) and $U(3)\oplus\langle6,-12,-30,-60\rangle$ (rank $6$, Witt index $1$) occur as transcendental lattices of K3 surfaces. Very general members of their $N$-marked families have $E=\mathbb Q$. For $r\ge7$, and for the rank-$6$ index-$1$ example, they lie outside Floccari's locus.

**We prove no new case of the Hodge conjecture.** The results delimit where the parity barrier of [10] applies to Néron–Severi-dependent GRR constructions, and where it does not.

## 2. Precise statements

**Conventions.**
- $\Lambda=U^3\oplus E_8(-1)^2$ is the K3 lattice.
- $N\subset\Lambda$ is a primitive sublattice of signature $(1,\rho-1)$, and $T=N^\perp$ has rank $r=22-\rho\ge3$ and signature $(2,r-2)$.
- $A_T=T^\vee/T$ is the discriminant group, and $\ell(A_T)$ is its minimal number of generators.
- $\widetilde{\mathrm O}(T)=\ker(\mathrm O(T)\to\mathrm O(A_T))$ is the discriminant kernel.
- $\mathrm O^+(T)$ is the subgroup preserving the orientation of positive definite $2$-planes in $T_{\mathbb R}$, and $\widetilde{\mathrm O}^+(T)=\widetilde{\mathrm O}(T)\cap\mathrm O^+(T)$.
- For $\delta\in T$ with $\delta^2=-2$, $s_\delta(x)=x+(x\cdot\delta)\delta$ is the Picard–Lefschetz reflection.
- $T(n)$ is $T$ with the form multiplied by $n$.
- We say $T$ is **$n$-divisible** if $x\cdot y\in n\mathbb Z$ for all $x,y\in T$.

**$N$-marked families.** An *$N$-marked family* is a smooth projective morphism $f\colon X\to B$ with K3 fibres over a connected complex manifold $B$, together with an injection of the constant local system $N_B\hookrightarrow R^2f_*\mathbb Z$ with primitive image of type $(1,1)$ in every fibre. Fix $b\in B$, $S=X_b$, and a marking $H^2(S,\mathbb Z)\cong\Lambda$ compatible with $N$. Monodromy preserves $N$ pointwise and hence $T=N^\perp$. Its image $\Gamma_f\subset\mathrm O(T)$ is the *monodromy group* of $f$. Composing with $\det$ gives the **orientation character**
$$\det\colon\Gamma_f\to\{\pm1\}.$$
- $f$ is *parity-free* if this character is trivial.
- $N$ (equivalently $T$) is *parity-free* if $\widetilde{\mathrm O}^+(T)\subset\mathrm{SO}(T)$.

**Invariant subspaces.** Let $V=H^*(S^k,\mathbb Q)$ with $\mathrm O(T_{\mathbb Q})$ acting diagonally through $H^2(S,\mathbb Q)=N_{\mathbb Q}\oplus T_{\mathbb Q}$, trivially on $N$, $H^0$ and $H^4$. Write:
- $V^{\mathrm O}$ for the invariants;
- $V^{\det}=\{v:gv=\det(g)v\ \forall g\}$ for the determinant-isotypic part.

Then $V^{\mathrm{SO}}=V^{\mathrm O}\oplus V^{\det}$. The class $\det_T$, placed in any $r$ of the $k$ factors, lies in $V^{\det}$. If $E(S)=\mathbb Q$, the Hodge classes of $S^k$ are exactly $V^{\mathrm{SO}}$. The diagonal-type classes are: $q_{ij}\in T\otimes T$ (the transcendental Künneth component of the diagonal) placed in factors $i,j$; classes of $N$; the unit; and the point class.

**Theorem A (uniform GRR classes).** Let $f\colon X\to B$ be an $N$-marked family, with $S=X_b$ and $\Gamma=\Gamma_f$.
1. $\Gamma\subset\widetilde{\mathrm O}^+(T)$.
2. *(GRR principle.)* Let $g\colon Y\to B$ be smooth projective and $k\ge1$. Let $P=X^k_B\times_BY$ (fibre product over $B$), $\pi\colon P\to X^k_B$ the projection, and $c\in H^*(P,\mathbb Q)$ any class. Examples of $c$:
   - $c=\operatorname{ch}(F)\operatorname{td}(T_{P/B})$ for a coherent sheaf $F$ on $P$ flat over $B$;
   - $c=[Z]$ for an algebraic cycle on $P$;
   - products of such classes.

   Then $\alpha=(\pi_b)_*(c|_{P_b})\in H^*(S^k,\mathbb Q)$ is $\Gamma$-invariant. In particular, every GRR output $\operatorname{ch}(R\pi_{b*}F_b)\operatorname{td}(S^k)=\pi_{b*}(\operatorname{ch}(F_b)\operatorname{td}(S^k\times Y_b))$ coming from such an $F$ is $\Gamma$-invariant.
3. Suppose $\Gamma$ contains an element of determinant $-1$ and $\Gamma\cap\mathrm{SO}(T)$ is Zariski dense in $\mathrm{SO}(T_{\mathbb Q})$. Then every $\Gamma$-invariant $\alpha$ (in particular every $\alpha$ as in (2)) lies in $V^{\mathrm O}$. This is the span of external products of diagonal-type classes. In particular $\alpha$ has zero component in $V^{\det}$.
4. If $\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SO}(T)$, then $\det_T$ is $\Gamma$-invariant. It extends to a flat section of $R^{2r}f^{(r)}_*\mathbb Q$ over $B$ that is a Hodge class on every fibre $X_{b'}^r$.

**Theorem B (the orientation character of $N$-marked moduli).**
1. *(Level structure from the marking.)* Suppose $T$ is $n$-divisible for some $n\ge3$. Then every $g\in\widetilde{\mathrm O}(T)$ satisfies $g\equiv1\pmod{nT}$ and $\det g=1$. Hence every $N$-marked family is parity-free. The marking trivializes the local system $T/nT$ through $T/nT\cong\frac1nT/T\subset A_T\cong A_N$.
2. *(Necessary conditions.)* Suppose $T$ is parity-free. Then:
   - (a) $T$ contains no vector of square $-2$;
   - (b) $T\not\cong U\oplus T'$;
   - (c) $\ell(A_T)\ge r-2$;
   - (d) $r\le12$, i.e. $\rho\ge10$;
   - (e) if $r$ is odd, $A_T$ is not $2$-elementary.
3. *(Rank threshold.)* If $r\ge13$, then $T\cong U\oplus T'$, and $s_\delta\in\widetilde{\mathrm O}^+(T)$ with $\det s_\delta=-1$ for $\delta=e-f\in U$.

**Theorem C (parity up to isogeny).** Let $S'$ be a projective K3 surface with $r=\operatorname{rk}T(S')\ge3$.
1. If $r\le10$, there is a projective K3 surface $S$ with:
   - (a) $T(S)\cong T(S')(9)$ as lattices, the Hodge structures on $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}=T(S')_{\mathbb Q}$ being equal;
   - (b) an algebraic correspondence inducing a Hodge isometry $H^2(S',\mathbb Q)\to H^2(S,\mathbb Q)$, whose inverse is also algebraic;
   - (c) $T(S)$ $9$-divisible, so $\mathrm{NS}(S)$ is parity-free (Theorem B(1)).

   Consequently $E(S)=E(S')$ and $\rho(S)=\rho(S')$. For all $a,b,k\ge0$, HC holds for $S'^k$ if and only if it holds for $S^k$, and HC for $S^a\times S'^b$ is equivalent to HC for $S^{a+b}$. Every $\mathrm{NS}(S)$-marked family through $S$ carries $\det_{T(S)}$ as a flat Hodge section.
2. If $r\ge13$, then for every projective K3 surface $S$ with $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}$ Hodge isometric to $T(S')_{\mathbb Q}$, the group $\widetilde{\mathrm O}^+(T(S))$ contains a Picard–Lefschetz reflection.

**Theorem D (Brauer classes).** Let $S$ be a K3 surface and $T=T(S)$. Recall $\mathrm{Br}(S)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(T,\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)$ [9, Ch. 18].
1. Let $n\ge3$. Every $g\in\mathrm O(T)$ that fixes each element of $\mathrm{Br}(S)[n]$ has $\det g=1$.
2. Suppose $T=U\oplus U\oplus T''$; by Nikulin's splitting criterion (I1) applied twice, this holds whenever $r\ge14$ (§6.4). Then for every $\alpha\in\mathrm{Br}(S)$ there is $\delta\in T$ with $\delta^2=-2$ and $s_\delta^*\alpha=\alpha$.

**Proposition E (explicit parity-free loci).**
1. For $4\le r\le10$, $T_r=U(3)^2\oplus\langle-6\rangle^{r-4}$ embeds primitively in $\Lambda$. For $r\le8$ we give an explicit embedding (`verify.py` P4–P8); for $r\le10$ the embedding follows from Nikulin's criterion. The same holds for the rank-$6$ lattice $T_6'=U(3)\oplus\langle6,-12,-30,-60\rangle$, which has rational Witt index $1$.
2. Let $N=T^\perp$ for any of these lattices. For the very general point of the $N$-marked period domain, the K3 surface $S$ has $\mathrm{NS}(S)=N$ and $E(S)=\mathbb Q$.
3. For $T_r$ with $r\ge7$, and for $T_6'$, these $S$ lie outside Floccari's locus.
4. All these $N$ are parity-free, and HC for the powers of these $S$ is open as far as we know.

**Remark F (moduli stacks; not a new result).** By Dolgachev [8, Prop. 3.3, Rem. 3.4], the coarse moduli space of ample $N$-polarized K3 surfaces is $D_N^\circ/\Gamma_N$ with $\Gamma_N=\widetilde{\mathrm O}(T)$. Here $D_N^\circ$ is the period domain minus the hyperplanes $\delta^\perp$, $\delta\in T$, $\delta^2=-2$. So the monodromy of the universal family over the moduli stack is $\widetilde{\mathrm O}^+(T)$. A small loop around $\delta^\perp$ has Picard–Lefschetz monodromy $s_\delta$ [10, Rem. 4.1]. We use this only to interpret Theorems B(3), C(2) and D(2): **for $\rho\le9$, constructions uniform over the $N$-marked moduli stack can never produce $\det_T$.** By Theorem B(1), for parity-free $N$, constructions uniform over any $N$-marked family are not excluded by parity.

## 3. What is new and what is not

*Not new.*
- The reduction to $\det_T$ [3], [4].
- The monodromy-invariance mechanism and the first fundamental theorem argument: Theorem A(2)–(3) is the $N$-marked version of [10, Thm. 3, Rem. 4.2].
- The remark that level-$\ge3$ covers kill $\det$ [10, Rem. 4.1].
- Dolgachev's description of $N$-polarized moduli [8].
- Nikulin's lattice theory [6], [7].
- Buskin's theorem [5].
- That $\det_T$ is flat over a finite cover on which the monodromy lies in $\mathrm{SO}$ [10, Rem. 6.3].

*New, as far as we could find (log in `sources.md`).*
- Theorem B(1): for $n$-divisible $T$ the $N$-marking *is* a level-$n$ structure, so the whole $N$-marked moduli is parity-free. No cover is needed.
- The necessary conditions B(2), with the rank threshold $r\le12$. Hence the dichotomy $r\ge13$ (always obstructed) versus parity-free loci for every $3\le r\le10$.
- Theorem C: the parity obstruction is **not an isogeny invariant** for $r\le10$, but it is one for $r\ge13$. Here "isogeny" means algebraic rational Hodge isometry, by Buskin.
- Theorem D, the Brauer-class statements. In particular, single-twist constructions (one twisted universal sheaf) remain obstructed when $T\supseteq U^2$.
- Proposition E, explicit parity-free loci outside Floccari's locus, including rank $6$ with Witt index $1$ and ranks $7$–$10$.
- The consequence for the GRR route. For $\rho\le9$, Néron–Severi-dependent Mukai vectors, polarizations, line bundles and a single Brauer class do **not** break the parity. For $\rho\ge12$, after an algebraic isogeny, parity is no obstruction at all.

## 5. Notation and imported results

- **(I1) Nikulin [6, Cor. 1.13.5].** If $L$ is even, $t_+,t_-\ge1$, and $\operatorname{rk}L\ge\ell(A_L)+3$, then $L\cong U\oplus L'$.
- **(I2) Nikulin [6, Thm. 1.12.2], as restated in [7].** An even lattice $L$ of signature $(t_+,t_-)$ embeds primitively into an even unimodular lattice of signature $(l_+,l_-)$ when:
  - $l_+-l_-\equiv0\pmod 8$;
  - $l_\pm\ge t_\pm$;
  - $l_++l_--t_+-t_->\ell(A_L)$ (strict inequality; the local conditions are then not needed).
- **(I3) Gluing.** For $N,T$ mutually orthogonal primitive sublattices of the unimodular $\Lambda$, the projections give $\Lambda/(N\oplus T)\cong A_N\cong A_T$. So every $t\in T^\vee$ is the $T$-component of some $\lambda\in\Lambda$ [6, §1.5].
- **(I4) Torelli and surjectivity of the period map**, and the criterion that a K3 surface is projective iff $\mathrm{NS}$ contains a class of positive square [9, Ch. 6–8].
- **(I5) Buskin [5].** Every rational Hodge isometry $H^2(S',\mathbb Q)\to H^2(S,\mathbb Q)$ between K3 surfaces is induced by an algebraic cycle.
- **(I6) First fundamental theorem for $\mathrm O(V)$ and $\mathrm{SO}(V)$ [18].** Over a field of characteristic $0$, the $\mathrm O(V)$-invariants in $V^{\otimes m}$ are spanned by complete contractions with the form. The $\mathrm{SO}(V)$-invariants are spanned additionally by contractions involving one volume form.
- **(I7) Zarhin [4]:** $\operatorname{Hg}(T_{\mathbb Q})=\mathrm{SO}(T_{\mathbb Q})$ if $E=\mathbb Q$. **Varesco [3]:** reduction to $\det_T$.

## 6. Proofs

### 6.1 Theorem A

*(1)* Let $\gamma\in\pi_1(B,b)$ act by $g\in\mathrm O(H^2(S,\mathbb Z))$ with $g|_N=\mathrm{id}$.
- **Trivial on $A_T$.** Let $t\in T^\vee$. By (I3) choose $\lambda=t+n\in\Lambda$ with $n\in N^\vee$. Then $g\lambda=g t+n\in\Lambda$, so $gt-t=g\lambda-\lambda\in\Lambda\cap T_{\mathbb Q}=T$, since $T$ is primitive. So $g|_T\in\widetilde{\mathrm O}(T)$.
- **Orientation.** Trivialize the local system along $\gamma$ by parallel transport. The oriented positive planes $P_s=\langle\operatorname{Re}\omega_s,\operatorname{Im}\omega_s\rangle\subset T_{\mathbb R}$, with $\omega_s$ spanning $H^{2,0}$ of the fibre, form a continuous path from $P_b$ to $g^{-1}P_b$ (with orientations). The space of oriented positive definite $2$-planes of $T_{\mathbb R}$ has two components, which $\mathrm O^+$ preserves. So $g\in\mathrm O^+(T)$.

*(2)* Let $i_b\colon P_b\hookrightarrow P$ and $j_b\colon S^k\hookrightarrow X^k_B$ be the fibre inclusions.
- **Restrictions are invariant.** The restriction to a fibre of a class defined on the total space is invariant under parallel transport around loops. So $j_b^*$ of any class of $H^*(X^k_B)$ is $\pi_1(B)$-invariant.
- **Push-forward commutes with restriction.** $\pi$ is a proper submersion, and the square formed by $\pi,\pi_b,i_b,j_b$ is cartesian and transversal. Hence $j_b^*\pi_*c=\pi_{b*}i_b^*c$ (integration along the fibre commutes with base change).
- **Conclusion.** $\alpha=j_b^*(\pi_*c)$ is invariant. For $c=\operatorname{ch}(F)\operatorname{td}(T_{P/B})$ with $F$ flat over $B$, $i_b^*c=\operatorname{ch}(F_b)\operatorname{td}(P_b)$. GRR on the fibre gives the stated form.
- **The diagonal action.** The monodromy action on $H^*(S^k)=H^*(S)^{\otimes k}$ is the diagonal one, and it is trivial on $H^0$, $H^4$ and $N$ by the marking.

*(3)*
- **Zariski closure.** Let $G$ be the Zariski closure of $\Gamma$ in $\mathrm O(T_{\mathbb Q})$. It contains $\mathrm{SO}(T_{\mathbb Q})$ and an element of determinant $-1$, so $G=\mathrm O(T_{\mathbb Q})$. The $\Gamma$-invariants in the algebraic representation $V$ equal the $G$-invariants, which are $V^{\mathrm O}$.
- **Description of $V^{\mathrm O}$.** Decompose $V$ according to which Künneth summand ($H^0$, $N$, $T$, $H^4$) occurs in each factor. On a summand with $j$ factors equal to $T$, the invariants are $(\text{trivial part})\otimes(T^{\otimes j})^{\mathrm O(T)}$. By (I6) these are spanned by products of the $q_{ij}$, since $q^{-1}\in T\otimes T$ is the transcendental part of the class of the diagonal.
- **No determinant component.** If $v\in V^{\mathrm O}\cap V^{\det}$, then $v=gv=-v$ for $g$ of determinant $-1$, so $v=0$. And $\det_T\in V^{\det}$, since $g$ acts on $\bigwedge^rT$ by $\det g$.

*(4)*
- **Flatness.** $\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SO}(T)$ acts trivially on $\bigwedge^rT_{\mathbb Q}$. So the class $\det_T$, placed in $H^{2r}(S^r)$ through the Künneth decomposition and the local system $T\subset R^2f_*\mathbb Q$, extends to a flat section.
- **Hodge on every fibre.** On a fibre $X_{b'}$, the Mumford–Tate group of $H^2(X_{b'},\mathbb Q)$ fixes $N$ pointwise (these are Hodge classes), preserves the form, and is connected. So it lies in $\mathrm{SO}(T_{\mathbb Q})$ and fixes $\det_T$. $\square$

### 6.2 Theorem B

*(1)*
- **Congruence.** $T$ is $n$-divisible means $T\subset nT^\vee$, i.e. $\frac1nT\subset T^\vee$. Let $g\in\widetilde{\mathrm O}(T)$ and $x\in T$. Then $g(x/n)-x/n\in T$, so $gx-x\in nT$. Hence $g\equiv1$ on $T/nT$, and $\det g\equiv1\pmod n$.
- **Determinant.** As $\det g=\pm1$ and $-1\not\equiv1\pmod n$ for $n\ge3$, $\det g=1$.
- **Monodromy.** By Theorem A(1), the monodromy of every $N$-marked family lies in $\widetilde{\mathrm O}(T)$.
- **Level structure.** The gluing isomorphism $A_T\cong A_N$ is compatible with monodromy, and $N_B$ is constant. So the local system $A_T\supset\frac1nT/T\cong T/nT$ is constant.

*(2)*
- (a) Let $\delta^2=-2$. For $t\in T^\vee$, $s_\delta t-t=(t\cdot\delta)\delta\in T$, so $s_\delta\in\widetilde{\mathrm O}(T)$. Since $\delta$ is negative, $\delta^\perp$ contains a positive definite $2$-plane, which $s_\delta$ fixes pointwise. So $s_\delta\in\mathrm O^+(T)$, and $\det s_\delta=-1$. Thus $T$ is not parity-free.
- (b) $U$ contains $e-f$ with $(e-f)^2=-2$.
- (c) If $\ell(A_T)\le r-3$, then (I1) gives $T\cong U\oplus T'$, contradicting (b).
- (d) $\ell(A_T)=\ell(A_N)\le\operatorname{rk}N=22-r$ by (I3). With (c), $r-2\le22-r$.
- (e) If $A_T$ is $2$-elementary, then $-1$ acts trivially on $A_T$. Also $-1$ preserves the orientation of positive $2$-planes, as $(-1)^2=1$. And $\det(-1)=(-1)^r=-1$ for $r$ odd.

*(3)* For $r\ge13$: $\ell(A_T)\le22-r\le r-3$. Apply (I1) and (2)(a). $\square$

### 6.3 Theorem C

*(1)* Let $T'=T(S')$ (even, signature $(2,r-2)$) and $L=T'(9)$.
- **Discriminant group.** $L$ is even and $9$-divisible, and $A_L\supseteq\frac19L/L\cong(\mathbb Z/9)^r$. So $\ell(A_L)=r$: the $3$-part has length $r$, and every $p$-part has length $\le\operatorname{rk}L=r$.
- **Embedding.** For $r\le10$ we have $22-r>r=\ell(A_L)$, so (I2) gives a primitive embedding $j\colon L\hookrightarrow\Lambda$.
- **The surface $S$.** Let $\omega'$ span $H^{2,0}(S')\subset T'_{\mathbb C}=L_{\mathbb C}$ and put $\omega=j(\omega')$. Then $\omega^2=9\,\omega'^2=0$ and $\omega\cdot\bar\omega=9\,\omega'\cdot\bar\omega'>0$. By (I4) there is a marked K3 surface $S$ with period $\omega$.
- **$T(S)=j(L)$.** Let $x\in\mathrm{NS}(S)=\omega^\perp\cap\Lambda$, and write $x=x_L+x_\perp$ in $\Lambda_{\mathbb Q}=j(L)_{\mathbb Q}\oplus j(L)^\perp_{\mathbb Q}$. Then $x_L\cdot\omega=0$. Viewed in $T'_{\mathbb Q}$, $x_L$ is a rational class orthogonal to $\omega'$, hence a rational $(1,1)$-class of $S'$ lying in $T(S')_{\mathbb Q}$. So $x_L=0$. Thus $\mathrm{NS}(S)=j(L)^\perp$, and $T(S)=j(L)$ since $j(L)$ is primitive.
- **Projectivity.** $\mathrm{NS}(S)$ has signature $(1,21-r)$, so $S$ is projective (I4).
- **The isometry.** The map $\psi\colon T(S')_{\mathbb Q}\to T(S)_{\mathbb Q}$, $x\mapsto\frac13x$, is a Hodge isometry: $\psi x\cdot_L\psi y=9\cdot\frac19\,x\cdot y$.
- **Extension to $H^2$.** $H^2(S)_{\mathbb Q}\cong H^2(S')_{\mathbb Q}\cong\Lambda_{\mathbb Q}$. By Witt cancellation $\mathrm{NS}(S')_{\mathbb Q}\cong\mathrm{NS}(S)_{\mathbb Q}$; choose such an isometry $\varphi$. Then $\Phi=\psi\oplus\varphi$ is a Hodge isometry.
- **Algebraicity.** $\Phi$ is algebraic by (I5). Its inverse $\Phi^{-1}$ is the adjoint, induced by the transposed correspondence, hence also algebraic.
- **Transport of HC.** Put $\tilde\Phi=\Phi\oplus\mathrm{id}_{H^0}\oplus\mathrm{id}_{H^4}$, using the classes $[S\times\mathrm{pt}]$ and $[\mathrm{pt}\times S]$. It is an algebraic isomorphism $H^*(S')\to H^*(S)$ of Hodge structures, with algebraic inverse. Its external powers transport Hodge and algebraic classes between $S'^a\times S^b$ and $S^{a+b}$. This gives the HC equivalences.
- **Parity.** $9$-divisibility and Theorem B(1) with $n=9$ give the parity statements. Theorem A(4) gives the flat section.

*(2)* $T(S)$ has rank $r\ge13$. Apply Theorem B(3). $\square$

### 6.4 Theorem D

*(1)*
- **Brauer classes as functionals.** $\mathrm{Br}(S)[n]=\operatorname{Hom}(T,\mathbb Z/n)=\operatorname{Hom}(T/nT,\mathbb Z/n)$. These functionals separate the points of $T/nT$.
- **Conclusion.** If $g$ fixes all of them, then $g\equiv1$ on $T/nT$. So $\det g\equiv1\pmod n$, and $\det g=1$ for $n\ge3$.

*(2)*
- **Matrix model.** Identify $U\oplus U$ with $M_2(\mathbb Z)$ and quadratic form $2\det$. In this model $X=\begin{pmatrix}x&z\\-w&y\end{pmatrix}$ has square $2(xy+zw)$. Then $(A,B)\colon X\mapsto AXB$, for $A,B\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$, is an isometry.
- **A $(-2)$-vector orthogonal to $\alpha$.** Represent $\alpha$ by $\beta\in T^\vee\otimes\mathbb Q$, so that $\alpha(x)=\beta\cdot x\bmod\mathbb Z$. Let $\lambda\in M_2(\mathbb Z)$ be an integral multiple of the $(U\oplus U)$-component of $\beta$. By the Smith normal form, $\lambda=ADB$ with $A,B\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ and $D$ diagonal. Put $\delta=AJB$ with $J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$. Then $\delta^2=2\det J=-2$. Also $\delta\cdot\lambda=J\cdot D=0$, since the polarization of $2\det$ pairs the diagonal of $D$ with that of $J$, which is zero.
- **Conclusion.** So $\beta\cdot\delta=0$, and $s_\delta\beta=\beta+(\beta\cdot\delta)\delta=\beta$. Hence $s_\delta^*\alpha=\alpha$. By Theorem B(2)(a), $s_\delta\in\widetilde{\mathrm O}^+(T)$ with determinant $-1$.
- **When $U\oplus U$ splits off.** For $r\ge14$: $\ell(A_T)\le22-r\le r-5$. (I1) gives $T=U\oplus T_1$, with $T_1$ of signature $(1,r-3)$, indefinite, and $\operatorname{rk}T_1=r-2\ge\ell(A_{T_1})+3$. Applying (I1) again, $T_1=U\oplus T''$. $\square$

### 6.5 Proposition E

*(1)*
- **$T_r$, $4\le r\le8$.** `verify.py` P4–P8 construct explicit vectors $x_1=e_1$, $y_1=3f_1+e_2$, $x_2=e_3$, $y_2=e_3+3f_3+w$, $z_i=a_i+b_i$ in $\Lambda$. Here:
  - the $a_i$ are mutually orthogonal roots of the first $E_8(-1)$;
  - the $b_i$ are mutually orthogonal vectors of square $-4$ of the second $E_8(-1)$;
  - $w=a_8+b_8$.

  The script checks exactly that their Gram matrix is $U(3)^2\oplus\langle-6\rangle^{r-4}$. It checks primitivity by the Smith normal form, and computes $N=T^\perp$ with signature $(1,21-r)$ and $|\det N|=|\det T|$.
- **$T_r$, $r=9,10$.** The lattice is even with $\ell(A_{T_r})=r<22-r$ (L9–L10), so (I2) applies.
- **$T_6'$.** $\ell(A)=6<16$ (W3), so (I2) applies.

*(2)* The very general period has $\mathrm{NS}=N$ and $E=\mathbb Q$.
- **Picard lattice.** For each $x\in T\smallsetminus0$, the locus $\{\omega\cdot x=0\}$ is a proper analytic hypersurface of the period domain $D_N$. There are countably many such $x$.
- **Endomorphisms.** Let $\phi\in\operatorname{End}(T_{\mathbb Q})$ be non-scalar; there are countably many. Then $\{\omega:\phi\omega\in\mathbb C\omega\}$ is contained in the union of the projectivized eigenspaces of $\phi_{\mathbb C}$. These are finitely many proper linear subspaces. The quadric $\{\omega^2=0\}$ is irreducible and nondegenerate for $r\ge3$, so it is not contained in their union. Its open subset $D_N$ is Zariski dense in it, so the intersection is a proper closed analytic subset of $D_N$.
- **Conclusion.** Outside countably many such subsets, $T(S)=T$ and every Hodge endomorphism is a scalar. Such points exist in $D_N^\circ$ by (I4).

*(3)* Floccari's locus is defined by an embedding $T_{\mathbb Q}\hookrightarrow W=(U^3\oplus\langle-m\rangle)_{\mathbb Q}$, where $W$ has signature $(3,4)$. Since $T$ has signature $(2,r-2)$, this forces $r\le6$. For $r=6$, $T_{\mathbb Q}$ is the orthogonal complement of a positive line in $W$. $W$ has Witt index $3$, so $T_{\mathbb Q}$ has Witt index $\ge2$. $T_6'$ has index $1$ (W1: $\langle6,-12,-30,-60\rangle$ is anisotropic at $5$), so it is not in the locus.

*(4)* Parity-freeness follows from Theorem B(1), since all lattices are $3$-divisible. HC for these $S$ is not covered by:
- [1], [2], by (3);
- the curve-product criterion of our rStation 99 [19], which need curve sources not known here;
- the RM results [16], [17], [20], [21], since $E=\mathbb Q$. $\square$

## 7. Discussion: what this means for the GRR route

1. **$\rho\le9$.** Every construction uniform over the $N$-marked moduli stack (Remark F) gives classes in $V^{\mathrm O}$ (Theorem A(3)) and never reaches $\det_T$. This covers relative moduli spaces $M_{X/B}(v)$ with $v\in\mathbb Z\oplus N\oplus\mathbb Z$, their (normalized) universal classes, tautological bundles on relative Hilbert schemes, and Fourier–Mukai kernels built over the family. For $\rho\le8$ it also covers twisting by one Brauer class (Theorem D(2)).

   The symmetry breaking must therefore come from data with no $\det=-1$ monodromy:
   - spin or Kuga–Satake data, as in curve products [19] and Floccari's Kummer-type input;
   - orientation choices;
   - full level-$n$ labellings with $n\ge3$, e.g. of $\mathrm{Br}(S)[3]$ (Theorem D(1)).
2. **$\rho\ge12$.** Up to an algebraic isogeny (Theorem C(1)), the determinant problem lives on a parity-free NL locus. On it, $\det_T$ is a flat Hodge section of **every** $N$-marked family, with no cover needed. So the barrier of [10] says nothing there, and a uniform GRR construction over these loci is not excluded by monodromy. We do not know such a construction.
3. **$\rho\in\{10,11\}$.** Parity-free lattices of rank $12$ would need $\ell(A_T)=10$. They cannot be $n$-divisible for $n\ge3$, since that would force $\ell(A_T)=r=12>10$. For $r=11$, $T'(9)$ fails Nikulin's strict inequality (G2), and our explicit embedding does not extend (L15). These ranks are left open.

## 9. Limitations

- **No new case of HC.** Parity-freeness removes one topological obstruction; it constructs no cycle.
- **Theorem A(3) needs its hypotheses.** It needs a $\det=-1$ element in the actual monodromy of the family used. Remark F, which identifies the monodromy of the moduli stack and realizes $s_\delta$ by Picard–Lefschetz, is quoted from [8], [10] and standard theory, not reproved. A construction that only exists over a finite cover of the moduli may escape it.
- **Rank window.** $r\in\{11,12\}$ is not decided, except for the necessary conditions B(2).
- **Imported results.** Theorem C(1) and Proposition E for $r=9,10$ and $T_6'$ use Nikulin's existence theorem (I2) as stated in [7]. We did not reprove it.
- **No new Hodge structures.** Theorem C changes only the integral lattice. $S$ and $S'$ have the same rational Hodge structure, so the determinant problem is unchanged in difficulty; only its "uniform" accessibility changes.
- **Markman's orientation character.** For hyper-Kähler monodromy it would refine Theorem A on moduli spaces. We do not treat it.
