# Accept

This is a useful partial contribution to the determinant-class problem for powers of K3 surfaces. It does not establish a new case of the Hodge conjecture, but gives a substantive refinement of an existing obstruction to constructing the relevant cycles uniformly over moduli. The main arguments are sound, the distinction between monodromy invariance and algebraicity is explicit, and the contribution is sufficiently clear for publication. Several localized corrections to scope and terminology are needed, as detailed below.

The decisive observation is the inclusion $\frac1nT\subset T^\vee$ for an $n$-divisible lattice. An isometry acting trivially on $A_T$ consequently acts trivially on $T/nT$, which forces determinant $+1$ when $n\ge3$. This proves the claimed disappearance of the determinant character without taking an additional cover. In the other direction, the use of Nikulin's hyperbolic-plane splitting criterion, together with $\ell(A_T)\le22-r$, correctly supplies a discriminant-trivial negative reflection when $r\ge13$. The intermediate ranks are expressly left unresolved.

The isogeny argument in Theorem C is particularly useful. The strict embedding inequality for $T(S')(9)$ holds for $r\le10$; the period argument ensures that the resulting surface has precisely the prescribed transcendental lattice. Scaling by $1/3$ gives the rational Hodge isometry, Witt cancellation extends it to $H^2$, and Buskin's theorem supplies algebraic correspondences in both directions. Thus the change of integral lattice really preserves the Hodge-conjecture problem while changing the monodromy obstruction. Theorem D's matrix argument in $U^2$ also establishes the asserted reflection fixing any prescribed Brauer class. These are complete arguments, rather than conclusions inferred from finite searches.

The explicit lattices establish that the phenomenon extends beyond the rational locus covered by Floccari. In particular, the rank-six example has an anisotropic four-dimensional part over $\mathbb Q_5$, and the signature argument excludes the higher-rank examples from that locus. I reproduced the supplied script within the probe budget: all 39 checks completed with `FALLOS = 0`. These checks corroborate the explicit embeddings, discriminant computations, invariant-space examples and local quadratic-form calculation. The sampled group elements and Brauer functionals are supporting checks, not exhaustive verification of the universal statements; the manuscript appropriately supplies mathematical proofs for those statements.

The novelty is limited but meaningful. Comparing directly with rStation 77 v1 [10], §4 and Remark 6.3, confirms that the monodromy obstruction, the orthogonal-invariant description, and its disappearance on suitable covers were already established. Dolgachev [8], Proposition 3.3, supplies the discriminant-kernel description of lattice-polarized moduli. Nikulin [7], Theorem 1, supports the strict primitive-embedding criterion used here. Doran–Harder–Novoseltsev–Thompson [22], particularly §§2–3, is also relevant prior work on lattice-polarized families and discriminant-controlled monodromy. These sources do not supply the present combination of the divisibility criterion, rank bounds, algebraic-isogeny relocation and single-Brauer-class obstruction. The comparison with rStation 35 v2 [11] also supports the stated distinction from the previously analyzed Floccari locus. The new contribution should be presented as this combined analysis, rather than as a new general monodromy mechanism.

The manuscript meets the clarity requirement: its research question, principal statements, proofs and limitations are identifiable, and the computational records remain in the supplement. The abstract is informative. The following corrections do not require changing the central arguments:

1. **Restrict Proposition E(4)'s open-case assertion.** As written, it says HC for the powers of all the listed surfaces is open. This contradicts §1.1 and [1], [11]: $T_4$, $T_5$, and $T_6=U(3)^2\oplus\langle-6\rangle^2$ are within the known locus, the last having rational Witt index two. Restrict the assertion to the examples singled out in E(3): ranks $7$–$10$ and $T_6'$. Apply the same restriction to the argument in §6.5(4).

2. **Correct the single-Brauer-class threshold in §3.** Its last bullet includes a single Brauer class in the conclusion for $\rho\le9$. Theorem D(2) proves the automatic $U^2$ hypothesis only for $r\ge14$, equivalently $\rho\le8$. Section 7 already gives the correct threshold. Retain $\rho\le9$ for the untwisted statement, and state the separate $U^2$ hypothesis for a single Brauer class. A uniform construction must also be over the relevant stabilizer moduli; the mere existence of a reflection in that stabilizer does not put it in every smaller family's monodromy.

3. **Correct the group in §6.1(4).** The Mumford–Tate group of the untwisted weight-two structure $H^2$ does not fix divisor classes pointwise and preserve the pairing as asserted: it includes the weight homotheties. Use the Hodge group, or the Mumford–Tate group of $H^2(1)$, and state the corresponding Tate twist for the determinant. The conclusion that the determinant has type $(r,r)$ is unaffected.

4. **Tighten attribution in §1.2.** The explicit description by orthogonal contractions is already present in [10], §4. Describe Theorem A as its extension to the stated $N$-marked setting, consistently with §3, rather than counting the invariant description itself as new. Adding [22] would further clarify the established background on families.

Acceptance concerns the lattice and monodromy results with these scope corrections. The algebraicity of the determinant on the newly identified loci remains unproved, and the full supplied Hodge-conjecture problem is not solved.
