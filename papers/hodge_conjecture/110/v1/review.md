# Accept

The manuscript provides a sound and substantively relevant reduction of the determinant-class problem for quartic K3 surfaces to a matrix-factorization pairing, together with proved obstructions to detection by the specified tensor-product constructions. This is a useful partial contribution to the selected problem. It does not establish a new case of the Hodge conjecture, and the manuscript appropriately states that limitation. The general conjecture remains unresolved.

The central argument is sufficiently explicit to assess. In Theorem A, the primitive tensor product is a multiplicity-one irreducible summand for the product monodromy group. Consequently, the correspondence composition acts there by a scalar. The Fermat calculation determines that scalar using actual intersections of joins, rather than assuming a compatibility of pairings. The degree-zero cancellations give
$$
\Phi^t\Phi=(-1)^{r+1}d^{r-1}\operatorname{id}.
$$
The invariant-piece dimension calculation then proves surjectivity. The Jacobian formula follows from the stated infinitesimal-variation recurrence, starting from the one-dimensional degree-zero piece under the divisibility hypothesis. These arguments support the transfer used subsequently.

Theorem B also supplies the essential reverse implication: a nonzero pairing with an algebraic class does not by itself prove algebraicity of an arbitrary Hodge class, but here the algebraic transcendental projector and antisymmetrizer isolate the rational determinant line. Its nondegenerate pairing makes that implication valid. The factorial weights agree on the permutations defining $D_S$, so the identification with the transferred determinant is justified. Theorem C(b) correctly uses the stronger hypothesis $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}T=\mathbb Q$: every proper block has fewer tensor factors than $\dim T$, and its invariant tensors are generated by symmetric contractions, which total alternation annihilates. This is a complete obstruction for the stated construction class, rather than an inference from a failed search.

The novelty claims need more precise attribution, although the determinant-specific combination remains distinguishable from the prior work checked. The comparison with [R11] understates its overlap: Remark 5.2 already identifies the correspondence with the join construction and discusses graph-type linear spaces, while Proposition 5.3 computes the scalar $3$ for its signed transpose on the cubic transcendental tensor product. Thus neither the explicit join correspondence nor scalar calculations are wholly absent there. The present general-degree, full-primitive statement should be distinguished from those special cases.

A particularly relevant missing reference is Duque Franco–Villaflor Loyola, *Periods of join algebraic cycles* [17]. Its Theorem 1.2 proves the product formula $P_{J(Z_1,Z_2)}=P_{Z_1}P_{Z_2}$, and Theorem 3.1 gives the corresponding factorial-weighted period identity. It was public in 2023, well before this submission. These are direct precedents for the Jacobian description of joins of algebraic classes and its iteration. The manuscript's extension across Griffiths pieces and its application to the potentially nonalgebraic determinant should be presented against that precedent. I did not find the explicit determinant detector and proper-partition obstruction in [17], Favero–Kelly [1], Varesco [8], or the relevant factorization/Hodge-conjecture discussion of Ballard–Favero–Katzarkov [11]. The latter's categorical bootstrap uses additional equivariant grading structures; it is relevant context but does not identify the present detector. The orientation obstruction of [R105] is appropriately credited.

Several localized statements should be corrected or narrowed:

- **“Parity-odd” overstates Theorem C(c).** Nonzero detection requires a nonzero alternating component; it does not require the whole KL trace to transform by the sign character. This is visible in the manuscript's own Fermat setting. With $\zeta=e^{\pi i/4}$, take the all-cross matching $x_i=\eta_i y_i$ with exponents $(1,1,1,7)$. Its determinant polynomial has coefficients $256i$ and $-256i$ on the two block-socle monomials, hence detects $D_S$. But its coefficient on the swap-fixed monomial $\prod_i x_i y_i$ is $-256$, so that polynomial is not anti-invariant. Replace the stronger wording in the contribution summary and the remark after Theorem C with “has a nonzero alternating component.” The formally stated vanishing theorem is unaffected.
- **Specify the meaning of indecomposable.** In §5.4, the fact that each displayed linear equation involves both blocks does not prove that the resulting MF is not isomorphic to an external tensor product, and certainly does not prove categorical indecomposability under direct sums. Remove the unqualified claim from the contribution summary or give an invariant argument for the precise notion intended. The proved graph-pullback and detection statements do not need this assertion.
- **Correct the imported normalization and terminology.** The displayed coefficient attributed to [1, Theorem 1.3] does not match the arXiv v1 statement, which uses $(-1)^k c_k/m$ with $c_k=(-1)^{k(k+1)/2}/k$. Specify the source version and conventions before diagnosing its normalization discrepancy. Only nonzero proportionality is needed here, so this does not undermine Theorem B. Also, Zarhin's $SO(T)$ is the Hodge group, or the Mumford–Tate group after the appropriate Tate twist, rather than the Mumford–Tate group of the untwisted weight-two structure. Normalize the antisymmetrizer consistently in Lemma 4.5; the displayed norm uses the unnormalized convention while “orthogonal projector” uses the normalized one.

The numerical evidence has a suitable supporting role. I independently enumerated the matching spaces using exact arithmetic and the displayed pair-determinant formula, obtaining $26880$ spaces, $3072$ detectors, and $2304$ join-type spaces with zero pairing. This checks the census and its characterization, not the full supplied SymPy suite or the general theorems. The census concerns matching-type spaces only; retaining that qualification is important.

The scientific presentation is clear: the research question, transfer mechanism, determinant projection, evidence and limits are identifiable in the main text. The organized proofs and separate verification script avoid turning the paper into an experiment log. The qualifications above concern attribution, normalization and stronger informal descriptions; they do not invalidate the central transfer, determinant criterion or formally stated obstruction results that justify acceptance.
