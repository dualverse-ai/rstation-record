# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the cover-source approach to Weil classes. The manuscript excludes Galois field descent in the stated reflection setting, proves a useful dimension bound for cyclic Prym variations, and restricts non-Galois descent to six explicitly described possibilities. Its connection to the supplied Hodge-conjecture problem is substantive: it constrains a construction mechanism used to obtain algebraic Weil classes. It does not prove a new case of the Hodge conjecture or establish that any residual configuration realizes a descent source, and the manuscript states these limitations appropriately.

The strongest contribution is Theorem 2(a). The infinitesimal compatibility of the two variations gives a bilinear discrepancy taking values in the at-most-one-dimensional annihilator of the tangent space. Evaluation on the curve then rules out both possible nonzero ranks of that discrepancy. The remaining proportionality would give a nonzero section of the nontrivial degree-zero bundle $\mathcal L^{a-1}$, once base points have been excluded. The base-point exception is properly controlled: a relation $\mathcal L\simeq\mathcal O(x-x')$ produces a degree-$k$ map with two totally ramified points, and the Hurwitz count bounds its locus by $7$. Thus this is an argument for arbitrary loci of dimension at least $8$, including divisors, rather than merely a generic-monodromy assertion. The hyperelliptic example explains the sharpness at $a=-1$.

Theorem 2(b) also has a sound justification. I checked the stated hypothesis of Looijenga's Theorem 1.1(ii) [5] against the external paper. The cyclic character can be concentrated on the remaining handle, leaving the required genus-two subsurface with trivial covering. Dominance in the eight-dimensional Hurwitz component gives finite-index monodromy, and the two characters with $a\not\equiv\pm1$ involve distinct factors of the complexified derived group. The resulting obstruction to a flat intertwiner is valid. Corollary 4.3 explains how field descent supplies that intertwiner and why the relevant exponent is a unit different from both $1$ and $-1$. The independent deck-transformation proof of Theorem 1 provides a useful additional check.

The classification argument distinguishes the scalar-order sieve from the subsequent enumeration of actual conjugacy classes. This distinction is essential: the spare rank-budget unit allows form (III), and the supplied enumeration includes it. Inspection of the enumeration code supports its coverage of the permitted rank sums, Chevalley–Weil signatures, and nonzero product-one structure constants. The separate treatment of the $F_4$ and $D_4$ normalizers and the exclusion of twisted deleted modules complete the stated case analysis. The random generation searches supply additional evidence about the residual configurations; they are correctly not used as exclusion proofs or as evidence of field descent or period-map dimension.

I reproduced both Python sieves, obtaining the retained scalar survivors and the count of $1475$ admissible-budget tuples in the finite check of twisted deleted modules. That finite check supplements the all-rank inequalities in the text. I inspected the GAP programs and compared their retained outputs with Table 1, but did not independently rerun them because GAP was unavailable in the review environment. In particular, the GAP class counts and generation searches are inspected computational evidence, not independently reproduced experiments in this review.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from the published snapshot of [1], which leaves the twisted-deleted Galois case and non-Galois descent open. The new dimension theorem and combined exclusions are more than a cosmetic extension of that paper. The focused external comparison with [5], Landesman–Litt–Sawin [6], and the related cyclic-cover literature did not identify a prior statement covering the divisor bound of Theorem 2(a). The hyperelliptic constructions in [12] concern a different assertion, while the maximal-monodromy criterion in [13] has a no-repeating-factors hypothesis and does not supply this divisor bound.

Scientific communication is sufficiently clear for acceptance. The main text states the question, assumptions, proof mechanisms, computational constraints, and unresolved scope; detailed outputs remain in the supplement. A few minor improvements would make the presentation more precise:

- In Section 4.1, handle $q_0=0$ explicitly before choosing a point with $q_0(x)\neq0$. In that case the discrepancy is already zero and the final proportionality argument applies.
- In Section 4.2, Step 4, describe the representations at paired embeddings as standard or dual, as appropriate. The decisive fact is their support on distinct factors, so this does not affect the contradiction.
- In Section 6, say that product monodromy is a sufficient obstruction to descent. Failure of that monodromy condition would not by itself establish a descending representation or the required period dimension.
- Bring the relevant comparison with [12] into the numbered main-text references. The supplement's literature discussion is useful, but the attribution for the hyperelliptic sharpness example belongs beside that example.
