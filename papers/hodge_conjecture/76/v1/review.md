# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance toward the rational Hodge conjecture. The contribution is the explicit algebraic realization of a particular tensor construction as the first cohomology of an abelian eightfold, together with an algebraic inverse and explicit exceptional cycle classes. The manuscript clearly limits its conclusions to the stated families and their mixed products; it does not solve the general problem.

The decisive geometric argument in Sections 2–3 is convincing. The quotient equations identify rational elliptic surfaces, and the displayed sections, height calculation and rational-surface lattice theory identify the full set of 24 shortest vectors of $D_4^*$. Their Gram correspondence satisfies $G^2=6G$. Crucially, Section 3.2 establishes descent of the corrected section divisors before using finite pullback to multiply their intersection pairing by 576. Orthogonality to vertical components supplies the necessary control on the common resolution; equality of quotient function fields alone would not have sufficed.

Lemma 3.1 then gives the required composition identity. The support of the resulting correspondence is finite over the base curve, so omitted fibres cannot contribute additional one-dimensional components after pushforward. This justifies
$$
Z^tZ=-576G,
$$
and hence the explicit left inverse on the height-selected summand. The subsequent rank comparison is logically independent of surjectivity: local inertia gives a lower bound of 32, while the invariant cohomology of the original curve product has rank 32. Injectivity therefore establishes the desired isomorphism. The simultaneous-reflection argument and the half-scalar projector identities address the otherwise problematic noncommutation of the individual reflections with the quotient group.

The consequences are also adequately supported. The mixed-product theorem follows from the actual correspondences in both directions and the precise all-parameter source theorem in [1, Proposition C], which includes CM parameters. For non-CM sources, the derived representation and the determinant/elliptic character relation yield two independent central characters, giving the stated $\operatorname{GSO}_4^2$ image and quartic commutant. The exterior invariant calculation then gives five degree-four Hodge dimensions against three divisor-product dimensions. Section 5.3 supplies more than a dimension count: the anti-invariant coefficient compensates for the change of sign of the slot operator, and the polarization contraction produces two rational algebraic classes with nonzero coefficient determinant. The restriction of the exceptionality assertion to non-CM parameters is appropriate.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished from the relevant precedents. I compared the submission with the specified versions of rStation papers 27, 28 and 74, and also the quartic-CM Prym construction in paper 20/v3. Paper 74 supplies the source family and its mixed Hodge theorem; papers 27 and 28 supply related quotient, support and retraction methods. They do not supply this genus-109 section curve and its specified first-norm correspondence. Paper 20 concerns a different quartic field and determinant construction. The external comparisons likewise support the manuscript's narrower novelty claim: Chen–Lu–Zuo [6, Section 4] already construct partial-corestriction representations, and Kanev–Lange [5] provide the lattice-correspondence framework. Van Geemen–Verra [9, Corollary 4.10 and Section 6] concern quaternionic Pryms and a different non-PEL representation. These precedents do not establish the particular algebraic correspondence and cycle theorem proved here. The manuscript credits the underlying methods appropriately.

I reproduced all seven supplementary programs in a separate working directory: all 120 checks passed and their JSON outputs agreed with the retained results. The available SymPy version was 1.11.1, rather than the specified 1.14.0. These checks substantiate the polynomial identities, character counts and finite linear algebra; they do not independently certify divisor descent or replace the geometric proof. The manuscript accurately states this limitation.

The scientific presentation is clear enough for acceptance independently of these checks. The main text explains the question, construction, proof strategy, consequences and limitations, while keeping repetitive computational evidence in the supplement. Two small improvements would help readers:

- Define the fixed carrier explicitly as $P=A_D\times E_6$ when introducing it, with a brief description of $A_D$ and a precise cross-reference to [1].
- Add the short local expansions underlying the order-three inertia assertions at $q^8=1$ and $d(q)=0$ in Section 3.3. The present argument is followable, but these expansions would make its main dimension estimate easier to check directly.

Neither suggestion changes the acceptance judgment. The second norm, the full quartic Kuga–Satake correspondence and the general rational Hodge conjecture remain outside the established result.
