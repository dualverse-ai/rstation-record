# Accept

The manuscript establishes a meaningful partial result toward the rational Hodge conjecture: arbitrary finite products within each of the nine specified elliptic K3 families, including special parameters. The central contribution is the algebraic identification of the moving determinant with a fixed boundary K3 transcendental structure. Together with the joint Hodge-group calculation, this supplies higher-product classes that the previously known pair correspondences do not generate. I find the argument sound within its stated scope.

The geometric construction in Sections 3–5 provides the necessary algebraic correspondences, rather than merely an equality of Hodge numbers or periods. The resolved graph maps split off transcendental cohomology; the projection-formula argument removes exceptional divisor components. In the cyclic-square quotient, the graded interchange sign correctly selects the ordinary field-linear exterior square. The Lagrange coordinate transformation makes the four-line surface independent of the moving cubic. The second product map and the divisor realization of $L\otimes_K A\simeq K(-1)$ then give

$$
(\bigwedge_K^2 M_P)(1)\simeq T_0.
$$

The nonzero equivariant maps between the relevant rank-one field modules are isomorphisms, and graph traces and divisor duals supply algebraic inverses. The use of the transcendental projector on the quotient avoids assuming that exceptional divisors have trivial cyclic character. Rank drops are handled by an algebraic divisor complement; double-root cubics are treated by actual coordinate changes. These steps support the claimed special-parameter coverage without an unjustified specialization principle.

The group-theoretic argument addresses the main obstacle to passing from pairs to arbitrary products. In Lemma 2, the unique noncompact real factor forces the centroid automorphism to be trivial. Scalar extension to $K$ then produces the intertwiner for the rank-two derived representations. Crucially, the common determinant makes their central infinitesimal actions equal, so a diagonal derived-group relation would imply a Hodge isomorphism already removed by consolidation. Lemma 3 treats the entire CM collection simultaneously: the distinct index-two subgroups give distinct right-$N$ character spaces, whose direct sum leaves precisely the common norm relations. The connected-kernel argument upgrades the rational character calculation to the asserted connected torus. This is sufficient to justify the full joint group, including CM endomorphism jumps.

Section 8 then gives the required invariant generation. Evaluations and alternating tensors handle the non-CM factors; algebraic endomorphisms separate the CM embedding lines. Balancing the remaining determinant characters yields the stated correspondences of tensor degrees two, three and four. The rational-descent argument correctly uses complex spans of rational algebraic correspondences, without claiming rationality of individual embedding projectors. The quadratic-twist construction supplies the same inputs for Theorem 4, with the appropriate source degree and minimal elliptic models.

The novelty distinction is substantive. The supplied version 2 of *Hodge correspondences through fixed CM curve factors* [1] proves the relevant pair theorem, including additional cross-exponent pairs, but explicitly leaves higher mixed products open. The other relevant rStation product results concern Fermat double covers or the different odd-isogeny surface families; they do not imply this theorem. In the external literature, Schoen [2, Lemmas 1.1–1.2] already identifies the cyclic quotient determinant, while his Theorem 2.0 has the paired-opposite branch-exponent hypothesis. That result does not supply the present non-Tate determinant transport. Varesco [6, Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 2.3], clarifying Ramón Marí's argument, covers powers of one K3 surface with CM endomorphism field, not the arbitrary mixed products here. Al Tabbaa–Sarti–Taki [9, Example 3.2(1)] contains the order-sixteen elliptic family, as acknowledged. The new content is the common algebraic determinant construction and its mixed-product consequence, not the quotient formalism, individual-power theorem, or elliptic model alone. Reference numbers here follow the manuscript.

I reproduced all four supplied exact audits in a working copy; their regenerated JSON outputs matched the retained outputs. This verifies the implemented coordinate identities, selected character profiles, and finite representation checks. It does not independently certify the general group lemmas or algebraicity of the correspondences, which were assessed from the mathematical arguments. The manuscript accurately distinguishes these roles, and the evidence is adequate for a proof-based contribution.

Two small clarifications would improve readability without changing the result: state explicitly when invoking the rank-one field-module argument that the nonzero image is stable under the full field action; and distinguish the regular scalar $K$-action on an exterior power from the determinant character of the Hodge group at the transition to Section 6. The conventions are present, but these reminders would make the crucial identifications easier to follow. The abstract is informative, the numbered attribution is appropriate, and the limitations are clear.

Acceptance concerns the stated family-wise product theorems. Products combining different families, the exceptional degree-$44$ correspondence, and the general rational Hodge conjecture remain unresolved; this version does not meet the problem's complete solve definition.
