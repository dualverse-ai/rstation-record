# Common determinant cycles, chord quotients, and products of elliptic surfaces

## 1. The result

For $7\leq n\leq12$ and $(a,b)\in\mathbb C^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}$, let $S_{n,a,b}$ be the smooth minimal elliptic model of

$$
y^2=t^n+x^3+ax+b.
$$

These are K3 surfaces. Their pair products satisfy the rational Hodge conjecture by the fixed-curve transfer theorem [1, Theorems 11 and 13]. The following result treats higher products with independent parameters.

**Theorem 1.** Fix $n\in\{7,8,9,10,11,12\}$. For every $r\geq1$ and every collection $(a_i,b_i)\ne(0,0)$, the rational Hodge conjecture holds for

$$
\prod_{i=1}^r S_{n,a_i,b_i}.
$$

Repeated factors, CM specializations, transcendental rank drops, and cubics with a double root are allowed. The exponent $n$ is fixed throughout the product.

The geometric ingredient is an algebraic realization of the determinant of a moving rank-two cyclotomic structure by one fixed K3 surface. Put

$$
K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_n),\qquad
S_{n,0}:\quad y^2=t^n+x^3-\frac{x}{3}+\frac{2}{27},\qquad
T_0=T(S_{n,0}).
$$

Here $T(S)=\operatorname{NS}(S)_{\mathbb Q}^{\perp}\subset H^2(S,\mathbb Q)$ is the transcendental Hodge structure. If $T=T(S_{n,a,b})$ has $K$-rank two, we construct an algebraic isomorphism

$$
(\bigwedge_K^2 T)(1)\simeq T_0. \tag{1}
$$

The exterior square has weight four, followed by one Tate twist. If $T$ has $K$-rank one, there is instead an algebraic isomorphism $T\simeq T_0$. These statements include algebraic inverses on the specified cohomological summands.

For two nonisomorphic rank-two structures, (1) supplies classes on $S_i^2\times S_j^2$ that are not generated by pair correspondences. We prove that these determinant correspondences, the corresponding classes on $S_i^2\times S_j$ when $T_j$ has rank one, and the pair correspondences generate all Hodge tensors. Thus the new conclusion is not inferred just from the known pair theorem.

Section 9 proves the same finite-product conclusion for each fixed $n\in\{14,16,18\}$ in the quadratic-twist family $Y^2=X^3+a u^2X+b u^3+u^{n/2+3}$, by an explicit source of degree $2n$.

**Theorem 5**, proved in Sections 10–12, additionally permits arbitrary finite products mixing $S_{8,a,b}$, $R_{16,a,b}$ and their quadratic-base-change surfaces $Z_{a,b}:y^2=t^{16}+x^3+ax+b$. It includes every allowed parameter, with no CM-field nonincidence condition. The new geometric input is an elliptic-chord threefold whose general fibers are rational surfaces. Its curve-supported third cohomology realizes the norm correspondences needed at every incident CM field. **Proposition 7** gives the underlying compatible-curve transfer for all even exponents.

The general rational Hodge conjecture remains open. Beyond the three-family combination in Theorem 5, we do not assert the conjecture for arbitrary products with different exponents. The exceptional degree-$44$ correspondence between the K3 and general-type surface blocks discussed in [1] also remains open.

## 2. Prior work and the additional step

The quotient of a power of a cyclic cover by the kernel of the product character and permutations is classical. Schoen identifies its primitive part with a field-linear determinant, including the graded permutation sign [2, Lemmas 1.1–1.2]. His cycle theorem [2, Theorem 2.0] treats determinant Hodge classes under a hypothesis that the branch exponents pair with their negatives. Our four-branch determinants have nonzero holomorphic components; we transport them through a fixed surface and then a K3 carrier. Shioda–Katsura's product decompositions provide the underlying split-variable geometry [3]. We specialize these constructions to cyclic covers with three finite branch points, give the resulting common four-line surface explicitly, and identify its contribution with the transcendental structure of $S_{n,0}$ using divisor correspondences.

Zarhin determines the individual Hodge groups of K3-type structures [4]; we also use the presentation in [5, Chapter 3, Theorems 3.7 and 3.9]. Powers of one K3 surface with a CM endomorphism field already satisfy the Hodge conjecture. This follows from the algebraicity of Hodge isometries and the unitary invariant-theory argument of Ramón Marí, with its scope clarified by Varesco [6, Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 2.3]. We do not claim those self-product results as new.

Commelin defines the determinantal motive of a unitary K3-type structure and proves that it is again of K3 type [7, Definition 11.9 and Lemma 11.10]. His Theorem 16.1 deliberately excludes unitary rank two from a statement recovering a motive from its derived group. Remark 16.2 explains the obstruction: the derived group does not determine the determinant, and products of a fixed non-CM elliptic curve with different CM elliptic curves give counterexamples. Our argument retains an actual common determinant and uses it explicitly to remove the central ambiguity. The abstract determinantal motive is not the novelty here.

Half-twist embeddings through a CM curve factor are established by van Geemen–Izadi [8]. Their existence does not by itself put the half twist in the image of curve correspondences. The new construction in Section 11 proves this support using an explicit rational-surface fibration; Section 12 uses it at every incident CM field, including special parameters.

The earlier transfer [1] makes every Hodge map between any two members of a fixed-exponent family algebraic, including special parameters. It does not supply the determinant classes on higher products. The additional contribution is their geometric realization, a joint group calculation accounting for all special CM fields, and the resulting arbitrary finite-product theorem. The torus calculation is joint: we do not assume that pairwise independence implies independence of an arbitrary collection of tori.

## 3. Algebraic summands and the curve source

We work with rational cohomology. An algebraic map between specified summands means a map induced by a rational algebraic correspondence after applying the specified algebraic projectors. Tensor products, permutations, compositions, and contractions by nondegenerate algebraic pairings retain this property. Statements about motives below are only shorthand for these operations modulo homological equivalence.

For a K3 surface the projector to $T(S)$ is algebraic. Choose a rational basis $D_i$ of its Néron–Severi group and invert its intersection matrix $(q_{ij})$. The algebraic Néron–Severi projector on $H^2$ is $\sum q^{ij}D_i\times D_j$. Subtract it from the degree-two Künneth projector. Curve Künneth projectors are algebraic as well. Finite-order automorphisms give rational cyclotomic projectors by polynomials in their graphs.

If a number field acts algebraically, the projector to a balanced tensor product is algebraic: its separability idempotent is a rational combination of tensor products of field operators. Exterior and symmetric powers then use the appropriate permutation projectors. The scalar field action on a field-linear determinant is implemented on one balanced tensor position; applying a scalar on every position would instead give its determinant power. This regular scalar action must be distinguished from the determinant character of the Hodge group used in Section 6. On $H^1\otimes H^1$, geometric interchange is minus ordinary tensor flip. We keep this sign when using curve surfaces.

For completeness, the relevant product source and graph identities from [1] are recalled next. Let $P=x^3+ax+b$, $m=\operatorname{lcm}(2,n)$, and take smooth projective normalizations

$$
C_n:\ v^2=u^n+1,
\qquad B_m:\ w^m=P(x).
$$

The cubic has a simple root whenever $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$, so $B_m$ is connected, including the double-root case. There is a dominant rational map of degree $m$,

$$
q:C_n\times B_m\dashrightarrow S_{n,a,b},
\qquad(t,x,y)=\bigl(uw^{m/n},x,vw^{m/2}\bigr). \tag{2}
$$

The source kernel is generated by

$$
(u,v;x,w)\longmapsto(\zeta_n^{-1}u,-v;x,\zeta_mw).
$$

The automorphism $\alpha:(u,v)\mapsto(\zeta_nu,v)$ intertwines with $\tau:t\mapsto\zeta_nt$. On $H^{2,0}(S)$, $\tau$ acts as $\zeta_n$. All other cyclotomic parts of $T(S)$ are pure $(1,1)$ and vanish. Thus $K$ acts on $T(S)$.

The primitive summand $L=P_nH^1(C_n)$ has $K$-rank one. Its holomorphic embedding set consists of the primitive $e$ with $0<e<n/2$. Resolve (2) as $C_n\times B_m\xleftarrow{e}Z\xrightarrow{h}S$. If $v\in T(S)$ and $E$ is exceptional for $e$, then $(h^*v,E)=(v,h_*E)=0$. The same projection formula for divisors shows that $h^*T(S)$ lies in the transcendental mixed part. Consequently the graph maps

$$
b=e_*h^*:T(S)\longrightarrow L\otimes H^1(B_m),
\qquad
a=\Pi_T h_*e^*:L\otimes H^1(B_m)\longrightarrow T(S)
$$

are algebraic and satisfy $ab=m\,1_T$. The image is invariant under the source kernel.

The paired $B_m$ character for a primitive $C_n$ character $e$ is

$$
j(e)=\frac m2+\frac mn e\pmod m.
$$

All these characters have one conductor $N$. The actual quotient $B_m\to B_N$, given by $w\mapsto w^{m/N}$, identifies their summand with $W=P_NH^1(B_N)$, where $B_N:z^N=P(x)$. The possibilities are

| $n$ | $m$ | $N$ | $[K:\mathbb Q]$ | maximal $\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}T$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | 14 | 14 | 6 | 12 |
| 8 | 8 | 8 | 4 | 8 |
| 9 | 18 | 18 | 6 | 12 |
| 10 | 10 | 5 | 4 | 8 |
| 11 | 22 | 22 | 10 | 20 |
| 12 | 12 | 12 | 4 | 8 |

Identify $K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_N)$ on this selected curve block by letting $\zeta_n$ act as $-\delta$, where $\delta:z\mapsto\zeta_Nz$. Thus corresponding embeddings satisfy

$$
\zeta_N^k=-\zeta_n^e,
\qquad \frac{k}{N}\equiv\frac12+\frac en\pmod1. \tag{3}
$$

With this action the invariant product is the balanced tensor

$$
M_P=L\otimes_KW.
$$

For a separable cubic, each primitive $B_N$ eigenspace has dimension two, since its four branch points have nontrivial primitive local characters. The Hodge profile computed below gives

$$
\dim_KM_P=2,
\qquad h(M_P)=(1,2[K:\mathbb Q]-2,1). \tag{4}
$$

The maps $a,b$ restrict to this algebraic summand. Hence

$$
M_P=bT(S)\oplus A_P, \tag{5}
$$

algebraically, using the projector $ba/m$. The complement is rational pure $(1,1)$ by (4), and all its classes are divisors on the product surface. In particular $\dim_KT(S)$ is one or two.

We also recall why all pair morphisms are algebraic. Every Hodge map between two $T(S)$ commutes with the $K$-actions: its commutator with the two deck generators vanishes on the holomorphic and antiholomorphic lines, so its image is a rational pure-$(1,1)$ substructure of the target $T$, hence zero. For the fixed rank-one CM structure $L$, every $K$-linear map on the full rational tensor products

$$
F:L\otimes_{\mathbb Q}U_1\longrightarrow L\otimes_{\mathbb Q}U_2
$$

can be written $F=\sum k_j\otimes A_j$, with $(k_j)$ a rational basis of $K$. If $F$ is Hodge, restriction to each pure one-dimensional $L_\sigma$ shows that $\sum\sigma(k_j)A_j$ preserves both weight-one Hodge types. Inverting the embedding matrix shows that every $A_j$ is Hodge. Lefschetz $(1,1)$ represents these maps of curve cohomology by divisors. Given $f:T(S_1)\to T(S_2)$, apply this argument to $F=b_2fa_1$ on the full spaces $L\otimes_{\mathbb Q}H^1(B_{m,i})$. The graph maps are $K$-linear, and $a_2Fb_1=m^2f$. Thus $f$ is algebraic. The balanced summands enter through the graph maps; the decomposition of $F$ is asserted on the full rational tensor products. This is [1, Lemma 10 and Theorem 13], and also makes every Hodge endomorphism algebraic.

## 4. A common four-line quotient

Assume first that $P(x)=\prod_{i=1}^3(x-r_i)$ has distinct roots. On $B_N^2$, take the quotient by anti-diagonal $\mu_N$ and factor interchange. Its invariant function field is generated by

$$
b=-(x_1+x_2),\qquad c=x_1x_2,\qquad v=z_1z_2,
$$

with $v^N=\prod_i(r_i^2+br_i+c)$. Over $\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathbb P^1=\mathbb P^2$, the homogeneous equation is

$$
v^N=a^{N-3}\prod_{i=1}^3(ar_i^2+br_i+c).
$$

Set

$$
X_i=\frac{ar_i^2+br_i+c}{P'(r_i)}.
$$

Lagrange interpolation gives $a=X_1+X_2+X_3$, and the coordinate change is invertible. Choose $\rho\in\mathbb C$ with $\rho^N=\prod_iP'(r_i)$. After replacing $v$ by $v/\rho$, the quotient is the fixed surface

$$
Z_N:\quad z^N=X_1X_2X_3(X_1+X_2+X_3)^{N-3}. \tag{6}
$$

Thus there is an actual degree-$2N$ rational quotient map $B_N^2\dashrightarrow Z_N$ for every separable $P$. Root orderings and constant rescalings are choices over $\mathbb C$ and define algebraic correspondences. The residual action on the first curve induces $z\mapsto\zeta_Nz$ on (6).

Let $E=\mathbb Q(\zeta_N)$. Anti-diagonal invariance pairs equal characters in $H^1(B_N)\otimes H^1(B_N)$. Because geometric interchange is minus ordinary flip, its invariant primitive part is

$$
\bigwedge_E^2W. \tag{7}
$$

There is no contribution from the unmixed $H^2$ pieces to a nontrivial residual character. This is also Schoen's determinant description [2].

Let $Z$ be a smooth projective model of (6), chosen with the residual cyclic action. Its irregularity is zero: the anti-diagonal invariants in $H^1(B_N^2)$ vanish because $H^1(B_N)^{\mu_N}=H^1(\mathbb P^1)=0$. Finite quotient singularities are rational, so resolution preserves this vanishing of $H^1(\mathcal O)$. Thus $H^1(Z)=H^3(Z)=0$, and its degree-two Künneth projector is the diagonal minus the point and fundamental-class projectors. Subtracting the Néron–Severi projector gives an algebraic projector onto $T(Z)$.

We use $P_NT(Z)$ to avoid any assertion that every exceptional divisor has trivial character. The resolved graph pulls $P_NT(Z)$ injectively into (7). Invariant holomorphic two-forms descend to the resolved finite quotient: its structure-sheaf cohomology is the invariant cohomology, and rational singularities preserve it under resolution. The primitive invariant space in (7) has a nonzero holomorphic two-form component, by the profiles below. The image is therefore nonzero. Since (7) has $E$-rank one, the equivariant injection is onto.

Its graph trace supplies an inverse up to the degree $2N$. Hence all spaces (7) are algebraically identified with the same $P_NT(Z)$. This is the first transport step, and it is a correspondence statement, not an identification of periods alone.

## 5. The fixed K3 carrier and the boundary

Consider the smooth projective normalizations

$$
A_N:\ v^N=u(1-u),\qquad B_0:\ w^N=x^2(1-x).
$$

There is a degree-$N$ dominant rational map

$$
A_N\times B_0\dashrightarrow Z_N,
\qquad(X_1,X_2,X_3,z)=(xu,x(1-u),1-x,vw). \tag{8}
$$

Indeed $\sum X_i=1$ and $X_1X_2X_3=u(1-u)x^2(1-x)$. Conversely $x=X_1+X_2$ and $u=X_1/(X_1+X_2)$, and there are $N$ choices of $v$. The kernel is anti-diagonal $\mu_N$. Both primitive curve blocks

$$
A=P_NH^1(A_N),\qquad W_0=P_NH^1(B_0)
$$

have $E$-rank one. Their branch tuples are $(1,1,N-2)$ and $(2,1,N-3)$, and every primitive character is nontrivial at all three branch points. The same graph argument identifies $P_NT(Z)$ algebraically with $A\otimes_EW_0$. Transfer the field action by (3).

Here are the profiles and the Tate factor needed to contract (8). For a branch tuple $\mathbf a$, the holomorphic multiplicity of the character $k$ is

$$
h_{\mathbf a}(k)=-1+\sum_j\left\langle-\frac{ka_j}{N}\right\rangle, \tag{9}
$$

where $\langle\cdot\rangle$ is fractional part. In our tuples none of the displayed fractions is integral. Denote the three multiplicities for $A,W_0,W$ by $h_A,h_0,h_W$, respectively, and put $h_L(e)=1$ for $0<e<n/2$ and $0$ otherwise. Formula (3) gives

$$
h_A(k)+h_L(e)=1,\qquad h_A(k)+h_0(k)=h_W(k). \tag{10}
$$

For example, $h_A(k)$ is $1$ when $0<k/N<1/2$, and $0$ when $1/2<k/N<1$. The first identity follows directly from (3). The second uses $\langle2k/N\rangle+\langle-2k/N\rangle=1$ in (9).

If $0<e<n/2$, then $k/N=1/2+e/n$ and $h_0(k)=1$ exactly when $e/n<1/6$. Among primitive $e$ for $7\leq n\leq12$, this occurs only at $e=1$. The possible equality at $n=12,e=2$ is not primitive. Conjugation supplies the other half. Thus

$$
h_L(e)+h_0(k)=
\begin{cases}
2,&e=1,\\
0,&e=-1,\\
1,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases} \tag{11}
$$

Equations (10)–(11) prove (4). They also show that $L\otimes_K A$ is pure $(1,1)$. It is an algebraic summand of the cohomology of $C_n\times A_N$, so Lefschetz $(1,1)$ represents every rational class in it by a divisor. Any nonzero rational class is a $K$-basis because this space has $K$-rank one. Its $K$-orbit and intersection-dual divisors give an algebraic isomorphism

$$
L\otimes_K A\simeq K(-1). \tag{12}
$$

The pairing used here is nondegenerate. The balanced summand pairs each character with its conjugate. Alternatively, it lies in the mixed curve cohomology, is orthogonal to a product ample class, and is pure $(1,1)$, so the Hodge index theorem gives a negative definite rational intersection pairing. The notation $K(-1)$ means $[K:\mathbb Q]$ copies of the Tate object with the regular $K$-action; its matrix correspondences are algebraic.

Combining (7), (8), and (12) gives actual algebraic identifications

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_P:=(\bigwedge_K^2M_P)(1)
&\simeq\bigl[L^{\otimes_K2}\otimes_K\bigwedge_K^2W\bigr](1)\\
&\simeq\bigl[L^{\otimes_K2}\otimes_K A\otimes_K W_0\bigr](1)\\
&\simeq L\otimes_KW_0.
\end{aligned} \tag{13}
$$

The permutation of the middle two degree-one factors introduces a common minus sign, which can be absorbed into a correspondence. All projectors and inverse contractions in (13) are algebraic.

Now put $X=1/3-x$. Then

$$
x^2(1-x)=X^3-\frac X3+\frac{2}{27}.
$$

The curve $B_0$ is exactly the selected quotient of the curve source (2) for $S_{n,0}$. That graph map injects $T_0$ into $L\otimes_KW_0$, is nonzero on $H^{2,0}$, and its image is stable under the full $K$ action. Since the target has $K$-rank one, the map is an algebraic isomorphism, with graph-trace inverse. This proves

$$
D_P\simeq T_0,\qquad \dim_KT_0=1. \tag{14}
$$

If $T(S)$ has $K$-rank two, (5) has no complement, and (14) is (1). If $T(S)$ has $K$-rank one, its complement $A_P$ in (5) also has $K$-rank one and consists of divisors. A divisor basis and its dual identify it algebraically with $K(-1)$. The determinant of (5) is $T(S)\otimes_KA_P$, so (14) now gives an algebraic identification $T(S)\simeq T_0$.

Finally every depressed cubic with a double root and $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$ is $(x-r)^2(x+2r)$ with $r\ne0$. Scaling $x,t,y$ over $\mathbb C$ identifies its K3 model with $S_{n,0}$, compatibly with $t\mapsto\zeta_nt$. This treats the double-root boundary by actual isomorphisms. No specialization principle for Hodge classes is used.

## 6. The joint derived group

We now prove the group statement needed for the tensor conclusion. Let $T_i=T(S_i)$. The field $E_i=\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T_i)$ embeds in $\mathbb C$ through its action on the holomorphic line. Its kernel would have pure-$(1,1)$ image and is zero. Under these embeddings all copies of $K$ have the same image and the same distinguished embedding. Zarhin's theorem says $E_i$ is a CM field, since it contains $K$. The rank bound leaves only three possibilities:

1. $\dim_KT_i=1$, $E_i=K$;
2. $\dim_KT_i=2$, $E_i=K$;
3. $\dim_KT_i=2$, $[E_i:K]=2$, $\dim_{E_i}T_i=1$.

The first and third cases are of CM type, meaning their Hodge group is a torus. The second is not of CM type, despite its CM endomorphism field. All Hodge isomorphisms between factors and all their endomorphisms are algebraic by Section 3. We may therefore consolidate Hodge-isomorphic factors when calculating the joint group and restore repeated factors afterward.

Put $F=K^+$. For a factor of the second kind, Zarhin gives

$$
G_i=\operatorname{Res}_{F/\mathbb Q}U(T_i,h_i),\qquad
\mathfrak g_i=\operatorname{Lie}(G_i^{\mathrm{der}})
=\operatorname{Res}_{F/\mathbb Q}\mathfrak{su}(T_i,h_i).
$$

The Lie algebra $\mathfrak g_i$ is simple over $\mathbb Q$ and its centroid is $F$. At the distinguished real embedding of $F$ the Hermitian signature is $(1,1)$; at every other embedding it is definite. Thus $\mathfrak g_i\otimes\mathbb R$ has precisely one noncompact factor, and all factors are forms of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$.

**Lemma 2.** After consolidating isomorphic factors of the second kind, their joint derived Hodge group is the product of their individual derived groups. This remains true on adjoining the CM factors.

*Proof.* For two factors, the joint Hodge group is connected, reductive, and surjects onto each $G_i$. If its derived Lie algebra is a proper subdirect product, simplicity and the Lie-algebra Goursat argument make it the graph of a rational isomorphism $\phi:\mathfrak g_i\to\mathfrak g_j$.

The induced automorphism $\alpha$ of the centroid $F$ must carry the unique noncompact real factor to the unique noncompact factor. Their distinguished embeddings are the same, say $\tau_0$, so $\tau_0\alpha=\tau_0$ and $\alpha=1$. Hence $\phi$ is $F$-linear. Extension to $K$ uses the given module structures: $X\otimes k\mapsto kX$ identifies each $\mathfrak{su}(T_i,h_i)\otimes_FK$ with $\mathfrak{sl}_K(T_i)$. Consequently $\phi\otimes_F1_K$ is a $K$-linear isomorphism between split $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ algebras. Every such isomorphism is inner. There is therefore an invertible $K$-linear map $A:T_i\to T_j$ intertwining the derived actions. This rank-two step uses that type $A_1$ has no diagram automorphism; it produces a rational map with the specified $K$-linearity.

The image of the joint central Lie algebra on each $T_i$ consists of $K$-scalars, because it commutes with the full projected unitary group. Write these scalars as $c_i,c_j$. The algebraic identifications (1) make the determinant representations equal; differentiating gives $2c_i=2c_j$. The map $A$ thus intertwines the center as well. Connectedness makes it invariant under the full joint Hodge group, so it is a Hodge isomorphism. This contradicts the consolidation.

For finitely many factors, a semisimple subdirect Lie algebra of a product of simple rational Lie algebras is a product of diagonal blocks: a simple ideal maps either trivially or isomorphically to each simple quotient. The two-factor argument excludes any block joining distinct factors. Equality of connected derived groups follows from equality of Lie algebras. Adding a torus creates no new semisimple quotient, proving the last assertion. $\square$

The determinant hypothesis in this proof is indispensable. Without it, the central actions on the intertwiner can differ, as in Commelin's example [7, Remark 16.2].

## 7. All joint CM relations

For a rank-one structure over a CM field $E$, normalized to have the inclusion $E\subset\mathbb C$ as its unique holomorphic embedding, the Hodge group is

$$
U_E=\ker\bigl(\operatorname{Res}_{E/\mathbb Q}\mathbb G_m
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Res}_{E^+/\mathbb Q}\mathbb G_m\bigr).
$$

Indeed the Galois conjugates of the K3 cocharacter are the differences of conjugate embedding coordinates, which span the full cocharacter space of this torus. The same applies to $T_0$ with $E=K$.

Two normalized rank-one CM K3 structures are Hodge isomorphic precisely when their embedded endomorphism fields in $\mathbb C$ agree. Equality gives a field-linear isomorphism preserving their holomorphic line and all other types, as in [7, Lemma 16.11]. Conversely a Hodge isomorphism identifies endomorphisms and their actions on that line, hence their embedded fields. After consolidation, the fields $E_i$ of the third kind are therefore distinct quadratic extensions of the same embedded $K$.

**Lemma 3.** Let $K/\mathbb Q$ be a Galois CM field and $E_1,\ldots,E_s$ distinct embedded CM fields with $[E_i:K]=2$. For rank-one K3 structures normalized as above, adjoin the rank-one $K$ structure. Their joint Hodge group is

$$
U_{E_1}\times_{U_K}\cdots\times_{U_K}U_{E_s}, \tag{15}
$$

with the $K$ structure carrying the common norm. For $s=0$ it is $U_K$.

*Proof.* Choose a finite Galois CM extension $L/\mathbb Q$ containing the normal closures of all $E_i$. Write

$$
G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q),\quad
N=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K),\quad
H_i=\operatorname{Gal}(L/E_i).
$$

Complex conjugation $c$ is central in $G$. Since $K$ is Galois, $N$ is normal. Each $H_i$ is a normal subgroup of index two in $N$; the distinct fields give distinct characters $\chi_i:N\to\{\pm1\}$.

Identify rational character spaces of norm-one tori with odd functions on embedding cosets. More explicitly, let

$$
R^- =\{f:G\to\mathbb Q:f(cg)=-f(g)\}.
$$

Then $X^*(U_{E_i})_{\mathbb Q}$ is $(R^-)^{H_i}$, the functions constant on right $H_i$-cosets. The common space $X^*(U_K)_{\mathbb Q}$ is $(R^-)^N$. Right translation by $N$ commutes with left $G$-translation and with $c$. Since $N/H_i$ has order two,

$$
(R^-)^{H_i}=(R^-)^N\oplus R^-_{\chi_i},
\qquad
R^-_{\chi_i}=\{f:f(gn)=\chi_i(n)f(g)\}. \tag{16}
$$

The spaces for distinct $\chi_i$ are linearly independent. This remains true when the $E_i$ are not Galois over $\mathbb Q$; only their degree-two relation to $K$ was used.

Pair a tuple of characters with every Galois conjugate of the joint K3 cocharacter. Each normalized cocharacter is supported at the identity embedding and its conjugate with opposite coefficients. Up to the same nonzero factor, the pairing at $g$ is the sum of the corresponding functions evaluated at $g$. Thus the rational relations are the kernel of

$$
(R^-)^N\oplus\bigoplus_i(R^-)^{H_i}
\longrightarrow R^-,\qquad(f_0,f_1,\ldots,f_s)\longmapsto\sum_i f_i.
$$

By (16) this kernel consists exactly of the relations among the repeated $(R^-)^N$ summands. Those are the common norm relations defining (15).

This establishes equality at the level of rational character spaces. It is equality of connected groups as well: over $\mathbb C$, each norm $U_{E_i}\to U_K$ is coordinatewise a product map $\mathbb G_m^2\to\mathbb G_m$, with connected kernel. The fiber product is connected, and containment with equal dimension gives equality. $\square$

Adjoin $T_0$ to the original collection. This does not enlarge its joint Hodge group when a rank-two factor is present, because $T_0$ is its determinant tensor construction; if all factors have rank one they are already isomorphic to $T_0$. Lemmas 2–3 now determine the full joint group. It is the fiber product over $U_K$ of the rank-two unitary groups from the second case and the tori from the third case, with any rank-one $K$ factor carrying the common determinant character. The kernel over the toric quotient is the full product of the special unitary groups by Lemma 2; the quotient is (15). Connectedness then gives the asserted full group.

## 8. Algebraic invariant generators

Twist each $T_i$ by $(1)$ so that Hodge tensors have weight zero. Complexify and fix one of each conjugate pair of $K$-embeddings. A non-CM unitary factor acts on a two-space $V_i$ and its dual. The first fundamental theorem for $SL_2$ says that its tensor invariants are generated by evaluations and alternating two-tensors. The latter are semi-invariants for the determinant, with weights $d$ and $d^{-1}$. This is the usual invariant theory of the standard two-dimensional representation; tensor polarization gives the multilinear form of the assertion.

For a quadratic CM factor, its two $E_i$-lines above the chosen $K$-embedding have characters $x_i,y_i$ with $x_iy_i=d$. Their duals have inverse characters. After pairing each line with its dual, invariance under the norm kernel forces equal remaining multiplicities in the two lines. The remaining tensors are products of determinant two-tensors or their duals. The two possible orderings are obtained from an alternating tensor by projecting one position to an $E_i$-line. Such embedding-level projectors lie in the complex span of the algebraic $E_i$-endomorphisms proved in Section 3.

Repeated isomorphic factors create no additional issue: the algebraic isomorphisms allow evaluations and alternating tensors in any of their positions. A rank-one $K$ factor carries weight $d$ itself. After taking invariants under the independent derived and norm kernels, the common $U_K$ has independent coordinates at the conjugate embedding pairs. The remaining $d$'s and $d^{-1}$'s must balance, and can be paired. Consequently all invariant tensors are generated, up to tensor products and permutations, by:

* pair correspondences and endomorphisms;
* maps between a rank-two determinant and a rank-one $T_j$;
* maps between two rank-two determinants.

The last two are algebraic by their identifications with $T_0$, composing with its polarization and $K$-action. They give classes of tensor degree three and four, respectively. The inverse pairings are algebraic: on each surface transcendental summand they come from the diagonal and the algebraic projector, and on the determinant carrier they come from $T_0$.

We make the rational descent explicit. In any fixed tensor degree, let $\mathcal A$ be the rational span of algebraic cycle classes on the appropriate product. Tensor products, the algebraic correspondences above, and factor permutations preserve these spans. The complexification of the rational $K$-action on $T_0$ separates its embedding lines, so the complex span of the rational determinant correspondences supplies every embedding-level determinant pairing. Similarly, the algebraic $E_i$-actions separate the individual CM lines. Thus $\mathcal A\otimes\mathbb C$ contains every complex invariant just described. Hodge-group invariants commute with scalar extension, and algebraic classes are rational Hodge classes. It follows that $\mathcal A$ is the entire rational Hodge space. No individual complex eigenprojector is asserted to be a rational cycle.

Finally, $H^0$, $H^4$, and $\operatorname{NS}_{\mathbb Q}$ of a K3 surface are algebraic, and $H^1=H^3=0$. The Künneth decomposition reduces the Hodge conjecture on the stated products to exactly these transcendental tensor spaces. This proves Theorem 1.

To see the additional content beyond pair products, take nonisomorphic non-CM factors $T_i,T_j$. Their Hom space is zero. On $S_i^2\times S_j^2$, the common determinant gives a nonzero invariant with complex component

$$
\bigwedge^2(T_i)_\sigma\otimes\bigwedge^2(T_j)_{\bar\sigma}.
$$

Pair classes within $T_i$ or $T_j$ pair conjugate embeddings, and cross pair classes vanish. They cannot generate this component. The determinant construction supplies precisely this missing type of algebraic class.

## 9. Three quadratic-twist families

The same construction applies to three further families, using a different explicit source map. For $n\in\{14,16,18\}$ and $k=n/2$, let $R_{n,a,b}$ be the smooth minimal model of

$$
Y^2=X^3+a u^2X+b u^3+u^{k+3},\qquad(a,b)\ne(0,0). \tag{17}
$$

**Theorem 4.** For each fixed $n\in\{14,16,18\}$, the rational Hodge conjecture holds for every finite product of members $R_{n,a_i,b_i}$ of (17), including repetitions, CM specializations, transcendental rank drops, and double-root cubics.

*Proof.* The equation is minimal at $u=0$: the nonzero coefficients have orders $(2,3)$, $(2,k+3)$, or $(\infty,3)$. At nonzero finite points it is minimal because either $a\ne0$ or the nonzero roots of $b+u^k$ are simple. At infinity, with $z=1/u$, $X'=z^4X$, $Y'=z^6Y$, it becomes

$$
Y'^2=X'^3+a z^6X'+b z^9+z^{9-k}.
$$

Here $9-k$ is $2,1,0$, respectively. Thus this is a minimal elliptic model with fundamental line bundle $\mathcal O(2)$, and its smooth minimal surface is K3 by the canonical bundle formula [9]. The automorphism

$$
\sigma(u,X,Y)=(\zeta_n^2u,\zeta_n^2X,\zeta_n^3Y)
$$

has order $n$ and acts by $\zeta_n$ on $du\wedge dX/Y$. It follows that $K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_n)$ acts on $T(R_{n,a,b})$. For $n=16$ this is the known family in [10, Example 3.2(1)]; no novelty of the elliptic models or quadratic twisting is claimed.

Take $C_n:v^2=s^n+1$ and $B_n:w^n=x^3+ax+b$. There is a degree-$2n$ dominant rational map

$$
Q:C_n\times B_n\dashrightarrow R_{n,a,b},\qquad
(u,X,Y)=(s^2w^2,s^2w^2x,s^3v w^{k+3}). \tag{18}
$$

Substitution verifies (17). Conversely $x=X/u$, there are $n$ choices of $w$ over the generic point, two choices of $s$ with $s^2=u/w^2$, and then $v$ is determined. The kernel is generated by the commuting maps

$$
d:(s,v,w)\mapsto(\zeta_n^{-1}s,-v,\zeta_nw),\qquad
\rho:(s,v,w)\mapsto(-s,-v,w).
$$

The second generator is independent of the first, so the group has order $2n$. The map is equivariant for $s\mapsto\zeta_ns$ and $\sigma$, and

$$
Q^*\left(\frac{du\wedge dX}{Y}\right)
=2\frac{ds}{v}\wedge\frac{dx}{w^{k-1}}. \tag{19}
$$

Use $L=P_nH^1(C_n)$, again of $K$-rank one with holomorphic characters $0<e<n/2$. Invariance under $d$ selects $j=e+k$ modulo $n$ on $B_n$; $\rho$ acts trivially since $e$ is odd. These selected characters have conductors

| $n$ | $N$ | $[K:\mathbb Q]$ | $\dim_{\mathbb Q}M_P$ for separable $P$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14 | 7 | 6 | 12 |
| 16 | 16 | 8 | 16 |
| 18 | 9 | 6 | 12 |

The actual quotient $B_n\to B_N$, $w\mapsto w^{n/N}$, realizes their primitive summand $W$. With $\zeta_n$ acting as $-\delta_N$, the embedding relation (3) holds unchanged. In particular the profiles (9)–(10) still apply. For these three $n$, the only primitive integer in $0<e<n/6$ is $e=1$, so (11) holds as well. Thus $M_P=L\otimes_KW$ has $K$-rank two and Hodge numbers $(1,2[K:\mathbb Q]-2,1)$.

The resolved graph of (18) gives $K$-linear maps $b:T(R)\to M_P$, $a:M_P\to T(R)$ with $ab=2n$. The same projection-formula argument as in Section 3 removes exceptional divisor classes. Consequently $M_P=T(R)\oplus A_P$ algebraically, with $A_P$ consisting of divisor classes. For arbitrary two parameters, the common holomorphic scalar makes each Hodge map $f:T(R_1)\to T(R_2)$ $K$-linear. Apply the rank-one CM curve-factor argument to $b_2fa_1$ on $L\otimes_{\mathbb Q}H^1(B_{n,i})$; composing recovers $(2n)^2f$. Thus all pair maps and endomorphisms are algebraic.

The moving curve square has exactly the four-line quotient (6) for this $N$. The degree-$N$ map (8) and the divisor contraction (12) identify its determinant with $L\otimes_KW_0$. Applying (18) to $P_0=x^3-x/3+2/27$ identifies that rank-one space algebraically with $T(R_{n,-1/3,2/27})$. Hence

$$
(\bigwedge_K^2M_P)(1)\simeq T(R_{n,-1/3,2/27})
$$

is an algebraic, $K$-linear identification. Rank drops use the divisor complement $A_P$ as in Section 5. If $P(x)=\lambda^3P_0(x/\lambda)$ is a double-root cubic, the coordinate change

$$
u=\lambda^{6/n}u_0,\qquad
X=\lambda^{1+6/n}X_0,\qquad
Y=\lambda^{3/2+9/n}Y_0
$$

identifies its surface with the fixed carrier; choose compatible powers of the nonzero complex number $\lambda$. This change commutes with $\sigma$.

All inputs of Sections 6–8 are now established: $K$ is Galois CM, each $T(R)$ has $K$-rank at most two, all pair maps are algebraic, and the determinant has a common algebraic $K$-linear carrier. The joint-group and invariant-generation arguments apply verbatim, proving the theorem. $\square$

Theorems 1 and 4 alone concern products within a single one of the nine families. The quadratic-base-change surface $y^2=t^{16}+x^3+ax+b$ has both $S_8$ and $R_{16}$ transcendental pieces. The next sections construct the additional norm correspondences that permit this mixed combination and its higher products.


## 10. A common norm and the mixed field-incidence criterion

We now allow products mixing the families
$$
S_{a,b}=S_{8,a,b},\qquad R_{a,b}=R_{16,a,b},\qquad
Z_{a,b}:\ y^2=t^{16}+x^3+ax+b.
$$
All parameter pairs are nonzero and independent. Put
$$
K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_8),\quad F=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{16}),\quad
D_K=T(S_{-1/3,2/27}),\quad D_F=T(R_{-1/3,2/27}).
$$

**Theorem 5.** The rational Hodge conjecture holds for every finite product of arbitrary members of the three families $S$, $R$, and $Z$. Repetitions, CM specializations, transcendental rank drops, and double-root cubics are allowed.

First there is an actual common-norm bridge. On $A=S_{1,0}$ the formulas
$$
u=\frac{t^2}{x},\qquad X=\frac{t^{10}}{x^4}+\frac u3,\qquad
Y=\frac{t^{11}y}{x^6}
\tag{20}
$$
give a degree-two map to $R_{-1/3,2/27}$. Substitution verifies its equation; the inverse recovers
$$
x=\frac{X-u/3}{u^5},\qquad t^2=\frac{X-u/3}{u^4}.
$$
Its differential pulls $du\wedge dX/Y$ back to $2\,dt\wedge dx/y$. Both transcendental structures have rational rank eight: the target is the rank-one boundary structure over $F$, and the source has $K$-rank at most two. Graph and transpose identify $D_F$ with $T(A)$ algebraically. This is $K$-linear: $t\mapsto\zeta_8t$ becomes $(u,X,Y)\mapsto(\zeta_8^2u,\zeta_8^2X,\zeta_8^3Y)$, the square of the order-sixteen automorphism. Theorem 1 consequently also realizes
$$
\bigwedge\nolimits_K^2D_F(1)\simeq D_K
\tag{21}
$$
algebraically. The models and the quadratic-twist operation are established geometry [9,10]; the argument needs the compatible graph map.

For a rank-two CM $S$ factor write $E=\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}T(S)$, quadratic over $K$. For a rank-two CM $R$ factor write $H=\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}T(R)$, quadratic over $F$. Embed the fields through their holomorphic actions as in Section 7. Call a pair *incident* if
$$
E\ne F,\qquad E\subset H.
\tag{22}
$$
Then $H=EF$ is biquadratic over $K$. Each $E\ne F$ can be incident with only one embedded $H$, and each $H$ with at most two such $E$ fields. We do not assume $H/\mathbb Q$ is Galois.

**Lemma 6.** The $S,R$ conclusion of Theorem 5 holds for every collection with no incident pair.

*Proof.* Adjoin $D_F$, represented by the actual $S$ surface $A$ through (20). Choose a Galois CM normal closure $\Lambda$ containing all fields and set
$$
G=\operatorname{Gal}(\Lambda/\mathbb Q),\quad
N=\operatorname{Gal}(\Lambda/K),\quad
M=\operatorname{Gal}(\Lambda/F).
$$
Both $N,M$ are normal in $G$, with $[N:M]=2$. Let $V^-$ be the odd functions on $G$ from Lemma 3. The character spaces contributed by quadratic $E/K$ and $H/F$ decompose as
$$
(V^-)^{\operatorname{Gal}(\Lambda/E)}
=(V^-)^N\oplus V^-_{\chi_E},\qquad
(V^-)^{\operatorname{Gal}(\Lambda/H)}
=(V^-)^M\oplus W^-_{\psi_H},
$$
where $\chi_E$ and $\psi_H$ are right-$N$ and right-$M$ quadratic characters, respectively. Distinct $H$ give distinct $\psi_H$, so their $W$ spaces are independent. For $E\ne F$, the restriction $\chi_E|_M$ is nontrivial, and
$$
V^-_{\chi_E}\subset W^-_{\psi_H}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\chi_E|_M=\psi_H
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad E\subset H.
\tag{23}
$$
Different characters give orthogonal spaces for the invariant inner product on functions. Thus, without (22), all $H/F$ norm-kernel directions are independent of the $S$ factors. This is a calculation of the entire joint relation space: decompose first by right-$M$ characters and then by distinct right-$N$ characters. The shared $(V^-)^M$ part is exactly the common $D_F$ norm. The norms have connected kernels over $\mathbb C$, so containment and equality of rational character spaces give equality of connected tori as in Lemma 3.

For non-CM factors the simple rational derived Lie algebras have dimensions $3[K^+:\mathbb Q]=6$ on the $S$ side and $3[F^+:\mathbb Q]=12$ on the $R$ side. No simple diagonal can join the two sides. Within each side Lemma 2 applies after algebraic consolidation, and adjoining tori creates no semisimple quotient.

Take invariants under the independent $R$ special-unitary groups and relative norm kernels. Section 8 expresses them through pairings and $R$ determinants, realized algebraically by copies of $D_F$. Rank-one $R$ factors already equal $D_F$ algebraically. Via (20), the remaining tensors involve only $S$ surfaces, so Theorem 1 supplies their cycles. Reverse the algebraic replacements and use Section 8's rational algebraic-span descent. $\square$

When incidence occurs, (23) identifies the new relation as the norm $U_H\to U_E$. Its Hodge-theoretic generator is
$$
\bigwedge\nolimits_E^2T(R)(1)\simeq T(S).
\tag{24}
$$
The left side has normalized rank-one $E$ K3 type. Its algebraic exterior projector alone does not supply the displayed isomorphism algebraically. The next section constructs the additional map.

## 11. An elliptic-chord construction of a fixed curve source

The construction works for every even integer $N\ge4$. Its application to (24) is $N=8$. This uniform statement concerns specified summands, rather than all Hodge classes on arbitrary powers of the curves.

For a separable $P(x)=x^3+ax+b$, put
$$
B:\ w^{2N}=P(x),\quad C:\ v^2=u^N+1,\quad
K_N=\mathbb Q(\zeta_N),\quad W=P_{2N}H^1(B).
$$
Write $\theta(w)=\zeta_{2N}w$, $\alpha(u,v)=(\zeta_Nu,v)$ and $h(u,v)=(u,-v)$. The primitive block $W$ has rank two over $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2N})$ and rank four over $K_N$, acting by $\theta^2$. On $W\otimes W$ define
$$
A=\theta^2\otimes\theta^{-2},\quad
\tau=\theta\otimes\theta^{-1},\quad
p_V=e_A\,\frac{1-\tau}{2}\,\frac{1+\sigma}{2},
\tag{25}
$$
where $e_A$ averages the order-$N$ group and $\sigma$ is geometric interchange on $B^2$. The primitive and degree-one projectors are understood. On the $e_A$ image, $\tau^2=1$ and $\sigma\tau\sigma=\tau^{-1}=\tau$, so (25) is an algebraic idempotent. The graded sign makes its image $V_P$ the cross-quadratic part of the ordinary $K_N$-exterior square: it pairs the two embeddings of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2N})$ over each $K_N$ embedding. Its $K_N$-rank is four.

The factors in (25) are self-adjoint for intersection: deck adjoints are inverses and conjugate characters are retained. Thus $V_P$ is nondegenerate. Mixed curve cohomology is orthogonal to a product ample class, so it is primitive in $H^2(B^2)$. Its algebraic $K_N$ action has adjoint complex conjugation.

**Proposition 7.** Let $L_N=P_NH^1(C)$, with its natural $K_N$ action. For some finite direct sum $U_P$ of first cohomologies of smooth projective curves there is an algebraic $K_N$-linear surjection
$$
a_P:L_N\otimes_{\mathbb Q}U_P\twoheadrightarrow V_P.
\tag{26}
$$
Its transpose is a compatible algebraic injection. Every $K_N$-linear rational Hodge map between two such $V_P$, or between their nondegenerate $K_N$-stable summands with algebraic projectors, is algebraic.

*Proof.* Put $z_i=w_i^N$ on the elliptic curve $E_P:z^2=P(x)$ and $q=w_1w_2$. The quotient of $B^2$ by $A$ and interchange has degree $2N$ and is the cyclic cover $q^N=z_1z_2$ of $\operatorname{Sym}^2E_P$. Let the two elliptic points sum to $(r,s)$; write their chord as $z=m(x-r)-s$. Then
$$
s^2=P(r),\qquad q^N=sm^3+(3r^2+a)m^2+3rsm+s^2.
\tag{27}
$$
The two other intersections with the cubic are the roots of
$$
x^2+(r-m^2)x+(a+r^2+m^2r+2ms)=0.
$$
This also recovers the unordered pair. The involution induced by $\tau$ sends $(r,s,m,q)$ to $(r,-s,-m,q)$.

On the product with $C$, the coordinates
$$
T=uq,\qquad z=sm,\qquad Z=svq^{N/2},\qquad r=r
$$
give a degree-$2N$ quotient
$$
Y:\ Z^2=P(r)T^N+z^3+(3r^2+a)z^2+3rP(r)z+P(r)^2.
\tag{28}
$$
Its kernel is generated by
$$
\begin{aligned}
d:(u,v,s,m,q)&\mapsto(\zeta_N^{-1}u,-v,s,m,\zeta_Nq),\\
\eta:(u,v,s,m,q)&\mapsto(u,-v,-s,-m,q).
\end{aligned}
$$
Conversely choose $s^2=P(r)$ and then $q^N=Z^2/P(r)-T^N$; recover $m=z/s$, $u=T/q$ and $v=Z/(sq^{N/2})$. There are exactly $2N$ generic choices.

Equivalently, (28) is the quotient function field of $X=C\times B^2$ by interchange and the generators
$$
D=(\alpha^{-1}h,\theta^2,1),\qquad E=(h,\theta,\theta^{-1}).
\tag{29}
$$
The two diagonal generators have orders $N,2N$ and independent cyclic subgroups on the three factors; $E^2=(1,A)$. Interchange normalizes their subgroup. The full group $G$ has order $4N^2$, equal to the composite degree. We will use its projector on the original smooth curve product.

The general $T$-fiber of (28) is rational. Set
$$
I=a^2-3ar^2-9br,\quad
J=2a^3+18a^2r^2+27abr+27b^2-27br^3.
$$
After $x=z+(3r^2+a)/3$ its equation is
$$
Z^2=x^3-\frac{I(r)}3x+\frac{J(r)}{27}+P(r)T^N.
\tag{30}
$$
For generic fixed $T$ this is an elliptic surface over $\mathbb P^1_r$ with a section and coefficient degrees at most two and three. Its discriminant, up to a nonzero conventional scalar, is
$$
P(r)\bigl[P(r)(-4a^3-27b^2-27T^{2N})-2J(r)T^N\bigr].
\tag{31}
$$
Its $T^{2N}$ coefficient is $-27P(r)^2$, so it is nonzero. Since $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$, $I$ has degree one or two and is nonzero. No finite place has valuation at least four in the $x$ coefficient. At infinity, the degree-one fundamental-line-bundle model has valuation two or three there, and valuation three in the constant coefficient for generic $T$. It is minimal everywhere and is a rational elliptic surface [9, Sections 4.10 and 8.4].

Choose a smooth projective model of $Y$. A rational parametrization of its geometric generic $T$-fiber is defined over a finite extension of $\mathbb C(T)$. The corresponding smooth projective curve $D_0$ gives a dominant generically finite rational map $D_0\times\mathbb P^2\dashrightarrow Y$. Resolve it by blow-ups in smooth centers. The resulting third cohomology is a sum of $H^1(D_0)(-1)$ and the $H^1$ of the blown-up curves with twist $(-1)$, via algebraic blow-up and projective-space correspondences. Graph push-pull equals the degree on $H^3(Y)$. Therefore this group is generated by algebraic correspondences from curves with twist $(-1)$.

Here is the quotient-support step with its boundary contributions retained. Resolve the graph of $X\dashrightarrow Y$ by $p:X'\to X$ and $f:X'\to Y$. For $\xi\in H^3(X)^G$ put $\beta=f_*p^*\xi$. On a common free quotient open, finite étale trace gives
$$
p_*f^*\beta=|G|\xi.
$$
The difference on $X$ is supported on a divisor. The supported and Gysin descriptions of coniveau express such degree-three classes through $H^1$ of divisor resolutions with twist $(-1)$ [11, Section 3]. Those $H^1$ groups are algebraically generated by curves: restriction to a smooth ample curve is injective; semisimplicity gives a weight-one Hodge retraction; Lefschetz $(1,1)$ on the product of that curve and the surface represents the retraction by a divisor. Composing with the divisor inclusion into $X$ is a codimension-two curve-to-threefold correspondence. The term $p_*f^*\beta$ is already curve-generated. Select finitely many curves spanning the finite-dimensional invariant space and apply the algebraic group projector. This proves curve generation of $H^3(X)^G$ without asserting an uncorrected graph inverse or vanishing of exceptional contributions.

Identify the required summand. The two primitive $B$ characters above a $K_N$ character $j$ are $j,j+N$. Invariance under $E$ in (29) requires their difference to be $N$ modulo $2N$, because $h$ acts by $-1$ on $H^1(C)$. Invariance under $D$ requires the $C$ character $e$ to satisfy
$$
e=j+\frac N2\pmod N.
\tag{32}
$$
Interchange selects the ordinary alternating cross tensor. Let $\widetilde L_N$ be this $C$ summand, with $K_N$ action relabeled by $\zeta_N\mapsto-\alpha$. Its $j$-embedding comes from the $e$ in (32). If $4\mid N$, this is the primitive-$N$ summand; if $N\equiv2\pmod4$, it is the primitive-$(N/2)$ summand. Each has $K_N$-rank one. Its holomorphic type is $j>N/2$, complementary to the type $j<N/2$ of $L_N$. The primitive-$N$ projector for $-\alpha$, the group average, and the degree-one Künneth projectors thus select
$$
\widetilde L_N\otimes_{K_N}V_P\subset H^3(X)^G.
\tag{33}
$$
All projectors are algebraic and commute on this component. The curve-generation argument gives an algebraic surjection $U_P(-1)\to(33)$.

The balanced tensor $L_N\otimes_{K_N}\widetilde L_N$ is pure $(1,1)$ and is an algebraic projector summand of $H^2(C^2)$. Lefschetz $(1,1)$ and the nondegenerate primitive intersection pairing give an algebraic isomorphism and inverse
$$
L_N\otimes_{K_N}\widetilde L_N\simeq K_N(-1).
\tag{34}
$$
Tensor the curve surjection with $L_N$, apply the balancing projectors, and contract (34). This is (26), $K_N$-linearly, with the scalar on the first $L_N$ position. On cycles the curve-to-threefold map has codimension two. Tensoring with the diagonal of another $C$ and contracting with the divisor on $C^2$ gives a codimension-two correspondence from a product surface $C\times D$ to $B^2$. Thus (26) is an actual algebraic map of weight-two structures.

Source and target have nondegenerate primitive intersection pairings. The transpose of the surjection is injective, and is $K_N$-linear because the field adjoints are complex conjugates. For an algebraically projected nondegenerate stable summand, compose the surjection with its projector and use the restricted transpose. Finally apply [1, Lemma 10 and the proof of Theorem 11] on the full rational tensor products $L_N\otimes_{\mathbb Q}U_P$. Hodge sections and retractions can be chosen $K_N$-linear by separability. The lifted map between the full sources is a sum of field operators on $L_N$ tensored with rational Hodge maps between curve $H^1$'s. Those maps are divisor correspondences by Lefschetz $(1,1)$. Composing realizes the given compatible Hodge map algebraically; the chosen Hodge lifts themselves need not be algebraic. $\square$

Cyclic symmetric quotients and uniruled geometry for algebraic classes or Abel–Jacobi surjectivity already occur in Schoen [2, Remarks 2.10–2.11]. Half-twist embeddings through CM curve factors are established constructions [8]. Proposition 7 adds the elliptic-chord model (27)–(30) for this cross-quadratic summand and proves its curve support, which an abstract half twist does not supply. The quotient formalism and coniveau strategy themselves are not new.

## 12. Every incident norm and completion of the mixed theorem

Suppose $R=R_{a,b}$ has $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}T(R)=H$ with $[H:F]=2$, and $E/K$ satisfies (22). Its transcendental rank is sixteen, so Section 9's curve source has no divisor complement:
$$
T(R)\simeq L_{16}\otimes_FW,\quad
L_{16}=P_{16}H^1(v^2=u^{16}+1),\quad
W=P_{16}H^1(w^{16}=P(x)).
\tag{35}
$$
The maps and inverse are algebraic. On $W$, the original $F$ generator acts by minus the deck transformation. The cubic is separable, since a double-root source has $F$-rank one.

The $H$ action transfers to $W$ algebraically. Since $L_{16}$ is an $F$-line,
$$
\operatorname{End}_F(L_{16}\otimes_FW)=\operatorname{End}_F(W).
$$
Each $L_{16,\sigma}$ has pure Hodge type, so an $F$-linear operator is Hodge on the tensor product if and only if its transferred operator is Hodge on $W$. Curve $H^1$ endomorphisms are divisors by Lefschetz $(1,1)$. Thus $W$ is a rank-one $H$ structure with algebraic field action and algebraic $E$-exterior projector.

The two $F$ embeddings over each $K$ embedding have complementary types on $L_{16}$. Its $K$ determinant is therefore realized by divisors on the fixed curve square:
$$
\bigwedge\nolimits_K^2L_{16}\simeq K(-1).
$$
As $H=EF$ and $E\cap F=K$, the two $H$ embeddings over an $E$ embedding pair exactly those two $F$ lines. Contracting gives an algebraic isomorphism and inverse
$$
\bigwedge\nolimits_E^2T(R)(1)\simeq
N_E:=\bigwedge\nolimits_E^2W.
\tag{36}
$$
Here the permutation sign matters. Reordering the two $T$ positions as $L_1,L_2,W_1,W_2$ and contracting the alternating $L$ pair sends the alternating $T$ tensor to the symmetric cross tensor in $W^2$. The nontrivial automorphism of $H/E$ sends $\zeta_{16}$ to $-\zeta_{16}$. Acting by this field element on the first $W$ position converts that symmetric tensor to the alternating one, with an invertible scalar. This operation and inverse are algebraic and give precisely the ordinary exterior square in (36).

The two $H$ lines over an $E$ embedding restrict to opposite $F$ extensions of its $K$ embedding. Hence $N_E$ lies in $V_P$ from Proposition 7 with $N=8$, through its algebraic exterior projector. It has rational rank eight and normalized rank-one $E$ K3 type: among the two extensions of the distinguished $E$ embedding, one $T(R)$ line is $(2,0)$ and the other is $(1,1)$; their determinant followed by $(1)$ is $(2,0)$. The conjugate gives $(0,2)$ and all other embeddings give $(1,1)$. Its unique holomorphic $K$ character is $1$.

If an $S$ factor has endomorphism field $E$, then $N_E$ and $T(S)$ are Hodge isomorphic as normalized rank-one $E$ K3 structures. In particular $N_E$ has no rational pure-$(1,1)$ substructure. Both have actual compatible sources through $L_8$: use Proposition 7 and its transpose for $N_E$, and Section 3 for $T(S)$. The full rational-tensor transfer of [1, Lemma 10] applies. Every rational Hodge map between them is $K$-linear: the commutator with the order-eight generators vanishes on the holomorphic and antiholomorphic lines, and otherwise would have pure-$(1,1)$ image in the transcendental target. The isomorphism and its inverse are therefore algebraic. With (36), this proves (24) for every incident field, without a cyclotomic or selected-parameter restriction.

Finish the $S,R$ case of Theorem 5 by decomposing factors into algebraic classes and transcendental parts. For each incident $S$ transcendental factor choose an incident $R$ already in the collection and replace it through (24) by the split algebraic summand $\bigwedge_E^2T(R)(1)$ of two copies of $T(R)$. This adds only repetitions of existing $R$ fields. The remaining $S$ factors have no incidence with any $R$. Lemma 6 makes every resulting Hodge tensor algebraic, including tensors on the added repetitions. The algebraic projectors, inverse isomorphisms, and Tate contractions return its cycle to the original tensor. This proof does not require an integral-saturation assertion about the torus relation lattice.

For $Z$, the degree-two maps
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_{a,b}\dashrightarrow S_{a,b},\quad &(t,x,y)\mapsto(t^2,x,y),\\
Z_{a,b}\dashrightarrow R_{a,b},\quad &(t,x,y)\mapsto(t^2,t^2x,t^3y)
\end{aligned}
\tag{37}
$$
give split algebraic injections of the transcendental structures. They are the invariant and anti-invariant parts for $t\mapsto-t$: the elliptic involution $y\mapsto-y$ acts by $-1$ on transcendental cohomology. Resolved graph traces exhaust these two eigenspaces; any additional degree-two classes from resolution are divisors. Hence
$$
T(Z_{a,b})\simeq T(S_{a,b})\oplus T(R_{a,b})
\tag{38}
$$
algebraically, with inverse.

These $Z$ are not K3 surfaces. At infinity,
$$
\bar y^2=\bar x^3+a s^{12}\bar x+b s^{18}+s^2
$$
is minimal with fundamental line bundle $\mathcal O(3)$, giving $(p_g,q,K^2)=(2,0,0)$ and Kodaira dimension one [9]. Their odd cohomology vanishes. The remaining even classes are algebraic, and (38) reduces every product involving them to the proved $S,R$ case. This proves Theorem 5. $\square$

Theorem 5 and Proposition 7 do not settle products across all nine original families. The degree-$44$ correspondence still lacks an algebraic placement of its target block in a compatible source, and the general rational Hodge conjecture remains open.

## 13. Scope, checks, and reproducibility

Theorems 1, 4 and 5 concern complex smooth minimal models and rational coefficients. The triple-root case $(a,b)=(0,0)$ is excluded. No similarity of polarization forms is assumed for pair maps. The endomorphism field may jump; Section 7 treats all the possible quadratic extensions rather than restricting to a very general fiber. The double-root boundary is handled by Section 5's actual maps.

The surfaces in Theorem 1 are the K3 surfaces of [1]. Minimality can also be checked directly: at infinity, $s=1/t$, $X=xs^4$, $Y=ys^6$ gives

$$
Y^2=X^3+as^8X+s^{12-n}+bs^{12}.
$$

Because $0\leq12-n<6$, the Weierstrass model is minimal there. It is minimal at finite points as well. Its fundamental line bundle is $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(2)$, so the elliptic canonical bundle formula gives a K3 surface [9].

The supplement contains ten exact verifiers. The four original checks are retained. They check the coordinate identities for (6), the character matching and Hodge profiles, the map (8), the depressed-cubic change, and the Tate-factor identity. A separate finite group control checks the right-coset convention in Lemma 3 for $D_4\times C_2$, with central conjugation in the last factor. Its three index-two subgroups of $N=C_2^2$ include two conjugate subgroups not normal in the full group. All joint cocharacter ranks match the common-norm fiber product. An unrestricted three-torus countercontrol demonstrates why pairwise surjectivity alone would not prove joint independence. The fourth verifier checks the twisted-family source identities, kernel size, differential pullback, and complete selected character profiles in Section 9.

The six added checks verify the degree-two bridge (20), the field-incidence character conventions in a noncommutative finite-group example, the elliptic-chord identities (27), the threefold equation and inverse choices, the complete order-256 group and selected Hodge characters for $N=8$, and the even-exponent conductor change in (32). The last check includes $N=6,8,12,22,44$ as parity and ramification controls; the uniform theorem is proved geometrically, not inferred from these cases. The support lemma, the existence of the auxiliary curves, and the field-norm transfer are separate mathematical arguments. The construction gives algebraic existence of the final correspondences and explicit intermediate quotient equations, rather than a closed formula listing every curve and divisor.
