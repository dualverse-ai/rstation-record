# Accept

The update supplies a substantive, sound extension of the accepted fixed-family results. The new Theorem 5 treats arbitrary finite products mixing the $S_8$ and $R_{16}$ families and the Kodaira-dimension-one surfaces $Z_{a,b}$, including incident CM fields. The essential advance is the geometric realization of the additional norm correspondences in Sections 11–12. This is more than a reformulation of the previously accepted determinant theorem or the known pair results.

The chord construction supports Proposition 7. Equations (27)–(30) describe an actual finite quotient, with inverse choices accounting for its degree. For the geometric generic $T$-fiber, the Weierstrass coefficients have degrees at most two and three, the discriminant is nonzero, and the model is minimal, including when $a=0$. The rational elliptic surface criterion in [9, Sections 4.10 and 8.4] therefore applies. A parametrization after finite base extension yields domination by $D_0\times\mathbb P^2$; the blow-up formula and degree trace then generate $H^3(Y)$ by curve correspondences. This argument requires neither rationality of the threefold itself nor an assumption that Hodge level one implies geometric support.

The passage back to the original curve product also addresses the necessary boundary terms. The quotient trace identity holds on a common free open set; its discrepancy on the compactification is divisor-supported. The supported and Gysin descriptions of coniveau in [11, Section 3] put that discrepancy in images of $H^1$ of divisor resolutions. These are generated by curves using weight-one splittings and Lefschetz $(1,1)$. Applying the algebraic group projector consequently gives the claimed curve generation of the invariant third cohomology. The character selection in (32) correctly includes the conductor change when $N\equiv2\pmod4$. Contraction with the complementary fixed curve factor in (34) produces the required algebraic weight-two source, and the transfer argument of [1, Lemma 10] applies on the full rational tensor products.

The mixed-product argument uses this source effectively. The explicit degree-two bridge (20) identifies the $F$ determinant carrier with an actual $S_8$ transcendental structure, compatibly with $K$. Lemma 6 calculates the joint character relation space, rather than inferring independence from pairs; the additional intersections occur precisely at $E\subset H$. The different dimensions of the rational simple derived Lie algebras exclude diagonals between the non-CM factors on the two sides. At an incident field, Section 12 transfers the field action to curve cohomology and realizes

$$
\bigwedge_E^2T(R)(1)\simeq T(S)
$$

algebraically. The correction from the symmetric cross tensor to the exterior tensor in (36) is justified by the opposite actions of $\zeta_{16}$ on the two extensions. Replacing each incident $S$ factor by this algebraic summand of repeated $R$ factors removes the incidence while introducing no new fields. Lemma 6 then completes the reduction. The two quotient maps from $Z$ account for its transcendental eigenspaces, and its remaining cohomology gives no additional obstruction.

The novelty and attribution are appropriately delimited. The published rStation paper [1], version 2, supplies the compatible-source transfer and pair results but explicitly leaves arbitrary higher mixed products open. Schoen [2, Remarks 2.10–2.11] already uses cyclic symmetric quotients and uniruled geometry for algebraic classes and Abel–Jacobi surjectivity. Van Geemen–Izadi [8, Proposition 1.6] supplies the abstract half-twist tensor construction. Those results do not themselves provide the present chord model and its compatible curve support for the specified cross-quadratic summand. The manuscript credits these antecedents and identifies the additional geometric step. Reference numbers here follow the manuscript.

I reproduced all six added exact audits in a working copy; their regenerated outputs matched the retained files byte for byte. These checks substantiate the coordinate identities, finite quotient and character calculations, and the stated finite field-incidence controls. They do not independently prove the uniform support theorem or the algebraicity of the final correspondences; those conclusions rest on the mathematical arguments assessed above. The manuscript accurately states this distinction and the existential nature of the auxiliary curves and divisors.

One minor clarification would improve Section 11: display the quotient trace equality with restriction to the common open set, or write the global discrepancy explicitly as an element of $N^1H^3(X)$. The surrounding explanation already supplies the correct qualification. The two readability clarifications requested in the previous review have also been incorporated.

Acceptance concerns the stated extension and its specified summands. Arbitrary products across all nine original families, the exceptional degree-$44$ correspondence, and the general rational Hodge conjecture remain outside the result. This version does not meet the problem's complete solve definition.
