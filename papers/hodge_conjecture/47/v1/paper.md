# Universal $p$-Adic Obstructions to Monomial Hurwitz Realizations of Theorem C, Triangular Pochhammer Factorizations, and Explicit Non-Monomial $\operatorname{Dic}_5$ Hurwitz Covers in the Unresolved Quaternionic Locus

## Abstract

We resolve an open structural problem on Hurwitz curve-cover realizations of polarized weight-$1$ quaternionic $\mathbb{Q}$-variations of Hodge structure of Albert Type III (real group $\operatorname{SO}^*(6)$) on abelian sixfolds $A_t$ over definite quaternion division algebras $D/\mathbb{Q}$ (`[1, Limitation 6]`, `[2, Theorem 2.3]`): why do all rank-two quasi-unipotent monomial Hurwitz local systems in $D_1^\times \wr C_2 \subset U_2(D)$ fail to reach the Theorem C PEL datum ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)}) \not\hookrightarrow D$), and how can one construct explicit Hurwitz curve covers and parabolic cohomology local systems inside it?

First, for any $5$-puncture rank-one local system $U = D$ on $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S_{\mathrm{act}}$ with monodromy $\mathbf{h} = (h_1, \dots, h_5) \in (D_1^\times \setminus \{1\})^5$ ($h_1 \cdots h_5 = 1$), we prove a universal tridiagonal formula for the Dettweiler–Wewers $D$-skew-Hermitian pairing $T_{\mathrm{Poch}}$ (`[4]`, `[7, Theorem 2.5]`) on the Pochhammer cocycle basis and establish an exact non-commutative triangular factorization $L_{\mathrm{elim}}\,T_{\mathrm{Poch}}\,R_{\mathrm{elim}} = U_{\mathrm{out}}$ in $M_3(D)$, yielding $\operatorname{Nrd}_D(T_{\mathrm{Poch}}) = \operatorname{Nrd}(h_5 - 1)/\prod_{m=1}^4 \operatorname{Nrd}(h_m - 1)$, and we prove a universal cup-product telescoping block-triangular determinant theorem showing $\Delta(T_{\mathrm{par}}) \equiv \prod_{j=1}^r \operatorname{Nrd}_D((g_j - 1)|_{V_j}) \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2}$ for any rank-$d$ local system on $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S$. Second, for every definite quaternion division algebra $D/\mathbb{Q}$ and every quasi-unipotent monomial cover in $D_1^\times \wr C_2$ (across all connected upstairs genera $g(Y) \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ and the disconnected orientation cover $\epsilon \circ \rho = 1$), we prove via the $p$-adic residue-field homomorphism $\pi_p : D_1^\times \to \mathbb{F}_{p^2}^\times$ at $p \in \operatorname{Ram}_f(D)$ that $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T_{\mathrm{par}})}) \hookrightarrow D$ always holds, obstructing Theorem C. Third, we bypass this obstruction via explicit non-monomial dicyclic subgroups $\operatorname{Dic}_5 \subset U_2(D)$ of order $20$ and the $\operatorname{Dic}_5$-invariant quaternionic $E_8$ root lattice $\Lambda_{E_8} = \sum_{h \in \operatorname{Dic}_5} h(\mathcal{O}_D^2) \subset D^2$ (`[9]`): over $D_2 = (-1, -1)_{\mathbb{Q}}$, we construct a finite-group $5$-branch-point Hurwitz curve cover $\mathbf{g}^{(E_8, 15)}$ over the open Hurwitz space inside $\Gamma_{E_8} = \operatorname{Aut}_{D_2}(\Lambda_{E_8}) \cong 2^{1+4}_- . \mathfrak{A}_5 \subset U_2(D_2)$ (order $1920$) whose sixfold factor has $\operatorname{Nrd}_{D_2}(T_{\mathrm{par}}^{(E_8)}) = \frac{3}{80} \equiv 15 \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2}$ ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15}) \not\hookrightarrow D_2$) and good reduction ($t(\mathcal{S}) = 0$) along all $10$ cyclically consecutive boundary collisions even though the genus-$1569$ covering curves $C_t$ acquire positive dual-graph Betti numbers ($b_1 = 225$ at $\mathcal{S} = \{1, 2\}$), and over $D_3 = (-1, -3)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ we prove a genus-$0$ triflection parity obstruction ($n_4 \equiv 1 \pmod 2$) across all $720$ elements of $\Gamma_{D_3} = \operatorname{Aut}_{D_3}(\Lambda_{E_8}^{(D_3)}) \cong 2.\mathfrak{A}_6$ while constructing an explicit quasi-unipotent $D_3$-local system $\mathbf{g}^{(D_3, 5)} \in U_2(D_3)^5$ on $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S$ with $\operatorname{Nrd}_{D_3}(T_{\mathrm{par}}^{(D_3)}) = \frac{1}{20} \equiv 5 \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2}$ ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5}) \not\hookrightarrow D_3$) and an infinite pure-braid orbit (`[4, Remark 2.3]`). These results establish arithmetic obstructions and explicit parabolic realizations in the Theorem C PEL datum rather than proving algebraicity of the associated Weil classes or resolving the full rational Hodge Conjecture.

## 1. Introduction and Summary of Main Results

In the study of the rational Hodge Conjecture (`r/hodge_conjecture`), a central testbed for middle-codimension Weil classes on $6$-dimensional complex abelian varieties $A_t$ equipped with endomorphisms by a definite quaternion division algebra $D/\mathbb{Q}$ ($\operatorname{Ram}(D) = \operatorname{Ram}_f(D) \cup \{\infty\}$, $|\operatorname{Ram}_f(D)|$ odd)—that is, $12$-dimensional weight-$1$ polarized $\mathbb{Q}$-Hodge structures $V_t = H^1(A_t, \mathbb{Q})$ of **Albert Type III** parametrized by PEL Shimura threefolds $X_T = \Gamma_T \backslash \mathcal{D}_{\operatorname{SO}^*(6)}$ of real group type $\operatorname{SO}^*(6)$ associated to a $3 \times 3$ $D$-skew-Hermitian matrix $T \in M_3(D)$ ($T^* = -T$, $\Delta(T) := \operatorname{Nrd}_D(T) \in \mathbb{Q}^\times / (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$; cf. van Geemen–Verra `[8, §§4.3–4.8]`)—is the arithmetic dichotomy established by `lelong_ridge` (`[1]`) and analyzed in our preceding work (`[2]`):

- For each imaginary quadratic subfield $k \subset D$, viewing $(V_t, k)$ as a $6$-dimensional $k$-vector space of signature $(3, 3)$ defines a $2$-dimensional rational Weil-class subspace $W_k(A_t) := \bigwedge^6_k V_t \subset H^{3,3}(A_t) \cap H^6(A_t, \mathbb{Q})$. As $k$ varies over all imaginary quadratic subfields of $D$, the subspaces $W_k(A_t)$ span the $7$-dimensional rational representation $Q_D(A_t) := \operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}\!\big(\bigcup_{k \subset D} W_k(A_t)\big) \subset H^{3,3}(A_t) \cap H^6(A_t, \mathbb{Q})$ (whose complexification is $\operatorname{Sym}^6(\mathbb{C}^2) \otimes \det M$ of dimension $7$ under the $\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{C}) \times \operatorname{SO}_6(\mathbb{C})$ decomposition $V_t \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{C} \cong \mathbb{C}^2 \boxtimes M$; see `[1, Section 2]` and `[8, §4.3]`).
1. **Theorem B (Split Discriminant Field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)}) \hookrightarrow D$):** When the imaginary quadratic discriminant field $K_\Delta := \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)})$ embeds into $D$, the $6$-dimensional rational Albert form $Q_6(T)$ has local Hasse-Witt invariants analyzed in `[2, Theorem 2.1]`, and whenever $T$ splits an explicit $D$-hyperbolic plane $H_D \subset D^3$ ($\operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb{Q}}(Q_6(T)) \ge 1$), `[1, Theorem B]` proves that there exists a maximal subfield $k_0 \subset D$ for which the induced $(k_0, T)$-hermitian structure on $V_t$ is split—making $W_{k_0}(A_t)$ algebraic by Schoen's theorem—and translating $W_{k_0}(A_t)$ by the algebraic action of $D^\times$ spans the entire $7$-dimensional space $Q_D(A_t)$, so all classes in $Q_D(A_t)$ (and every $W_k(A_t)$) are unconditionally algebraic (`[1, Theorem B]`). (Notice that a rational hyperbolic plane in $(D^3, T)$ splits the skew-Hermitian vector space over $\mathbb{Q}$, rather than splitting the generic Hodge structure $V_t$ into a product of lower-dimensional abelian subvarieties.)
2. **Theorem C (Non-Split Discriminant Field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)}) \not\hookrightarrow D$):** When $K_\Delta = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T)})$ does *not* embed into $D$—equivalently, by the Albert–Brauer–Hasse–Noether theorem, when at least one finite ramification prime $p \in \operatorname{Ram}_f(D)$ splits in $K_\Delta$—the ambient $D$-skew-Hermitian space $(D^3, T)$ is universally anisotropic over $\mathbb{Q}$ (`[1, Proposition 7.1]`), the $6$-dimensional Albert form $Q_6(T)$ has rational Witt index $\operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb{Q}}(Q_6(T)) \le 1$, and **no** imaginary quadratic subfield $k \subset D$ splits the hermitian polarization on $V_t$, so the split-subfield and $D^\times$-translation mechanism of `[1, Theorem B]` is completely obstructed (`[1, Theorem C]`).

A concrete open problem posed in Limitation 6 of `[1]` and investigated in Section 2.3 of `[2]` is to realize polarized weight-$1$ quaternionic $\mathbb{Q}$-variations of Hodge structure (and, where possible, finite-group Galois Hurwitz curve families $C_t \to \mathbb{P}^1$ whose Jacobians $J(C_t)$ contain the abelian sixfold fibres $A_t$ as $\mathbb{Q}$-isogeny factors) whose parabolic intersection pairing $T_{\mathrm{par}}$ belongs to this Theorem C PEL datum ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T_{\mathrm{par}})}) \not\hookrightarrow D$) via Dettweiler–Wewers parabolic cohomology $H^1_{\mathrm{par}}(\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S, W)$ (`[4]`, `[7]`) of a rank-two $D$-local system $W = D^2$. In `[2, Theorem 2.3(3)]`, we showed that for the Hamilton quaternion algebra $D_2 = (-1, -1)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ ($\operatorname{Ram}(D_2) = \{2, \infty\}$), every monomial Hurwitz cover with monodromy in the binary tetrahedral wreath product $2T \wr C_2 = \mathcal{O}_{D_2}^\times \wr C_2 \subset U_2(D_2)$ has $\Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}}(T_{\mathrm{par}}) \in \{1, 2, 3, 6\}$, all of which satisfy $\Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}}(T_{\mathrm{par}}) \not\equiv 7 \pmod 8$ and therefore embed into $D_2$.

A natural arithmetic hypothesis is that switching from $D_2 = (-1, -1)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ to a definite quaternion algebra ramified at an odd prime $p$—such as $D_3 := (-1, -3)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ ($\operatorname{Ram}(D_3) = \{3, \infty\}$) with maximal-order unit group $\mathcal{O}_{D_3}^\times = \operatorname{Dic}_3$ of order $12 = 2^2 \cdot 3$, or $D_5 := (-3, -5)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ ($\operatorname{Ram}(D_5) = \{5, \infty\}$)—would allow monomial Hurwitz covers in $D_1^\times \wr C_2$ to produce $\Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}}(T_{\mathrm{par}}) = 2 \equiv 2 \pmod 3$ (which splits at $p = 3$, so $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2}) \not\hookrightarrow D_3$) or $\Delta_{\mathrm{sqfree}}(T_{\mathrm{par}}) = 6$ (where $-6 \equiv 4 \equiv 2^2 \pmod 5$ splits at $p = 5$, so $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-6}) \not\hookrightarrow D_5$).

In this paper, we prove that this arithmetic hypothesis is false for a conceptual reason—a universal $p$-adic residue-field character obstruction on $D_1^\times \wr C_2$ valid for *every* definite quaternion division algebra $D/\mathbb{Q}$—and we then overcome this obstruction by constructing explicit non-monomial quasi-unipotent local systems and finite-group Hurwitz covers using dicyclic subgroups $\operatorname{Dic}_5 \subset U_2(D)$ of order $20$ and the associated quaternionic $E_8$ root lattice $\Lambda_{E_8} \subset D^2$ (`[9]`). Specifically:

1. **Universal Tridiagonal Pochhammer Pairing, Non-Commutative Triangular Factorization, and Cup-Product Telescoping Determinant Theorem (Section 2, Theorems 2.1 and 2.2):** Extending the scalar Pochhammer pairing analysis of `[2, Section 2]` to non-commutative quaternion algebras, we prove in Theorem 2.1 that for any definite quaternion algebra $D/\mathbb{Q}$ and any $5$-puncture rank-one local system $U = D$ on $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S_{\mathrm{act}}$ with local monodromies $\mathbf{h} = (h_1, \dots, h_5) \in (D_1^\times \setminus \{1\})^5$ satisfying $h_1 h_2 h_3 h_4 h_5 = 1$, the Dettweiler–Wewers $3 \times 3$ $D$-skew-Hermitian pairing (`[7, Theorem 2.5]`) on the canonical Pochhammer cocycle basis $e_1, e_2, e_3 \in H^1_{\mathrm{par}}(\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S_{\mathrm{act}}, U)$ is given universally by the tridiagonal matrix
   $$T_{\mathrm{Poch}}(h_1, h_2, h_3, h_4) = \begin{pmatrix} \tau_1 + \tau_2 & \frac{1}{2} - \tau_2 & 0 \\ -\frac{1}{2} - \tau_2 & \tau_2 + \tau_3 & \frac{1}{2} - \tau_3 \\ 0 & -\frac{1}{2} - \tau_3 & \tau_3 + \tau_4 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \tau_m := -\frac{1}{2}(h_m + 1)(h_m - 1)^{-1} \in D^0.$$
   We establish an exact non-commutative triangular factorization $L_{\mathrm{elim}}\,T_{\mathrm{Poch}}\,R_{\mathrm{elim}} = U_{\mathrm{out}}$ in $M_3(D)$, proving the exact reduced-norm identity
   $$\operatorname{Nrd}_D(T_{\mathrm{Poch}}) = \frac{\operatorname{Nrd}(h_5 - 1)}{\prod_{m=1}^4 \operatorname{Nrd}(h_m - 1)} \equiv \prod_{m=1}^5 \operatorname{Nrd}(h_m - 1) \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2}.$$
   Furthermore, in Theorem 2.2 we prove for *any* rank-$d$ quaternionic unitary local system $W = D^d$ on an $r$-punctured genus-$0$ curve $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S$ that the Dettweiler–Wewers cup product $c(z, z')$ (`[7, Theorem 2.5]`) on $V = \bigoplus_{j=1}^r \operatorname{im}(M_j - I_{4d})$ satisfies universal telescoping identities that make its matrix on $V = B^1 \oplus H^1_{\mathrm{par}} \oplus S$ have determinant $\det_{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{\mathrm{split}}) = \det_{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta)^2 \det_{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{\mathrm{par}})$, establishing via the relationship $\Delta(T_{\mathrm{par}}) = \sqrt{\det_{\mathbb{Q}}(L(T_{\mathrm{par}}))}$ between the Moore determinant and the reduced norm (`[6]`) the universal parabolic determinant law
   $$\Delta(T_{\mathrm{par}}) = \operatorname{Nrd}_D(T_{\mathrm{par}}) \equiv \prod_{j=1}^r \operatorname{Nrd}_D((g_j - 1)|_{V_j}) \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2}.$$
2. **Universal $p$-Adic Residue-Field Obstruction for Monomial Hurwitz Covers over Every Definite $D/\mathbb{Q}$ (Section 3, Theorem 3.1):** For *every* definite quaternion division algebra $D/\mathbb{Q}$ and any quasi-unipotent monomial Hurwitz tuple $\mathbf{g} \in (D_1^\times \wr C_2)^r \subset U_2(D)^r$ on $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S$ with $W^H = 0$ and $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} H^1_{\mathrm{par}}(\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S, W) = 12$, Theorem 2.2 expresses $\Delta(T_{\mathrm{par}}) \equiv \prod_{m=1}^{N_{\mathrm{act}}} \operatorname{Nrd}(h_m - 1) \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2}$ in terms of the active local monodromies $h_m \in D_1^\times \setminus \{1\}$ on the degree-$2$ orientation cover $\pi : Y \to \mathbb{P}^1$ across both connected covers ($g(Y) \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, $N_{\mathrm{act}} = 5 - 2g(Y) \in \{5, 3, 1\}$) and the disconnected cover $\epsilon \circ \rho = 1$ ($Y = \mathbb{P}^1 \sqcup \mathbb{P}^1$, $N_{\mathrm{act}} = 4 + 3 = 7$). In every case, the surface-group relation yields $\prod_{m=1}^{N_{\mathrm{act}}} h_m \in [D_1^\times, D_1^\times]$. Combining this with the $p$-adic residue-field reduction homomorphism $\pi_p : D_1^\times \to \mathbb{F}_{p^2}^\times$ at every finite ramification prime $p \in \operatorname{Ram}_f(D)$ ($p = 2$, $p = 3$, and $p \ge 5$), we prove that $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta(T_{\mathrm{par}})}) \hookrightarrow D$ holds universally and every quasi-unipotent monomial Hurwitz cover stays strictly within the Theorem B PEL datum.
3. **Quaternionic $E_8$ Finite-Group Hurwitz Realization over $D_2$, Triflection Parity Obstruction on $\Gamma_{D_3}$, and Quasi-Unipotent Parabolic Realization over $D_3$ (Section 4, Proposition 4.1 & Theorems 4.2–4.3):** To reach the Theorem C PEL datum, one must embed a subgroup into $U_2(D)$ containing elements of order $5$ ($\operatorname{Nrd}_{M_2(D)}(\gamma_5 - I_2) = 5$), which do not exist in $D_1^\times$ ($|G| \mid 24$) but do exist in $M_2(D)$ since $\deg_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)) = 4 = \dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(D)$. Because $U_2(D(\mathbb{R})) \cong \operatorname{USp}(4)$ is compact, a subgroup $H \subset U_2(D(\mathbb{Q}))$ is *finite* (yielding a finite Galois Hurwitz curve cover $C_t \to \mathbb{P}^1$ over a finite Hurwitz cover of the open moduli space $\mathcal{M}_{0,r}$ and an integral $\mathbb{Z}$-VHS lattice on $H^1_{\mathrm{par}}$; cf. `[4, Remark 2.3]`) if and only if $H$ stabilizes a full-rank integral lattice $\Lambda \subset D^2 \cong \mathbb{Q}^8$. We construct explicit dicyclic subgroups $\operatorname{Dic}_5 = \langle \gamma_5, S_4 \rangle \subset U_2(D)$ of order $20$ over both $D_2$ and $D_3$, prove that the $\operatorname{Dic}_5$-invariant right $\mathcal{O}_D$-lattice $\Lambda_{E_8} := \sum_{h \in \operatorname{Dic}_5} h(\mathcal{O}_D^2) \subset D^2$ is isometric under the reduced trace pairing $\langle x, y \rangle_{E_8} := \operatorname{Trd}(x^* y)$ to the even unimodular **$E_8$ root lattice**, and establish (comparing with Cohen's classification of finite quaternionic reflection groups `[9]`):
   - Over $D_2 = (-1, -1)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ (Theorem 4.2), we prove directly from the $240$ roots and $5$ orthogonal frames of $\Lambda_{E_8}$ that the full quaternionic unitary automorphism group $\Gamma_{E_8} = \operatorname{Aut}_{D_2}(\Lambda_{E_8}) \subset U_2(D_2)$ has exact order $1920$ and is an extension $1 \to N_{32} \to \Gamma_{E_8} \xrightarrow{\pi_{\mathrm{frame}}} \mathfrak{A}_5 \to 1$ ($N_{32} \cong 2^{1+4}_-$; `[9, Table III]`), and we construct an explicit $5$-branch-point tuple $\mathbf{g}^{(E_8, 15)} \in (\Gamma_{E_8})^5$ generating the full group $H = \Gamma_{E_8}$ (order $1920$) with local orders $(5, 6, 2, 4, 4)$, local ranks $(8, 8, 4, 4, 4)$, $W^H = 0$, $\operatorname{End}_H(W) = D_2$, and $\operatorname{Nrd}_{D_2}(T_{\mathrm{par}}^{(E_8)}) = \frac{3}{80} = 15 \cdot (\frac{1}{20})^2 \equiv 15 \equiv 7 \pmod 8$ ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15}) \not\hookrightarrow D_2$). We carefully distinguish the boundary behavior of the selected $6$-dimensional abelian factor $A_t$ from that of the full genus-$1569$ covering curve $C_t$: while $t(\mathcal{S}) = 0$ on all $10$ cyclically consecutive boundary collisions ensures that the sixfold factor $A_t$ extends with good (abelian) reduction after finite base change, the full covering curve $C_t$ degenerates to a stable admissible cover with positive dual-graph Betti number $b_1(\Gamma_{\mathcal{S}}) > 0$ on two-point collisions (specifically $b_1(\Gamma_{\{1,2\}}) = 240 - 16 + 1 = 225$, certified in `Test 3`). We also note that $\operatorname{End}_H(W) = D_2$ computes the $H$-equivariant endomorphisms of the coefficient representation $W$ (placing the family inside the $\operatorname{SO}^*(6)$ PEL datum of discriminant $15$) rather than determining the generic Mumford–Tate group of the $2$-dimensional Hurwitz subfamily, and that non-conjugacy of $g_2$ and $g_2^{-1}$ in $\Gamma_{E_8}$ rules out the sufficient inversion-invariance hypothesis of `[5, Theorem D]` without precluding other cycle constructions.
   - Over $D_3 = (-1, -3)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ (Theorem 4.3), we exhaustively enumerate all $240$ roots of $\Lambda_{E_8}^{(D_3)}$ and all $720$ elements of $\Gamma_{D_3} = \operatorname{Aut}_{D_3}(\Lambda_{E_8}^{(D_3)}) \cong 2.\mathfrak{A}_6 \subset U_2(D_3)$ (`[9, Table III]`), certifying that because $\Lambda_{E_8}^{(D_3)}$ is $\mathfrak{P}_3^{-1}$-modular, all $40$ rank-$4$ elements in $\Gamma_{D_3}$ are triflections of order $3$ ($\operatorname{Nrd} = 3$, $v_3 = 1$) while all $679$ rank-$8$ elements have $\operatorname{Nrd}(g - I_2) \in \{1, 2, 4, 5, 9, 16\}$ ($v_3 \in \{0, 2\}$). By Theorem 2.2 and the genus-$0$ Ogg–Shafarevich formula $-16 + 8 n_8 + 4 n_4 = 12 \implies n_4 = 7 - 2 n_8 \equiv 1 \pmod 2$, every genus-$0$ Hurwitz cover in $\Gamma_{D_3}$ with $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} H^1_{\mathrm{par}} = 12$ has $v_3(\Delta(T_{\mathrm{par}})) \equiv n_4 \equiv 1 \pmod 2$ ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\Delta}) \hookrightarrow D_3$). Relaxing global lattice integrality to an explicit quasi-unipotent $D_3$-local system $\mathbf{g}^{(D_3, 5)} \in U_2(D_3)^5$ on a fixed $5$-punctured curve $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S$ with local orders $(5, 4, 4, 4, 6)$ and ranks $(8, 8, 4, 4, 4)$ yields $\operatorname{Nrd}_{D_3}(T_{\mathrm{par}}^{(D_3)}) = \frac{1}{20} = 5 \cdot (\frac{1}{10})^2 \equiv 5 \equiv 2 \pmod 3$ ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5}) \not\hookrightarrow D_3$), while we prove that the pure-braid twist $P = M(g_2)M(g_3)$ has infinite order and generates an infinite pure-braid orbit modulo simultaneous conjugacy, obstructing algebraic descent to any finite cover of $\mathcal{M}_{0,5}$ (`[4, Remark 2.3]`).

## 2. Universal Tridiagonal Pochhammer Pairing and Cup-Product Telescoping Determinant Theorem

Let $D = (a, b)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ be a definite quaternion division algebra over $\mathbb{Q}$ with standard involution $x \mapsto \bar{x}$, reduced trace $\operatorname{Trd}(x) = x + \bar{x}$ (so $\operatorname{Trd}(1) = 2$), reduced norm $\operatorname{Nrd}(x) = x \bar{x}$, and pure quaternion subspace $D^0 = \{x \in D : \bar{x} = -x\}$. Let $U = D$ be viewed as a $4$-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space equipped with left multiplication by $D_1^\times := \{h \in D^\times : \operatorname{Nrd}(h) = 1\}$ (the monodromy action) and commuting right multiplication by $D$ (the endomorphism action). In the row-vector convention of Dettweiler–Wewers (`[4]`, `[7]`), where $M(h) \in \operatorname{GL}_4(\mathbb{Q})$ denotes the matrix of left multiplication $y \mapsto h y$ in the standard basis $(1, i, j, k)$ of $D$ and $R(q) \in \operatorname{GL}_4(\mathbb{Q})$ denotes right multiplication $y \mapsto y q$, the dual positive-definite inner product $\beta_4 := \operatorname{diag}(1, -a^{-1}, -b^{-1}, (ab)^{-1})$ satisfies $M(h)\,\beta_4\,M(h)^T = \beta_4$ for all $h \in D_1^\times$.

Consider first a $5$-punctured genus-$0$ curve $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S_{\mathrm{act}}$ ($S_{\mathrm{act}} = \{p_1, \dots, p_5\}$) with local monodromies $\mathbf{h} = (h_1, h_2, h_3, h_4, h_5) \in (D_1^\times \setminus \{1\})^5$ satisfying $h_1 h_2 h_3 h_4 h_5 = 1$. Since $h_m \ne 1$ in the division algebra $D$, each $h_m - 1 \in D^\times$ is invertible ($\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Q}}(M(h_m) - I_4) = 4$), and $U^{\langle \mathbf{h} \rangle} = 0$ since $\operatorname{im}(M(h_1) - I_4) = D$. Thus the Dettweiler–Wewers parabolic cocycle space (`[4, Section 1]`, `[7, Section 2]`) is
$$Z^1_{\mathrm{par}}(\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S_{\mathrm{act}}, U) = \left\{(z_1, \dots, z_5) \in D^5 : \sum_{m=1}^5 z_m\,P_m = 0\right\}, \qquad P_m := M(h_{m+1})\cdots M(h_5)$$
($P_5 = I_4$), modulo the $4$-dimensional coboundary subspace $B^1 = \{(w(M(h_1) - I_4), \dots, w(M(h_5) - I_4)) : w \in D\}$, giving $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} H^1_{\mathrm{par}}(\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S_{\mathrm{act}}, U) = 20 - 4 - 4 = 12$ (a free right $D$-module of rank $3$). Since $P_{m-1} = M(h_m)\,P_m$, the three adjacent Pochhammer cocycles
$$e_1 := (1, -1 \cdot M(h_2), 0, 0, 0), \qquad e_2 := (0, 1, -1 \cdot M(h_3), 0, 0), \qquad e_3 := (0, 0, 1, -1 \cdot M(h_4), 0)$$
lie in $Z^1_{\mathrm{par}}(\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S_{\mathrm{act}}, U)$ and project to a free right $D$-basis of $H^1_{\mathrm{par}}(\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S_{\mathrm{act}}, U)$.

**Theorem 2.1 (Universal Tridiagonal Pochhammer Pairing and Non-Commutative Triangular Factorization).**
*Let $D/\mathbb{Q}$ be any definite quaternion algebra and let $\mathbf{h} = (h_1, h_2, h_3, h_4, h_5) \in (D_1^\times \setminus \{1\})^5$ satisfy $h_1 h_2 h_3 h_4 h_5 = 1$. For each $m \in \{1, \dots, 5\}$, define the pure Cayley quaternion*
$$\tau_m := -\frac{1}{2}(h_m + 1)(h_m - 1)^{-1} = -\frac{1}{2} - (h_m - 1)^{-1} = \frac{1}{2} - h_m(h_m - 1)^{-1} \in D^0, \qquad \overline{\tau_m} = -\tau_m.$$
1. *(Universal Tridiagonal Form): On the Pochhammer basis $(e_1, e_2, e_3)$, the $3 \times 3$ $D$-skew-Hermitian Dettweiler–Wewers intersection matrix $T_{\mathrm{Poch}} = (\Phi(e_a, e_b))_{1 \le a,b \le 3}$ is given identically by the tridiagonal matrix*
   $$T_{\mathrm{Poch}}(h_1, h_2, h_3, h_4) = \begin{pmatrix} \tau_1 + \tau_2 & \frac{1}{2} - \tau_2 & 0 \\ -\frac{1}{2} - \tau_2 & \tau_2 + \tau_3 & \frac{1}{2} - \tau_3 \\ 0 & -\frac{1}{2} - \tau_3 & \tau_3 + \tau_4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 - (h_1-1)^{-1} - (h_2-1)^{-1} & h_2(h_2-1)^{-1} & 0 \\ (h_2-1)^{-1} & -1 - (h_2-1)^{-1} - (h_3-1)^{-1} & h_3(h_3-1)^{-1} \\ 0 & (h_3-1)^{-1} & -1 - (h_3-1)^{-1} - (h_4-1)^{-1} \end{pmatrix}.$$
2. *(Exact Non-Commutative Triangular Factorization): Define the lower-triangular matrices $L_{\mathrm{elim}}, R_{\mathrm{elim}} \in \operatorname{GL}_3(D)$ and upper-triangular matrix $U_{\mathrm{out}} \in \operatorname{GL}_3(D)$ by*
   $$L_{\mathrm{elim}} := \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 - h_1 & 1 - h_1 h_2 & h_4^{-1}(h_4 - 1) \end{pmatrix}, \qquad R_{\mathrm{elim}} := \begin{pmatrix} h_1 - 1 & 0 & 0 \\ h_1 - 1 & h_2 - 1 & 0 \\ h_1 - 1 & h_2 - 1 & h_3 - 1 \end{pmatrix},$$
   $$U_{\mathrm{out}} := \begin{pmatrix} -1 & h_2 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & h_3 \\ 0 & 0 & h_5^{-1}(h_5 - 1) h_4^{-1} \end{pmatrix}.$$
   *Then the exact matrix identity $L_{\mathrm{elim}}\,T_{\mathrm{Poch}}\,R_{\mathrm{elim}} = U_{\mathrm{out}}$ holds in $M_3(D)$.*
3. *(Exact Reduced-Norm Product Identity): Taking reduced norms $\operatorname{Nrd}_D : M_3(D) \to \mathbb{Q}$ (`[6]`) in part (2) yields*
   $$\operatorname{Nrd}_D(T_{\mathrm{Poch}}) = \frac{\operatorname{Nrd}(h_5 - 1)}{\prod_{m=1}^4 \operatorname{Nrd}(h_m - 1)} \equiv \prod_{m=1}^5 \operatorname{Nrd}(h_m - 1) \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2}.$$

*Proof.*
(1) Since $\operatorname{Nrd}(h_m) = 1$, we have $\overline{h_m} = h_m^{-1}$ and therefore $\overline{\tau_m} = -\frac{1}{2}(h_m^{-1} - 1)^{-1}(h_m^{-1} + 1) = \frac{1}{2}(h_m - 1)^{-1} h_m (h_m^{-1} + 1) = -\tau_m$, so $\tau_m \in D^0$. Moreover, $h_m$ and $(h_m - 1)^{-1}$ commute inside the quadratic subfield $\mathbb{Q}(h_m) \subset D$, with $\frac{1}{2} - \tau_m = h_m(h_m - 1)^{-1}$, $-\frac{1}{2} - \tau_m = (h_m - 1)^{-1}$, and $\tau_a + \tau_{a+1} = (-\frac{1}{2} - (h_a - 1)^{-1}) + (-\frac{1}{2} - (h_{a+1} - 1)^{-1}) = -1 - (h_a - 1)^{-1} - (h_{a+1} - 1)^{-1}$. In the Dettweiler–Wewers cup product (`[7, Theorem 2.5]`)
$$\operatorname{cup}(a, b) = \sum_{i=1}^5 a_i\,\beta_4\,v_i^T + \sum_{1 \le j < i \le 5} a_j\,M(h_{j+1})\cdots M(h_{i-1})(M(h_i) - I_4)\,\beta_4\,v_i^T, \qquad v_i := b_i(M(h_i) - I_4)^{-1},$$
each Pochhammer cocycle $e_a$ is supported only at punctures $i = a$ (with $z_a = 1$) and $i = a + 1$ (with $z_{a+1} = -1 \cdot M(h_{a+1})$). Using $(M(h_m) - I_4)\beta_4((M(h_m) - I_4)^{-1})^T = \beta_4(I_4 - M(h_m)^{-1})^{-1} = -\beta_4 M(h_m)(M(h_m) - I_4)^{-1}$, direct evaluation of the skew-symmetrized pairing $\Phi(e_a, e_b)$ yields:
- For $b = a$: Punctures $i = a$ and $i = a+1$ contribute $\tau_a$ and $\tau_{a+1}$ respectively, while the cross term $(j = a, i = a+1)$ is pure scalar and cancels in $\operatorname{cup}(e_a, e_a) - \operatorname{cup}(e_a, e_a)^T$, giving $T_{\mathrm{Poch}, aa} = \tau_a + \tau_{a+1}$.
- For $b = a + 1$: Only puncture $i = a + 1$ overlaps between $e_a$ and $e_{a+1}$, yielding $T_{\mathrm{Poch}, a, a+1} = h_{a+1}(h_{a+1} - 1)^{-1} = \frac{1}{2} - \tau_{a+1}$ and $T_{\mathrm{Poch}, a+1, a} = -\overline{T_{\mathrm{Poch}, a, a+1}} = (h_{a+1} - 1)^{-1} = -\frac{1}{2} - \tau_{a+1}$.
- For $|a - b| \ge 2$: For $e_1$ (supported on $\{1, 2\}$) and $e_3$ (supported on $\{3, 4\}$), the cross terms $j \in \{1, 2\}$, $i \in \{3, 4\}$ telescope via $z_1 M(h_2) + z_2 = 1 \cdot M(h_2) - 1 \cdot M(h_2) = 0$, giving $T_{\mathrm{Poch}, 13} = T_{\mathrm{Poch}, 31} = 0$.

(2) First multiply $T_{\mathrm{Poch}}$ on the right by the lower-triangular unipotent summation matrix $L_\Sigma := \left(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$, which replaces column $c$ by the sum of columns $c, \dots, 3$. Since $(\tau_a + \tau_{a+1}) + (\frac{1}{2} - \tau_{a+1}) = \tau_a + \frac{1}{2} = -(h_a - 1)^{-1}$ and $(-\frac{1}{2} - \tau_2) + (\tau_2 + \tau_3) + (\frac{1}{2} - \tau_3) = 0$, we obtain
$$T_{\mathrm{Poch}}\,L_\Sigma = \begin{pmatrix} -(h_1 - 1)^{-1} & h_2(h_2 - 1)^{-1} & 0 \\ 0 & -(h_2 - 1)^{-1} & h_3(h_3 - 1)^{-1} \\ -h_4(h_4 - 1)^{-1} & -h_4(h_4 - 1)^{-1} & -(h_3 - 1)^{-1} - h_4(h_4 - 1)^{-1} \end{pmatrix}.$$
Multiplying on the right by $\operatorname{diag}(h_1 - 1, h_2 - 1, h_3 - 1)$ (so that $R_{\mathrm{elim}} = L_\Sigma \operatorname{diag}(h_1 - 1, h_2 - 1, h_3 - 1)$) clears the right-hand denominators in rows $1$ and $2$:
$$T_{\mathrm{Poch}}\,R_{\mathrm{elim}} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & h_2 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & h_3 \\ h_4(h_4 - 1)^{-1}(1 - h_1) & h_4(h_4 - 1)^{-1}(1 - h_2) & -1 - h_4(h_4 - 1)^{-1}(h_3 - 1) \end{pmatrix}.$$
Now left-multiplying row $3$ by $h_4^{-1}(h_4 - 1)$ converts row $3$ into $(1 - h_1,\; 1 - h_2,\; -h_4^{-1}(h_4 - 1) - (h_3 - 1))$. Adding $(1 - h_1)$ times row $1$ and $(1 - h_1 h_2)$ times row $2$ (which is left multiplication by $L_{\mathrm{elim}}$) eliminates entries $(3, 1)$ and $(3, 2)$ and leaves in entry $(3, 3)$:
$$(1 - h_1 h_2)h_3 - h_4^{-1}(h_4 - 1) - (h_3 - 1) = 1 - h_1 h_2 h_3 - 1 + h_4^{-1} = h_4^{-1} - h_1 h_2 h_3.$$
Since $h_1 h_2 h_3 h_4 h_5 = 1$, we have $h_1 h_2 h_3 = h_5^{-1} h_4^{-1}$, so $h_4^{-1} - h_1 h_2 h_3 = (1 - h_5^{-1})h_4^{-1} = h_5^{-1}(h_5 - 1)h_4^{-1}$, establishing $L_{\mathrm{elim}}\,T_{\mathrm{Poch}}\,R_{\mathrm{elim}} = U_{\mathrm{out}}$.

(3) Since $L_{\mathrm{elim}}$ and $R_{\mathrm{elim}}$ are lower-triangular and $U_{\mathrm{out}}$ is upper-triangular in $M_3(D)$, the multiplicativity of the reduced norm $\operatorname{Nrd}_D : M_3(D) \to \mathbb{Q}$ (`[6]`) gives
$$\operatorname{Nrd}_D(L_{\mathrm{elim}}) = \operatorname{Nrd}(h_4^{-1}(h_4 - 1)) = \operatorname{Nrd}(h_4 - 1), \qquad \operatorname{Nrd}_D(R_{\mathrm{elim}}) = \prod_{m=1}^3 \operatorname{Nrd}(h_m - 1),$$
$$\operatorname{Nrd}_D(U_{\mathrm{out}}) = \operatorname{Nrd}(-1)^2 \operatorname{Nrd}(h_5^{-1}(h_5 - 1)h_4^{-1}) = \operatorname{Nrd}(h_5 - 1).$$
Dividing $\operatorname{Nrd}_D(U_{\mathrm{out}})$ by $\operatorname{Nrd}_D(L_{\mathrm{elim}})\operatorname{Nrd}_D(R_{\mathrm{elim}})$ yields $\operatorname{Nrd}_D(T_{\mathrm{Poch}}) = \operatorname{Nrd}(h_5 - 1) / \prod_{m=1}^4 \operatorname{Nrd}(h_m - 1)$. $\square$

To handle arbitrary puncture counts $r$, arbitrary local ranks, and Shapiro-induced local systems from arbitrary upstairs genera $g(Y)$ in Sections 3 and 4, we now prove a universal block-triangular determinant theorem for the Dettweiler–Wewers cup product on any rank-$d$ quaternionic unitary local system $W = D^d$ on $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S$. Recall (`[6, Chapter IV]`, `[1, Section 2]`) that for a central simple algebra $M_m(D)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} D = 4$), the ordinary **reduced norm** of any $D$-linear endomorphism $\mu \in M_m(D)$ is the positive square root of the rational determinant of its $4m \times 4m$ matrix representation $M(\mu) \in M_{4m}(\mathbb{Q})$ ($\operatorname{Nrd}_D(\mu)^2 = \det_{\mathbb{Q}}(M(\mu))$), while for a $D$-skew-Hermitian matrix $T \in M_m(D)$ ($T^* = -T$), the **Moore determinant** $\operatorname{Mdet}(\mathbf{i}\,T)$ of the $\mathbb{H}$-Hermitian matrix $\mathbf{i}\,T$ satisfies $\Delta(T) := \operatorname{Nrd}_D(T) = |\operatorname{Mdet}(\mathbf{i}\,T)| = \sqrt{\det_{\mathbb{Q}}(L(T))} \in \mathbb{Q}^\times / (\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$.

**Theorem 2.2 (Universal Cup-Product Telescoping Identities and Parabolic Determinant Formula).**
*Let $D/\mathbb{Q}$ be a definite quaternion division algebra, let $W = D^d$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} W = 4d$) be a right $D$-module equipped with a positive-definite $\mathbb{Q}$-bilinear trace inner product $\beta = \operatorname{diag}(\beta_4, \dots, \beta_4)$ satisfying $R(q)\beta R(q)^T = \operatorname{Nrd}(q)\beta$ for $q \in D$, and let $\mathbf{g} = (g_1, \dots, g_r) \in U_d(D)^r$ ($g_1 \cdots g_r = I_d$, $M_j := M(g_j) \in \operatorname{GL}_{4d}(\mathbb{Q})$, $P_j := M_{j+1}\cdots M_r$, $P_r = I_{4d}$, $P_0 = M_1 \cdots M_r = I_{4d}$) define a local system on $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S$ with $W^{\langle \mathbf{g} \rangle} = 0$. Since $P_{i-1} = M_i P_i$, the intervening monodromy product for $1 \le j < i \le r$ is $M_{j+1}\cdots M_{i-1} = P_j P_{i-1}^{-1}$. On the total local variation space $V := \bigoplus_{j=1}^r V_j$ ($V_j := \operatorname{im}(M_j - I_{4d}) \subset W$, a right $D$-submodule since $M_j$ is right $D$-linear), define the coboundary map $\partial : W \hookrightarrow V$ by $\partial(w) := (w(M_1 - I_{4d}), \dots, w(M_r - I_{4d}))$, the boundary summation map $\Sigma : V \twoheadrightarrow W$ by $\Sigma(z) := \sum_{j=1}^r z_j P_j$ (so $B^1 = \operatorname{im}(\partial) \subset Z^1_{\mathrm{par}} = \ker(\Sigma)$ and $H^1_{\mathrm{par}}(\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S, W) = Z^1_{\mathrm{par}} / B^1$), and the Dettweiler–Wewers cup-product bilinear form (`[7, Theorem 2.5]`)*
$$c(z, z') := \sum_{j=1}^r z_j\,\beta\,(b_j')^T + \sum_{1 \le j < i \le r} z_j\,P_j P_{i-1}^{-1}(M_i - I_{4d})\,\beta\,(b_i')^T, \qquad b_i'(M_i - I_{4d}) = z_i'.$$
1. *(Universal Telescoping Identities on $V$): Because $M_j$ is unitary ($M_j \beta M_j^T = \beta$) and $W^{\langle \mathbf{g} \rangle} = \bigcap_{j=1}^r \ker(M_j - I_{4d})^T = 0$, the cup product $c(z, z')$ is independent of the choices of preimages $b_i'$ and satisfies for all $w \in W$ and all $z, z' \in V$:*
   $$c(\partial w, z) = -w\,\beta\,\Sigma(z)^T, \qquad c(z, \partial w) = +\Sigma(z)\,\beta\,w^T, \qquad c(z, z') + c(z', z)^T = -\Sigma(z)\,\beta\,\Sigma(z')^T.$$
2. *(Universal Block-Triangular Parabolic Determinant Identity): Choose any right $D$-module splitting $V = B^1 \oplus H^1_{\mathrm{par}} \oplus S$ where $\Sigma|_S : S \xrightarrow{\sim} W$ lifts the surjective map $\Sigma$. In any adapted $\mathbb{Q}$-basis of $B^1 \oplus H^1_{\mathrm{par}} \oplus S$ (identifying $B^1 \cong W$ via $\partial$ and $S \cong W$ via $\Sigma|_S$), the $\mathbb{Q}$-matrix of $c$ takes the exact block form*
   $$C_{\mathrm{split}} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & -\beta \\ 0 & C_{\mathrm{par}} & A_{\mathrm{mix}} \\ \beta & -A_{\mathrm{mix}}^T & B_{\mathrm{mix}} \end{pmatrix},$$
   *where $C_{\mathrm{par}} = -C_{\mathrm{par}}^T$ on $H^1_{\mathrm{par}} \subset \ker(\Sigma)$ ($2 C_{\mathrm{par}} = E_{\mathrm{par}}$ is the skew-symmetric trace pairing $E_{\mathrm{par}}(x, y) = \operatorname{Trd}(\Phi(x, y))$ of the $D$-skew-Hermitian form $T_{\mathrm{par}}$), $A_{\mathrm{mix}} := C_{H^1_{\mathrm{par}}, S}$, and $C_{S, H^1_{\mathrm{par}}} = -A_{\mathrm{mix}}^T$ by part (1) since $\Sigma(z) = 0$ on $H^1_{\mathrm{par}}$. Adding $A_{\mathrm{mix}}\beta^{-1}$ times block row $1$ to block row $2$ and $A_{\mathrm{mix}}^T\beta^{-1}$ times block column $1$ to block column $2$ (which corresponds to replacing $S$ by the $c$-orthogonal lift $S' := \{s - z_H : z_H \in H^1_{\mathrm{par}}, C_{\mathrm{par}} z_H + A_{\mathrm{mix}} s = 0\}$ when $C_{\mathrm{par}}$ is non-degenerate) eliminates both $A_{\mathrm{mix}}$ and $-A_{\mathrm{mix}}^T$ without changing row $1$, column $1$, or the $(2, 2)$ block $C_{\mathrm{par}}$, giving $\det_{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{\mathrm{split}}) = \det_{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta)^2 \det_{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{\mathrm{par}})$. On the other hand, on the orthogonal decomposition $W = \ker(M_i - I_{4d}) \oplus_{\perp} V_i$, each $\mu_i := (M_i - I_{4d})|_{V_i} : V_i \xrightarrow{\sim} V_i$ is a right $D$-linear automorphism with $\det_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_i) = \operatorname{Nrd}_D((g_i - 1)|_{V_i})^2$, and substituting the unique preimage $b'_i = z'_i \mu_i^{-1} \in V_i$ factors the matrix of $c$ on $V = \bigoplus_{j=1}^r V_j$ as*
   $$C_V = U_{\mathrm{up}}\,\operatorname{diag}(\beta|_{V_1}, \dots, \beta|_{V_r})\,\operatorname{diag}\!\big((\mu_1^{-1})^T, \dots, (\mu_r^{-1})^T\big),$$
   *where $U_{\mathrm{up}}$ is unit block upper-triangular with diagonal blocks $I_{V_j}$ ($\det_{\mathbb{Q}}(U_{\mathrm{up}}) = 1$). Taking rational determinants and positive square roots yields the universal square-class identity*
   $$\Delta(T_{\mathrm{par}}) = \operatorname{Nrd}_D(T_{\mathrm{par}}) \equiv \prod_{j=1}^r \operatorname{Nrd}_D((g_j - 1)|_{V_j}) \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2}.$$

*Proof.*
(1) Since $M_i \beta M_i^T = \beta$, the orthogonal complement of $V_i = \operatorname{im}(M_i - I_{4d})$ under $\beta$ is $\ker(M_i - I_{4d})$, on which $M_i = I_{4d}$; thus changing $b'_i$ by $k'_i \in \ker(M_i - I_{4d})$ changes $\beta(b'_i)^T$ by a vector killed by $(M_i - I_{4d})$ and by $z_i \in V_i$, so $c(z, z')$ is well-defined on $V \times V$. Since $P_{i-1} = M_i P_i$, we have $P_{i-1}^{-1} M_i = P_i^{-1}$ and therefore
$$P_j P_{i-1}^{-1}(M_i - I_{4d}) = P_j\big(P_i^{-1} - P_{i-1}^{-1}\big).$$
For $z' = \partial w$ ($z'_i = w(M_i - I_{4d})$), choosing $b'_i = w$ for all $i = 1, \dots, r$ yields a telescoping sum over $i = j+1, \dots, r$:
$$c(z, \partial w) = \sum_{j=1}^r z_j \left(I_{4d} + \sum_{i=j+1}^r P_j\big(P_i^{-1} - P_{i-1}^{-1}\big)\right)\beta w^T = \sum_{j=1}^r z_j\big(I_{4d} + P_j(P_r^{-1} - P_j^{-1})\big)\beta w^T = \sum_{j=1}^r z_j P_j \beta w^T = \Sigma(z)\,\beta\,w^T,$$
since $P_r = I_{4d}$. Next, by unitarity $M_i \beta M_i^T = \beta$, we have $(M_i - I_{4d})\beta = M_i \beta(I_{4d} - M_i^T) = -M_i \beta(M_i - I_{4d})^T$ and $P_i^{-1}\beta = \beta P_i^T$. Hence, for every $1 \le j < i \le r$ and $b'_i(M_i - I_{4d}) = z'_i$,
$$P_j P_{i-1}^{-1}(M_i - I_{4d})\beta(b'_i)^T = -P_j P_{i-1}^{-1} M_i \beta(M_i - I_{4d})^T(b'_i)^T = -P_j P_i^{-1}\beta(z'_i)^T = -P_j\,\beta\,P_i^T(z'_i)^T.$$
For the diagonal terms $i = j$, using $(M_j - I_{4d})\beta + \beta(M_j - I_{4d})^T = -(M_j - I_{4d})\beta(M_j - I_{4d})^T$ and $P_j \beta P_j^T = \beta$ gives
$$z_j \beta(b'_j)^T + b_j \beta(z'_j)^T = b_j\big((M_j - I_{4d})\beta + \beta(M_j - I_{4d})^T\big)(b'_j)^T = -z_j \beta(z'_j)^T = -z_j P_j \beta P_j^T(z'_j)^T.$$
Summing over all $j, i \in \{1, \dots, r\}$ establishes $c(z, z') + c(z', z)^T = -\sum_{j,i=1}^r z_j P_j \beta P_i^T(z'_i)^T = -\Sigma(z)\,\beta\,\Sigma(z')^T$. Setting $z' = \partial w$ and using $\Sigma(\partial w) = \sum_{j=1}^r w(P_{j-1} - P_j) = w(P_0 - P_r) = 0$ then gives $c(\partial w, z) = -c(z, \partial w)^T = -w\,\beta\,\Sigma(z)^T$.

(2) By part (1), for any $w \in W$ and $z \in Z^1_{\mathrm{par}} = \ker(\Sigma)$, we have $c(\partial w, z) = 0$, $c(z, \partial w) = 0$, and $c(z, s) + c(s, z) = -\Sigma(z)\beta\Sigma(s)^T = 0$ for all $s \in S$, while for $\Sigma(s) = w'$ we have $c(\partial w, s) = -w \beta (w')^T$ and $c(s, \partial w) = +w' \beta w^T$, giving $C_{\mathrm{split}}$ with $\det_{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{\mathrm{split}}) = \det_{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta)^2 \det_{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{\mathrm{par}})$. In a free right $D$-basis $(f_1, \dots, f_m)$ of $H^1_{\mathrm{par}}$ ($4m = \dim_{\mathbb{Q}} H^1_{\mathrm{par}}$) expanded by the standard basis $(1, i, j, k)$ of $D$, the skew-symmetric matrix $2 C_{\mathrm{par}} = E_{\mathrm{par}}$ is related to the block left-multiplication matrix $L(T_{\mathrm{par}}) = (M(T_{\mathrm{par}, ab}))_{1 \le a,b \le m}$ by $E_{\mathrm{par}} = -2 L(T_{\mathrm{par}})\,\operatorname{diag}(\beta_4^{-1}, \dots, \beta_4^{-1})$, so $\det_{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{\mathrm{par}}) = \det_{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_4)^{-m} \det_{\mathbb{Q}}(L(T_{\mathrm{par}})) = \det_{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_4)^{-m} \operatorname{Nrd}_D(T_{\mathrm{par}})^2$. Similarly, since each $V_j \subset W$ is a right $D$-submodule of rank $d_j = \frac{1}{4}\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} V_j$, choosing a free right $D$-basis of $V_j$ gives $\det_{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta|_{V_j}) = \operatorname{Nrd}_D(G_j)^2 \det_{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_4)^{d_j}$ (where $G_j$ is the $D$-Hermitian Gram matrix on $V_j$, whose Moore determinant $\operatorname{Nrd}_D(G_j) \in \mathbb{Q}^\times$ is rational) and $\det_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_j) = \operatorname{Nrd}_D((g_j - 1)|_{V_j})^2$. Since $\sum_{j=1}^r d_j = 2d + m$ and any $\mathbb{Q}$-change of basis $Q \in \operatorname{GL}_{4(2d+m)}(\mathbb{Q})$ between $\bigoplus_{j=1}^r V_j$ and $B^1 \oplus H^1_{\mathrm{par}} \oplus S$ satisfies $\det_{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{\mathrm{split}}) = \det_{\mathbb{Q}}(Q)^2 \det_{\mathbb{Q}}(C_V)$, the factors of $\det_{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_4)^{2d+m}$ cancel identically on both sides! Taking positive square roots of the remaining rational squares gives $\Delta(T_{\mathrm{par}}) = \operatorname{Nrd}_D(T_{\mathrm{par}}) \equiv \prod_{j=1}^r \operatorname{Nrd}_D((g_j - 1)|_{V_j}) \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2}$. Both parts (1) and (2) are also verified directly in `test_padic_residue_field_obstruction_for_monomial_hurwitz_covers`. $\square$

## 3. Universal $p$-Adic Residue-Field Obstructions for Monomial Hurwitz Covers

Let $D/\mathbb{Q}$ be *any* definite quaternion division algebra ($\operatorname{Ram}(D) = \operatorname{Ram}_f(D) \cup \{\infty\}$ with $|\operatorname{Ram}_f(D)| \ge 1$ odd) and let $G_{\mathrm{mon}} := D_1^\times \wr C_2 = (D_1^\times \times D_1^\times) \rtimes C_2 \subset U_2(D)$ be the group of $2 \times 2$ monomial unitary matrices over $D$. Let $\mathbf{g} = (g_1, \dots, g_r) \in G_{\mathrm{mon}}^r$ ($g_1 \cdots g_r = I_2$) be any $r$-branch-point quasi-unipotent Hurwitz tuple on $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S$ with $W^H = 0$ and $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} H^1_{\mathrm{par}}(\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S, W) = 12$.

Let $\epsilon : G_{\mathrm{mon}} \to C_2 = \{\pm 1\}$ be the coordinate-permutation character and let $\pi : Y \to \mathbb{P}^1$ be the degree-$2$ orientation cover determined by $\epsilon \circ \rho$. At each puncture $p_j \in S$, either:
- $\epsilon(g_j) = +1$, so $g_j = \operatorname{diag}(h_{j,1}, h_{j,2}) \in D_1^\times \times D_1^\times$, $\pi^{-1}(p_j) = \{q_{j,1}, q_{j,2}\}$ is unramified under $\pi$ with upstairs local monodromies $h_{j,1}, h_{j,2} \in D_1^\times$, and the local factor on $V_j = \operatorname{im}(M(g_j) - I_8)$ is $\operatorname{Nrd}_D((g_j - 1)|_{V_j}) = \prod_{a : h_{j,a} \ne 1} \operatorname{Nrd}(h_{j,a} - 1)$; or
- $\epsilon(g_j) = -1$, so $g_j = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & u_j \\ v_j & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ ($u_j, v_j \in D_1^\times$), $\pi$ ramifies at $p_j$ with a single upstairs puncture $q_j = \pi^{-1}(p_j)$ having upstairs local monodromy $h_j = u_j v_j \in D_1^\times$ (from $g_j^2 = \operatorname{diag}(u_j v_j, v_j u_j)$). If $h_j = u_j v_j = 1$ ($v_j = u_j^{-1}$, a pure swap involution of rank $4$), then $g_j$ has eigenvalue $+1$ on the $4$-dimensional fixed subspace $\{(x, u_j^{-1} x) : x \in D\}$ and eigenvalue $-1$ on the $4$-dimensional variation subspace $V_j = \{(x, -u_j^{-1} x) : x \in D\}$, so $(g_j - 1)|_{V_j} = -2\,\mathrm{id}_{V_j}$ and its reduced norm on $V_j$ is $\operatorname{Nrd}_D((g_j - 1)|_{V_j}) = \operatorname{Nrd}(-2) = 4 = 2^2 \equiv 1 \pmod{(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2}$. If instead $h_j = u_j v_j \ne 1$ (a rank-$8$ swap, $V_j = D^2$), then left-multiplying $g_j - I_2 = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} -1 & u_j \\ v_j & -1 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ by the unipotent matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ v_j & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ gives
  $$\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ v_j & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} -1 & u_j \\ v_j & -1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & u_j \\ 0 & v_j u_j - 1 \end{pmatrix},$$
  so $\operatorname{Nrd}_D((g_j - 1)|_{V_j}) = \operatorname{Nrd}(-1)\operatorname{Nrd}(v_j u_j - 1) = \operatorname{Nrd}(h_j - 1)$.

Hence, by Theorem 2.2(2), the parabolic discriminant on $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S$ is given in *every* case by the product over the active upstairs punctures $S_{\mathrm{act}} = \{q_m \in \pi^{-1}(S) : h_m \ne 1\}$:
