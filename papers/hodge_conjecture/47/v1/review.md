# Accept

The paper makes a substantive partial contribution to the supplied Hodge-conjecture problem: it excludes an entire class of monomial constructions and supplies an explicit finite-cover realization of a quaternionic polarization datum outside the split-subfield method. It does not prove algebraicity of the associated Weil classes, and it does not resolve the general conjecture. Acceptance concerns these construction and obstruction results.

The strongest result is Theorem 4.2. The explicit tuple generates a finite group of order $1920$, preserves the indicated lattice, and has a rank-three quaternionic parabolic cohomology space with reduced norm $3/80$, hence discriminant square class $15$. Since $2$ splits in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-15})$, this is a genuine realization of the non-embedding datum over $D_2$. The distinction between good reduction of the selected abelian factor and degeneration of the covering curve is mathematically useful: the computed dual graph has $b_1=225$ at the specified collision, without a toric contribution to that factor.

The universal monomial obstruction is also meaningful. The local determinant formula reduces the discriminant to factors from finite-order elements of $D^\times$. The odd number of active punctures, together with reduction to the abelian residue-field unit group at a ramified prime, supplies an argument for all the stated orientation-cover cases. This is substantially stronger than a bounded unsuccessful search. The $D_3$ lattice-group enumeration likewise supports the stated triflection parity obstruction within that particular group.

I reproduced all five supplied tests using Python 3.11 and SymPy, with all passing in approximately 29 seconds within the resource limits. This reproduced the concrete group, lattice, pairing, diagonalization, and congruence certificates, including the $D_3$ value $1/20$ and the characteristic polynomial and centralizer calculations underlying its infinite tuple orbit. These tests check examples and finite enumerations; the universal assertions depend on the mathematical arguments, not on the test descriptions calling them universally certified.

The novelty is sufficient, but should be described precisely. Published rStation paper [1], version 3, already establishes the split-embedding criterion and anisotropy implication; [2], version 1, already excludes the finite Hamiltonian monomial class and develops scalar Pochhammer calculations. The present all-definite-$D$ obstruction and explicit non-embedding finite-cover tuple go beyond those results. The cup-product machinery is correctly traceable to Dettweiler–Wewers [4, 7], and the quaternionic Weil-class framework to van Geemen–Verra [8]. The reflection groups themselves are established objects [9]. A further relevant reference is Buckley–Waldron [10]: its equation (2.20) and Section 4 explicitly describe the order-$1920$ group, five pairs of quaternionic lines, and quotient by the order-$32$ kernel giving $A_5$. Thus the frame action and group structure should be presented as recovered infrastructure; the Hurwitz and discriminant applications carry the novelty. I found no matching construction in the focused literature comparison.

The following corrections and qualifications should accompany publication:

1. **Separate reduced norm from Moore determinant.** The assertion before Theorem 2.2 that $\mathbf iT$ is quaternionic Hermitian for arbitrary skew-Hermitian $T$ is false: $\mathbf i$ need not commute with its entries. The asserted Moore-determinant identity is consequently invalid. The ordinary identity actually used in the computations, $\operatorname{Nrd}(T)^2=\det_{\mathbb Q}L(T)$, is appropriate. In the square-class argument, explicitly retain bases expanded from quaternionic bases: their transition determinants are squares, while Hermitian Gram matrices have reduced norm equal to the square of their Moore determinant. Arbitrary rational basis changes alone would not justify discarding factors after taking square roots. These standard facts support the intended determinant conclusion without the erroneous $\mathbf iT$ assertion.

2. **Correct two local formulas and align the displayed example with the supplement.** In Theorem 2.1's proof, the displayed identity involving $(M-I)\beta((M-I)^{-1})^T$ is incorrect; unitarity gives this expression as $-M\beta$. The tridiagonal formula and triangular factorization remain supported by direct evaluation and the subsequent algebra. Also, the disconnected tuple displayed at the end of Section 3 has product
   $$
   \operatorname{diag}(\bar\omega^{,2},-\omega^2)\ne I_2.
   $$
   It is therefore not a Hurwitz tuple as written. Test 5 uses a different valid six-entry disconnected tuple, with reduced norm $1/4$ and square class $1$. Replace the erroneous illustration with the actual verified example and its values; it is not needed for the general obstruction proof.

3. **State the descent conclusion at the level proved.** The $D_3$ centralizer argument establishes an infinite braid orbit of the coefficient representation modulo conjugacy. Remark 2.3(i) of [4] consequently obstructs extending that coefficient local system over a finite cover of the configuration space. It does not, by itself, prove that the resulting parabolic Hodge variation considered independently cannot descend: that stronger assertion would require an additional argument recovering the coefficient system. Make this distinction explicit. Likewise, the order-$5$ construction is a successful mechanism; the introduction's unrestricted assertion that order $5$ is necessary is not established by the monomial obstruction.

4. **Correct the account of prior algebraicity and preserve the geometric limits.** Theorem B of [1] assumes the embedding condition, not the additional existence of a quaternionic hyperbolic plane, and its general split-sixfold input is Markman's theorem rather than Schoen's theorem alone. Neither the coefficient commutant calculation nor the present construction establishes the generic Hodge group, simplicity, or period-map rank of the sixfold family. A five-point Hurwitz base has dimension two and cannot dominate the three-dimensional PEL space. The manuscript appropriately disclaims algebraicity of the Weil classes; retain that limitation prominently.

These corrections do not undermine the independently reproducible finite-cover realization or the substantive monomial obstruction. The metadata abstract is informative, although shortening it and replacing internal paper identifiers with a self-contained description would improve accessibility.
