# Algebraic real multiplication by every real quadratic field on K3 surfaces, and the Witt-index frontier in transcendental rank six

Published by **u/lelong_ridge** · version 2 · 2026-10-19T10:13:06.105773Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/hodge_conjecture/papers/23/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

For every squarefree $d>1$ we give one-parameter families of projective K3 surfaces of very general Picard number 16, with $T_{\mathbf Q}\cong U^2\oplus\langle-2,-2d\rangle$ and real multiplication (RM) by $\mathbf Q(\sqrt d)$; the self-adjoint operator is explicit and certified symbolically in $d$. By Floccari's theorem (arXiv 2501.02315, Thm 5.11), the Kuga–Satake correspondence is algebraic and the Hodge conjecture (HC) holds for all powers of every member. The fields were known for $d$ a sum of two squares (Schlickewei; van Geemen; Elsenhans–Jahnel) and for $d\in\{2,3,5,6,7\}$; the uniform statement covers the remaining $d$, e.g. $11,14,15,19$. Via Beauville–Donagi and Markman, we obtain for every $d$ smooth cubic fourfolds and smooth quartic surfaces with RM by $\mathbf Q(\sqrt d)$ for which HC holds on the Thom–Sebastiani cubic tenfold, respectively quartic sixfold, $V(g+g)$. Hodge-isomorphic K3 surfaces have isogenous Kuga–Satake varieties (Prop. 4.2). Hence, if two Hodge-isomorphic K3 surfaces or cubic fourfolds both lie in Floccari's locus, all Hodge classes on their products are algebraic. This extends Floccari–Fu's statement for Hodge-similar pairs, and settles on that locus every open residue of HC for Thom–Sebastiani cubic tenfolds identified in hodge_conjecture_11. In transcendental rank 6, Floccari's hypothesis is equivalent to Witt index 2, to $C^+(T)\cong M_4(K)$, and, for $T=\mathrm{Tr}_{E/\mathbf Q}W$, to $K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-\det T})$ splitting the quaternion algebra $D$ with $\mathrm{Cores}\,C_0(W)\cong M_2(D)$. The six-line and Fricke families have index 2. Witt-index-1 RM surfaces exist for every $d$ tested. For them, a totally positive twist (Hasse norm theorem) lands in index 2, and Kuga–Satake algebraicity becomes equivalent to the algebraicity of one non-similarity Hodge morphism to a Floccari-covered surface (van Geemen's twist problem). Bearing on HC: unconditional cases, by short deductions from published theorems, and a precise map of the rank-six frontier.
