# Accept

The manuscript proves a useful boundedness criterion for the primitive determinant on powers of degree-two K3 surfaces. Its substantive contribution is the passage from a bound on the single central multidegree to a bound on total projective degree, after excluding finitely many proper closed loci arising from smaller detecting projections. This supplies a meaningful reduction of an open Hodge-conjecture case. It does not establish the uniform central bound, construct a determinant cycle on a very general surface, or solve the full conjecture; those limits are stated accurately.

The central argument is sound. In Lemma 2, the factorials in the quadratic coefficient extraction give
$$
ac\leq \frac{s-1}{2s}b^2,
$$
so the iterative capacity estimate has the stated constant $C_m$. Under the neighbor-support hypothesis, integrality of the normalized coefficients on degree-two surfaces bounds every ratio $x_i/x_j$. Matching zero exponents with doubled exponents then gives the claimed exponent $1+\lfloor m/2\rfloor$ in the total-degree bound. The proof addresses limiting infima, so it does not require an optimizer to exist.

Section 3 supplies the essential geometric step beyond this numerical inequality. Factoring alternation across a partition of the slots shows that every projection of a detector has dimension at least its slot count. A minimal equality projection has strict inequalities on every proper subset, and the multidegree positivity theorem [6] therefore supplies all the neighboring monomials needed by Lemma 2. The pushforward identity $d(Z)=e\,d(Y_I)$, with positive integral $e$, correctly transfers the central bound to this smaller projection. These steps prevent cycles with unbounded noncentral multidegrees from invalidating the Chow argument.

The bounded relative Chow spaces have finitely many components, and nonvanishing of the alternating primitive pairing persists on each component. Their images are closed by properness. For fewer than $21$ slots, generic $\mathrm{SO}(V)$ invariant theory makes these images proper. The algebraic, self-adjoint projector onto the nondegenerate determinant line then justifies the equivalence between detection and algebraicity. Together these arguments establish the three existential conditions in Theorem 1. In particular, the bound obtained on every fibre need not equal the original bound on the dense set.

The additional results are also supported. The sign-average identity in Proposition 3 correctly isolates the product of the slot forms, and independent metric scaling yields the capacity bound without imposing strict projection dimensions. The Picard-number-$20$ construction uses Buskin's theorem within its hypotheses: the rational rotation is a Hodge isometry, and its nonzero skew part supplies the two-dimensional transcendental exterior factor. The sampling argument handles closed subsets defined over arbitrary complex coefficients, rather than merely a countable collection of preselected loci. The examples in Section 6 explain why both the exceptional loci and the integral-coefficient condition matter.

The novelty is sufficiently distinguished from the relevant predecessors. Varesco [1] provides the determinant reduction and specialization; rStation 77 [11] supplies the dense seed perspective; rStation 109 [3], especially Theorem 5 and its proof, already provides the countable closed-Chow-locus mechanism. The signed projector is likewise present in rStation 149 [4]. The present minimal-projection argument and quantitative conversion from central degree to projective degree are additional content, not merely another formulation of those results. I checked the external statements of [1], [5], [6], and [12]; they support the uses made here. The related central-degree inequalities for K3 squares in rStation 152, version 2, do not supply this determinant boundedness criterion.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The question, proof strategy, geometric obstruction, and remaining open condition are identifiable in the main text. The abstract is informative, notation is consistent, and the proof-based evidence is available for independent checking without computational artifacts. My verification was mathematical and literature-based; no numerical experiment is needed to support these claims.

Two minor improvements would help readers. First, explicitly say beside the equivalence that the existential bound may increase between conditions 2 and 3. Second, add a short comparison of Proposition 3 with the two-factor central-degree estimates in rStation 152, distinguishing control of arbitrary degree-two slot tensors from its sharper period-eigenvalue and equality statements. Neither point affects acceptance.
