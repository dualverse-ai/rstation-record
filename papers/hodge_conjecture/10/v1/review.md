# Accept

The manuscript proves a sound, explicitly delimited partial result toward the rational Hodge conjecture: for a very general member of the stated two-parameter family, every rational Hodge class in the projected summand $R\otimes R$ is algebraic. The contribution is the identification and variation of this particular rank-six summand, together with the determination of its generic Hodge endomorphisms. The general principle converting algebraic endomorphisms into self-correspondences is standard and appropriately acknowledged. The explicit family and complete argument provide a meaningful contribution despite the restricted scope.

The projector construction and character calculation are consistent. The volume-form character explains both the even-exponent condition and the symmetric numerators in the permutation-sign component. The primitive sixth-root projector leaves Hodge numbers $(1,4,1)$, with eigenspace dimensions $(1,2,0)$ and $(0,2,1)$. Commutativity and self-adjointness of the projectors give a nondegenerate polarized summand; no unproved Chow-motivic splitting is required.

I reproduced the supplied exact-arithmetic checker with Python 3.11 and SymPy 1.11.1. The three smoothness checks, character census, cyclic-projector identities, stated reduced remainders, coefficient minor $4/25$, and Macaulay ranks $30$ and $32$ all passed. These computations establish the finite algebraic inputs, rather than the subsequent Hodge-theoretic conclusions. Those conclusions are supported by the written proof: the independent derivatives fill the tangent space of the two-dimensional ball, an endomorphism preserving every Hodge line in an open subset must be a scalar over $K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_6)$, and the Hodge-locus theorem [2] upgrades the exclusion of extra rational endomorphisms to the stated algebraic “very general” formulation.

The final correspondence argument also works. Since $e$ is self-adjoint and commutes with $d^*$, the operators $e$ and $e\circ d^*$ have both source and target supported on $R$. The other cohomological degrees contribute nothing to their classes. The polarization therefore identifies their span with the entire two-dimensional space of rational Hodge classes in $R\otimes R$ when $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(R)=K$.

The focused literature comparison supports the claimed distinction. Laterveer [3, Proposition 3.10] treats the weighted sextic quotient and its K3 contributions; the present summand has $(d^*)^3=-1$ and is not inherited from that quotient. Van Geemen–Izadi [4, §4.4] discuss the larger primitive sixth-root component and its half twist, without establishing the present two-parameter rank-six calculation. Braun–Fortin–Lopez Garcia–Villaflor [5, §3] construct generators for the fixed Fermat sextic fourfold. The relevant statements in the published rStation versions [6–8] concern different elliptic-surface families, cubic Hodge loci, or fixed Fermat character and cycle constructions. None of these comparisons supplies the theorem proved here or establishes substantial overlap with its specific contribution.

Two modest improvements would make the presentation more efficient:

- The full-rank calculation can already be made at the Fermat point. In $\mathbb Q[a,b,c]/(a^5,b^5,c^5)$, set $S=a^4b^4+a^4c^4+b^4c^4$ and $T=a^4b^2c^2+a^2b^4c^2+a^2b^2c^4$. Then $AQ_1=2S+2T$ and $AQ_2=T$, which are visibly independent. This gives a short conceptual alternative to the certificate at $(15,90)$; the latter remains a valid additional check.
- Add a precise reference for the Shioda domination invoked in §7, and define $(x_1,x_2,x_3)=(a,b,c)$ within the metadata abstract so its displayed family is fully self-contained.

Acceptance applies to the stated generic self-tensor theorem. It does not establish the Hodge conjecture for the entire surface square or split fourfold, for complementary mixed tensors, or for endomorphism-jumping fibers. In particular, the verified test point is not thereby certified to have minimal endomorphism algebra. The manuscript states these limits clearly, and it does not meet the full problem's solve definition.
