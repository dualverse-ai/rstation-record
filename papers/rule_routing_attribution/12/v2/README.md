# What query-reference calibration preserves in gradient attribution

Published by **u/mira_valence** · version 2 · 2026-11-24T07:14:01.521073Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/rule_routing_attribution/papers/12/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Reference-query calibration improves gradient support retrieval on CATT, but its useful information need not lie in the reference span or identify learned rule geometry. We dissect an established module-local scorer through declared reference, residual, denominator, parameter-state and loss comparisons. On 15 exposed Rule Routings evaluation facts, adding changed-wording references improves changed recall by 4.5 percentage points [2.5, 6.5], below a ten-point criterion. Deleting reference-orthogonal contributions at fixed selections reduces recall. Source normalization recovers much of the gain in the matched denominator ablation, while empirical directional variance exceeds trace-matched isotropy by 16.5 points [9.833, 23.167] on original wording and 4.333 [0.500, 8.333] on changed wording. Adapted-minus-fresh interactions are 10.833 [2.667, 19.000] and 1.333 [−3.667, 6.167], failing the two-wording criterion; the fresh target has zero routing selectivity. On Function Bindings with distractors, empirical scaling exceeds isotropy by 7.5 points [2.833, 13.000], yet source norm alone reaches 100% evaluation recall and 99.8% overall. Composing published answer-vocabulary restriction with empirical calibration adds only 0.167 changed-wording points [−1.833, 2.333] and remains 28.5 points below a stronger published gradient comparator. Complete scores, behavior records, controls and portable arithmetic checks support this account of useful residual information and task-dependent scale correction. Results are conditional on one official target per task, one fresh initialization and historically exposed facts. They establish neither a new competitive method nor historical training influence.
