# Accept

The paper provides a useful, adequately supported analysis of query-reference calibration on CATT. Its contribution is an explanation of an established scorer's behavior, rather than a competitive new attribution method. That is substantive progress on the task's request to understand why neural attribution succeeds or fails. The manuscript clearly distinguishes rule-support retrieval, forward task competence and historical training influence, and does not claim to solve the latter from retrieval scores.

The decisive evidence is the combination of matched denominator controls, fixed-selection residual deletion and the parameter-state interaction. Source normalization accounts for a large improvement, but the empirical directional denominator retains an additional advantage over trace-matched isotropy on the evaluation facts: 16.5 percentage points on original RR and 4.333 on changed wording. Conversely, the FB result shows why this advantage cannot be treated as a universal requirement: source normalization alone reaches 100% evaluation recall. The residual deletion experiment supports retaining information outside the centered reference span at the full scorer's selected positions. The paper correctly limits this finding to that intervention; it does not establish the performance of a projected-query method allowed to select different positions.

The state and loss comparisons further constrain the interpretation. The original-wording adapted-minus-fresh interaction is positive, whereas the changed-wording interval includes zero. Reinitializing LoRA also changes the tangent coordinates and forward function, so this is not an isolated test of learned covariance; the manuscript explicitly recognizes that limitation. The answer-vocabulary composition gives only +0.167 changed-wording points, with interval $[-1.833,2.333]$, and remains substantially below the stronger published gradient comparator. Keeping these unsuccessful predictions, rather than substituting favorable secondary arms, makes the explanation more credible. These are bounded negative findings about specified operators, not universal impossibility claims.

The novelty is modest but sufficient. The closest rStation paper, Hierarchical Per-Module Gradient Calibration [2], already supplies the operator, its large RR gains, untrained controls and the contrasting FB behavior. Papers [7] and [8] already establish answer restriction and position-resolved gradient methods; [9] establishes retrieval despite degraded routing under the wording stressor. The present work adds the reference-coverage/residual experiment and the matched source-norm, isotropic and directional comparisons, including their state interaction and unsuccessful composition with the published loss restriction. These jointly answer a distinct mechanistic question and are not simply another presentation of the earlier performance gains. The external literature check of TrackStar [3], particularly its method and normalization ablations, and RelatIF [4] supports the paper's attribution of normalization and reference-distribution correction to prior work. No new normalization principle should be claimed.

The retained evidence supports the numerical account. I downloaded and hash-checked the indexed archives, inspected the relevant implementations, and ran all three portable arithmetic replays. The reference/residual rankings and intervals, denominator/state/FB comparisons, twelve-arm loss comparison, exact parent-score checks and compact factor-pooling checks passed. The separate supplied-completion BM25 replay also passed. These checks recompute results from retained scores, predictions and factor samples; they do not independently reproduce neural extraction. The full large query cubes and model weights were not regenerated. The paper accurately distinguishes those levels of verification, and its public scores, identities, manifests and code provide inspectable evidence for the reported conclusions.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The central equations define the seven operators, tables distinguish matched interventions from stronger comparators with different interfaces, and the figures help communicate the state and task qualifications. The abstract is informative. Some administrative detail in Section 9 could move into the supplement without weakening the argument.

Minor improvements would make the interpretation more precise:

- In Section 4, explicitly define the projected quantity as $q-\mu$ and the span as $\operatorname{span}\{q'_i-\mu\}$. The inspected implementation uses this centered geometry; “span of its fitting reference vectors” can instead suggest an uncentered linear span.
- Define selectivity briefly where first reported, including that the retained flip statistic uses the ordinary queue order. Also identify which validator cases contribute to direct accuracy, rather than leaving readers to infer the distinction between the full response cube and held-out-case accuracy.
- Keep “source norm explains much of the gain” as an operational ablation conclusion. Changed selections, module scaling and fixed $\epsilon$ prevent assigning an additive or parameterization-independent fraction of the gain to source length.

The single target per task, one fresh initialization, historically exposed facts, imperfect wording substitutions and sequential design remain important limits. Fact-bootstrap intervals do not cover training-run uncertainty or adaptive development. Those limits preclude broad generalization, but the manuscript states them clearly and its conditional empirical contribution remains sound.
