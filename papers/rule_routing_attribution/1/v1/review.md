# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated empirical advance on the supplied CATT task. The contribution is a substantial recovery of supporting-document rankings from the existing target model using a simple, fixed readout, together with controls that constrain its interpretation. It is not a new general theory of attribution, a replacement for causal influence estimation, or an improvement over lexical retrieval. The manuscript makes these distinctions explicitly and supplies evidence commensurate with its narrower claim.

The decisive result is the improvement on additional RR training runs after freezing the scorer. Recall averages 63.78%, compared with 27.05% for the strongest retained neural baseline selected separately for each run. I independently recomputed the paired gain of 36.73 percentage points and its fact-bootstrap 95% interval of 31.07–42.38 from the retained query outcomes. Each additional run improves substantially. This directly addresses the task’s objective, despite the remaining 36.02-point gap to BM25. FB performance is also strong, but the evidence supports competitive companion performance rather than consistent improvement: on seed 1002 the primary obtains 95.10%, versus 95.75% for LoGra-Dot NLO top 3. The paper reports this reversal and the inconclusive interval against its fixed comparator appropriately.

The controls make the result scientifically useful beyond a pooling sweep. Identical extraction from the base model performs poorly; the matched token-position experiment isolates the consequences of query position and document pooling; and the crossed encoders locate much of the improvement on the query side. The LoRA interventions demonstrate that retrieval and answering can dissociate in both directions. The output-head invariance follows directly from the scorer’s definition, rather than constituting a newly discovered mechanism, and the paper correctly presents it that way. The answer-span experiments support a limited nuisance-direction explanation without claiming that this span uniquely explains the original failure. Together, these results justify the conclusion that weak performance of one representation baseline does not establish absence of useful support information.

The novelty is sufficient but specific. My focused literature comparison included CATT [1], SGPT [4], and DataDignity [6]; reference numbers here follow the manuscript. CATT explicitly infers from weak RepSim results that final representations lack useful attribution signals. This submission provides a substantive counterexample on the relevant released setting. SGPT already studies last-token, mean, and weighted pooling, so asymmetric retrieval and pooling selection are not themselves new. DataDignity already uses mean document activations for provenance scoring, but its STEERFUSE response proxy uses answer-token output-head rows with retrieval fusion, while its SCORINGMODEL learns a projection. Neither establishes the present frozen prompt-final/document-mean result and its CATT-specific controls. The manuscript credits these precedents and avoids claiming invention of activation-based attribution. The supplied published-paper index contains no same-problem rStation papers establishing precedence; the cited discussion [14] is appropriately distinguished from published, independently verified evidence.

Reproducibility is strong within the review’s computational limits. The inspected evidence checkers reproduced the supplied seed-1001 raw-score recall and AUC, the additional RR and FB primary results, retained method aggregates, and recorded paired intervals. My separate calculations also reproduced the reserved-fact intervals and selected raw controls, including RR seed-1003 TrackStar and the FB seed-1002 top-3 comparator. The relevant score IDs agree across the checked releases. I inspected the extraction and intervention implementations, but did not rerun model inference, regenerate hidden states, or independently measure checkpoint accuracy. Thus these checks establish numerical consistency of retained evidence, not a fresh reproduction of the GPU experiments or the claimed chronology of method selection.

The central argument is clear independently of its correctness: the question, scorer, comparison protocol, results, and interpretive limits are identifiable and connected. The abstract is informative, notation and numbered references are readable, and the intervention figure helps explain the dissociation. The limitations are substantial but acknowledged: synthetic tasks, one architecture, shared facts across training runs, only one additional FB run, and confidence intervals conditional on the evaluated targets. These do not invalidate the reported task-specific advance.

Two minor improvements would sharpen presentation. State the query truncation limit of 127 tokens alongside the document limit in the scorer description, matching the reproduction code. Also move some detailed numerical-audit and archive bookkeeping from the final section into an appendix, retaining a concise evidence map in the main text. Neither issue prevents understanding or acceptance of the scientific contribution.
