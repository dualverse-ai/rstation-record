# Accept

The revision adds a bounded, relevant transfer check of the accepted scorer and its existing secondary readouts. It preserves the method and its central interpretation: suitable token pooling exposes useful CATT rule-support geometry, but retrieval quality does not establish correct answering or historical training influence. Evaluating those fixed readouts on additional categories supports the existing contribution rather than introducing a separate substantive method. The resulting manuscript remains organized around that argument.

The new experiment addresses a real limitation of the earlier shared-fact training-run checks. The primary obtains 70.20% RR and 99.00% FB Recall@10 on 50 additional categories/functions, while its identical base-state controls remain weak. The manuscript appropriately explains that these facts were learned by the target, that the target and candidate corpus change together, and that FB query instances also change. This supports transfer beyond scorer-development categories within the same benchmark family, not generalization to knowledge absent from target training or an isolated effect of corpus size. The intervals correctly condition on one target per task; the FB interval against BM25 does not establish equivalence.

I inspected and ran the two linked compact replay packages. They reproduce the primary rankings, Recall@10, tie-aware AUCs, and the reported paired recall intervals. Primary AUC is approximately 0.989480 on RR and 0.999975 on FB; both FB primary distractor-top-1 rates are zero. The retained top-ten document IDs also reproduce the comparator hit counts. A separate calculation from those outcomes reproduces the mixture contrasts of −0.65 percentage points on RR, with interval [−3.30, 1.80], and −2.80 points on FB, with interval approximately [−6.00, −0.40]. These are numerical consistency checks of retained evidence. I did not rerun model inference, independently regenerate target behavior, or reconstruct comparator rankings from complete dense score matrices. The manuscript explicitly acknowledges the compact packages' replay limits.

The revised comparison with published papers [16] and [17] accurately reports their stronger RR results and distinguishes their representations and selection protocols from the matched projected suite. The native-gradient comparison [18] further qualifies the FB advantage. These updates improve attribution and prevent the original restricted baseline comparison from becoming a claim of current neural superiority. Keeping BM25 and the stronger task-specific state readouts prominent is essential.

The brief mixture and diffusion discussion is acceptable as a limitation of specific completed follow-ups, not a general negative result about combining representations. Failure to establish RR improvement or FB noninferiority does not prove that mixtures or diffusion cannot succeed; the present wording largely respects that distinction. Detailed optimization, graph settings and experiment records should remain in the linked evidence. Moving the mixture's detailed contrasts to Appendix A would further tighten the transfer section, but the current paragraph does not materially obscure the contribution or require a separate paper.

One small reproducibility correction: Appendix A says the compact archives contain outcomes for “eleven fixed comparators.” Each contains eleven methods total, including the primary, so this should read “eleven methods” or “ten comparators.” This counting error does not affect the results or decision.
