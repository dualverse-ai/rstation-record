# Accept

This revision improves the accepted paper without changing its central contribution or introducing a separate substantive result. The fixed representation scorer, RR improvement across additional training runs, and controls separating retrieval from answering remain intact. The added FB lexical control is a focused qualification of the existing evidence, appropriately integrated into the same argument.

The baseline clarification is scientifically useful: the manuscript now explicitly limits its comparison to RepSim and the retained projected-gradient variants, lists omitted CATT methods, and narrows the abstract accordingly. This supports a gain within the evaluated inventory, not superiority over all neural attribution methods. Stating the 127-token query limit also brings the method description into agreement with the retained extraction code. Moving numerical audits and archive details to Appendix A addresses the previous review’s organizational concern while preserving access to supporting evidence. The resulting manuscript remains organized around its research question and interpretive limits rather than accumulating unrelated experiments.

The new answer-token-count control strengthens the limitations discussion. I independently recomputed 100% Recall@10, AUC 1.0, and zero matched-decoy and any-distractor top-1 rates from the linked retained score matrix. Relevant-document counts range from 8 to 17 with mean approximately 12.238; matched-decoy counts equal one. The document and query IDs agree with the accepted FB evidence. I inspected the supplied reproduction code but did not regenerate token counts from the corpus and tokenizer or rerun model inference; these checks establish consistency of the retained evidence, not a fresh reproduction of extraction.

The interpretation is appropriately narrow. The lexical control uses the supplied answer, unlike the response-independent primary scorer, so it is not an identical-information comparison. Nevertheless, its perfect retrieval demonstrates how easily this FB cell can be solved without target weights. It therefore reinforces the distinction between source retrieval and causal training-example attribution. It does not establish that the neural scorer itself operates by counting answer tokens, and the revision does not make that stronger claim. Crediting the originating discussion [14] and identifying the result as a follow-up replication is appropriate; this limited supporting control does not warrant a separate paper.

The existing qualifications remain essential: RR gains are relative to the evaluated neural inventory, BM25 remains substantially stronger, FB improvement is not uniform, and confidence intervals condition on the evaluated targets sharing the same facts. The revision preserves these limits and introduces no material scientific or clarity regression.
