# Accept

The paper supports a useful, bounded empirical contribution: a conventional relevance ranker trained on a small set of annotated facts transfers to a separate natural-fact panel, improving recovery of annotated sources over both its starting state and the reported lexical controls. Its CATT results establish a substantive applicability boundary rather than a new best attribution method. The manuscript makes this distinction clear enough for acceptance.

The decisive evidence is the fresh-panel comparison. My independent calculation from the saved candidate margins, source annotations and control scores reproduces 91.276% Recall@$R$ for the selected base-initialized ranker, 77.474% for the transferred lexical anchor, and 82.292% for exact-token IDF. It also reproduces the primary gain of 13.802 percentage points with paired fact-bootstrap interval [9.375, 18.359], the 8.984-point advantage over exact-token IDF, and the 7.747-point improvement over the unchanged starting state. The last two comparisons matter: the result is neither solely a consequence of a weak transferred lexical mixture nor solely the pretrained model's existing ranking ability. The original primary remains separate from the two additional training-order runs.

The evaluation is appropriately structured for this claim. Checkpoint selection uses development validation facts; the fresh panel receives fixed rankers and transferred lexical weights. Candidate construction uses retrieval scores without inserting evaluation positives. Shared-source annotations are unioned, and component resampling addresses the resulting dependence. The supplied arithmetic replay passes all four audits, including checkpoint selection, CATT ranking and intervals, fresh-panel metrics and saved target predictions. I inspected the relevant preparation and training code as well. These checks validate saved-output consistency; they do not independently reproduce optimization, neural inference, corpus annotation or hardware timings.

Relevance to the selected problem is sufficient, although the main positive result is on natural factual evidence. The work directly evaluates the learned scorer on RR and FB-distr and retains the strong contextual comparator. On changed RR, relevance training raises base recall from 89.20% to 93.95%, while same-pool contextual coverage reaches 98.00%. Thus the CATT shortfall persists even after controlling candidate access. Original RR and FB offer little remaining headroom. This is useful evidence about transferring relevance supervision to the task, but it does not establish superiority over the strongest published CATT method. Separating answer-target competence from external-ranker retrieval is essential and is handled correctly: training the fresh answer target does not make these external scores estimates of that target's historical training influence.

The novelty is empirical rather than algorithmic. The focused external check confirms that relevance-token scoring and transfer already appear in monoT5 [5], pairwise/listwise ranking losses in RankT5 [6], and relevance tuning within a generation workflow in RankRAG [7]. The manuscript acknowledges these precedents. Compared with the supplied versions of Papers 4, 11, 13 and 15 [1–3, 8], the present experiment changes the learned object to a source-relevance judge and supplies a distinct fresh-panel comparison with initialization and training-order controls. Those results are not established by the earlier gradient-calibration or contextual-index studies. The contribution is modest but substantive; it should retain its present restriction to this measured setting.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The question, scoring rule, fitting and selection boundaries, primary comparison, negative CATT result and interpretation limits are identifiable in the main text. The tables and figure communicate the argument without requiring readers to reconstruct an experiment chronology. The abstract is informative. Three improvements would strengthen the presentation without changing the decision:

- State “32 fitting facts plus 32 validation facts” alongside the small-supervision claim, especially in the abstract, so readers do not mistake the fitting count for the total selection-supervision budget.
- Use “annotation-defined support” consistently where “literal support” could imply independently verified sentence-level entailment. For example, the retained Huittinen query asks about its official language, while one labeled source describes a Finnish-speaking college there. Recovering the benchmark annotation remains a valid endpoint, but that sentence alone does not establish official-language status. A few representative annotation and ranking errors would clarify this limitation.
- Bring a compact relation-level breakdown into the main results or a clearly referenced appendix. The aggregate effect spans only four selected relations, and the paired fact intervals do not quantify uncertainty over relation populations, model families or training-data samples. The absent fresh-panel contextual baseline and omitted trained weights also remain practical comparison and reproduction limits, as the paper already acknowledges.

Acceptance rests on the demonstrated annotation-retrieval gain and the controlled CATT transfer boundary, not on a new ranking objective, causal attribution, general retrieval superiority or improved generated answers.
