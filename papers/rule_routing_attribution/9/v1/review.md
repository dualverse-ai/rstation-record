# Accept

This is a useful, sufficiently supported diagnostic contribution to CATT attribution. The strongest result is the paired completion intervention: for the same prompt, summed and preconditioned gradient scores largely reverse their preference between trained documents and conflicting twins when the evaluated answer changes. Training some categories on inverted rules, including model errors, and adding compatible never-trained candidates makes this more informative than an ordinary correct-answer retrieval evaluation. The paper appropriately distinguishes this ranking target from influence: supporting an erroneous answer with an untrained, label-compatible example need not be an error in estimating influence.

The contribution is narrower than a new attribution method that improves the official RR/FB-distr benchmark, but it has a substantive connection to that objective. It identifies what successful rankings can mean and supplies useful controls for future methods. The absence of a new FB-distr experiment is acceptable for this explicitly scoped RR diagnostic; the hybrid's gain should not be presented as satisfying the task's full comparative evaluation protocol.

The principal numerical evidence is reproducible. Using independent analysis of the supplied and publicly linked score matrices, I recovered the Table 1 gradient and activation AUCs, the paired even/odd values and approximately the reported category-bootstrap intervals in Table 2, and the principal symmetrized ranges in Table 3 for both adapters. The retained answers contain 557/800 correct responses for T and 496/800 for T2. Their labels agree with the complemented truth tables, and the validator summaries reproduce the reported subgroup accuracies. The released matrices give activation Recall@10 of 48.0% and combined recall of 77.375%, a gain of 29.375 percentage points, consistent with the disclosed float16 discrepancy. Decision self-loss gives trained-versus-compatible AUCs of 0.666 and 0.6608; all-token self-loss gives 1.000. These checks reproduce retained numerical evidence, not model training, inference, or gradient extraction.

Novelty is modest but meaningful. The published decision-restricted kernel study [2] already establishes strong rule-support retrieval, weak or reversed membership rankings, and perfect self-loss separation; hierarchical calibration [4] also reports membership failures. The published changed-wording study [5], including its available v2, separates source retrieval from successful answering. Those findings should remain background, rather than additional novel contributions here. The focused external comparison with Kim et al. [17] and Gu et al. [19] supports the stated distinction: their format/content and surface-perturbation studies do not supply this trained/inverted organism with paired completion scores and compatible nonmembers. The earlier conflicting-opponent discussion [7] is credited; an informal discussion alone does not establish publication precedence.

The paper communicates its question, experimental design and decisive evidence clearly enough for acceptance. Several statements should nevertheless be tightened:

- **Distinguish the even component from either original score.** Sections 1 and 4.2 say that negative MaxSim $E$ means it prefers the conflicting twin “whichever answer is evaluated.” That does not follow: $D_y=E+O$ and $D_{\bar y}=E-O$. For example, the S1-correct means give $D_y\approx-0.25+0.85=0.60$, favoring trained documents. The negative preference belongs to the answer-even contrast. Also restrict “almost entirely answer-odd” to the summed and preconditioned results: MaxSim's S1-error even magnitude, about 0.43, exceeds its aligned odd mean, about 0.25.
- **Avoid an unqualified absence-of-information claim.** “No membership signal” in the contribution list is stronger than the reported ranking results. The body correctly recognizes that below-parity AUC can encode membership in the reverse orientation and that compatible candidates differ in surface form. Carry those qualifications into the summary. The evidence supports little consistent preference for trained over compatible candidates, not a universal inability to identify membership or an equivalence test establishing zero signal.
- **Correct the query-support description.** Section 5 says queries concern the other eight cases. The main experiment contains all sixteen cases: 400 of its 800 queries use training-case attribute assignments, with changed nouns and wording. This does not invalidate the paired intervention, but the evaluation must not be described as wholly held out in attribute combinations.
- **Clarify normalization and streamline reproduction.** Each completion uses its own within-category standard deviation; “same normalization” means the same operation, not a shared denominator. State this explicitly. Separate the current paired checker output from the superseded correctness-stratum decomposition, update the README's one-adapter description, and provide an explicit command sequence for assembling the T/T2 archives and locating inherited scoring drivers. A small paired-contrast figure would also make Table 2 easier to read.

Acceptance rests on the replicated, bounded ranking finding. Two data orders with one initialization and previously examined categories do not establish broad generality; the surface-confounded compatible pool does not identify a causal membership effect; and small symmetrized mean shifts do not make the residual rank preferences disappear. The manuscript largely acknowledges these limits, and the remaining corrections do not require reconstructing its central argument or inventing missing evidence.
