# Accept

This is a useful, bounded advance in CATT rule-support retrieval. The contribution is a supervised combination of existing projected-gradient blocks followed by query-reference calibration at individual document positions. The manuscript explains the scoring rule, separates fitting from calibration, and supplies meaningful component and reference-pool controls. Its main empirical result is supported: on the stated seed-1001 fact split, the combined method substantially improves over the original CATT projected-gradient baselines. Acceptance is for this restricted empirical contribution, not for a new best CATT retriever, causal training-example attribution, or demonstrated robustness across training runs.

I independently recomputed metrics from the retained score matrices, using stable corpus-order ties and direct positive–negative comparisons for AUC. Hybrid's test Recall@10, AUC and MRR reproduce as $42.83\%$, $0.894609$ and $81.92\%$. Combining these fact-level recalls with the task bundle's original baseline records reproduces gains of $24.83$ percentage points over LoGra-Dot, with a paired fact-bootstrap 95% interval of $[16.67,32.50]$, and $23.33$ points over TrackStar top-3, with interval $[15.00,31.00]$. The supplied verifier also passes. The reference-category exclusion results reproduce, and the untrained Hybrid control reproduces $2.00\%$ test recall. These checks validate retained ranking evidence; I did not rerun model extraction, adapter fitting, accuracy validation or the strict-denominator GPU experiment. Quantized secondary matrices exhibit the disclosed small recall differences.

The work is relevant to the task despite remaining well below BM25 and stronger published neural methods. Comparison with the exact supplied versions of [6] and [8] shows that the broad findings about pooling, template interference, model dependence and the distinction between support retrieval and provenance are already established. Those findings should not be counted again as this paper's novelty. The distinct contribution is the calibration-and-weighting procedure within the existing all-token projected representation, together with its matched pre-/post-pooling comparison. This is more than a renamed version of asymmetric state pooling or position-pair MaxSim. The substantial original-baseline improvement and the comparison showing when simpler document calibration suffices make it a useful partial advance.

A focused external literature check also narrows the conceptual novelty. Original TrackStar [3] explicitly uses evaluation-gradient covariance to suppress components shared by task templates; this is closer to the motivation here than the discussion of All-but-the-Top alone suggests. Cross-query correction of candidate popularity also has precedents in hubness correction [11] and inverted softmax [12]. These do not establish duplication of the specific CATT procedure, but they deserve discussion alongside the distinction between gradient-space covariance correction and standardizing each document-position score before nonlinear pooling.

The following qualifications and corrections would improve the paper without changing the supported principal result:

1. **Temper the Token-Z superiority claim.** Table 3's weighted RR test advantage over matched Doc-Z is $6.33$ points, but its 95% interval is $[-0.50,12.83]$. Across all 50 RR facts the advantage is only $1.05$ points, with interval $[-3.10,5.20]$; unweighted non-transductive Token-Z is slightly worse than Doc-Z on that aggregate. Conversely, I reproduce the FB-distr Doc-Z advantage of $3.75$ points with interval $[0.35,7.35]$. Thus “clean task-dependent crossover” is too strong: the RR ordering is suggestive and uncertain. Describe $P(>0)$ as a bootstrap fraction, not a posterior probability or conventional significance test. Section 6.1's “outperforms every baseline” should explicitly refer to the listed original gradient baselines and component controls; it excludes BM25 and the stronger published methods.

2. **Keep the mechanism descriptive throughout.** The caveats about learned weights and $B=0$ are appropriate. Section 3 nevertheless refers to modules “where category-specific rules are encoded,” and Figure 2 still says “Mechanistic Drivers.” Weight concentration alone does not establish that localization. Moreover, the supplied token-role audit shows rule-clause selection increasing on irrelevant documents too, from approximately $13.2\%$ to $32.1\%$, compared with $13.8\%$ to $29.2\%$ on relevant documents. The shift demonstrates changed pooling behavior, not by itself improved identification of relevant clauses. Present it as evidence consistent with the proposed explanation.

3. **Preserve the single-run scope.** The distinction between target-model training and scorer training is sufficiently explained, and the strict-denominator and reference-exclusion controls address concrete leakage alternatives. Nevertheless, these are results conditional on one exposed target, one split and one projection seed. “Strictly unseen facts” should consistently mean unseen by scorer fitting, not unseen by the target or by the broader development process. Evaluation of a frozen method on additional official adapters remains necessary before claiming training-run robustness. Its absence does not invalidate this explicitly limited study.

4. **Correct the regeneration instructions and evidence descriptions.** The lightweight score checker works, but the advertised full command sequence is incomplete: fitting expects `task/fact_splits.json`, whereas the archive places that file at its root; strict Hybrid extraction precedes the script that creates its strict weights; analysis requires `fb_layer_reps_nlo.pt` without a supplied FB extraction command; and the CPU audit needed by subsequent analysis is omitted from the sequence. Document the required directory layout and dependency order, provide the missing generation command, and identify exact upstream code/model/data revisions. The FB score archive also lacks document and query UIDs, unlike RR. Distinguish directly regenerable scores from aggregate-only checks, and identify the source of the FB accuracy figures, since the included GPU driver validates RR accuracy only. These are reproducibility-documentation gaps rather than a contradiction of the verified main score result.

Scientific communication is otherwise adequate for acceptance: the question, equations, principal comparisons and limitations are identifiable without reconstructing the study from execution logs. The figures help explain the calibration and failure patterns. The introduction and metadata abstract would benefit from fewer repeated control statistics, and different symbols should denote projection dimension and pooling size, but these are presentation improvements rather than material clarity defects.
