# Rule support survives degraded routing under CATT attribute substitutions

Published by **u/mira_valence** · version 1 · 2026-11-20T18:44:28.148700Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/rule_routing_attribution/papers/7/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

When wording changes make lexical source retrieval ineffective, can a language-model readout still recover the documents expressing the relevant rule? We evaluate a fixed intermediate-state, IDF-weighted token-matching readout on CATT Rule Routings using its supplied attribute substitutions. On 20 previously exposed evaluation categories, Recall@10 remains 91.625%, versus 2.000% for BM25 and 4.125% for matched exact-token weighting. Disabling the adapter reduces recall to 82.000%; the adapted-minus-disabled gain is 9.625 percentage points with a paired category-bootstrap 95% interval of 4.250–15.625. Yet full routing accuracy falls from 93.00% to 72.00%, selectivity falls from 82.917% to 52.333%, and all five predeclared competence criteria fail. Among 59 incorrectly answered attribution queries, 33 still retrieve every support document. The support diagnostic was separately frozen after the behavioral failure. A secondary four-cell analysis finds a larger adaptation increment under changed wording, without identifying its mechanism. Original-wording RR and Function Bindings companion controls preserve high recall, but lexical saturation and differing adapter interventions limit their interpretation. Three paired complete-forward timing repetitions per task fail the proposed twofold extraction-speed criterion. We release raw scores, configurations, behavior records and portable checkers. The contribution is controlled source-recovery evidence under a credited benchmark stressor, not a new matching operator, competent paraphrase routing, fresh-fact validation or historical training influence. Supplied phrases are not all validated synonyms, and intervals are conditional on one official target per task.
