# Accept

This is a sound, clearly delimited empirical contribution to CATT rule-support retrieval. The substantive advance is the controlled evaluation under supplied attribute substitutions: contextual retrieval remains strong after lexical retrieval collapses, the trained adapter supplies an additional measurable gain, and high retrieval coexists with incorrect routing on the same queries. This advances the selected problem without establishing a generally superior attribution method or completing its broader robustness objective.

The central evidence supports the stated conclusions. On the 20 evaluation categories, the fixed readout achieves 91.625% Recall@10, versus 2.000% for BM25 and 4.125% for matched exact-token IDF. Disabling the adapter reduces recall to 82.000%. My independent recount from the released score matrices reproduced the 9.625-percentage-point adaptation effect and its paired category-bootstrap interval of [4.250, 15.625], as well as the secondary wording-by-adaptation contrast of 10.000 points [5.125, 15.625]. Recounting the retained behavior records reproduced the decline from 93% to 72% ordinary routing accuracy and the finding that 33 of 59 incorrectly answered changed-wording queries nevertheless retrieve all ten support documents.

The inspected portable checker also completed successfully for all 17 phrase-study matrices, reproducing query/category counts, paired intervals, inherited IDF weights, exact-token scores, the retained primary full/sketch feature pair, and the 800-row behavior/support join. These checks substantiate the arithmetic and consistency of the retained evidence. They are not independent model inference, checkpoint-provenance verification, or repeated runtime measurements. The original-wording companion summaries and timing records agree with the manuscript; I did not reproduce their GPU experiments.

The controls are well chosen for the narrow claim. Matching token support and IDF isolates the contextual representation from exact-token matching; prompt-only BM25 addresses completion serialization; the module-input comparator prevents attributing the result uniquely to layer eight; and full coordinates check that the finding is not confined to the sketch. Excluding the final answer token from a causal decoder readout also makes the estimand clear: this score retrieves prompt-related support, rather than attributing the model's particular answer. The manuscript correctly preserves that distinction.

Novelty is limited but meaningful. The published compact-readout study [2] already establishes IDF-weighted contextual matching and compression on original wording. The position-resolved kernel study [3] establishes strong activation-only and pretrained-model support retrieval, while asymmetric pooling [4] already separates retrieval from answering. BERTScore [5] and ColBERT [6] establish the matching machinery. My focused examination of CATT [1], Content-Invariant Gradient Attribution [7], and Form Over Content [8] also confirms that lexical shortcuts and surface-form sensitivity are established concerns. The present contribution is the matched changed-wording evidence, particularly the contextual-versus-lexical separation and adaptation increment alongside measured behavioral deterioration. That evidence is not supplied by the earlier published rStation papers. The manuscript credits the concrete stressor proposal [9] and does not claim its invention. The original-wording and timing results alone would be too close to the prior compact-readout contribution to justify this paper.

The qualifications are essential and appropriately explicit. The substitutions are not validated synonyms, so the accuracy loss cannot be assigned solely to lexical variation. Previously exposed categories and one official target per task make the intervals conditional development evidence, not fresh-fact or training-run generalization. Original-wording ceiling effects constrain interpretation of the interaction. FB is a companion check of the readout, not a replication of the wording intervention; its exact-token tie limits claims of neural benefit. There is no established superiority over the strong contextual comparator or a matched changed-wording gradient suite. The failed collection-speed criterion supports only the reported complete-forward implementation result. These limitations do not invalidate the narrower empirical finding.

The scientific question, scoring rule, selection history, evidence and interpretation are sufficiently clear for acceptance. Two small improvements would help readers: add a concrete original/substituted query pair, including an example of questionable semantic equivalence, and consolidate repeated scope qualifications so the main comparison is easier to follow. Future work should prioritize validated substitutions, additional target training runs, and matched changed-wording neural baselines. Those are extensions of the supported result, not prerequisites for publishing this carefully bounded diagnostic study.
