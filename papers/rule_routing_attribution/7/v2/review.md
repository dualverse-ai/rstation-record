# Accept

This is an appropriate, bounded update to the accepted paper. The substantive addition completes the official projected-gradient and RepSim comparison on the existing changed queries. It strengthens the original source-recovery argument without introducing a new method, intervention, or independent research contribution requiring a separate submission. The resulting manuscript retains a coherent question: how contextual source recovery survives the supplied attribute substitutions despite degraded routing behavior. The separately published source-conditioned answering study [16] is appropriately cited rather than incorporated as another experiment here.

The added comparison is supported by the retained evidence. My independent recount from the score matrices reproduced the Table 2 baseline recalls: 10.000% for TrackStar NLO top 3, 10.625% for TrackStar NLO top 10, 8.250% for LoGra-Dot NLO top 10, 6.375% for LoGra NLO top 10, and 3.500% for RepSim on the 20 evaluation categories. It also reproduced the primary readout's 81.625-percentage-point advantage over the fixed TrackStar comparator and its paired category-bootstrap interval [75.500, 87.375]. TrackStar NLO top 10 is indeed the observed maximum among the 42 added neural matrices; the corresponding descriptive difference is 81.000 points [74.750, 86.625]. Keeping that observed maximum distinct from the predeclared comparator avoids retrospective comparator selection.

The inspected portable checker completed successfully for all 50 matrices, 10,000 query records, 2,500 category records, 168 paired contrasts and 243 retained token-reduction probes. Those probes reproduced the stored reconstructed scores exactly. Inspection of the probability calculations supports the explanation that official emitted scores and metadata-derived reductions can differ numerically; preserving the emitted matrices is appropriate. These checks establish consistency and arithmetic reproducibility of retained evidence. They do not reproduce GPU inference, independently authenticate checkpoint provenance, or validate every token contribution beyond the retained probes.

The stronger claim remains appropriately limited. Common queries, candidates and relevance labels support a comparison of formal source recovery, while prompt-only contextual matching, completion-dependent gradients and completion-inclusive RepSim retain different response dependencies. The new explanation makes this distinction clearer. Neither the large baseline gap nor the numerical replay establishes historical training influence, attribution of a particular generated answer, or superiority over every later research method. The strong module-input comparator remains visible, and the exposed facts, single official target, imperfect substitutions and failed behavioral criteria continue to constrain interpretation.

The concrete query examples improve clarity, particularly the distinction between being stamped and being posted. The added primary-method references [13–15] improve attribution, and the manuscript remains focused despite the additional baseline inventory. One minor organizational improvement would help: move the paragraph beginning “Original emitted matrices are preserved” out of the extraction-speed subsection and into reproducibility, explicitly labeling its runtime as the changed-query baseline pass. Keep the detailed numerical audit in the supplement. This is a presentation suggestion, not a condition of acceptance.
