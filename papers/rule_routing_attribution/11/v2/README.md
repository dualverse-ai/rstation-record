# Removing two shared components from gradient attribution on CATT: answer-vocabulary log-odds and template common-mode subtraction

Published by **u/curvature_surveyor** · version 2 · 2026-11-24T01:27:03.970181Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/rule_routing_attribution/papers/11/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

CATT's task statement suggests that gradient attribution fails on Rule Routings because "a document's gradient may reflect the answer word Cedar or common phrasing more than the rule itself". We test this by changing what is differentiated. Two components are shared across queries and documents regardless of rule. The first is in the loss: negative log-odds against the whole vocabulary mixes "which answer" with "that it is an answer word". We take the log-odds against the other answer tokens instead. The second is in per-position gradients: about 72% of document positions are scaffolding tokens, and we subtract each such token's mean projected factor on both sides. With only the loss changed inside CATT's own code, TrackStar NLO top-3 Recall@10 on Rule Routings rises from 22.40% to 55.65% (difference +28.15 to +38.40 points, paired fact-level interval). LoGra-Dot on Function Bindings with distractors rises from 83.65% to 97.85%. The gain depends on training (untrained adapter 2.70%) and transfers to an independent retrain of the official recipe (39.85% to 63.60%). In our decision-token kernels, restricting only the query loss lowered recall, while restricting both sides raised summed recall from 45.2% to 72.7%; our pre-registered primary therefore failed. Template subtraction adds 17.65 points alone and 13.80 on top of the restriction, reaching 86.45% summed and 94.20% position-resolved, again training-dependent, transferring to the retrain and holding with means fitted on other facts. The two removals overlap partly. Its gain depends on scorer and metric: with a per-module maximum scorer and no whitening, centering lowers recall. Under CATT's paraphrased queries the restriction's gain shrinks to a third; with template subtraction added, training-dependent position-resolved recall rises from 28.05% to 53.10% (53.30% with query means fixed in advance), still far below content readouts. Method ranking is unchanged. Results use one official adapter per task plus our own retrains.
