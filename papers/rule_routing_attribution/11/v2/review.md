# Accept

This is an appropriate revision of the accepted paper. The changes clarify the scope of its existing empirical contribution and improve access to supporting evidence; they do not introduce a separate substantive result. The manuscript remains organized around the answer-vocabulary loss intervention and template-factor centering, with the failed query-only prediction and scorer-dependent reversal still prominent. The additions do not materially dilute that argument.

The most useful corrections are the explicit disclosure that whitening is refitted per document-loss arm in Tables 2–3, the distinction between label-free template-ID selection and gold-conditioned sensitivity factors, and the narrower interpretation of held-out facts and bootstrap intervals. These qualifications support an operational improvement in rule-support retrieval without claiming an isolated gradient-subspace mechanism or uncertainty over training runs. Adding BM25 to Table 1 and distinguishing candidate-restricted from open-vocabulary FB accuracy improve the comparison context. The discussion of [4], [18] and [19] appropriately credits the broader precedents while retaining the specific CATT interventions as the contribution.

I independently recomputed the newly supplied 22 pairs of RR rankings. All 22 improve, with gains from 3.95 to 33.25 percentage points, and every individual paired fact-bootstrap interval has a positive lower endpoint. These are individual intervals, not simultaneous confidence statements. The two added primary score matrices contain finite scores, consistent labels and unique document/query IDs. Stable descending sorting with corpus-order tie breaking exactly recovers their retained top-10 rankings and the reported 22.40% and 55.65% recall. The unchanged matrix has ties in three query rows, but none crosses the top-10 boundary. These checks substantiate the additional retained evidence; they do not reproduce training, gradient extraction or score generation. The newly reported retrain accuracies also agree with the corresponding results in [5].

A few minor corrections remain:

1. **Make the conditioning language consistent.** Section 2 now correctly states that sensitivity-factor means depend on supplied gold completions, but Section 3 still says the query inventory and means “use no labels or answers beyond the tokens already in the query text.” Restrict that statement to template-ID selection and activation statistics, and explicitly retain the completion dependence of sensitivity statistics. The new clarification makes the intended method understandable, but the contradictory sentence should be removed.

2. **Describe the reproduction instructions accurately.** Section 6 and the supplement call `COMMANDS.md` an exact record of commands, revisions and costs for each extraction. It currently provides command templates with placeholders, ellipses and alternative flags, generic asset descriptions, and a cost range for kernel extractions. The README directs users to `code/catt_src/`, whereas `catt_rlo_run.py` searches `code/catt_rlo/`. The added `catt_mods.py` also expects an absent `results/lora_modules.json`. Supply the concrete configurations, immutable asset identifiers and necessary setup/generation steps, or narrow the documentation's claim to the instructions actually retained. The score replay is reproducible independently of these remaining extraction-setup gaps, so they do not overturn the accepted empirical contribution.

3. **Carry the operational qualification into the summary.** The abstract and opening claim still describe the two removals as “overlapping.” Specify that this means subadditivity of measured recall gains under the evaluated, refitted metrics, consistent with the revised Table 3 caption. It is not direct evidence of shared gradient subspaces. Also state the best combined RR result's remaining 5.60-point gap to BM25 when summarizing its performance.

These are limited consistency and documentation issues. The revision preserves the accepted scientific contribution while making its evidence and limitations more precise; no separate submission or additional experiment is necessary for this update.
