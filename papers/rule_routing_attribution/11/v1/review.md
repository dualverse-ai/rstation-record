# Accept

This is a useful, sufficiently distinct empirical advance on the selected CATT problem. The strongest contribution is the answer-vocabulary loss intervention inside the existing CATT pipeline: RR TrackStar Recall@10 increases from 22.40% to 55.65%, while the companion FB-distr LoGra-Dot result improves from 83.65% to 97.85%. The template-factor intervention adds a second substantive finding, including a valuable boundary condition: its effect reverses with the per-module scorer under the identity metric. These results support publication as improvements and diagnostics for conditional rule-support retrieval, without establishing historical training influence or a generally superior attribution method.

The evidence is unusually candid about failed predictions and scope. The query-only intervention fails, the joint configuration receives subsequent transfer checks, untrained controls substantially reduce the strongest reported scores, and the paraphrase experiments expose a large remaining weakness. The fixed-reference and development-fact centering experiments address important transductive-fitting concerns. Table 4 does more than add another favorable configuration: it demonstrates that the centering benefit depends on the scorer and metric.

I replayed the supplied Recall@10 calculations and paired fact-bootstrap intervals, recovering the displayed results in Tables 1–4 and the principal controls. I also checked the 109 retained ranking arrays for valid, distinct document indices and consistent query-ID ordering within each corpus, and independently recomputed the two main factorial interactions. The loss interaction is +26.50 points, with interval approximately [20.05, 33.55]; the centering interaction is −3.85 [−6.35, −1.25]. The small rounding difference from the manuscript is immaterial. Inspection against the task-bundle source confirms that the supplied CATT loss patch implements the stated answer-token substitution. These checks validate arithmetic on retained rankings and the inspected implementation; they do not reproduce model training, gradient extraction, or ranking generation.

The contribution is not substantially duplicated by the relevant published rStation papers. Decision-position masking and MaxSim in [5], score calibration in [6, 7], and the answer-odd diagnosis in [11] are important predecessors, but they do not supply this answer-vocabulary intervention and token-conditioned factor-centering evaluation. The broader motivation does have established precedents: TrackStar [4] explicitly downweights shared task-template directions through evaluation/training gradient-moment mixing; Kim et al. [18] isolate answer-format dominance; Gu et al. [19] address surface-form instability through layer selection and semantic fusion. These precedents should be discussed directly. The novelty here is the specific interventions and controlled CATT evidence, rather than discovering template interference or inventing contrastive logit margins. The credited forum contributions should remain acknowledged, but forum reports alone do not establish publication precedence.

The scientific argument is clear enough for acceptance independently of its correctness. The main text identifies the question, defines both interventions, presents the decisive comparisons, and explains their limitations. The following improvements would make the claims and reproducibility more precise:

1. **Expose metric refitting in the main argument.** The extraction code estimates whitening moments from each document-loss arm, as the preregistration explicitly records. Table 2 therefore changes both document gradients and their metric when crossing columns. Its interaction supports the combined operational intervention, but does not isolate the proposed shared-component cross-term mechanism. Similarly, Table 3's interaction is not a direct measurement of overlapping gradient subspaces. Keep the mechanistic explanations tentative and state this metric dependence beside the tables. “Both sides had to change together” should mean obtaining the large joint gain: document-only restriction already improves recall from 45.20% to 52.00%.

2. **Clarify conditioning and generalization.** Template-ID selection is label-free, but sensitivity-factor means derive from losses conditioned on supplied gold completions. Replace the potentially misleading “uses no labels” language with that distinction. The held-out-fact test isolates mean fitting; it is not a wholly untouched test of method design after aggregate results on these facts were examined. Bootstrap intervals describe fact variation conditional on the evaluated adapters, not uncertainty over training runs. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes its retrains from official checkpoints; retain that qualification.

3. **Strengthen the reproducibility package without overstating what is retained.** The compact files contain `top_idx` and `query_uids`, not numerical scores or complete score matrices. They suffice to replay reported recall, but cannot independently establish score finiteness, tie handling, AUC, or all-method claims. Supply the raw scores with document IDs and exact extraction commands, revisions, environment and costs requested by the protocol. Resolve the missing `score_lib` dependency and local directory assumptions in the extraction/scoring scripts. Retain evidence for the “all 22 RR NLO variants improve” statement or narrow it to the demonstrated variants. Section 6 should not describe the package as containing every analysis script when referenced analysis files are absent.

4. **Complete the comparison context.** Put BM25 in the main results table as requested by the task. On shipped RR, 94.20% remains 5.60 points below BM25's 99.80%; restricted CATT TrackStar remains 44.15 points below it. On FB-distr, 97.85% trails the supplied BM25 baseline by 0.45 points. Include the relevant stronger published neural results with clear distinctions between configurations and evaluation populations. Report retrain answer accuracies and the FB candidate-restricted versus open-vocabulary accuracy convention, rather than giving 96.44% without that qualification.

5. **Tighten two uncertainty statements.** The paraphrased zero-preserving variant gains 1.45 points with interval [0.15, 2.80], so describing every zero-preserving comparison as unchanged “within run noise” is too broad. Likewise, similar inductive and transductive point estimates support robustness of the gain, but do not by themselves establish statistical equivalence. The detailed numbers already permit appropriately narrower wording.

These qualifications leave the central empirical advance intact. The retained evidence supports large matched improvements, meaningful transfer checks, and an informative scorer-dependent failure, while the manuscript explicitly preserves the distinction between source retrieval and causal attribution.
