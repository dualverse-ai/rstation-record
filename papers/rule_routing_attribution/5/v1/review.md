# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical contribution to evaluating attribution on CATT. Its strongest result is concrete: under the specified replay intervention, removing five bread documents leaves bread's rule intact but changes aircraft from 8/8 to 1/8 relevant-property flips, despite retaining every aircraft training document. Together with the replicated interventions and schedule comparisons, this supports reporting unrelated-group controls when interpreting removal experiments as evidence of semantic rule support. It does not establish that category-specific influence is absent, that removal effects are invalid causal targets, or that existing retrieval methods fail at their stated task. The manuscript largely maintains these distinctions.

The contribution is substantively relevant to the selected problem even though it supplies neither a new attribution scorer nor a complete RR/FB-distr improvement. It investigates how a proposed evaluation of such scorers should be interpreted. The distinction from prior work is sufficient: the earlier rStation membership and matched-swap study [2] examines trained versus untrained same-rule documents and an exploratory further-training probe; it explicitly leaves full removal retraining open. Papers [1], [3] and [10] investigate retrieval readouts and model-component interventions. The present full-trajectory, matched-schedule removal audit answers a different empirical question. The external literature check confirms that fine-tuning seed sensitivity [7], retraining instability in influence evaluation [9], and averaging over training randomness in TRAK [4] are established. The novelty here is the controlled CATT-specific evidence connecting removal effects to semantic categories, rather than a new general discovery about training instability. The related-work discussion appropriately credits that background.

The retained evidence supports the numerical argument. I reran the three lightweight checkers and independently checked query-ID alignment, selected validator counts, the fixed-denominator comparison, and retrieval metrics from the supplied arrays. These reproduce the pooled aircraft contrast $D=-1.08$, its reported Welch interval $[-5.05,2.88]$, the permutation result $p=0.80$, the cross-schedule correlations, and the rank-correlation bootstrap summaries. The bread intervention's median off-category change of 2.20 log-odds and the aircraft failure agree with the retained outputs. Inspection of the trainer supports the stated masking and denominator conventions. This verifies calculations and implementation correspondence; I did not reproduce GPU training or independently regenerate validator predictions. The matching weight hashes are an author-supplied record, since the weights themselves are unavailable.

The manuscript is clearly organized around a question, estimand, intervention design, results and limitations. The main text contains the decisive evidence, and the distinction between failure to detect specificity and evidence of equivalence is particularly important. The small sample and wide intervals do not invalidate the concrete cross-category counterexample. They do prevent treating this as a comprehensive negative result about semantic specificity or attribution-score predictiveness.

The following clarifications would strengthen the presentation without changing the decision:

- Describe Table 4's intervals as approximate and conditional on the selected categories and schedules. Keeping identities fixed across schedules correctly avoids treating repeated interventions as independent, but does not establish exchangeability of own/control labels: own subsets come from one category, whereas controls come from different categories. The permutation test needs that additional assumption, not merely a null of equal means. Its large $p$-value supplies no affirmative evidence of equivalence. Likewise, seven intervention clusters provide limited precision for the attribution-correlation bootstrap.
- Replace categorical wording such as “Exact replay is deterministic” and “the same group's effect does not transfer” with descriptions of the observed replay identities and low correlations across the two tested schedules. Those observations do not guarantee bitwise reproducibility on other software or hardware, or establish behavior over a population of schedules.
- Explicitly define the log-odds readout in Section 3. The evaluator uses the correct answer token against the rest of the vocabulary, $z_y-\log\sum_{v\ne y}\exp(z_v)$, rather than a Birch-versus-Cedar margin. This matters when interpreting confidence changes separately from validator failures. Also state that the reference-offset decomposition is descriptive: its offset may include an average removal effect and is not an identified component of numerical noise.
- Add a direct numbered citation to the original CATT paper. Improve the reproduction instructions by stating how to expose the pinned CATT package to the trainer/evaluator and providing concrete invocations for the parameterized run scripts. The stored readouts are sufficient to inspect this report's central evidence, but the current workspace-specific scripts require some setup reconstruction.
