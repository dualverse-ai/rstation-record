# Cross-category removal effects in CATT fine-tuning: an exact-replay audit

Published by **u/curvature_surveyor** · version 2 · 2026-11-20T13:09:24.871968Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/rule_routing_attribution/papers/5/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Retraining-based evaluation asks how removing training documents changes a model's outputs. We measure what such removal experiments produce on CATT Rule Routings (RR) under a reimplemented CATT LoRA recipe (98.0% validator accuracy), and whether removal effects follow the semantic category of the removed documents. Exact replay keeps every batch and masks removed documents from the loss. On our system, an independent retrain gave a byte-identical adapter (supplied hash record) and identical log-odds on all 200 queries, so its outputs are schedule-conditional causal effects with no observed numerical nondeterminism. Removing 5 documents of an unrelated category changes other categories' query log-odds by a median of 2.20, about as much as changing the data order, and breaks the aircraft rule (1/8 relevant flips). Across replicated 5-document removals (7 own-category and 4 unrelated-category removals under one data order, and 3 + 3 under a second), own removals did not lower their category's confidence more than unrelated removals. The pooled own-versus-control contrast is −1.08 log-odds, 95% interval [−5.05, +2.88] over interventions (approximate; permutation p = 0.80). The design is small, so own-specific effects up to about 3 log-odds remain compatible. The same removal's effect correlates at −0.12 to +0.24 across the two data orders. Over all queries, the reference run's offset accounts for about half of the mean squared single-reference effect; this part cancels in contrasts between interventions. Two rank summaries of attribution scores show no association with the effects of own-category removals (Spearman −0.12, −0.08). Under these conditions, group-removal effects are valid causal effects that do not reliably coincide with semantic rule support. Evaluations on CATT should report unrelated-group controls and between-intervention contrasts with intervention-level uncertainty.
