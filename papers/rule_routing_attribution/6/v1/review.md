# Accept

The manuscript supports a useful, bounded empirical contribution: a compact token-level readout that preserves near-ceiling CATT rule-support retrieval across RR and FB-distr, with matched storage, scoring, lexical and adapter-intervention controls. It is relevant to the task as an advance in support retrieval and its interpretation. It does not establish target-specific training influence, superiority to BM25, or generalization to unseen facts, and the paper states these limits clearly.

The central numerical evidence checks out. I independently recomputed retrieval metrics from the released score matrices and reproduced evaluation Recall@10 of 100%, 99.875% and 100% for the two official targets and the additional RR target. The paired category bootstrap reproduced the additional-target gain over the selected retained baseline of 9.25 percentage points, with interval [5.00, 14.25], and the official-RR gain over exact-token IDF of 1.75 points [0.25, 3.75]. The trained-minus-zeroed RR contrast also reproduces: 0.875 points [0, 2.125]. The three supplied portable replays passed, including exact-token matrix reconstruction, fitting-document frequency checks, score-derived counters and intervals, and storage/timing arithmetic. The original and additional-target corpora are identical at the parsed-record level.

These checks reproduce calculations from retained evidence, not model training, feature extraction, behavioral inference or hardware timings. I inspected the relevant extraction, calibration, projection and weighting implementations; their central operations agree with the stated construction. The reported 12.916% index fraction and 25.530% scoring-time fraction describe logical FP32 storage and the recorded scoring stage. They do not establish an end-to-end speedup, given the much larger extraction cost. The manuscript appropriately reports all three timing repetitions and the earlier unsuccessful timing implementation.

Novelty is modest but sufficient for this empirical contribution. The closest published same-problem work, [2], already establishes strong position-resolved module-input retrieval, including 99.10% RR recall and substantial retrieval without a trained adapter. Papers [3] and [8] establish other successful gradient and state readouts, while [9] develops the distinction between support labels and removal effects. The external literature check confirms that normalized IDF-weighted contextual maximum matching is already present in BERTScore [4], token-level late interaction in ColBERT [5], substantial representation compression in ColBERTv2 [6], and SRHT in [7]. The submission attributes these ingredients rather than claiming them as inventions. Its additional evidence concerns their joint CATT operating point: coordinate compression versus the tested position subsets, the companion-task failure of uniform weighting, and a frozen application to another trained target. That is more than a cosmetic renaming of the preceding papers, although it should not be described as a new retrieval architecture or a demonstrated advantage of Hadamard mixing.

The controls materially strengthen the interpretation. Exact-token IDF already reaches 100% on FB, and BM25 ties the proposed method on both RR targets. Coordinate sampling is at least as competitive as SRHT in these evaluations. Furthermore, high retrieval survives zeroing the adapter outputs despite chance answering behavior. These findings limit the method's scientific meaning to recovering semantic support with a particular representation and cost profile. The paper does not convert them into evidence of learned causal attribution.

The remaining qualifications are important but do not undermine the stated central result. The official cells were adaptively reused; the additional target supplies a prospective application on previously examined facts, with a changed training recipe, rather than the requested official multi-seed robustness study. Category-bootstrap intervals therefore do not measure training-run uncertainty or correct selection bias. The original FB full-vector numerical probe remains failed: passing score replays cannot certify the provenance or numerical accuracy of the underlying features. Because that failure is disclosed, the full-coordinate FB comparison is explicitly qualified, and the compact result and additional-target evidence have separate support, it does not warrant rejecting the bounded contribution.

Scientific communication is clear independently of these correctness checks. The main text identifies the research question, defines the scorer and fitting boundary, presents decisive comparisons, and separates retrieval from target behavior. The abstract is informative, and the figure complements the numerical table. Two small improvements would help:

1. Explicitly label whether each uniform comparator includes the supplied final answer position. The implementation averages all positions for some uniform scores, whereas IDF excludes the final answer. The supplement already reports the more closely matched `uniform_causal` controls, including zero FB recall; citing those values in the main text would make the weighting interpretation cleaner without requiring new experiments.
2. Keep the additional-target baseline description consistently restricted to the retained, validation-selected inventory. Its 9.25-point gain does not establish superiority over every published neural method, and the near-ceiling full-coordinate comparator answers a different question about compression fidelity.

Acceptance is for the documented CATT quality–storage–scoring result and its useful interpretive controls, within the stated limits.
