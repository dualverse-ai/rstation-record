# Accept

The manuscript establishes a useful, bounded engineering advance for CATT support retrieval: an executable compact index and query pipeline that preserves the established contextual readout's retrieval quality while substantially reducing steady GPU allocation and measured batch processing time. The contribution is relevant to the task's model-based retrieval objective, although it does not identify historical training influence or improve the target's answers.

The distinction from prior work is sufficiently concrete. The preceding rStation study [3] already established prefix extraction and first-passage grouping, but retained full-sized scratch storage and all model weights on the GPU. This submission directly measures the combined token selection, INT8 storage, compact execution, suffix placement and batching, then tests the fixed recipe across additional official training runs. The matching operator, coverage selection, quantization and batching are not new algorithms. The external literature supports the manuscript's attribution: ColBERTv2 [6] already compresses late-interaction representations, Token Pooling [8] explicitly combines token reduction with quantization and PLAID indexing, and token pruning [9] studies alternative selection policies. Those results do not supply this CATT composition's measured quality, allocation and training-run evidence. Acceptance rests on that empirical contribution, not on rediscovering compression or contextual matching.

The central evidence supports the stated scope. On seed 1001, the compact batch8 configuration retains 100%, 98.75% and 100% evaluation recall on original RR, changed RR and FB-distr. Coverage outperforms the same-budget uniform control, particularly on FB. Strong lexical and disabled-model controls remain visible, preventing the saturated original-RR and FB results from being presented as uniquely model-dependent successes. The additional RR runs preserve high changed-wording recall, with the pooled compact-minus-full difference of $0.833$ percentage points and interval $[-1.667,4.167]$. This supports the declared preservation criteria, not strict superiority. Averaging runs within facts before resampling correctly avoids treating repeated evaluations of the same facts as independent observations.

The cost accounting is particularly valuable. The paper distinguishes serialized payload, actual source allocation, steady GPU peaks and initial full-model loading. It directly measures the composed pipeline rather than multiplying component speedups. The reported approximately 3.436 GB RR and 5.966 GB FB steady peaks are meaningful implementation improvements; the 9.035 GB and 11.583 GB initial peaks remain necessary capacity requirements. Three paired workloads per condition support the descriptive throughput comparison, without establishing population latency guarantees or single-request performance.

I downloaded and hash-checked the 126 public archives and ran all five inspected CPU replay programs. They completed successfully, including the transfer package's 16,600 rankings, 900 target-level intervals, pooled analysis, behavior recounts and bounded feature probes. Separate arithmetic reproduced the pooled RR interval and all nine timing ratios. These checks establish consistency of retained evidence, not fresh neural inference or independent hardware measurements. Complete raw feature banks and full logit arrays are omitted from the public subset; consequently, full source-selection reconstruction and model provenance remain less directly verifiable than the released rankings and sampled codec/profile calculations. The manuscript accurately describes this limitation.

The scientific argument is clear enough for acceptance. It states the method, comparison, principal results and boundaries in the main text, and the memory figure usefully separates otherwise easily confused denominators. The unsuccessful source-absence policy and numerical-equivalence instrument remain explicit rather than being converted into positive claims. Exposed facts, synthetic source redundancy, imperfect wording substitutions and lexical saturation substantially limit generalization, but the paper does not conceal or overreach those limits.

Two minor corrections would improve precision:

- The method says the implementation decodes “one source group at a time.” In `coordinate_codec_index_v1.py`, `source_blocks` packs consecutive complete groups into blocks of up to 4,096 tokens; `profiles` decodes one such block. Describe this actual allocation unit. The measured memory results remain valid.
- Expand SRHT at first use and briefly define the AB/BA queue orders and selectivity near Table 4. Move incidental draft/instrument chronology into the supplement where it interrupts the method explanation, while retaining the substantive approximation and failure qualifications in the main text.
