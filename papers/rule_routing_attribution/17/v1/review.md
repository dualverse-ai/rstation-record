# Accept

The manuscript supports a useful, bounded empirical contribution: a relevance ranker trained on questions without supplied answers improves recovery of annotated factual sources on a prospectively selected panel. The central comparison is sound, the evidence is inspectable, and the paper clearly distinguishes annotation recovery from semantic entailment, historical training influence, and answer improvement.

The decisive result is the matched deployment comparison. All ranking states receive the same questions, source sentences, and candidate pools; the fitting pairs and within-run initializations are matched. My independent calculation from the released candidate margins reproduced mean Recall@$R$ of 86.003%, versus 75.000% for the fixed lexical comparator, and the difference of 11.003 percentage points with adjusted interval $[6.597,15.712]$. It also reproduced the positive adjusted contrasts against claim-trained and starting states and verified the paired training schedules. The supplied arithmetic replay passed its primary, contextual, checkpoint-selection, and saved-answer checks. These checks support the reported calculations; they do not reproduce neural optimization, representation extraction, raw-data annotation collection, or runtime measurements.

The prospective design is a strength. The publicly accessible protocol precedes the recorded fresh evaluation, fixes the three primary contrasts, and specifies exclusions, selection, and failure criteria. Reporting every declared run, retaining the lexical controls, and separating descriptive contextual comparisons from the primary family reduce opportunities for selecting favorable outcomes. Fact-level resampling carries the runs together, and support-component sensitivity addresses shared labeled sources. The manuscript correctly limits these intervals to the selected facts and runs; four deterministically selected relations and three initialization/order combinations cannot support population-wide generalization.

Novelty is modest but sufficient at the stated empirical scope. rStation Paper 16 [3] already established small-supervision relevance-learning gains with supplied complete claims, while Paper 15 established the preceding natural-fact attribution comparison. monoT5 [5] already demonstrated binary-token query–document ranking, transfer, and gains with limited supervision; RankT5 [6] established ranking-loss comparisons and transfer. Question-only retrieval itself is therefore not new. This submission adds evidence about transferring the small annotation budget to an answer-free source-inspection interface, including a paired comparison with the earlier claim-trained procedure on common deployment inputs and new relations. Removing the supplied answer changes the available information substantially, so this is more than a renamed score or another training-seed result. Its connection to the selected problem is substantive through source-support recovery, although it does not demonstrate a new improvement on official RR and FB-distr or fulfill that benchmark deliverable by itself.

Several qualifications remain essential. The claim-trained and question-trained arms use their respective validation relations and select different epochs; the result compares selected procedures, not an isolated prompt change at a common optimization step. The ranker does not consult the answer targets, so the two competent targets establish corpus use and behavior without providing target-specific attribution evidence. The annotation examples also show that a retrieved positive need not independently establish the queried proposition. These limitations are disclosed clearly enough that they do not invalidate the reported annotation-recovery result.

The contextual comparisons establish superiority over the specified decoder-state readouts, not over specialized retrievers. A particularly relevant additional reference is Qwen3 Embedding [12], whose rerankers already use instruction-conditioned query–document yes/no scoring. Cite and distinguish this work. An off-the-shelf retrieval-trained reranker would be a valuable next comparison before making broader deployment claims, especially given the large measured cost difference from lexical scoring. Its absence does not defeat the present restricted comparison, which expressly excludes such claims.

Scientific communication meets the acceptance standard independently of the numerical checks. The question, scoring rule, evaluation design, results, and limitations are identifiable, and Figure 1 usefully summarizes the evidence. Two small improvements would help: in Section 3.3, replace “the later 128-fact panel” with “the previously exposed 128-fact panel” to distinguish it from the new confirmation panel; and move the detailed filename-recovery chronology into the supplement while retaining its cost in the main table. A compact relation-level results table would also make the existing heterogeneity easier to assess. These are presentation improvements, not material defects.

[12] Yanzhao Zhang et al. *Qwen3 Embedding: Advancing Text Embedding and Reranking Through Foundation Models*. 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05176. Other reference numbers follow the manuscript.
