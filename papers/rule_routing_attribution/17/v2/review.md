# Accept

This revision appropriately strengthens the accepted contribution with a limited supporting comparison. The frozen Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B baseline directly addresses the previous review's concern about comparisons restricted to raw decoder-state readouts. It evaluates the existing question-only source-inspection task without changing the original training procedure, panel, annotations or primary contrasts. This is a useful baseline addition, not a separate substantive contribution requiring another paper.

The new numerical claims are supported by the released evidence. My independent calculation from the specialist score matrix and original candidate pools reproduced full-corpus and same-pool Recall@$R$ of 76.237% and 75.716%, respectively, and Recall@10 of 91.406% and 88.281%. Using the retained question-ranker per-fact reference metrics, I reproduced specialist-minus-question differences of $-9.766$ and $-10.286$ percentage points, with descriptive 95% fact-bootstrap intervals $[-13.759,-5.816]$ and $[-13.802,-7.010]$. The inspected portable replay also passed its checks of both ranking policies, all eight contrasts, sixteen intervals, relation summaries and recorded allocation costs. These checks reproduce retained-score arithmetic; they do not reproduce neural scoring, tokenization, training or runtime measurements. The old neural reference metrics were reused rather than independently reconstructed in this revision review.

The interpretation is appropriately bounded. Sections 3.5 and 4.3 explicitly distinguish the late comparison on an exposed panel from the original prospective confirmation. Reporting all comparisons, retaining the common candidate pool, and disclosing the default instruction and fixed checkpoint make the result interpretable. The specialist's higher full-corpus Recall@10 and shorter recorded same-pool scan prevent an unsupported claim of practical dominance. Likewise, intervals containing zero against lexical and claim-trained controls are correctly not presented as equivalence. Unequal training, different backbones, unknown item exposure and separate timing configurations remain important limitations, all acknowledged in the revision.

The added attribution to Qwen3 Embedding [9] is accurate: its original report describes instruction-conditioned query–document yes/no reranking and substantial relevance supervision. The revision therefore preserves the distinction between an established scoring interface and the paper's bounded empirical finding. It does not establish historical training influence or a new improvement on the official RR and FB-distr benchmarks.

The resulting manuscript remains organized around the original research question. Clarifying the previously exposed development panel and the annotation-dependent evaluation cutoff improves interpretation; the compact relation table makes heterogeneity visible without claiming population-wide transfer. Moving the filename-recovery chronology to the supplement also improves the main text. One minor editorial improvement remains: the specialist archive bundles broad applicability matrices and notes about unrelated experiments. Clearly label these as background provenance, or omit them from a focused revision release, so readers can readily identify the evidence supporting this comparison. No further experiment or separate submission is needed for this revision.
