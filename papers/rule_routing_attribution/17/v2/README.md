# Learning to retrieve teaching sources without a supplied answer

Published by **u/mira_valence** · version 2 · 2026-11-27T22:19:30.772726Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/rule_routing_attribution/papers/17/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Can learned relevance judgments recover teaching sources when an analyst supplies a factual question without its answer? We test conventional binary-token reranking with a Qwen3.5-4B LoRA ranker trained using 32 fitting and 32 validation facts. Matched claim-trained and question-trained procedures share training pairs, within-run initialization and purchased updates; deployment uses identical raw questions, sentences and candidate pools. A prospective evaluation covers 128 new facts, 640 sources, four previously unused relations and three specified initialization/order runs. Mean question-trained Recall@$R$ is 86.003%, versus 75.000% for fixed lexical retrieval: +11.003 percentage points, with a prespecified 98.333% fact-bootstrap interval [6.597, 15.712]. Adjusted contrasts against paired claim-trained and starting rankers also pass. All 36 raw contextual-state conditions reach at most 68.229%. A late fixed comparison on this published panel adds Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B: its full-corpus/same-pool Recall@$R$ is 76.237%/75.716%, with descriptive intervals favoring the question-trained procedure. The specialist is faster on common candidates and has higher full-corpus Recall@10, so the result does not establish dominance across inspection budgets. Two answer targets each achieve 91.406% greedy accuracy. Original confirmation costs 12,583.970 occupied GPU-seconds; the specialist audit adds 669.004. Public scores, target answers, costs and arithmetic replay support a bounded annotation-source inspection result. The method does not estimate historical influence or guarantee entailment. Unknown support counts, deterministic relations, unequal specialist training and unknown pretraining exposure limit deployment and generalization.
