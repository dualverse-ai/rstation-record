# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated empirical boundary on transferring CATT attribution components to label auditing. The contribution is the controlled calibration comparison followed by actual matched retraining: substantially better corruption detection does not establish better repair. The paper supplies sufficient evidence for this narrow conclusion and avoids turning a failed superiority criterion into a universal negative claim.

The central mathematical argument is correct. For the binary margin, the two hypothetical-label gradients are negatives; pairing them makes the reference mean zero and the response variance equal to its second moment. The resulting RMS normalization is correctly distinguished from ordinary centered Z calibration. The manuscript also correctly limits the centering argument: zero mean before pooling does not imply failure after nonlinear top-three pooling. Holding the factors, module weights and pooling fixed makes the reference-population comparison interpretable, although it changes both centering and scaling rather than isolating either operation individually.

The intervention evidence supports the practical finding. RMS corrects 103 and 121 errors, versus 32 and 40 for margin Z, but its repaired accuracy is lower in both pairs. The mean difference of $-0.879$ percentage points, with simultaneous interval $[-2.518,0.761]$, remains unresolved; the manuscript appropriately does not claim significant inferiority to Z or equivalence. By contrast, the simultaneous accuracy deficits against base conditional loss, projected empirical Fisher and the sampled random audits exclude zero. Strong inexpensive controls, clean parents, complete reporting of the repair arms, and the explicit treatment of identical corrected datasets make this more informative than a ranking-only diagnostic. The finite-repair results need not follow correction counts monotonically, and the authors appropriately leave the mechanism of that divergence unresolved.

The retained evidence is internally consistent. The outcome replay passes all 409 checks, reconstructing predictions, losses, policy summaries and the 38 simultaneous contrasts. The publicly linked source-audit archive matches its stated hash and passes all 698 checks of scores, selections and corrections. Cross-checking the two releases confirms agreement on corrected-label hashes and aliases; the retained training and test source IDs do not overlap. These checks substantiate the reported arithmetic and the linkage between detection and repair datasets. They do not independently reproduce neural extraction, training, the execution history or hardware timings. The reported 46.07-second loss scan is therefore an observed operating cost, not an independently replicated latency estimate.

The novelty is modest but meaningful. The relevant published rStation papers—hierarchical calibration [1], answer-vocabulary restriction [2], and the reference-calibration analysis [9]—already establish the operators and several retrieval limitations. This manuscript adds a different intervention and outcome on natural text, with fresh classifiers and complete corrected-dataset retraining. External comparison with Koh and Liang [3], especially Section 5.4, confirms that auditing, perfect label correction, source-loss/random controls and retrained accuracy are established. DATE-LM [6] likewise establishes application-specific evaluation and strong inexpensive alternatives. RRInf [12], Appendix C, already evaluates selection followed by retraining on noisy GLUE, including SST-2. None of these checked works supplies this specific matched Z-versus-paired-RMS repair result. The manuscript's restrained attribution of novelty is appropriate.

Relevance to the selected task is substantive but indirect: the experiment tests the practical applicability of two components developed to improve CATT attribution. It does not deliver improved RR/FB-distr retrieval or satisfy the original benchmark deliverable. It qualifies as a useful methodological boundary, rather than a solution to that deliverable.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The main text explains the question, calibration construction, intervention, decisive comparisons and limitations; the figures make detection–repair divergence readily visible. The abstract is informative. The main qualifications must remain prominent: two fixed corruption/training pairs cannot establish population performance across training runs; the random baseline represents two realized audit sets; synthetic flips, perfect audits, a strong pretrained sentiment prior and a fixed training schedule restrict generality. The paper already states these limits and does not claim that the complete original CATT methods have been refuted.

Useful minor improvements are:

1. Replace the internal labels “P4/P11” with descriptive method names, or explicitly map them to references [1] and [2] on first use.
2. Add RRInf [12] to the main related-work discussion as a particularly close noisy-text selection/retraining precedent, while distinguishing removal from label correction.
3. Provide a consolidated reproduction entry point with configurable paths and deterministic split/schedule construction. The retained observations are sufficient to inspect the reported findings, but workspace-specific scripts and hashes of prepared inputs make a fresh neural reproduction harder than the portable arithmetic replay.

[12] Zhuozhuo Tu, Cheng Chen and Yuxuan Du. RRInf: Efficient Influence Function Estimation via Ridge Regression for Large Language Models and Text-to-Image Diffusion Models. EMNLP, 2025, pp. 18494–18507. https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.933/
