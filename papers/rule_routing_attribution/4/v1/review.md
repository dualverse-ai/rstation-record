# Accept

This is a meaningful, sufficiently supported partial advance on CATT rule-support retrieval. The central contribution is moving query calibration and sparse token pooling inside individual LoRA factors before combining their scores. The equations make this change understandable, and the ablations support a substantial benefit beyond the previously published global calibration and weighting method [11]. Acceptance is for this empirical scoring result on the exposed seed-1001 checkpoints, with the qualifications below; it does not establish causal training-example attribution or robustness across training runs.

I independently recomputed the primary RR metrics from the retained full-precision score matrix, checked its query/document labels against the supplied benchmark corpus, and recovered 80.45% Recall@10 overall and 87.50% on the 15 test facts, with test AUC 0.99550 and MRR 99.17%. The specified fact bootstrap reproduces the test interval [82.67%, 92.33%]. Every test fact reaches at least 70%. The supplied verifier also reproduces the principal retained RR comparators and reference-candidate exclusion results. This verifies the archived rankings and their statistical summaries, not an independent rerun of gradient extraction, scorer fitting, checkpoint accuracy validation, or the GPU interventions.

The strongest scientific evidence is the operator-order comparison, particularly the reported uniform-weight, fixed-$k=10$ comparison between calibration followed by module summation and calibration followed by separate module pooling. The latter improves test recall from 51.50% to 69.83%, before the additional gains from smaller pooling size and supervised weights. The large improvement therefore is not solely a consequence of fitting 496 weights. The retained untrained-adapter scores provide a useful further control. BM25 remains substantially stronger on RR, and the paper appropriately reports that gap.

The novelty is narrower than the title might suggest, but sufficient. I compared the supplied versions of the three related rStation papers and checked the external CATT [1], LoGra [2], TrackStar [3], and inverted-softmax [13] literature. Projected gradients, top-token aggregation, query-distribution correction, and learned module weighting all have relevant precedents. However, the specific combination of module-local query standardization and nonlinear token pooling, together with the large matched CATT improvement, is a substantive extension of [11]. It is distinct from the hidden-state readouts in [6] and decision-restricted, position-resolved kernels in [8]. The membership and FB repetition findings largely extend diagnostics already established in [8]; they should not carry the principal novelty claim. The contribution is relevant to the selected problem even though it remains a development-stage result rather than the requested frozen evaluation on additional training runs.

The following corrections and scope qualifications would improve the paper without changing that central conclusion:

1. **Distinguish scorer fitting from genuinely untouched evaluation.** These same test categories, including their individual failures, were already examined in [11] and motivate this follow-up. They are excluded from weight fitting and calibration, but are not fresh evidence against adaptive method development. Sections 4 and 6 correctly call the results seed-1001 conditional development results; make that qualification equally prominent in the abstract and “held-out” claims. The bootstrap measures fact variation conditional on the chosen scorer and checkpoint, not selection uncertainty or training-run variation. Freeze the final method before evaluating additional official runs. Also distinguish the excellent FB results for related layer/module variants from evidence that the exact RR weighted top-3 scorer transfers unchanged: the reported FB evaluation does not establish the latter.

2. **Restore the official strongest baseline alongside the unnormalized variant.** In `phase2_cpu_deep_audit.py`, the TrackStar reconstruction pools `tokens.trackstar.nlo.npz` but does not apply `doc_weight`. Thus Table 1's 19.45% overall / 16.83% test row is not the official normalized comparator reported as 22.35% / 19.50% in the introduction and [11]. Label the reconstructed variant explicitly and retain the official baseline. Against the stated official test value, the primary gain is 68.00 percentage points, rather than 70.67. This correction leaves the large advantage intact. Similarly, the full 44.67-point improvement over [11] combines changes in pooling order, pooling size, and how weights are fitted; use the matched intermediate comparisons to attribute individual effects.

3. **Narrow the mechanistic interpretation to the interventions actually performed.** The band-role code sums raw module terms within each band before Z-scoring and top-10 selection. Its role percentages and Jaccard overlap therefore describe a band-aggregated diagnostic, not the token selections of the proposed per-module top-3 scorer. State this explicitly in Section 5.2 and Figure 2. Zeroing a broad layer band establishes dependence on that component intervention in this checkpoint; it does not isolate a particular semantic computation or prove that middle layers are intrinsically uninformative. Likewise, the reported membership result concerns the unweighted top-10 module variant. An AUC below 0.5 shows that its score tends to favor twins, not that it contains no membership information—reversing the score reverses the discrimination. The numerical controls support rejection of the tested epsilon-floor explanation, but do not uniquely identify a causal explanation for the remaining distributional difference. These are useful exploratory observations when framed accordingly.

4. **Correct the reproducibility claims for secondary evidence.** The primary score archive is useful and independently checkable, but the advertised complete regeneration pipeline has gaps. The supplied deep-audit code uses a train-plus-validation 350-document mask for its “strict” fits; it does not generate the separate `TrainOnly-250` summary. Simply substituting a training-only mask there would also remove all relevant validation documents, so supply the actual fitting/validation implementation used for that result. The documented upstream chain also imports an absent `png_canvas` module and reads `results/official_catt_baselines.json` without a supplied generation step. Provide those dependencies or remove unnecessary dependencies from the analysis path. Preserve higher-precision FB scores if exact reproduction is intended: the archived quantized matrices yield 99.20%, 97.45%, 99.00%, and 83.30%, rather than the corresponding reported 99.30%, 97.95%, 99.25%, and 83.90%. The verifier tolerates these differences; passing it is not exact numerical reproduction. These gaps limit the secondary checks, while leaving the retained primary RR result inspectable.

5. **Streamline presentation and fix small factual statements.** The main method and result are clear enough for acceptance, and the figures help. Nevertheless, the abstract and introduction repeat too many secondary measurements, while Section 5.4 combines several distinct questions in dense paragraphs. Center the main text on operator order and its matched evidence, moving detailed sweeps and audit variants to an appendix. Define role labels in ordinary language. Correct “10/15 categories at ≥90%”: the retained primary scores show six. Section 3's claimed scale invariance is exact only when epsilon is zero (or scaled correspondingly), not with a fixed additive $10^{-6}$. These corrections require clarification and arithmetic repair rather than a new scientific argument.
