# Accept

This is a useful, clearly communicated partial advance on the selected CATT problem. The contribution is a controlled study of rule-support retrieval: decision-restricted losses and position-resolved matching substantially improve the evaluated gradient rankings, while the controls expose important limits of interpreting those rankings as training-example influence. Acceptance does not establish robustness across official training runs or improved causal attribution.

The strongest evidence is Table 1. Estimating the block metric once and applying it to both losses makes the loss comparison interpretable. Under the all-token metric, decision restriction increases summed recall from 24.75% to 68.00%, and position-resolved recall from 75.25% to 82.10%. The corresponding gains also hold under the decision-token metric. The manuscript correctly explains that the aggregation intervention changes both position matching and the unit of normalization; it does not isolate MaxSim alone. The larger-projection result is appropriately presented as a different, factored metric.

I independently recomputed metrics from the primary matrices and the publicly linked evidence archives. The primary results reproduce exactly: 95.05% RR recall and 100% FB-distr recall. The common-metric comparisons and paired fact-bootstrap intervals reproduce within the disclosed float16 rounding differences; for example, the all-token-metric summed gain is 43.20 percentage points in the compressed release versus 43.25 in the manuscript. The trained-minus-untrained primary gain reproduces as 20.40 points, with an interval approximately [16.40, 24.35]. These checks validate retained scores and their analysis, not a fresh execution of model extraction or training.

The diagnostic evidence is substantive. Using the supplied corpus and score IDs, I verified that the RR membership groups correspond to documents 1–10, 11–20 and 21–30, with matching rules, truth tables and example-assignment sets within categories. The released scores reproduce the below-chance membership AUCs and the perfect all-token self-loss separator. The position-resolved swap contrast reproduces as $-0.03796$, with a fact-bootstrap interval approximately $[-0.05060,-0.02526]$. On FB-distr, I independently recovered the answer-count heuristic's 100% recall, the gold-document count range of 8–17, and the drop to 18.05% after dividing the primary scores by decision count. This is stronger evidence than an unexplained negative search result: it identifies a concrete shortcut and demonstrates its effect on the reported method.

The novelty is appropriately narrow but meaningful. Using the manuscript's reference numbering, CATT [1] already diagnoses token dilution and studies NLO and token aggregation; TrackStar [2] already introduces evaluation/training moment mixing and distinguishes factual retrieval from influence; ColBERT [8] supplies MaxSim; and Grosse et al. [5] localize influence by token. I checked these external papers and the exact published rStation version [3]. The latter already demonstrates strong state-based CATT retrieval and its dissociation from answering, including the stronger secondary readouts now acknowledged in Table 2. The present common-metric gradient comparisons and extended-corpus membership audit supply distinct evidence beyond that work. Neither the general retrieval-versus-influence distinction nor the borrowed scoring components should be advertised as new inventions.

The main text explains the question, formulas, comparisons and interpretation in a conventional scientific structure. Detailed experimental chronology is kept in the supplement. The abstract is dense but informative, and the distinction between development results and independent validation is explicit. The following qualifications and corrections would improve the accepted presentation:

1. **Keep the swap conclusion descriptive.** Section 7's statement that training on a document “therefore makes” it rank lower is stronger than the design identifies. Adapter B differs from A in training recipe and initialization as well as training documents. The difference-in-differences removes fixed group offsets, but cannot exclude interactions between document group and these other adapter differences. Describe the observed reversal as evidence consistent with a training-associated effect. A matched recipe and initialization across swapped corpora, preferably repeated, would support the stronger causal wording. This qualification does not undermine the measured reversal or the retrieval results.

2. **Keep membership discrimination separate from influence validity.** The manuscript largely handles this correctly: official candidates are already known training examples, and hypothetical upweighting scores need not identify membership. Replace “0.5 = no membership information” with “0.5 = chance pairwise discrimination in the specified orientation.” AUC alone does not characterize all information in a score. The inverted AUCs and the limitations of self-loss adjustment should remain explicit.

3. **Retain the development-only scope prominently.** Fact-bootstrap intervals condition on the selected checkpoint and do not cover adaptive method selection or training-run variation. The absence of unused official runs prevents a claim of protocol-complete, robust improvement. Nevertheless, the matched within-run findings and supported benchmark diagnostics are sufficient for a useful partial contribution. The 95.05% result remains 4.75 percentage points below BM25.

4. **Tighten the evidence map and reproduction instructions.** Replace the general discussion-thread link with immutable archive links and an explicit table-to-artifact mapping. The linked A–H archives do not contain raw matrices for every Table 3 row, notably the all-token summed and output-only rows, despite the blanket coverage statement. Add these or narrow that statement. The frozen manifest also references shell wrappers absent from the submission; provide their expanded commands. Correct the older checkers' tie handling directly, and identify the retained source of the full FB validator figures separately from the attribution-query accuracy records.
