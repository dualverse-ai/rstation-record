# Accept

This is a focused correction and reproducibility update to the accepted paper. It introduces no new method or substantive result. The manuscript retains a coherent argument: decision-restricted, position-resolved gradient comparisons improve rule-support retrieval, while the controls limit interpretation as attribution to particular training examples. The changes improve that argument without expanding its scope or obscuring its contribution.

Section 6.1 now appropriately describes similar observed gains on development and additional rules, rather than equality or general independence from development choices. The explicit limitations—two data orders, one initialization, shared rule grammar, and author-trained rather than official adapters—match the evidence. Section 8 likewise correctly treats the count-matched result as consistent with a count-confounded interpretation, while acknowledging population selection and uncontrolled surface differences. These changes address the prior review's scientific qualifications.

Archive N resolves the previously identified portability gap. Its secondary score matrices, document counts and checker work with the two Kronecker matrices from archive J. Running the inspected checker retains 786 documents and recovers matched membership AUCs of approximately $0.473$ and $0.506$ for the Kronecker scores, $0.534$ and $0.527$ for activation cosine and dot scores, and $0.537$ for output-gradient cosine. The latter is about $0.001$ below the manuscript's $0.538$; the reproduced intervals also agree to approximately $0.001$, consistent with the documented float16 release. Separate checks confirm identical document/query axes and membership labels across the five matrices. This verifies aggregation of the released evidence, including the previously unavailable secondary rows; it does not independently reproduce model inference or validate the supplied counts against source texts.

One minor wording refinement remains in Section 8: rather than saying the positive effects have intervals “touching or near 0.5,” explicitly distinguish the activation-dot interval, which includes $0.5$, from the activation-cosine and output-gradient intervals, which narrowly exclude it. These are small positive effects under the stated bootstrap, not evidence of exact chance performance. The numerical intervals already make this clear, so this wording issue does not warrant rejection.
