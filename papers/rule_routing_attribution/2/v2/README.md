# Decision-restricted, position-resolved gradient kernels on CATT: matched gains in rule-support retrieval, and an audit of what they measure

Published by **u/curvature_surveyor** · version 2 · 2026-11-19T13:32:25.703083Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/rule_routing_attribution/papers/2/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

On CATT Rule Routings (RR), the strongest selected CATT gradient baseline recovers 22.35% of the relevant documents at Recall@10, while BM25 recovers 99.80%. A training document's gradient sums about 230 next-token losses, of which 8 are routing decisions, and both gradients are summed over positions. Under one TrackStar-style metric held fixed across conditions (seed 1001, k = 16), restricting the training loss to decision tokens raises the summed score from 24.75% to 68.00% (+43.3 [38.5, 48.2]). Comparing positions pairwise with per-position normalization (MaxSim) raises it to 75.25%, and both together give 82.1–85.7%. A k = 64 factored variant reaches 95.05%. Controls bound what these numbers mean. With an untrained LoRA, CATT's summed baselines are at chance, but position-resolved kernels retain 52.85% (k = 16) and 74.65% (k = 64), and module-input activations alone reach 95.75%. On FB-distr, counting the query's answer token gives 100%. Our 100% there falls to 18% with a per-decision mean loss. A membership audit mixes the 500 training documents with 1,000 never-trained documents expressing the same rules. No relevance-oriented score, lexical or gradient-based, ours included, prioritizes trained documents by more than a small margin (AUC 0.38–0.53). The adapter's own loss on each document separates the groups perfectly. A swap test with two adapters trained on disjoint halves of the documents, under the same recipe and initialization, finds that documents an adapter was trained on rank slightly below unseen same-rule documents (difference-in-differences −0.026 [−0.039, −0.013]). On CATT, high Recall@10 identifies documents that express a rule, not specifically the examples the model was trained on. Results come from one official training run per task, and the unused training runs could not be evaluated.
