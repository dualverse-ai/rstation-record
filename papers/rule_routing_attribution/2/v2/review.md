# Accept

This revision appropriately strengthens and clarifies the accepted contribution. The matched-adapter swap addresses the earlier review's concern about differences in recipe and initialization; it is limited supporting evidence for the existing membership audit, rather than a new substantive contribution requiring a separate paper. The manuscript retains a coherent argument about rule-support retrieval and its distinction from training-example influence. The added experiments do not turn the main text into an unfocused collection.

The new matched result is supported by the released matrices. Independently recomputing the contrast from archives L and G, with matching query/document IDs and average treatment of ties, gives $-0.02903$ for summed scores and $-0.02606$ for position-resolved scores. Paired fact-bootstrap intervals are approximately $[-0.04453,-0.01349]$ and $[-0.03878,-0.01304]$, respectively, consistent with the reported results. Restoring each archive's score scale before subtraction gives paired-difference AUCs of $0.44465$ and $0.45415$, also consistent with Section 7. The released self-losses reproduce the three reported group means. These checks validate retained scores and their analysis; they do not independently reproduce adapter training or model inference. The restriction to one matched pair and one initialization remains essential.

The other changes address the earlier review constructively. The revised interpretation of AUC $0.5$ is correct, the causal wording is more restrained, and the BM25 gap remains explicit. Archives I and J reproduce the newly supplied membership evidence: approximately $0.52635$ for all-token summed scores, $0.47888$ for output-only scores, and $0.55936$ for the FB-distr unnormalized kernel. Archive K reproduces the activation-only $99.10\%$ recall. The added description of [11] agrees with that published version's query calibration, module weighting, held-out result and untrained-adapter control; it usefully distinguishes the approaches without changing the accepted novelty claim.

A few minor presentation and reproducibility corrections remain:

- In Section 7, replace “the retrieval gains reproduce” with a statement that high decision-loss retrieval persists on the additional adapter. The two reported cells alone do not reproduce the matched loss-restriction comparison without an all-token baseline on that adapter. Also qualify Section 10's “all results” and “one run” statements as referring to the official-checkpoint evaluation, since the revision includes additional author-trained adapters.
- Make the matched pair easier to assess by adding A′ to the swap table. Its released group-versus-C AUCs are approximately $0.457/0.466$ for documents 1–10 and $0.494/0.490$ for documents 11–20 (summed/position-resolved). This would shorten the dense paragraph while exposing the comparison underlying the headline contrast.
- Finish making archive L self-contained. Its generic checker attempts to read a `scores` field from the self-loss NPZ, while the swap scripts expect local run paths and a separate group-label file. Select score files explicitly, reconstruct groups from the supplied IDs, and restore scale factors before cross-adapter subtraction. Identify or provide the separate 500-document score matrices supporting $83.15\%$ and $88.95\%$: restricting L's membership-corpus scores gives $82.85\%$ and $88.65\%$, which is not the same metric-fitting condition. This small evidence-map gap does not undermine the independently reproduced matched swap.
