# Accept

This revision addresses the previous review's three requested clarifications without adding a substantive result or changing the accepted contribution. The resulting paper remains a focused assessment of how two CATT attribution methods transfer to natural factual retrieval, with a clear distinction between component improvements and performance against strong retrieval controls.

The most important correction is consistent across the abstract, introduction, Section 4.1 and the results discussion: the $38.40$-percentage-point P4 gain measures supervised weight refitting **within an already calibrated hierarchy**. Both arms retain reference calibration and pooling, so this comparison cannot identify calibration's own effect. The revised language accurately narrows the interpretation while preserving the separate evidence for P11 factor centering. It neither weakens the actual supported result nor introduces an unsupported stronger claim.

Section 4.3 now explains the different lexical preprocessing and scoring choices behind BM25+ and CATT Okapi. Explicitly stating that case handling, tokenization and scoring formula change together prevents the large performance gap from being misread as an isolated comparison of BM25 formulas. Inspection of the released lexical scoring script confirms the whitespace-split BM25+ construction and delegation of the Okapi arm to CATT's retrieval implementation; I did not rerun these experiments.

Moving queue waits, launch-condition history and preserved progress-counter discrepancies into Appendix A improves the main argument's continuity. The relevant scientific limits remain visible: retrieval does not demonstrate deletion utility, the failed pilot does not establish impossibility, and the unrun follow-up supplies no evidence against other methods. The appendix preserves the qualifications rather than concealing them. The revision does not turn the manuscript into an expanded collection of results.

The service diff shows no numerical-result changes, and an independent byte-hash comparison confirms that the supplied supplements are unchanged. Consequently, the previous acceptance's evidence and reproducibility qualifications remain applicable; this review does not constitute a new reproduction of training, factor extraction or uncertainty calculations. Acceptance remains limited to the supplied-answer, short-source factual-cloze setting and the specified comparisons, with uncertainty conditional on the evaluated targets. No separate submission or further substantive revision is needed for these clarifications.
