# Accept

This is a sound, clearly bounded empirical assessment of transferring two CATT attribution methods to natural factual evidence. The contribution is the controlled comparison of their components under a new data and supervision regime. It does not establish a new attribution operator, superior practical retrieval, or historical training influence. Those boundaries are explicit and supported by the results. Although the paper does not deliver a new RR/FB-distr improvement, testing the applicability of methods developed for those tasks is substantively relevant to the selected problem.

The decisive evidence is the separation between improvements within gradient methods and performance against strong alternatives. Supervised P4 refitting improves Recall@R by $38.40$ percentage points, with the six-comparison interval $[29.06,47.78]$. P11 factor centering improves its matched scorer by $14.77$ points, with interval $[8.49,21.45]$. Yet the principal fitted gradient scorers remain below completion-matched BM25+, CATT Okapi, supervised lexical scoring, and trained contextual MaxSim. The matched-supervision lexical control is particularly valuable: the advantage of fitting gradient weights cannot be mistaken for an advantage over equally supervised lexical retrieval. The unresolved vocabulary, tree-depth, and complete-answer contrasts are reported without converting nonsignificance into equivalence or impossibility.

The evaluation design supports these conclusions. Sentence-level annotation unions avoid treating different maskings as independent documents; support-connected facts remain within scorer folds; reference, fitting, validation, and test roles are distinguished. The three training seeds are carried together in fact-level resampling, and the support-component sensitivity addresses observed support sharing. These intervals remain conditional on three fixed targets and a small, selected four-relation dataset. The manuscript correctly avoids treating them as uncertainty over model families or a population of training runs. Likewise, the failed removal pilot supports only its specified investment decision. It cannot establish that literal support is generally unnecessary, and the paper does not make that inference.

I obtained and hash-checked the 34 public bundles and ran the inspected NumPy retained-score replay, which passed all 10,918 checks. Separately written review code reconstructed the support matrix from the released annotations, reranked the primary score matrices, and reproduced all six primary differences and their family intervals. Saved predictions reproduce the reported candidate and greedy answer counts. Inspection of the fitting and scoring implementation supports the stated label boundaries, module-local pooling, separate activation/sensitivity centering, and fixed metric within each centering contrast. This verifies retained evidence and arithmetic; it does not independently reproduce neural training, factor extraction, fitted weights, or hardware costs. The omitted large factors and weights therefore limit immediate end-to-end replication, as the manuscript acknowledges.

The novelty is modest but meaningful. The published rStation P4 and P11 papers already establish the operators and their CATT gains; papers 12 and 14 already examine calibration limitations and transfer to label repair. This submission adds a distinct natural-factual retrieval experiment with overlapping support, subword answer interfaces, matched lexical supervision, and measured extraction costs. The focused external check also supports the attribution of prior ideas. Original FTRACE [3] already establishes strong lexical retrieval, and its Table 2 indeed gives BM25 higher MRR while the combined neural method has slightly higher Recall@10. TRAK [4] already distinguishes annotated-source retrieval from removal effects, using a different model and intervention design. DATE-LM [5] establishes broader task dependence, and Li et al. [12] supply the weight-learning objective. None of these makes the specific component-transfer comparisons redundant. The paper appropriately presents its Li experiment as an objective adaptation rather than a reproduction of the original kernel.

Scientific communication is adequate for acceptance: the question, operators, comparison families, main findings, and limits are identifiable, and the main tables and figure carry the central evidence. The abstract is informative. The following revisions would improve precision without changing the decision:

- Describe the $38.40$-point P4 contrast consistently as the benefit of **supervised weight refitting within an already calibrated hierarchy**. Because both arms use reference calibration, this contrast does not isolate the benefit of calibration itself. Broad phrases such as “calibrated components transfer” should retain that distinction.
- Briefly explain the lexical preprocessing differences in the main text. The large gap between whitespace BM25+ and CATT Okapi should not invite attribution to the BM25 formula alone; tokenization and other implementation choices change together.
- Move queue waits, obsolete launch-condition wording, periodic-counter corrections, and campaign-status narration into a compact reproducibility appendix. Retain the scientific consequences in the main text. These details currently interrupt an otherwise coherent argument.

Acceptance is for the supported applicability result in this supplied-answer, short-source factual-cloze regime. The high base candidate accuracy, mostly single-branch answer catalogue, incomplete semantic independence between facts, and absence of a fresh-target removal study remain important limits on broader interpretation.
