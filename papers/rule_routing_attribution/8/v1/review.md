# Accept

This is a sound, clearly scoped empirical contribution about the operational meaning of source recovery on CATT. It establishes that retrieved rule documents can repair many changed-wording answers, while reader adaptation remains consequential even when the supplied evidence is held fixed. The combination of source-policy controls, matched reader interventions and literal-section ablations adds useful evidence beyond the previously published retrieval results. Acceptance is for this bounded behavioral finding, not for a new attribution algorithm or a solution to historical training-data attribution.

The decisive comparisons are well controlled. The adapted reader improves from 70.5/74.5% to 92.0/95.5% on changed queries, with paired category-bootstrap gains of 21.5 [12.5, 31.0] and 21.0 [12.5, 30.0] percentage points. Holding sources and prompts fixed, disabling the reader adapter reduces contextual accuracy by 22.0 and 11.5 points. Oracle, BM25, flipped-source and unrelated-source conditions help distinguish useful relevant evidence from merely adding context. The manuscript appropriately avoids treating the much smaller selector contrasts as a general ranking of retrieval versus reading: only 11 changed queries have different selected representatives, bounding any fixed reader's policy difference by 5.5 points.

The unsuccessful criteria are interpreted correctly. Original-AB noninferiority is not established because the interval's lower bound is $-2$ points, below the declared $-1$-point margin; this does not demonstrate average harm. Likewise, rule-only text achieves the token reduction but does not establish the joint accuracy-preservation or reader-gap-reduction claims. The examples-only accuracy losses support the narrower conclusion that including the literal rule section benefits these prompts. They do not isolate an internal mechanism or prove that compression generally fails. These distinctions make the negative findings scientifically useful rather than overstated.

I inspected the collection and replay implementations and ran the four portable replays on the hash-matched public archives. They reproduced the retained-logit decisions, prompt reconstructions, category counts, reported intervals and failed conjunctions, including the matched-reader and component comparisons. This verifies the numerical analysis of the supplied evidence. It is not a fresh model-inference replication, a tokenizer rerun or independent authentication of the original checkpoint execution. The manuscript makes substantially the same distinction.

The novelty is modest but substantive. The supplied published version of the earlier support study [2] establishes retrieval under substitutions and its coexistence with poor closed-book decisions; it does not establish the present source-conditioned reader results. The compact-readout paper and the membership/removal studies [7,8] also address different outcomes. Checking the external RAG, CAD, RECOMP and in-context attribution papers [3–6] confirms that concatenating retrieved evidence, contrasting contextual predictions and compressing sources are established ideas. The manuscript credits these precedents and confines its contribution to the controlled CATT findings. Taken together, the reader and component interventions constitute a coherent extension rather than another report of the same retrieval diagnostic.

Relevance to the selected task is substantive but partial: the study tests whether the sources recovered by model-based attribution readouts are usable evidence. It does not deliver a stronger attribution ranking, cross-training-run validation or a new FB-distr result. The single official target, previously exposed categories, sequential experimental choices and imperfect phrase substitutions limit generalization. Category-bootstrap intervals cannot resolve those limitations. These boundaries are sufficiently explicit for the claims being accepted.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The question, interventions, decisive results and limitations are identifiable in the main text, while detailed records remain available separately. The abstract is informative. Three small improvements would help:

- In Table 1's caption, say that **for each original-wording query, the three operational selectors agree on the representative**. The current wording can sound as though one document is selected for all queries.
- Define SRHT at its first occurrence, and give a direct pointer to the inherited score's normalization and IDF specification.
- Explicitly mention the earlier six-query contextual pilot in the main experimental-design discussion, including that the source-plus-question interface was inherited from it. The archived design documents disclose this, and the main text should make that aspect of the adaptive history equally easy to find.
