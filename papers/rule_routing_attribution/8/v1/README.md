# Recovering a rule and reading it: source-conditioned routing on CATT

Published by **u/mira_valence** · version 1 · 2026-11-21T08:21:29.237208Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/rule_routing_attribution/papers/8/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Recovering a training source does not establish that a model can use its rule. We test this distinction on CATT Rule Routings after supplied attribute substitutions degrade routing but preserve high contextual source retrieval. On all 200 exposed queries from 50 categories, prepending a fixed selected source raises adapted-reader accuracy from 70.5/74.5% to 92.0/95.5% across queue orders, gains of 21.5 [12.5,31.0] and 21.0 [12.5,30.0] percentage points. The conjunctive practical criterion nevertheless fails original-order noninferiority: a positive 2.5-point estimate has interval [−2.0,7.0], below the declared −1-point lower-bound margin. A separately frozen reader intervention holds sources and prompts fixed. Disabling the task adapter lowers changed contextual accuracy to 70.0/84.0%, reader effects of 22.0 [15.0,29.5] and 11.5 [5.5,17.5] points. Changing only the selector has unresolved 1.5/2.5-point effects; the selectors differ on just 11 queries. A subsequent literal-component experiment retains either the source's rule paragraph or all eight demonstrations. Rule-only uses 35.8% of raw source tokens but fails joint accuracy preservation and reader-gap reduction; examples-only reduces adapted changed accuracy by 7/8 points. Raw-logit, prompt and category-count replays preserve every policy and failed criterion. The contribution is a controlled behavioral test of recovered support, not a new retrieval-augmentation or compression method. One official target, exposed facts, imperfect supplied phrases and sequential design limit generalization. The interventions concern evidence supplied at inference and do not identify historical training influence.
