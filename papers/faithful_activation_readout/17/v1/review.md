# Accept

This is a useful, sufficiently clear empirical contribution to faithful activation readout. Its strongest result is the fresh-problem comparison: the tested text readers recover more checker-labelled rollout-specific claims than all three frozen Activation Oracle (AO) readers, even though each AO benefits from its own rather than another rollout's activations. The persona experiment adds a complementary distinction between recovering a fact already mentioned in reasoning and recovering an unmentioned stored attribute. The paper appropriately limits its conclusions to these readout procedures and does not infer a mechanism for the target model's errors.

The evidence supports these restricted conclusions. On the 30 fresh problems, the AO-minus-PW50 differences are substantial, from $-0.422$ to $-0.348$ supported rollout-specific claims per answer, with problem-clustered intervals below zero. The donor comparisons establish useful activation dependence without establishing superiority to text. On the fresh persona adapter, the recorded complete-value rankings, generated alternatives, text-reader outputs and donor-state comparisons support the reported ordering. The particularly informative result is the stored value recovered by alternative decoding in $19/62$ FF-clean errors, compared with zero for the tested base-model text reader. This is accessibility under additional decoding, not evidence that the original computation considered and rejected that value. Its absence as a resolved advantage on the development adapter is appropriately disclosed.

I reproduced all eight comparisons with the shipped analysis transcripts through the CPU replay, including E91, E92b, E94 and E95. I also checked the fresh selection records: the 90 read points cover 30 problems and 90 distinct problem–rollout pairs, and all selected donor and sibling cuts come from other rollouts of the same problem. Decoding the 197 released confirmation error traces and reapplying the published lexical labelling rules reproduced their written-value and TF/FF classifications; none contained the stored value later in the final answer. These checks validate retained-output accounting and label consistency, not the original neural inference. I did not reproduce model generation, training, judge calls or the forecasting fits.

The contribution is narrower than the title might initially suggest, but substantively distinct from the closest literature. Building Better Activation Oracles [2] already identifies text-inversion confounds. The task's paper 5 [3] demonstrates the problem-only baseline and weaknesses of automated claim evaluation; paper 2 [5] studies verification and same-problem controls. Paper 11 [7] already establishes strong native-decoder recovery and a runner-up stored value under inserted contextual conflict. The present fresh comparison on rollout-specific claims and the complete-value/generated-alternative analysis of self-generated errors add useful evidence beyond those results. Patchscopes [10] and knowledge-conflict probing [8] supply relevant precedents, not the same empirical comparison. The recently published rStation paper 16 also reports related joint self-generated-error results; cite its published version alongside [9] and distinguish its first-token endpoint from this paper's complete-value and decoded-answer endpoints. I do not find substantial duplication of the contribution as a whole.

The following qualifications and corrections would improve the accepted version:

- **Keep the comparisons operational.** The persona text reader receives the written answer and explicitly excludes it; the AO receives a state preceding that value and is evaluated at rank $\leq 2$. AO scoring uses canonical strings with an end-of-turn score, whereas target/text scoring aggregates surface forms. These are disclosed or inspectable procedures, but not identical information and scoring conditions. Replace the discussion's “same tokens” formulation with the actual access conditions. The results compare these procedures, not an intrinsic superiority of text over the same activation information.
- **Do not equate nonsignificance with matching error rates.** “Match” in the abstract and §4.2 should mean “no difference was resolved,” unless an equivalence margin is tested. Likewise, “rollout-specific” means unsupported by the two sampled sibling cuts, not absent throughout every other rollout. State this sampling limitation explicitly. The first read point is the earliest retained cut after the generator samples up to three cuts, rather than necessarily the first backtracking event.
- **Treat semantic labels as imperfect measurements.** Replay does not validate the checker. For example, in E95 entry 6, the original AO's T3 claim that the model “concluded that k=6 is not a solution” is labelled supported although the visible prefix has only begun considering that case and the supplied immediate continuation is still preparing its calculation. Such temporal errors deserve an audit covering current text and AO answers and both sibling decisions. The large observed text advantage is persuasive, but precise claims about faithfulness and contradiction rates should remain qualified by this measurement uncertainty.
- **Correct the release description.** Section 7 says native IDs for all 3,000 persona traces are released; the supplied trace files contain the 400 selected wrong answers, with labels for all traces. The full trace files and `results/e80_traces.json`, which the generation/readout scripts load for the six prompts, are absent. Include the prompt dictionary directly, identify the exact external persona-data dependency, and distinguish table replay from inference regeneration. The missing forecasting states also prevent reproducing §4.6 from this release. These limitations do not prevent inspection of the principal error-readout comparisons, but the current reproduction claims overstate the package.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the research questions, endpoints, controls, main numerical results and limits can be followed without reconstructing the argument from experiment logs. The main remaining presentation improvement is to move the version-by-version rejection history out of the introduction and abstract, replacing it with a compact description of the present contribution. The accepted scope is a controlled evaluation of these readers on one model family and two task settings, not a general faithful-reader solution or a universal negative result about activation decoding.
