# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated partial advance on faithful activation readout. The contribution is a controlled method for jointly recovering complete source records and forecasting the subject model's actual continuation, together with evidence that strong aggregate recovery can conceal poor prediction of departures from those records. The manuscript supports this restricted claim without presenting source reconstruction as a causal explanation or claiming to solve naturalistic error diagnosis.

The decisive positive evidence is the independently frozen ledger evaluation on 64 new factual groups, with three fitted seeds for each of two subject models. Complete four-claim reports average 89.23% for Qwen3-8B and 82.62% for Qwen3-1.7B. The respective gains over direct field classification are 75.72 percentage points, with crossed group/seed 95% interval [67.97, 82.91], and 44.79 points [23.63, 69.05]. Contradictions also decrease against that comparator. Both readers receive the same activation allocation, and mismatched-state and training-mean controls support dependence on the corresponding activations. Group-level uncertainty, retained adverse seeds, and the paired second model make this substantially stronger evidence than an exposed development demonstration. The second model supplies another fitted-model test, not another 64 independent factual groups.

The scientific significance lies in the joint evaluation and its component failures, rather than a new copying mechanism. The structured package adds alignment supervision, a public ordering grammar, shared content classifiers, and a behavior gate. Consequently, its comparison with direct fields does not isolate an architectural cause. The manuscript explains this distinction adequately. Its reduced inventory task is substantively relevant to the supplied problem: it tests complete owner–amount–unit claims, reports unsupported assertions alongside useful detail, and distinguishes source facts from actual behavior. New AObench or PersonaQA results are not necessary for accepting this explicitly narrower advance.

The novelty claim survives comparison with the closest published work, but should remain narrow. The same-problem resolved-reference and complete-numerical studies [17,16] already establish useful structured factual recovery and prospective numerical prediction, including intervention failures. The continuation studies [14,15] and LatentQA [3] already address forecasting from activations. Feng and Steinhardt [7] address causal binding representations, while *Legible Failures* [10] already demonstrates recoverable correct bindings when a model answers incorrectly. The present paper adds a joint complete-record and literal-future-tuple endpoint, a tested structured reader package, and paired-model evidence separating complete-report utility from exception recovery. This is a meaningful extension of those results, not publication precedence for the general observation that readable source information can coexist with erroneous behavior. Attribution and the stated distinctions are appropriate.

The negative evidence is central to the acceptance judgment, because it prevents the favorable aggregate from becoming an unsupported diagnostic claim. On 8B, the structured seeds recover only 16/29/8 of 285 outputs outside both source records; on 1.7B, they recover 121/120/181 of 1,023, fewer than direct fields. Every 1.7B exception is induced by the swap, and 1.7B source reconstruction also degrades markedly on JSON/tool presentations. These observations establish a supported failure mode of this method on this task, not a universal limit on activation forecasting. The manuscript correctly retains unfamiliar and schema-unresolved outputs, distinguishes spontaneous task errors from intervention outcomes, and reports the unfavorable completeness/contradiction tradeoff against the fixed 1.7B AO. Its unequal activation access precludes interpreting that AO comparison as an isolated architecture comparison.

I inspected the scoring and relevant reader/intervention code and ran both main public saved-output replays. Both passed: each reconstructed the 294,912-key forecast commitment and recomputed the primary and semantic-audit accounting and group/seed contrasts, matching the retained results within the replay's $10^{-12}$ numerical tolerance. This verifies consistency of the released predictions, references, and analysis. It does not reproduce neural training or generation, independently validate author-agent annotations, or authenticate the historical ordering of commitments and outcomes. Omitted weights, activation caches, and full tokenized prompts limit direct neural replication; the manuscript accurately distinguishes its portable accounting release from regeneration. The disclosed 8B runtime amendment also means the study must continue to be described as amended prospective evaluation. Retention of failed outcomes and unchanged forecasts makes the amendment assessable rather than hidden.

Clarity meets the acceptance requirement independently of these correctness checks. The four claims, their references, the structured decoder, and the interpretation of failures are understandable to a reader in the field. The figures help distinguish complete-report gains from exception failures, and the abstract is informative. Minor improvements would be to move detailed audit counts and timestamps into an appendix, add a compact worked example showing the gate selecting an incorrect source-consistent forecast, and consolidate the main access/supervision differences into one comparison table. Matched-supervision ablations and a broader unpatched-error population would be valuable follow-up work, but are not prerequisites for the bounded conclusions actually made here. Three seeds, ten wholly new prefix groups, and pointwise intervals should remain explicit qualifications throughout any summary of generalization.
