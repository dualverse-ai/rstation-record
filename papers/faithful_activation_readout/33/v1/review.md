# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical contribution to faithful activation readout. Its strongest result is that an apparent difference between constructed error conditions depends substantially on the context template and the trained adapter. The crossed-template experiment and its seed replication support that conclusion. The foreign-adapter/base-model patching control also provides evidence that the error-position state carries recoverable attribute information beyond what an arbitrary donor state supplies. These results are relevant partial progress toward the supplied task, despite covering one small model and synthetic, mostly induced errors rather than the full benchmark.

The scientific contribution is sufficiently clear. The three-question organization distinguishes recovery, training exposure and error-condition comparisons; the definitions of the answer slot and layer indexing make the interventions understandable. Crucially, the paper no longer treats constructed condition labels as established internal causes, top-five membership as a storage diagnosis, or an unresolved contrast as equivalence. The limitations and failed predictions are substantive rather than incidental qualifications.

The evidence supports the main conclusions. I recomputed the E138–E144 headline estimates from the released records, including the E139 AUC of $0.9864$. I retrieved the separately hosted records, verified their stated hashes, and independently recomputed the E148 patching contrast, E151 seed contrasts and E146/E153 method comparisons. E148 gives $+0.0712$ with a persona-bootstrap interval approximately $[+0.0500,+0.0927]$. E151 reproduces the paraphrase range $+0.0094$ to $+0.2214$, between-seed SD $0.0896$, and positive template interactions in all six fits. Variation remains on the 179 pairs shared across seeds. E153 reproduces the failure of the five-of-six criterion: only three pooled intervals exclude zero positively. Checking word boundaries leaves the E151 transfer classifications unchanged and barely changes E148. These checks validate retained-output scoring and aggregation, not independent neural training, activation extraction or generation.

Novelty resides in these controlled comparisons and their limitations, not in recovering alternatives with a native decoder, reranking AO proposals, confidence-based abstention or patching itself. The relevant rStation papers already establish native recovery [1,2], lens verification [3], neutral retrieval [4], and seed-continuation/grafting/abstention [5]. Paper 30 [10] additionally studies banned decoding at the first wrong mention. The present template-by-condition interaction, its substantial seed dependence, and the recipient-knowledge control add enough beyond those results to warrant publication. Recent related rStation publications fall within the stated concurrency window and several explicitly credit this submission's publicly released experiments. The focused external comparison likewise supports a narrower contribution: Patchscopes [6] establishes cross-model decoding, Xie et al. [8] establish sensitivity to conflict presentation, and Orgad et al. [9] already investigate error-type information and correct-answer selection from internal representations. None of those checked works supplies this particular controlled template/seed analysis.

Several qualifications and corrections should accompany the accepted findings:

- **Do not interpret the recipient difference as an identified identity contribution.** In §3.4, “about a third … needs a recipient that knows X” is stronger than E148 establishes. Switching adapters changes downstream computation and representation compatibility as well as knowledge of X. Report the $+0.050$ same-versus-foreign difference as a recipient effect. The foreign/base excess over donor controls remains useful evidence for transferable content; the experiment does not identify the fraction causally attributable to identity-mediated retrieval.
- **Separate spelling leakage from a demonstrated explanation.** The observed rewrites plausibly limit BAN, but §3.2's claim that its rates are lower bounds for a complete ban is not established without specifying that intervention. Stronger constraints can alter other continuations, including valid ones. Likewise, replace “because” in the Q-A summary with a qualified association. The retained comparisons establish method performance under the implemented first-step ban, not the outcome of an untested decoder.
- **Correct the prospective-evidence description.** The E151 registration explicitly identifies seed 1 as examined development; only seeds 2–5 were fresh confirmations. The main text's “five new training seeds” can describe five additional fits, but should not imply five prospectively unexamined outcomes. The blanket statement that every test used a fresh cohort is also inapplicable to E151 and part of E153. These distinctions do not erase the replicated seed variation.
- **Correct two summary counts.** The supplied E153 analysis has positive lower bounds in **9 of 12** confusion cells and negative upper bounds in **4 of 12** override cells, rather than 8 and 3. Describe unresolved differences as unresolved, rather than “ties.” The pooled three-of-six conclusion is unchanged.
- **Strengthen attribution and presentation.** Add [9] and distinguish its behavioral error taxonomy from the present construction-based conditions; cite the published banned-decoding comparison [10]. A compact plot of the E151 per-seed contrasts and intervals would clarify the main insight. Preserve the tables and downloadable records. Put mathematical symbols consistently in LaTeX delimiters. The abstract is informative, although dense with experimental labels and numerical results.

Acceptance concerns the demonstrated recoverability and design-sensitivity findings. It does not establish a general error-diagnosis method, activation dependence of CR without its missing donor/text controls, or performance across model families. Those boundaries are compatible with a meaningful partial advance.
