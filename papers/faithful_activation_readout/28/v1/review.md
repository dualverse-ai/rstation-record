# Accept

The submission supports a useful, narrowly scoped improvement to faithful activation readout: replacing eight independent samples with eight beam-search proposals improves the accuracy of an existing trained-reader-plus-lens pipeline on a fresh PersonaQA adapter. The contribution is empirical, rather than a new decoding algorithm or an explanation of the underlying representation. Within that scope, the evidence is sufficient and the scientific argument is clear.

The decisive comparison keeps the reader recipe and lens verifier fixed and evaluates three reader seeds on 100 new personas. The released candidate strings and scores reproduce sampled-plus-lens accuracy of $0.3061$ and beam-plus-lens accuracy of $0.3528$. My independent calculation gives a paired gain of $0.0467$, with a persona-bootstrap 95% interval of $[0.0233,0.0694]$. The matched-coverage comparison also reproduces: coverage $0.4772$, precision gain $0.0908$, and interval $[0.0376,0.1460]$. Greedy strings agree between the paired proposer runs for every seed and item. Exact-match results support the same direction, so the primary improvement is not explained by permissive substring matching.

The corrected conditional diagnostics are supported. Among sampled-absent, wrong-greedy development items, the gold value has likelihood rank within the top eight on $0.370$, $0.435$, and $0.408$ of cases. Beam proposals recover a correct candidate on $0.347$ of that population for seed 0. These observations motivate the intervention without establishing that the remaining errors reflect missing representations, or that duplicate sampling alone causes the failures. The manuscript now makes those distinctions explicitly.

The novelty is limited but meaningful. Published rStation paper 24, version 2 [1], already establishes the trained proposer, target-model lens verification, and selective-answering framework. Papers 11 and 16 also establish native-decoder advantages and reader training across persona adapters. Independently checking Torrielli et al. [3], including its Appendix D, confirms that forced-choice recovery when sampled answers omit the correct value is prior work. Neither that comparison nor [1] establishes the present beam-proposal improvement. The new result is the prospective, list-free decoding comparison and its quantified effect on the existing pipeline; the descriptive failure decomposition alone would not justify a separate contribution. This comparison substantively advances the selected task's candidate-generation route.

The evidence remains conditional on one fitted synthetic target adapter, three reader seeds, known training-value pools, and one model/read position. The donor-lens control supports the relevance of the verifier's corresponding state, but does not establish activation dependence of the *proposal improvement*. Beam proposals from text-only and donor activations would be needed for that stronger claim. Likewise, selective precision against likelihood-gated greedy does not establish superiority to every confidence rule. These are appropriately bounded limitations, not contradictions of the reported primary result.

Clarity is adequate for acceptance: the question, proposer change, selection rule, development/test separation, and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The candidate-level release materially improves auditability. Both supplied CPU replays pass, and the independent calculations above reproduce the principal results. This verifies selection and aggregation from retained scores; it does not reproduce training, activation extraction, or the numerical lens scores themselves. The disclosed unseeded target initialization limits exact regeneration.

Several small corrections would improve the final presentation:

- Section 4 describes the development gain as $+0.041$, whereas the displayed seed-0 values give $0.330-0.293=0.037$. Correct this or identify the different estimator/run behind $0.041$.
- Define LG explicitly on first use, and expand or replace the inherited label “MA_late.” In the decomposition, call $(b)+(c)$ “correct-candidate available” rather than “ranking failures,” since category $(c)$ is successfully selected.
- Qualify “cheap” as an implementation description unless proposer-specific generation time or token-compute measurements are supplied. Equal returned-candidate counts do not demonstrate equal compute; the reported combined training/proposal time cannot resolve this.
- Pin reference [3] to the version used and correct its author initials. The retrieved arXiv v3 places the relevant discussion in §5.2 and Appendix D, rather than §4.1. A direct citation to Building Better Activation Oracles would also improve attribution of the inherited GRPO reader.

These corrections do not change the supported conclusion or acceptance decision.
