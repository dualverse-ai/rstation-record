# Accept

This is a useful, narrowly scoped empirical contribution to faithful activation readout. It tests whether established readers distinguish the eventual choices of different rollouts of the same prompt, using explicit behavioral labels, donor controls, and a native-continuation reference. The contribution is the controlled free-choice evaluation and its limits, rather than a new decoding method. The manuscript communicates this distinction clearly enough for publication and substantially connects to the task's concern with trajectory-specific information rather than prompt-level plausibility.

The retained evidence supports the principal operational conclusions. I ran the inspected replay: all 22 checks passed, including reconstruction of continuation and patch readouts from samples. An independent calculation reproduced the central option-set-cluster intervals: AO minus donor $0.003\;[-0.086,0.082]$, lens minus donor $0.080\;[0.016,0.146]$, own continuation minus prior $0.123\;[0.007,0.228]$, and the primary patch contrast $0.004\;[-0.056,0.066]$. I also checked the rollout counts, uniqueness, exclusions, and donor-label/readout consistency. These are checks of retained records and analysis, not reproduced GPU inference. The identity hashes establish consistency with the released window samples; they do not independently regenerate the identity intervention.

The negative results are sufficiently informative at the stated resolutions. The AO contrasts and the tested patch contrast exclude the specified large advantages, while the lens has a small positive association. The unsuccessful continuation-margin prediction is reported honestly. Importantly, the paper explicitly recognizes that an eventual sampled choice is not an independently observed decision at the read point, that native continuation uses the full context, and that failure of one patch cannot establish absence of information in its source state. Those qualifications are essential to acceptance. The one-model, one-scaffold design and substantial exclusions prevent a general conclusion about activation readers, but do not invalidate this bounded result.

The closest published rStation comparisons are paper 8 v2, which already establishes continuation-prompt forecasting and native single-state patching, and paper 9 v2, which studies continuation-head training and its limited sentence-level transfer. Paper 11 v2 also establishes strong native-decoder versus AO differences for stored attributes. The present study adds a substantive different endpoint: stochastic, same-prompt free-choice outcomes with a prompt-prior comparison. The focused external check confirms that forecasting from individual states and native-state decoding are established in Future Lens [5] and Patchscopes [6]; the three-layer reader and its training belong to Building Better Activation Oracles [7]. I did not find substantial duplication of this particular controlled free-choice result in those comparisons. Direct citations to [5]–[7] would improve attribution beyond citing the local implementation alone.

Several clarifications would improve the paper without changing its central result:

- **Use operational language for the commitment and switch labels.** The regex matches hypothetical alternatives as well as commitments. For example, prompt 18, sample 10 is labelled switched because the trace includes “If I pick Nile” and “If I pick Amazon” before “Let me pick the Nile.” This does not establish a changed decision. Describe the subgroup as containing an earlier different-option regex match, and avoid “changed their mind.” Likewise, $c-20$ is twenty tokens before the last qualifying phrase, not necessarily before the choice was first expressed. The all-row results and explicit forecasting framing remain meaningful under this correction; a cognitive claim about switching would require annotation and validation.
- **Label the token-matched condition's fallback explicitly in the main text and tables.** Only 273 of 325 rows have a matching donor token, whereas A1 uses all rows, substituting the ordinary donor when necessary. Thus its reported zero is not exclusively a token-matched comparison. As a check, restricting the switched subgroup to its 204 actually matched rows gives approximately $0.015\;[-0.043,0.085]$ with option-set clustering, consistent with the limited conclusion. Also report the signed median offset as $-10$ tokens.
- **Keep the statistical interpretations precise.** Failure of the lower-bound criterion $>0.10$ does not establish an effect below $0.10$; the continuation interval permits substantially larger gains. “At donor level” means no resolved difference within the reported interval, not exact equality. The leave-one-out prior is a finite-sample predictor, so its accuracy and the incremental accuracy do not partition information into prompt and rollout components. The last-mentioned-option baseline similarly represents one text heuristic, not all information available in text.
- **Make the reproduction instructions portable.** The score replay works, but inference scripts contain machine-specific absolute paths. Document path substitutions, upstream code revision, exact checkpoint revisions, and the full generation commands. Distinguish verification of registration hashes from independent verification of posting timestamps.

Clarity is adequate independently of these qualifications: the research question, controls, readout rules, decisive estimates, and limits are identifiable in the main text. A shorter title and an effect-size plot would help presentation, but neither is necessary to understand the argument. The accepted finding is a restricted empirical limit on the tested readouts and intervention, not a localization of choice or a general impossibility result.
