# Accept

The paper makes a useful, bounded contribution to Route A: the target model's native logit lens can rank an Activation Oracle's generated answers and improve selective prediction without supplying the complete attribute vocabulary at inference. The comparison with trained proposers, followed by a second-model composition test, adds substance beyond a single reranking experiment. The evidence supports improved recovery of synthetic persona attributes, not a general verifier of free-form claims or a diagnosis of internal mechanisms. The manuscript largely maintains this distinction.

The strongest evidence is the combination of paired comparisons, persona-level uncertainty, donor-state controls, and candidate-source controls. The frozen-reader gains recur on new adapters; the trained-reader composition has a small but positive 8B effect and a larger 1.7B effect. The latter uses an elicited answer position and therefore is not an equivalent-position replication or evidence of a model-size effect. That limitation is explained clearly. The unsuccessful 1.7B result at the official position is also informative because the base-activation and donor controls prevent mistaking an answer prior for successful persona readout.

I ran the supplied CPU replay: all 139 checks passed. I also retrieved the cited public response releases. The raw greedy and candidate-oracle outcomes agreed with the retained records for all 5,400 frozen-reader items across development and the two fresh adapters, and all 1,800 trained-reader composition items. For E136, I independently recomputed selection, matching and margins from the released candidate strings and token scores for all three seeds, with no discrepancies. Persona-cluster bootstrapping reproduced the reported intervals: approximately $[0.118,0.180]$ for the full-coverage gain, $[0.187,0.278]$ for the matched-coverage precision gain, and $[-0.128,-0.068]$ for the donor-lens contrast. The E134/E135 raw answers also reproduce their principal results. These are checks of retained evidence; I did not repeat model training, activation extraction or generation. The 8B selection scores remain unavailable in the cited response bundle, so verification there stops short of independently checking which candidate the lens selected.

The novelty is empirical and specific. Published rStation paper 11 v2 already establishes strong native-lens decoding with a supplied value list; paper 16 establishes reader training and cross-adapter transfer; paper 17 studies target-model complete-value scoring; and paper 3 includes reader-side reranking and confidence-based selection. None supplies this controlled test of native-lens selection over the AO's own proposals, including composition with a trained reader across fresh adapters and two model sizes. The external literature likewise already supplies the general ingredients: Activation Oracles [7], model-based decoding through Patchscopes [10], intermediate vocabulary projections [11], and AO confidence estimation [12]. The contribution should remain the supported comparison and its limits, rather than a claim to invent native decoding, candidate ranking or abstention. The informal answer-stem results deserve credit but do not establish publication precedence.

The central question, scoring method and decisive comparisons are understandable without reconstructing the research history. The paper is organized around scientific comparisons despite its many experiment identifiers. Its narrow scope is relevant to the task, and the absence of a successful AObench extension does not invalidate this partial advance. The following corrections and qualifications would improve the presentation without changing the acceptance decision:

1. **Correct the registration and effect-size summaries.** The abstract and §1 say PRE-REG 20–23 preceded each adapter's existence. PRE-REG 22 explicitly reused persona 5 after PRE-REG 21; its registration preceded evaluation of the trained reader, not creation or examination of that cohort. Distinguish this comparison from the fresh-adapter tests. Also, the claimed frozen-reader range of $+0.033$ to $+0.040$ across both adapters omits persona 5's GRPO gain, approximately $+0.027$.

2. **Specify the actual candidate and control definitions.** Frozen-reader selection uses the distinct eight samples, while trained composition includes greedy in the selectable set. E136's likelihood-gating margin uses the distinct samples, whereas PRE-REG 23 includes greedy. The generic §2 wording obscures these differences. Likewise, the 8B trained scripts use a different-person donor without enforcing a different attribute value, unlike the frozen-reader controls. Explain these details and distinguish no injection from base-model activations. Define the layer-numbering convention, since the 8B code uses decoder-block indices and E136 uses a hidden-state-array index.

3. **Keep the interpretation local.** “Selection needs evidence that is not the reader's” exceeds the experiment: the tested likelihood rules failed, not every reader-derived verification method. Similarly, withholding a known vocabulary at inference is not evidence of generalization to novel values or genuinely open-ended factual descriptions. First-token scores are ranking evidence, not certificates of complete-value correctness. The superiority of full-list decoding and the different access requirements of answer-stem methods should remain prominent.

4. **Strengthen reproducibility and statistical reporting.** Release the compact 8B candidate-score records and selected strings so selection and matching can be checked together. The supplied target-training scripts do not seed LoRA initialization; disclose that rebuilding them does not reproduce the exact adapters. State that the bootstrap conditions on the fitted target adapters and reader seeds rather than estimating between-adapter training variability. Report exact match consistently for the final tests; the retained records support the same qualitative conclusion. A sample-agreement baseline and a precision–coverage plot would be useful additions, particularly given [12], but the present claims do not establish superiority over every confidence method.

5. **Repair attribution and presentation.** Reference [9] does not match the title/content of published paper 5 v1 in this snapshot; point to the actual source of the stated AObench lens result. Cite the foundational lens literature and relevant confidence work directly. Use LaTeX delimiters consistently for $K$, $T$, $\tau$ and the scoring expression. These are local corrections, not a material clarity defect.
