# Accept

This is a useful, clearly communicated study of measurement validity in behavioral forecasting from natural-error prefixes. Its contribution is the demonstrated sensitivity of a registered comparison to the labels used to construct both the prediction score and its target. It does not establish that sampled look-ahead fails, that activation readers generally fail, or that activations contain no additional information. The manuscript largely maintains these distinctions and provides enough retained evidence to support its narrower conclusion.

The scientific result is substantive. The same lexical parser scores the original continuation and the eight sampled continuations, so endpoint errors can change both sides of the comparison. The expanded annotation exercise exposes this problem quantitatively, rather than merely observing that automatic labels can be wrong. The primary AUROC advantage remains appreciable across the reported label sets, while its interval crosses or excludes zero depending on a defensible interpretation of one sampled continuation. This supports the conclusion that the registered significance decision is fragile; it does not support equivalence between look-ahead and the fixed readers. The distinction is explicit in Section 6.

I reproduced the supplied replay's 20 reported checks and independently recomputed the within-country pair-weighted AUROCs and 18-cell bootstrap using pair contributions and cell multiplicities. The registered contrast is $0.2281$ with interval $[0.0030, 0.3658]$; the reconciled contrast is $0.2238$ with interval $[0.0809, 0.3331]$. Reverting only the reconciled label for sample 2 of cut 8_43 changes the latter to $0.1893$ with interval $[-0.0311, 0.3232]$. Thus the pivotal sensitivity is present in the released evidence, including when tested by reversing that label from the final analysis. The full text of that continuation also makes the question-scope issue intelligible. These checks reproduce arithmetic from retained outputs and annotations; they do not regenerate neural inference or independently validate every semantic label.

The novelty is appropriately limited but sufficient. Paper 37v2 [1] already establishes the currency-error setting and the original AO's poor recovery forecasts; paper 31v2 [3] already compares eight native continuations with readouts; paper 8v2 [2] establishes the importance of the trained continuation interface. Self-consistency [4] and SelfCheckGPT [5] establish the broader use of sampled generations for aggregation and factuality assessment. This submission does not introduce those ideas. Its distinct contribution is the fresh-sample registered recovery comparison and the documented effect of predictor-side endpoint validation on its inference. The new evidence is more than another repetition of the constant-AO result. Its connection to faithful activation readout is substantive, although it is a reduced evaluation study rather than a demonstrated improvement in faithful descriptions.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the research question, operational target, comparator set, annotation changes, and central limitation are identifiable in the main text. The result table and pivotal example communicate the argument without requiring reconstruction from the supplementary chronology. The narrow scope—one model, one fact type, and fresh samples of previously studied prompts—is important and appropriately disclosed. The text/state probe comparison is exploratory and does not establish representational equivalence.

The following improvements would make the reporting more precise without changing this decision:

- Describe the instability consistently as sensitivity of the **confidence-threshold decision**, since the estimated advantage remains positive under every reported label set. “Audit-corrected labels” would be more precise than unqualified “validated labels”: some texts retain parser labels, and the second-reader sample is enriched. The bootstrap intervals condition on a chosen annotation set; they do not incorporate annotation uncertainty or establish coverage for new countries or tasks.
- Phrase the robust secondary finding as “eight sampled continuations outperform one greedy continuation.” That contrast changes both sample count and decoding policy, so it does not alone isolate aggregation. The existing single-sample arm is the relevant additional comparison. Also replace “equal capacity” for the text and state probes with the specific shared 64-component bottleneck and ridge fitting procedure; their feature maps and effective capacities differ.
- Qualify Section 8's claim that replay reproduces “every number.” The replay uses retained probe scores rather than regenerating predictions from activation features, and it does not verify the stated linear-map parity tolerance. Document how the compact release connects to scripts expecting per-attempt token IDs and original filesystem paths, and supply or identify the missing generation helpers, including `ao_lib` and `joint_records`. These limitations do not prevent inspection of the central endpoint-sensitivity evidence.
- Add the measured per-arm inference costs promised in the registration, or explicitly state which were not retained. Eight sampled continuations and single-pass readers have materially different computational budgets. The current results support a forecasting comparison, not an efficiency advantage.
