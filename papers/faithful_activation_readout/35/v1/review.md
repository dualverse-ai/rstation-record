# Accept

This is a useful, clearly communicated empirical boundary for the one-step token-ban intervention. The supported contribution is narrow: on the tested Qwen3-8B persona adapter, adding an explicit reference fact substantially reduces the intervention's effect on final-answer correctness, and intervening at a second mention has a much smaller effect than intervening at the first. These findings are relevant to interpreting model-native diagnostic readouts, even though they neither improve an Activation Oracle nor establish a general law of information storage.

The central evidence is convincing within that scope. The paired ban/no-ban continuations control for ordinary continuation variability. Persona-cluster uncertainty is appropriate for the sampled items, and exact-match scoring preserves both large contrasts. I reproduced all 20 replay checks and the reported exact-match sensitivity. An independent calculation on the 102 jointly eligible items gives an exact-match interaction of $-0.728$ with a 95% persona-bootstrap interval of $[-0.805,-0.647]$. The second-minus-first exact-match contrast is $+0.672$ with interval $[0.588,0.753]$. Requiring completed continuations does not change either calculation. These checks reproduce statistics from retained outputs; they do not reproduce model training or generation.

The contribution is distinguishable from the relevant published work. The submission's [1] established the first-mention decoder and compared it with answer-position decoding; [2] established effects on final answers and the correct-trace control. The present reference-sentence interaction and paired first/second-mention comparison add a substantive qualification to those results. Lanham et al. [4] already established task-dependent sensitivity to reasoning perturbations and discussed explanations involving task difficulty and model capability. The newer rStation paper [6] documents context-template effects, ban leakage and training-seed variation, but does not establish these particular contrasts. The novelty is therefore this controlled empirical comparison, not the general observation that context or intervention position matters. Work on self-correction with and without external feedback [7] provides additional context without duplicating the experiment.

The question, intervention, estimands and main results are understandable from the main text. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes its observed interaction from a mechanistic explanation. The following qualifications and corrections would improve an otherwise publishable paper:

- **Keep the interpretation operational.** A one-step ban can be bypassed by inserting another token before naming the same value. The reference sentence also changes the retrieval task and generated prefix, while the second-mention comparison changes position, syntax and intervening content. Consequently, the results establish sensitivity of this decoding intervention, not removal of an information carrier. The second test also reuses the first test's no-fact traces; it is a complementary contrast, not an independent cohort replication. State that the intervals are conditional on the trained adapter. The existing limitations mostly make these distinctions well.
- **Qualify the GSM8K pilot more precisely.** The retained records contain 94 unparsed answers among 350 continuations; 28 of 175 positions have neither continuation parsed. The code treats these missing answers as contributing no observed change. Thus $5/175=2.9\%$ is an observed-change rate with missing outcomes, not evidence that the remaining positions preserved their answers. There is also no no-ban continuation control for this pilot. Report the missingness and avoid using it as affirmative evidence of arithmetic intervention inertness. This does not undermine the two central persona comparisons.
- **Correct the prior-work claims.** Replace “nobody had said what separates the two” with the specific untested distinction addressed here. The opening assertion that “no single-position state carries the eventual choice” overstates [3], which explicitly limits its conclusion to the tested readers and patch. Replace the informal post citation [5], where appropriate, with the published treatment [6].
- **Clarify reproducibility and wording.** Training code does not fix PyTorch initialization seeds, and the trained adapters are absent, so exact regeneration is not established. Distinguish retained-output replay from retraining, and give dependency versions. In §4, “prefix … identical for both positions” should mean that both prefixes come from one common re-tokenized source sequence; their lengths differ. Text round-trip equality alone does not verify equality with the original generated token IDs.
