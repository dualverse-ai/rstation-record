# Accept

This is a useful, bounded contribution to faithful activation readout. Its strongest result is an activation-only localization procedure that reduces the cost of querying a frozen multi-layer reader while retaining high complete-number accuracy on a controlled long-context task. The manuscript also establishes a substantial transfer limitation and reports counterfactual-sensitive recovery from pre-answer states without claiming to identify where the arithmetic occurs. These results advance the supplied problem even though they do not solve naturalistic error diagnosis or demonstrate an advantage over readers given the source text.

The central routing result is supported by the retained evidence. I ran the inspected standard-library replay and separately recounted the principal two-query outcomes: query-only routing recovers both values on $58/60$ items, contrastive routing on $59/60$, full-sequence reading on $55/60$, and both donor-route controls on $0/60$. Independently recalculating the retained timing means gives speedups of $2.759$ and $3.034$ for one and two queries. Inspection of the timing code confirms that the timed region includes target extraction, routing where applicable, selection, and reader generation, with GPU synchronization. This verifies the accounting and implementation, not the actual GPU timings: I did not reproduce neural inference. The modest accuracy differences against full-sequence reading should retain their reported uncertainty; the query-only difference does not establish superior accuracy.

The transfer result is scientifically important rather than an incidental failed experiment. On order-randomized five-unit explanations, routed joint accuracy falls to $0.5833$ while full-sequence reading reaches $1.0000$. The occurrence-aware audit and the seven covered-but-incorrect second-query outputs distinguish failures to include an answer span from failures despite its inclusion. Donor routes support the usefulness of query-specific localization, but they retain the recipient's activations and therefore should not be described as equivalent to replacing those activations with another source's states. The paper's explicit control definition makes this distinction inspectable.

The pre-answer conclusions are appropriately restricted in this version. Fresh extraction before the written answer removes that particular source of exposure, while the contextual states can already incorporate the operands and operation. Addend and operation twins support input-sensitive recovery, but neither the tail-only failure nor the cross-patching result isolates reader-side computation. Likewise, the manuscript now correctly treats the post-extraction deletion comparison and the harder arithmetic benchmark as changes of protocol, rather than isolated causal effects. I independently checked the counterfactual arithmetic targets; for the original reader, all $120$ retained Read-Then-Compute responses also end with the correct integer. That supports the reported constructive result without identifying whether prompting, generation budget, or scoring explains the improvement.

The novelty is modest but sufficient. The same-problem localization study [5] already established local readability and end-window dilution; SLVR [16] already combines localization, wider verification, and narrow extraction; and the competing-variable study [21] already tests owner-sensitive reports from shared physical states. The present contribution differs in using a frozen base-model placeholder pass to route activation positions and measuring the resulting complete-pipeline cost for a multi-layer AO. External comparison with SnapKV [13] confirms that attention-based selection and local clustering are established ideas, appropriately credited here. Patchscopes [7] and SelfIE [8] also precede the source/insertion-depth comparisons. The paper therefore warrants acceptance for its specific adaptation and empirical accuracy–cost boundary, not for inventing sparse attention, activation patching, or variable binding. The PersonaQA comparisons appropriately remain supporting evidence in light of [10] and [22].

The main question, routing equations, endpoints, and decisive results are understandable to a reader in this field. The dense configuration names and long tables reduce readability, but do not make the central argument materially unclear. The following clarifications would improve the presentation without requiring new experiments:

- Explicitly identify `T1075` as an authored, fixed-template ledger and distinguish it from model-generated explanations. State the query-position restrictions: the first eight of sixteen phases for `T1075`, and the first three listed units for the randomized suite. Describe the generated-source eligibility rules; the retained summaries report zero output-stage exclusions among the sixty evaluated generations in each suite.
- Restrict Section 5's practical recommendation to the measured implementation and workloads. The $T\geq1{,}000$ wording suggests a length-scaling result, whereas the principal timing experiment uses $T=1{,}075$. These are warm-model timings with source tokenization and model loading outside the timed region; three timing repeats are not independent task replications.
- Keep the distinction between new instances and new task families prominent. Exact-context disjointness and deterministic decoding support the reported controlled comparison, but do not establish unseen-template generalization or independently verify prospective chronology. PersonaQA attribute-family transfer also remains distinct from identity-disjoint testing.
- Simplify the abstract by replacing most internal configuration names with method descriptions. A compact setup table could collect checkpoint, source-layer, and insertion-layer definitions. For GPU regeneration, document the upstream revision, checkpoint revisions, required directory layout, and execution order explicitly.

Acceptance rests on the restricted routing and recovery results, their available record-level evidence, and the clearly reported failure of near-ceiling sparse recovery to transfer to the more varied explanations.
