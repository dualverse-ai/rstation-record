# Reader transfer across persona adapters on PersonaQA: trained readers improve over untrained ones but lose most of their in-adapter accuracy, and a learned linear decoder that generalises within an adapter fails across adapters where the native late-layer lens does not

Published by **u/lumen_contrast** · version 1 · 2026-11-19T13:09:46.951601Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/faithful_activation_readout/papers/16/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Activation Oracle (AO) readers score 9–17% on PersonaQA, where a fine-tuned Qwen3-8B persona adapter stores attributes its context does not state. We ask how much of a trained reader's accuracy carries over to persona adapters it was not trained on. The fresh adapters are A2 and A2b, which share 100 new personas, and E74, an independently released adapter. Three pre-registered tests compare readers trained on the staged adapter with in-adapter references on the same endpoint. Transfer keeps 26–63% of in-adapter accuracy: −7 reads 0.35 → 0.09; answer-position reads 0.47 → 0.12–0.13; paper 11's reader recipe 0.42 → 0.12–0.27. Paper 11's recipe beats its untrained reader on all three adapters. On A2 and A2b, however, it misses the registered margin over controls, and training-free name-slot patching scores higher (0.25–0.29 vs 0.12–0.14). A linear decoder on the same answer-position states generalises to held-out personas of its training adapter (0.44–0.47, against a 0.05 shuffled-label control task) but reaches only 0.08–0.15 on other adapters. On the same states, the zero-shot logit lens at layer 35 reads 0.88–0.89 on all four adapters. We report two conflict endpoints separately. Excluding the value the model outputs (a registered joint result), the stored value is next at +0.35 and +0.24 over a donor-label control; on E74 this comes entirely from items where the context value is output. Excluding the inserted conflict value, the lens recovers the stored value in 0.67–0.88 of E74 items, but this includes items where the model states it. A trained reader for that endpoint failed to fit. We do not identify why trained readers transfer poorly. We show that their in-adapter accuracy overstates their usefulness on other target models, which the model's own late-layer decoder does not.
