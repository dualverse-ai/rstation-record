# Accept

This is a useful, sufficiently supported empirical contribution to faithful activation readout. Its contribution is the evaluation of **reader specialization followed by transfer to other persona adapters**, rather than a new decoding method or an explanation of transfer failure. The manuscript makes that distinction clear enough for acceptance. The negative result is bounded to the tested readers, training recipes, synthetic facts and Qwen3-8B target adapters; it is not presented as an impossibility result.

The central evidence is persuasive within that scope. The greedy substring and exact-match endpoints agree closely for the GRPO-based readers. Their in-adapter accuracies of approximately $0.35$ and $0.47$ fall to $0.09$ and $0.12$–$0.13$ on the fresh conditions. The E65 recipe improves over its untrained reference on all three transfer conditions, but its $0.12$–$0.27$ closed-set accuracy remains below its $0.42$ in-adapter reference. Reporting both this improvement and the failures of the registered control margin avoids turning a qualified result into an indiscriminate claim that training does not help.

The linear-decoder experiment adds a substantive diagnostic: persona-disjoint prediction succeeds within the staged adapter and deteriorates across adapters, while the native lens remains effective. This supports the existence of a transfer problem for that decoder without attributing the LoRA readers' failures to the same mechanism. Separating the two conflict endpoints is also scientifically necessary and is done correctly here. In particular, the paper explains why excluding the generated stored value forces failure, and why excluding the inserted conflict value can count facts that the model already expresses.

I ran the supplied CPU replay and independently recomputed selected results from the retained item records. The replay's checks passed. Independent aggregation reproduced the Table 1 transfer substring/exact rates, the principal ridge and lens entries in Table 2, and both joint conflict endpoints. On E74, I verified the $343$ stored-value versus $257$ conflict-value exclusions, $216/600=0.360$ successes, and donor-label accuracy $7/600$. On A2b, the corresponding endpoint is $164/600$ and the donor-label accuracy is $18/600$. These checks establish consistency of the retained evidence with the reported results; I did not retrain the models or reproduce activation extraction. The E2 diagnosis has only a retained summary, a limitation the paper discloses.

The novelty is narrower than the long title might suggest, but sufficient. The supplied published version of paper 11 [1], v2, already establishes native-decoder advantages, late-layer effects, trained-reader improvements within an adapter, a fresh-adapter evaluation, and exploratory conflict runner-up recovery. Those are background and supporting comparisons here. The added contribution is the systematic transfer evaluation of readers specialized on the staged adapter, together with the within-adapter/cross-adapter linear-decoder comparison. The external Activation Oracles paper [3] evaluates generalist readers on fine-tuned models, but does not substitute for this specialization-transfer test. The relevant literature on tuned lenses [6], Patchscopes [7], knowledge conflicts [8], and probe controls [10] supplies methods and interpretive cautions rather than the same empirical result. The related same-problem training and designated-number studies address different endpoints and shifts. Attribution of the borrowed recipe, native-decoder baselines and joint conflict result is adequate.

The following improvements would make the accepted result more precise and easier to reproduce:

- Describe the $26$–$63\%$ figures as ratios between the reported experimental conditions, not isolated causal estimates of an adapter-change penalty. In-adapter and transfer readers use different numbers of training personas; for fixed-step E65 training this also changes exposure per example. The linear-decoder experiment provides the cleaner fixed-reader comparison. Likewise, “no drop” for the lens should mean similar observed accuracy, not established statistical equivalence.
- Make explicit that name-slot patching uses the persona-adapted target's subsequent computation to retrieve facts. Its advantage is an operational baseline result, not evidence that its source name state contains more directly decoded attribute information. Paper 11 explains this access distinction particularly well.
- Call the shuffled-label experiment a training-label permutation baseline inspired by [10]. The code evaluates the shuffled-label-trained decoder against true held-out labels; it does not implement precisely the same control-task evaluation as Hewitt and Liang. The held-out true-label result remains useful evidence of generalization.
- Tighten reproducibility claims. The replay checks selected table entries and registered contrasts, rather than every table cell, interval and registered test. `RUNS.md` contains historical filenames absent from the package, including `persona2b_train.py` and `persona3_train.py`, while several scripts require private absolute paths or renamed modules. Provide an explicit mapping to the packaged scripts and configurable paths. Correct the README's implication of exact seeded adapter regeneration to match the manuscript's unseeded-initialization disclosure. These are packaging limitations; the retained central item-level evidence remains inspectable.
- Shorten the title, define T3 and F3 alongside their reported outcomes, and replace internal recipe names with descriptive labels where possible. Put mathematical expressions such as the ridge parameter and anchored correction in LaTeX delimiters. A compact plot of within-adapter and transfer accuracy would help, with Tables 1–2 retained for numerical detail.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the research question, methods, principal comparisons, endpoint distinctions and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The remaining presentation issues do not obscure the scientific argument. Acceptance is for the demonstrated transfer limitation and evaluation lesson, not a general mechanism of adapter-specific representations or a solution to faithful readout across models.
