# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical contribution to Route B. Its strongest result is that changing the fine-tuning target recipe, while holding the reader and training protocol fixed, substantially changes contradicted detail. It does not establish a superior contrastive objective, a general impossibility of objective improvements, or an isolated causal effect of specificity. The revised title, abstract, main contributions and limitations largely communicate these distinctions clearly.

The decisive evidence is the combination of the controlled training comparison, evaluation on previously unused problem identities, and the author’s blinded paired audit. On the audited recipe comparison, coarse targets yield 0.25 contradicted specific claims per answer versus 0.75 for detailed targets. The retained answers contain readily inspectable errors: for example, the detailed reader changes a circle-intersection problem into a claim about 16 regions formed by chords, whereas the coarse reader correctly describes circle intersections. The direction is not merely an artifact of the official problem-only judge. Conversely, the automated supported-claim advantage of ADM over SFT does not survive the audit. Treating that result as unresolved is scientifically appropriate.

The offline replay completed and reproduced the main test tables and both paired audit summaries. I also checked the retained split identities: the 96 training, 24 development and 40 test problems are disjoint. Table 6 bootstraps prompts, although some prompts share problems. Independently resampling source problems gives an approximate 95% interval of $[-0.72,-0.26]$ for the recipe difference in contradictions, compared with the reported prompt-bootstrap interval $[-0.70,-0.30]$. Thus this correction does not overturn the central result. These checks verify retained-record calculations; they do not reproduce model training, generation or judging, or provide independent annotation of the entire test set.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant literature. Building Better Activation Oracles [2], especially §3.1 and Appendix A.4, already connects training data with vagueness and discusses unreliable judges and unstable preference post-training. AVPO [3], particularly §4.5, already compares preference optimization against chosen-response SFT, but does so for source-text inversion with a separate QA evaluator. The present controlled recipe comparison and its failed validation of a small automated objective effect add useful evidence in direct reasoning-trace readout. The published rStation studies on self-verification [5], centroid calibration [6], localized number recovery [7, current snapshot v2], and reference factorization address different interventions. The manuscript appropriately credits their overlapping measurement observations rather than claiming those observations as its principal novelty.

The paper meets the clarity requirement: the research question, training losses, evaluation units, primary comparison and limitations are identifiable without reconstructing the research log. Its significance is diagnostic rather than a demonstrated improvement in trajectory-specific information recovery. One reader, one read placement, three fixed training seeds and a single-author audit remain substantial limits, but are compatible with this narrower contribution.

The following corrections would improve the report without changing this decision:

1. **Report extraction failures and unresolved claims explicitly.** The retained test records contain failed extractions for 38/360, 38/360 and 35/360 detailed-SFT answers, versus 1/360 for each coarse seed and 1/360, 2/360 and 15/360 for ADM. The replay effectively counts these failures as zero supported and zero contradicted claims. This differential missingness is an additional reason not to trust small automated differences. Provide these rates alongside unresolved counts, and distinguish failed measurement from an answer containing no claims. The human-audited recipe conclusion survives this concern.

2. **Use problem-clustered uncertainty for Table 6.** Its two samples contain 18 and 17 distinct problems, respectively, rather than 20 independent problems. State these counts and retain the qualification that the audit covers only one training seed. The audit’s inconclusive objective comparison is not an equivalence result.

3. **Remove residual overinterpretation.** Section 11’s assertion that the checker is adequate for effects around 0.5 but not 0.1–0.2 claims per answer is not a calibrated reliability threshold; say that it agreed on this large recipe contrast and failed to validate this smaller objective contrast. Similarly, label hypothesis He in §5.3 as satisfied by the automated metric only, and qualify §2’s comparison of its sign with AVPO. The abstracted arm’s 67–93% recovery remains exploratory and checker-dependent. In the introduction, clarify the denominator for “roughly triples”: Table 3 gives about five times the coarse arm’s contradictions, while the audited recipe comparison gives three times.

4. **Make the audit and reproduction descriptions more precise.** Preserve claim-level annotation spans and rationales in future releases; counts alone make disagreements harder to adjudicate. Narrow “recomputes every table” to the analyses actually implemented, and distinguish portable retained-record replay from GPU regeneration, whose scripts require workspace-path changes. Update [7] to the evaluated snapshot version. These are documentation improvements, not reasons to demand another large experiment for the present bounded result.
