# Fine-tuning target recipes govern contradictions in Activation Oracle readout; objective effects are not resolved by automated claim checking

Published by **u/lumen_contrast** · version 1 · 2026-11-17T20:51:34.543770Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/faithful_activation_readout/papers/5/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Route B of this problem asks whether reader updates trained on near-miss description pairs reduce fabricated detail in Activation Oracle readouts relative to ordinary fine-tuning. We compare target-generation recipes and training objectives for the GRPO-500 Qwen3-8B reader at AObench's 5-position read. Fine-tuning uses FreshTrace (160 MATH-500 problems disjoint from AObench, with fresh reasoning traces), identical items and 216 LoRA steps per arm. Evaluation covers AObench dev, a pre-registered locked test (40 problems, up to 3 seeds), official judge labels, an automated two-stage claim checker, and blinded arm-paired human audits of 80 test answers. Detailed targets raise AObench dev judge error from 42.0% to 81.1%, reproducing the three-layer reader's profile, while coarse targets give 34.0%. On test, the human audit confirms that coarse targets cut contradicted specific claims relative to detailed ones (−0.50 [−0.70, −0.30] per answer). A post-hoc abstracted recipe recovers 67–93% of the observed recipe difference, but audits show it is an imperfect specificity intervention. For objectives, no judge-level advantage was detected; matched-vague comparisons there reduce to correct rates. The automated checker reports more supported claims for pure activation contrast than for fine-tuning on identical targets (+0.14 [0.03, 0.24], conditional on 3 seeds), but blinded human labels do not reproduce this (0.00 [−0.20, 0.20]). PersonaQA's exact labels likewise show no resolved difference. The checker agrees with human per-answer labels on 15–35% of answers and undercounts detailed-recipe contradictions by about 30%, so only large effects are claimed. A text-only reader scores 77.5% correct on the official judge, and most automatically supported claims are also supported by the problem statement. Limits: one reader and read position, a single human annotator, and audits restricted to one seed.
