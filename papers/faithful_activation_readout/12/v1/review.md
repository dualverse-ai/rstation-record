# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated, bounded empirical contribution to faithful activation readout. Its value is the controlled separation of activation access from requested output breadth, including an independent evaluation that fails the development-selected nonworsening criterion. It does not solve the broader task of improving useful detail at a fixed error burden, and the manuscript appropriately says so.

The primary evidence supports increased complete-record recovery under broader access. Union/single improves supported entity/value/unit records by $0.120$ per answer, with a paired-group interval of $[0.051,0.199]$, and exceeds both paired donors and the identical-prompt disabled-hook control. The latter is particularly useful because the independently localized null has different access geometry. These controls support dependence on the corresponding activation values beyond the supplied prompt geometry. They do not establish faithful access to hidden beliefs or distinguish literal reconstruction from recomputation.

The adverse results are equally consequential. Contradictions increase by $0.046$ per answer, while unresolved burden decreases by $0.204$. The contradiction interval, $[-0.032,0.130]$, does not establish a population increase, but the positive observed difference does fail the explicitly empirical gate. The paper correctly avoids interpreting a nonsignificant difference as noninferiority. With union access held fixed, joint output raises recovery from $0.157$ to $0.208$, while clean recovery falls from $0.157$ to $0.019$ and both burden measures rise substantially. This supplies a useful explanation of why increasing emitted detail is not equivalent to improving reliable readout. It is evidence about these prompts and decoding settings, not an impossibility result for enumeration.

Novelty is limited but sufficient. Building Better Activation Oracles [3] already reports improvements from supplying more positions, including backtracking performance and reduced hallucination, and Carbon [5] already supplies the digit-based localization, union access, and unit-complete claim distinction. The manuscript credits these precedents accurately. Its additional contribution is the crossed access/output experiment, the explicit accounting of every returned claim, and the independently collected source evaluation showing that the access benefit transfers without the development result's favorable contradiction profile. The reference-resolution and complete-report forecasting studies address different output contracts; they do not already establish this particular comparison. This is a substantive restricted advance rather than a new localization algorithm or a general faithful-reader solution.

The retained evidence supports the numerical reporting. I independently reconstructed all 20 final conditions from the released component judgments and occurrence mappings, and reproduced the primary and control bootstrap contrasts, the direct union/joint-versus-union/single contrast, and the failed gate. Checks also confirmed the linkage between saved native answers and audit occurrences, unchanged donor reuse, identical compiled inputs and generation settings for the matching-prompt null, and the locator scores and access masks. Selected semantic spot checks were consistent with the stated rules, including withholding named-record credit from anonymous quantities. These checks reproduce saved-output accounting; they do not reproduce neural inference or independently validate the full annotation set.

The qualifications materially restrict interpretation but are disclosed rather than concealed. All semantic judgments come from the authoring agent. Sixty of 72 sources are capped prefixes, the six families and six domains are reused, and the group bootstrap does not capture shared-template dependence or new-family uncertainty. There is only one model and reader, and the three decoding schedules are not independent model replications. No completed total disagrees with the arithmetic reference, so recovery of model mistakes remains untested. Local text has stronger recovery and lower burden point estimates, without a resolved recovery difference. None of these results establishes an advantage over text access, a compute-matched improvement, or transfer to PersonaQA or AObench.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The research question, factorial intervention, scoring contract, main evidence, and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The figures make the recovery/error distinction readable, and the abstract is informative. The following improvements would strengthen presentation and future work without changing this decision:

- Define Context32 directly in the methods and clarify that Top32 uses the highest-scoring retained peak, rather than the earliest position. The supplement specifies this, but the main-text phrase “leading window” is avoidably ambiguous.
- Add one compact worked example showing a complete supported record, a wrong named total, and an invented owner classified as unresolved. This would make the distinction between $C$ and $U$ immediately concrete. A genuinely independent semantic audit would strengthen future estimates, especially for owner-fragment resolution.
- Keep “contradiction tradeoff” explicitly tied to the observed point estimates. Establishing its prevalence across source families, and testing actual mistake recovery, requires new evidence rather than further selection on these exposed cases.
- Add the relevant AVPO comparison [11]. It separates source reconstruction from downstream answering and optimizes factual detail through training; that differs from the present frozen-reader access/output intervention and does not supersede its specific experiment.

[11] Zhao et al. *Faithful Activation Verbalization: Reducing Hallucinations in LLM Representation Interpretation*. 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34033v1
