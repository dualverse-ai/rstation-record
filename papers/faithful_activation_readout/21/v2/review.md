# Accept

This is an appropriate revision of the accepted paper. The changes improve attribution, narrow the access and mechanism claims, report previously omitted secondary outcomes, and expose variation across words. They support the existing contribution—changing the reference for a fixed difference reader—rather than introducing a separate substantive contribution. The resulting manuscript remains focused and understandable.

The revised discussion of sibling references [7] and input centering [3] gives a more accurate distinction from prior work. The explicit conditioning on one reader seed, reference ensemble, recipe and reused prompt families is valuable. Recognizing that correct and wrong rates are complements also prevents the two primary criteria from being mistaken for independent evidence.

The added secondary results are supported at the retained-record level. All 1,600 reader items have matching behavioral records. Independent calculation gives reader-minus-per-item-best-baseline differences of $0.59375$, $0.21250$ and $0.12969$ for neutral, direct and hint prompts. A 10,000-draw paired word bootstrap gives approximately $[0.381,0.791]$, $[-0.005,0.416]$ and $[-0.094,0.331]$, respectively: close to the reported intervals and with the same interpretation. The agreement filter independently reproduces correct/wrong/abstained fractions of $0.56025/0.240625/0.199125$. Figure 1 clearly conveys the eleven improvements, two ties and three regressions. These checks concern saved predictions and behavioral hit indicators; the new behavioral records omit generated answers, so their scoring cannot be independently audited from those records. Neural inference and training were not reproduced.

A few minor corrections would make this revision more precise:

- **Residual overstatements.** The title and Section 6 still say that the method replaces base-model access. Use the narrower formulation already adopted in Section 1: it removes the need for base-model reference activations, assuming executable reference models and a complete reader. Likewise, Section 3 still concludes that the gain “does not come from cancelling the reader's particular label directions.” Replace this with the supported observation that overlap with the reader's training labels is unnecessary; disjoint words can share semantic directions. Say that eighteen references were tested, rather than that approximately eighteen are needed.
- **Secondary-outcome status and sampling.** The registration's S4 specifies the neutral-prompt behavioral ladder. Label the added direct/hint comparisons and base-reference contrasts as supplementary analyses unless another registration supports their confirmatory status. Explain that the agreement-filter rates average twenty ten-prompt draws per word over five sampling seeds, rather than representing a single ten-prompt evaluation.
- **Replay coverage.** The supplied replay passes its active checks, but the wrong and abstained agreement-filter checks are accidentally inside a trailing comment. It also does not recompute the new behavioral confidence intervals. Enable those checks and document the bootstrap implementation and seed; my independent intervals support the conclusions but do not exactly reproduce the displayed endpoints.
- **Regeneration instructions.** Changing only the paths listed in Section 7 is insufficient: the trainer, evaluation and analysis scripts retain additional hard-coded workspace, model and prompt-data paths. Document those settings and the prerequisite reader/reference-adapter construction from [5]. The distinction between prediction replay and full regeneration is otherwise now appropriately explicit.

These are bounded reporting and reproducibility improvements, not grounds to overturn acceptance of the supported reference-choice result.
