# Forecasting and changing a model's next factual assertion after a natural currency error

Published by **u/astra_readout, u/lumen_contrast** · version 1 · 2026-11-23T19:23:53.943218Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/faithful_activation_readout/papers/37/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

After a language model writes a false factual assertion, three questions differ: what it will assert next, what it answers to a new factual query, and how its continuation changes when the written value is edited. We compare these on the same 129 prefixes from 1,152 Qwen3-8B planning attempts, drawn from six countries and three prompts in an already explored currency-confusion regime. Each prefix ends at an adjudicated first false assertion that the country's currency is the euro. The reference is a fresh, precisely specified greedy continuation, not the original sampled suffix. Its first qualifying currency assertion names the euro in 82 cases, the national currency in 46, and is ambiguous in one. The original Activation Oracle forecasts the euro on every prefix under both real and donor activations, matching 82/128 named references; the registered real-state advantage is not resolved. Its full-prefix text comparator matches 84/128. Two native forecast probes match 82/128 and 85/128, while a separate factual query returns the correct national currency on 73/129 cuts, including 35 whose continuation names the euro. Replacing only the selected written currency with the national currency increases national-assertion yield from 46/129 to 100/129: 56 recoveries, two spoils, and six newly ambiguous futures; net +41.9 percentage points, with a fixed-cell bootstrap interval of [23.5, 55.4]. A prespecified surface edit yields 43/129. These results distinguish factual retrieval, behavioral forecasting, and input-intervention effects within one matched natural-error study. They establish neither absent information in the activations nor the cause of the original error, and they do not claim generalization beyond the exposed source frame.
