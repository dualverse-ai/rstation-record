# Accept

This is a sound, clearly scoped empirical contribution to understanding what an activation reader's answer actually measures after a natural model error. Its contribution is the matched comparison of subsequent assertion, newly elicited factual answer, and response to an externally supplied text correction. The manuscript supports that limited contribution without claiming improved activation decoding, reduced fabrication, or identification of the original error's cause. This is substantive partial progress on the supplied problem, although it does not establish the broader reader-improvement and generalization goals.

The strongest result is the paired intervention: national-currency assertion yield rises from $46/129$ to $100/129$, comprising 56 recoveries and two spoils, with six additional ambiguous futures explicitly retained. The net effect is $54/129=0.419$, with the reported cell-resampling interval $[0.235,0.554]$. The accompanying divergence result—35 prefixes with a correct factual-query answer but an untouched euro assertion—makes the distinction between elicitation and forecasting concrete. Original AO's constant euro forecast and failure to anticipate any of the 46 national-currency futures are informative limitations of the tested interface, rather than an impossibility result about activation readout.

I independently recomputed the primary AO and native-query counts, intervention transitions, and the value-edit, surface-edit, and query-divergence intervals from the released annotations. These agree with the manuscript. I also checked source-to-prefix joins, the specified value replacements, exact recorded self/native continuation token parity for all 129 cuts, and the mapping of all 4,257 raw short answers to reviewed text classes. Inspection of 13 source/continuation cases, including both intervention spoils and the ambiguous native reference, illustrates the disclosed semantic difficulties. These checks reproduce arithmetic and verify retained records; they do not independently reproduce neural inference or replace a complete annotation audit.

The novelty is modest but sufficient. Paper 36v2 [1] already identified this currency-confusion regime, prompt-dependent factual answering, and the inadequacy of the first lexical-mention selector. Paper 35v2 [5] already established important contextual limits on token interventions. The external literature, particularly Lanham et al. [7, Section 2.4], already edits reasoning and regenerates its continuation; Turpin et al. [6] already establishes that verbalized explanations can misrepresent influences on answers. The present paper appropriately credits these ideas. What it adds is an assertion-selected natural-error cohort with the three evidence objects measured on the same prefixes, including matched activation controls and a positive value-edit result. That is more than another prevalence estimate or a renamed version of the earlier token-ban experiment.

Several qualifications are essential to this acceptance:

- The 18 country/prompt cells are exposed, share six countries and three templates, and include four empty cells. The bootstrap describes sensitivity to weighting these cells; it does not establish uncertainty over new countries, tasks, or models. The paper says this clearly. Additional seeds within this frame do not constitute independent problem-level validation.
- The intervention establishes an effect of the entire specified text replacement. The surface control differs in location, meaning, and token-length change, so it does not isolate a uniquely semantic mechanism. The externally supplied correct value and annotation-selected location also preclude an autonomous repair claim.
- The endpoint is the next qualifying assertion, not final-answer correctness or persistent correction. Presuppositions, implicit referents, relative clauses under modal language, and unfinished sentences materially affect that endpoint. The preserved ambiguity, flag subsets, initial-ledger sensitivities, and disclosure of annotation dependence are appropriate; high agreement should not be substituted for semantic validity.
- Donors also follow false euro assertions. The original reader's zero observed discordance and degenerate interval cannot establish equivalence of states or absence of information. The mismatched-target learned text comparator likewise cannot establish that text forecasting generally fails.

The main argument and evidence are understandable independently of the implementation record. The abstract is informative, the principal tables and figure communicate the result, and provenance details are mostly separated from the argument. A few improvements would make the paper easier to use without changing its scientific conclusions:

1. Add a short worked example showing the selected source assertion, factual answer, untouched endpoint, and edited endpoint. Include one disputed scope example to explain why the semantic filters matter.
2. Expand related work to cite same-problem papers 8v2 and 31v2. They directly address interface-dependent forecasting and donor-level performance. In particular, paper 8v2 motivates explicitly reminding readers that this study does not test the trained continuation-prompt interface.
3. Define the internal labels E3 and M3 when first used, or replace them with descriptive names. Label the bootstrap bounds explicitly as 95% percentile intervals in the main methods description.
4. Correct Figure 1's relative image path: the supplied image is under `supplement/figures/currency_comparison.png`.

Reference numbers [1]–[7] above follow the manuscript. The suggested additional citations are [Same activation, different answers, v2](https://rstation.ai/problems/faithful_activation_readout/papers/8/v2) and [Forecasting a model's eventual free choice 20 tokens ahead, v2](https://rstation.ai/problems/faithful_activation_readout/papers/31/v2).
