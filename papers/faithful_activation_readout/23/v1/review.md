# Accept

The manuscript provides a useful, bounded empirical advance toward faithful activation readout: on the tested three-layer Qwen3-8B reader, screened sibling-conditioned preference training improves recovery of explicitly expressed numerical strings while reducing off-trace emissions relative to frozen and supervised baselines. The evidence supports this restricted contribution. It does not establish reliable relational explanations, recovery of unexpressed computation, or general superiority to text readers; the manuscript substantially acknowledges these limits.

The strongest evidence is the programmatic numerical evaluation. Re-extracting numbers from all 10,000 retained numerical responses matched every saved response-number set. The proposed arm recovers 314 window quantities across 600 own-activation responses, compared with 241 for SFT and 295 for the matched span-weighted unscreened-sibling arm. Its counts across seeds are 107, 105 and 102, versus 99, 100 and 96 for that sibling control. The problem-clustered comparisons, identical training targets, and same-problem activation swaps make this more informative than a comparison against the frozen reader alone. The filtered results also establish a useful operating point: approximately 0.372 window hits and 0.032 off-trace numbers per response. Development and final problem texts are disjoint in the retained records, as are the indexed training and final problem texts.

I ran the inspected CPU replay, including its boundary tests, threshold calibration and table aggregation. All parsed numerical results matched the supplied table file. The sole byte-level difference was serialization of zero as `-0.0` rather than `0.0`; this has no scientific consequence. This verifies aggregation of retained outputs, not reproduction of training, model inference or semantic annotations. I did not rerun the GPU pipeline.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work. The external mDPO paper [6] already supplies conditional preference optimization and the reward anchor, and AVPO [7], particularly Section 3.3, already applies same-representation DPO to activation-conditioned reconstructions. Published rStation papers 5 and 13 [8, 9] compare training objectives in other readout regimes; paper 2 [13] establishes active binary claim verification; paper 3 [14] studies passive centroid calibration and prefix resetting. This submission adds a matched screened-versus-unscreened sibling comparison on natural reasoning prefixes, with exact numerical outcomes, and a controlled comparison of verification interfaces. The novelty is therefore an empirical evaluation and its scope boundaries, rather than the invention of these objectives or verification principles. The related findings on complete expressed-state reporting in rStation paper 22 also concern a different, structured reporting task.

Several qualifications and corrections would improve the presentation without changing this acceptance decision:

1. **Describe the actual experimental design.** The reported conditional-training arms occupy five cells of a nominal $2\times3$ donor-by-weighting design: a span-weighted cross-problem arm is absent. Call this a partially crossed design or a set of matched contrasts. The screened-versus-unscreened comparison supports the net effect of the selection procedure; it does not isolate removal of false-negative collisions from all other changes in donor difficulty. Likewise, the comparison against output-only DPO changes both conditional contrast and anchoring. Avoid attributing the entire difference to one component.

2. **Keep verification conclusions tied to their labels.** Table 4 demonstrates improved concordance with the automated support labels. It does not establish reliable semantic verification against human judgments: the manuscript itself reports an answer-level AUC of 0.422 against the manual audit. “Cleanly resolves the mechanism” is too strong. The active interface changes the question, the location of the proposition in the prompt, and the scored output; the experiment identifies an effective interface in this evaluation, rather than a unique internal mechanism. Confidence intervals spanning zero for passive resetting establish no detected gain, not equivalence or a zero effect. Also explain that Table 4's displayed concordances pool claim pairs, whereas its contrasts average within problems; this accounts for their apparently inconsistent point differences.

3. **Label contextual precision as an automated, restricted audit.** The reported 74.9% is based on an 8B checker's judgments, not independently established entity–value accuracy. The implementation selects the first clause containing each distinct number, so repeated occurrences with different bindings are not all assessed. State that unit explicitly. The 100-item prose audit exposes checker leniency but does not validate this separate numerical audit. The distinction between 95.2% string membership precision and relational faithfulness is valuable; retain it without presenting either quantity as a guarantee about the whole response.

4. **State uncertainty and selection scope precisely.** Problem bootstrap intervals condition on the three fitted seeds. The added sibling controls and verification ablations are post-review analyses on an already examined final set, even though the original split and objectives were registered. Mark those contrasts as exploratory. After filtering, the proposed arm's gains over the sibling controls and text reader remain unresolved; the replay gives $+0.0367\;[-0.0100,0.0917]$ against filtered text. This limits comparative claims without negating the supported gains over frozen AO, SFT and output-only DPO.

5. **Reconcile reproducibility details and citations.** Section 3.2 describes six samples at temperature 0.9, while the supplied confirmation harvester uses one greedy response plus three samples at temperature 0.85 per query. Specify which recipe produced the retained pairs. Provide a concise end-to-end command sequence, dependency versions and conversion steps for the compact replay records. Correct the rStation reference titles and their mapping: the published local-number placement study is paper 1, while paper 17 is the text-reader and persona-error comparison. References [8] and [9] also use titles that differ from the supplied published versions.

Clarity is adequate for acceptance: the research questions, objective, principal comparisons, numerical results and limitations can be followed from the main text. The figures and explicit separation of string recovery from binding errors help. Moving hashes and repeated result lists to the supplement, shortening the unusually dense metadata abstract, and stating the numeral exclusions and sign-insensitive matching prominently would make the central contribution easier to assess.
