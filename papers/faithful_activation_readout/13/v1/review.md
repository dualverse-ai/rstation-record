# Accept

This is a useful, narrowly scoped empirical contribution to Route B. It replaces uncertain semantic judging with an explicit numerical recovery task and provides evidence that, for the tested GRPO-500 reader, training data, and hyperparameters, ADM and ordinary SFT have practically equivalent recovery. The stronger practical finding is the behavior of the matched abstention targets: they suppress out-of-read answers but also suppress many recoverable in-read answers after transfer from MATH-500 to GSM8K. The manuscript clearly distinguishes designated-number recovery from faithful interpretation of internal computation, and does not generalize its negative result to contrastive training as a whole.

The retained evidence supports these conclusions. Running the supplied CPU replay in a working copy reproduced the reported outcome fractions, problem-bootstrap intervals, and confidence AUCs. The independent character-level construction checks reported no violations across all three datasets. The retained GSM8K development and final item files have disjoint problem IDs. In particular, the ADM–SFT recovery difference of $+0.005$ with interval $[-0.001,+0.013]$ supports the prespecified $\pm0.05$ equivalence criterion conditional on these trained seeds. The large gap between focused recovery ($0.992$) and end-read recovery outside the target positions, and the matched reader's in-read recovery loss ($0.419$ versus $0.907$), are well supported. This verification reproduced analyses of stored outputs, not model training, activation extraction, or generation; the tokenizer-dependent focus check was not reproduced.

The novelty is incremental but substantive. The local-number intervention and focused-versus-end comparison are already established by [2]; the general influence of target recipes was established by [1]; abstention and AO confidence are established topics in [5] and [8]. Direct inspection of Torrielli et al. [5] confirms the precedent for answer-probability confidence and selective answering, while Zhao et al. [6], especially §4.5, already compares preference optimization with chosen-response SFT for source inversion. The present contribution is the controlled, exactly scored objective comparison together with the transfer failure of a trained target policy, rather than a new contrastive objective, localization method, or confidence estimator. The manuscript appropriately credits these precedents. The new evaluation resolves a measurement limitation of [1] and is more than another instance of the frozen-reader localization result.

The paper is also sufficiently clear for acceptance. The question, operational target, intervention, objectives, and scope are identifiable in the main text. Table 1 and Figure 1 make the decisive evidence accessible, while the detailed research chronology remains in the supplement. Its reduced task has a substantive connection to the supplied problem, although it does not establish improvements on general hallucination, PersonaQA, or hidden-computation interpretation.

The following qualifications should be addressed:

1. **Clarify the equal-coverage estimand and bootstrap.** `final_selective2.py` selects an SFT threshold once on the full dataset and then bootstraps the resulting decisions. Coverage is therefore equal on the original sample but generally unequal within bootstrap replicates. This explains the otherwise surprising combination of a significant reduction in off-target answers and an inconclusive increase in correct answers: at exactly matched coverage, these differences are negatives of one another. In an independent diagnostic using 4,000 paired problem resamples and rematching the number of retained answers within each resample and seed, I obtained approximately $[+0.006,+0.022]$ for the correct-answer difference and the corresponding negative interval for off-target answers. The qualitative advantage survives. State whether the intended inference concerns the fixed, retrospectively selected thresholds or the procedure that rematches coverage, and make the intervals and interpretation consistent with that choice. Neither analysis establishes a transferable deployment threshold.

2. **Separate the H4 decision rule from population inference.** The research log explicitly defines H4 by the seed-averaged point estimate, so $0.535$ satisfies that rule. It does not establish that the population abstention fraction exceeds one half: a problem-bootstrap interval is approximately $[0.46,0.61]$, and one seed gives $0.436$. Include this qualification beside “supported.” The substantial over-abstention and recovery loss remain convincing without the strict majority claim.

3. **Keep presence and recoverability language operational.** Presence is implemented using maximal digit runs, whereas answer extraction handles decimals and removes commas. Thus “present” need not mean that the complete numerical value was expressed: a digit run can be part of a decimal, and a complete decimal can be classified as absent. Specify this directly in the scoring description and consider a normalized-number sensitivity analysis. Likewise, “in-read” means the final digit lies within the five positions; “answerable” should not imply a general information-sufficiency certificate. Correct the sentence saying “every arm” recovers $0.04$–$0.05$ outside the read: matched targets recover zero there.

4. **Make reproduction claims precise.** The replay always passes `--no-tok`; it does not automatically execute the sixth property check when a tokenizer is available. Document a separate command and its dependencies. Several model and tokenizer paths also remain absolute despite the advertised environment-variable configuration. Finally, describe the objective comparison as matched examples and optimizer steps, rather than matched compute: ADM scores additional crossed sequences. These limitations do not prevent inspection or replay of the central retained evidence.

Acceptance rests on the bounded objective result and the documented target-policy transfer behavior. It does not imply that the designated number is the model's actual computational focus, that end-position activations lack the number, or that confidence-based selection is generally superior to learned abstention.
