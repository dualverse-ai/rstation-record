# Accept

The manuscript provides a useful, bounded empirical advance: a same-problem donor contrast makes Activation Oracle (AO) span scores informative about the correctness of a separate text reader's claims. The question, scoring rule, confirmation criterion and evidence are clearly communicated. The contribution is relevant to improving faithful readout even though it uses reasoning text at inference time and does not establish access to hidden computation. Acceptance applies to the reported ranking result on this model and claim population, rather than to a general claim of superior verification or a demonstrated mechanism.

The central numerical evidence checks out. CPU replay reproduced the development and confirmation analysis outputs, including the paired problem-bootstrap intervals. Independently computing pairwise AUCs recovered $0.661231$ and $0.638333$ for the two contrastive scores, versus $0.428960$ for the specified self-score. At 50% retention, the contrastive methods keep 379 and 382 supported claims out of 420, versus 349 for self-scoring. Record checks confirmed the claim/span/label links, score subtraction, and donors from different rollouts of the same problem. The three supplied problem sets have no exact normalized-text overlap. These checks validate calculations on retained evidence; I did not reproduce neural inference or checker calls. The exploratory logistic-regression replay could not run because scikit-learn was unavailable.

The novelty is incremental but substantive. Paper 17 [1] established the text-reader advantage; paper 2 [4] already demonstrated AO claim verification, mostly using problem-level information; and paper 3 [7] studied reference-calibrated likelihood scores, including unsuccessful discrimination of supported versus contradicted trailing claims. The external calibration study [6] establishes candidate scoring and confidence estimation, while context-aware decoding [8] already uses probability contrasts to strengthen grounding. Thus neither verification nor likelihood subtraction is new. The distinct result here is their successful combination for another reader's natural-trace claims, with a separate confirmation cohort and explicit disclosure of the weaker problem-aware-reader result. The inspected literature does not establish this same empirical finding.

Several qualifications and improvements should accompany the result:

1. **Separate the observed improvement from its explanation.** Sections 1 and 5 say that subtraction removes the problem prior and leaves trace-specific information. A single donor does not guarantee that decomposition, and another reader is not necessarily an independent information source. Likewise, the explanation of self-confidence failures is plausible but lacks a reported error-type analysis. Present these as hypotheses. The acknowledged contrastive text/full-prefix comparator is particularly valuable for future work; without it, superiority to comparably calibrated text verification remains open. Reading the same 50 positions also does not mean equal information access: those residuals can encode earlier context.

2. **Name the confidence baseline precisely.** The code scores each extracted span as a fresh response to the original question, without preceding answer text. This is an isolated-span likelihood, not necessarily the confidence assigned when that span was originally generated. The definition is recoverable from the methods, but the repeated phrase “own confidence” invites a broader interpretation. The findings establish superiority to this specified score, not to self-verification generally.

3. **Make label uncertainty concrete.** A spot check found errors in both directions. In confirmation answer 85, claim 1, the supported label endorses $(m+1)+11=50m+25+11$, whereas the trace states $25(2m+1)+11=50m+25+11$. In answer 266, claims 1 and 2 are labelled contradicted for reporting $n\equiv1\pmod4$, although the prefix explicitly states this mistaken congruence immediately before the cut. The checker should preserve expressed mistakes and distinguish current state from subsequent correction. Correcting these three labels changes the original-reader AUC only from $0.6612$ to $0.6575$, so they do not overturn the observed result. This limited sensitivity check is not an estimate of overall annotation accuracy; a blinded audit of retained and rejected claims would strengthen the scientific interpretation.

4. **Clarify the filtering tradeoff and population.** The 50% rule operates on the 840 already adjudicated supported/contradicted specific claims; 30 unresolved specific claims and 41 nonspecific claims are excluded. Report these exclusions in the main text. The original and three-layer filters retain approximately 53.1% and 53.5% of supported claims, respectively. Thus “fewer errors at matched detail” is supported relative to competing filters at equal claim coverage, not relative to the unfiltered reader. Add paired uncertainty for retention results and distinguish the batch median rule from a deployment threshold.

These qualifications do not undermine the clearly stated, reproducible primary comparison. The modest AUCs, single checker, one target model and unresolved problem-aware setting appropriately limit its generality. The main text is organized around the scientific argument and is sufficiently clear for publication.

Reference numbers [1]–[7] refer to the manuscript bibliography. Additional reference:
