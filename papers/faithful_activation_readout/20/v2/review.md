# Accept

This is an appropriate, focused update to the accepted contribution. Section 4.4 supplies the missing contrastive text baseline on the existing claim sets; Section 4.5 exposes the answer-level cost of the existing filtering rule. These additions qualify the original result rather than introduce an independent substantive contribution. They belong with the accepted paper, and the resulting manuscript remains organized around a clear scientific question. The relationship to papers [1] and [7] remains intact; these controls do not require a separate novelty claim or submission.

The added numerical evidence is reproducible from the retained records. I reconstructed the combined scores, checked both text-score subtractions, and reproduced the new analysis output, including its paired problem-bootstrap intervals, exactly. The answer-level utility output also reproduced exactly. Independent pairwise calculations give fresh-set AUCs of $0.542284$ for window-text contrast, $0.637577$ for full-prefix contrast, and $0.661231$ for original-AO contrast. This validates the reported calculations, not the neural inference or the checker labels, which I did not regenerate.

The scientifically useful conclusion is that the AO's advantage over the previously tested uncontrasted full-prefix baseline does not establish an advantage over contrastive full-prefix text scoring. The answer-level analysis also usefully shows why improved claim ranking need not yield uniformly more useful answers. Both findings sharpen the interpretation of the existing contribution. The post-confirmation status is appropriately disclosed.

The following clarifications should accompany this update:

1. **Distinguish similar estimates from equivalence.** In Section 4.4, the abstract and Section 5, “matches,” “serves as well,” and “adds over text only” are too categorical. The original-AO minus full-prefix contrast difference is $0.024$ with interval $[-0.054, 0.106]$. An interval containing zero and a point estimate within $0.03$ do not establish equivalence within $0.03$; registering this interpretation rule does not change that. Say that the point estimates are similar and an AO advantage over full-prefix contrast is unresolved. The data support contrastive text scoring as a competitive comparator on these claims, without identifying what fraction of the AO's signal causally comes from activations versus contrast.

2. **Make the comparison consistent throughout.** The introduction still highlights superiority over the uncontrasted full-prefix verifier without mentioning the new control. Add the Section 4.4 qualification there. “Matched window-only access” means reading the same positions, not matching information: the residuals can encode earlier context. Also specify that the stated $+0.10$–$+0.12$ advantage over window-text contrast is for the fresh cohort; development W50 differences are approximately $+0.070$ and $+0.075$.

3. **State the utility denominator and endpoint precisely.** Section 4.5 averages over answers containing at least one eligible supported/contradicted specific claim: 266 fresh answers, comprising 90 T1, 89 T2 and 87 T3 answers. It does not average over all 270 generated answers. Identify that conditioning explicitly, or include excluded answers with zero eligible claims. Likewise, retaining no supported claim is not necessarily emptying an answer: contradicted claims may remain. Describe the table as retained supported-claim coverage, with descriptive point estimates; a claim of a population-level utility advantage would need paired uncertainty. The filtering operates on already adjudicated eligible claims, not a demonstrated end-to-end deployment pipeline.

These are local interpretation and reporting qualifications. They do not invalidate the added comparisons or require new experiments for this revision. Acceptance retains the previous scope: one target model, reused claim cohorts for the new controls, and labels from a single checker.
