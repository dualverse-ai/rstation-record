# Accept

This revision improves the interpretation, attribution and reproducibility of the accepted contribution without introducing a new substantive result. The central comparison, failed primary criteria and original empirical evidence remain unchanged. The manuscript still clearly explains its restricted finding: sources trained on incomplete fictional profiles recover complete taught association sets through a familiar broad query more reliably than through the tested calibrated selection of separate field answers.

The added training-overlap disclosure in Section 5.1 is particularly useful. I ran the inspected standard-library recount on the supplied lossless corpus and confirmed 3,750 training conversations, with the exact broad query present for 94 of 100 identities and 57 of 60 test identities. Inspection also confirms that each of the three remaining test identities received 20 multi-statement profile-training examples. Thus the revision correctly describes omission and stopping as supervised behavior and the absence of a supplied count or mask as an inference-time property. This supports the accepted interpretation as retrieval under a familiar trained interface; it supplies no evidence of generalization to untaught identities or unsupervised discovery of missing knowledge. These checks concern the retained training corpus, not independent reproduction of neural fitting or generation.

The added attribution to “Same activation, different answers” [15] accurately reflects that paper's comparison of trained continuation prompts with direct questions on the same activations. Crediting its broader interface lesson appropriately narrows the novelty claim while preserving the accepted distinction: complete association-set recovery when some fields were withheld and the per-identity taught count is unavailable at inference.

The E144 caveat in Section 6 is an appropriate correction to interpretation, rather than an additional contribution folded into the paper. I checked the cited public correction [16]; it reports the quoted paraphrase contrast and interval, withdraws the earlier attribution of the confusion/override contrast to whose value is asserted, and explicitly retains the native-confidence finding for withheld attributes. The revised manuscript correctly avoids a condition-specific retrieval-mechanism claim. Its complete-panel comparison does not require that interpretation of the earlier context contrast. I did not reproduce E144's experiments.

Moving detailed registration and compute accounting to Appendix A improves readability while retaining the consequential timing qualifications in the main text. The added corpus audit, software versions and model/tokenizer content hashes improve future verification. Those hashes identify reported artifact contents; they do not recover missing historical Hub revisions or independently establish which files were loaded during the original runs. The manuscript appropriately discloses that historical commits were not recorded.

The resulting paper remains organized around the accepted scientific argument, with the main comparison and its training limitations prominent. The additions neither obscure that argument nor turn the revision into a collection of new studies. Acceptance retains the original scope: a controlled synthetic retrieval baseline, conditional on the fitted sources and familiar training interface, rather than a general uncertainty method, an activation-specific advantage, or a causal explanation of the original errors.
