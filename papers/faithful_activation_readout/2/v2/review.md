# Accept

This revision improves the interpretation and documentation of the accepted contribution. The same-problem activation swap and claim-length analyses are focused controls on the existing verification result. The sentence-filter benchmark adds limited supporting evidence about its practical trade-off. These additions do not constitute a separate substantive contribution or turn the manuscript into an unfocused collection. The distinction from constrained-candidate confidence scoring in [7] and yes/no margins in [8] is now appropriately explicit.

The most useful addition is the same-problem swap in §4.3. The retained scores reproduce AUCs of $0.750$, $0.665$, and $0.655$ with another trajectory's activations, compared with $0.800$, $0.701$, and $0.714$ with the original activations. The paired problem-bootstrap intervals also reproduce. This substantially qualifies the accepted finding: verification depends on corresponding activation information, but much of its discrimination survives changing the trajectory within a problem. It does not establish faithful access to trajectory-specific computation. The new introductory qualification and abstract convey this limitation usefully.

The official-label addition also supports its stated, limited interpretation. Replay reproduces filtered-minus-unfiltered error changes of $-0.254$, $-0.200$, and $-0.314$, alongside decreases in the benchmark's correct category of $-0.054$, $-0.095$, and $-0.062$, with the reported clustered intervals. Approximately 50%, 58%, and 34% of retained filtered answers are empty and labelled vague. Reporting the matched end-50 comparison prevents the benefit of a larger read window from being attributed to filtering. This is evidence for a lower-error, lower-coverage operating point on development data, not demonstrated superiority at matched useful coverage.

I recomputed these statistics from retained outputs; I did not regenerate model responses or independently relabel the examples. The supplied replay completed the swap and benchmark calculations but stopped at the newly added, undocumented `scikit-learn` dependency. An independent rank-based AUC calculation reproduced the claim-length results, including residualised AUCs of $0.802$, $0.708$, and $0.717$ and within-quartile means of $0.807$, $0.711$, and $0.731$.

Minor improvements remain:

1. **Apply the narrower interpretation consistently.** Section 4.3 still says the signal comes from activations “not from how plausible a claim sounds,” despite the more careful sentence immediately afterward. Use the qualification about the tested no-activation control throughout. Likewise, the length controls rule out the tested simple length explanations, not every possible dependence on length. Describe the three-layer swap difference as nominal evidence: its interval barely excludes zero, and “clearly non-zero” is too emphatic for this exploratory comparison.

2. **Complete the new reproducibility documentation.** Add `scikit-learn` to the replay requirements, or use the existing rank-based AUC implementation. Preserve the numerical frozen sentence thresholds and their calibration scores, as well as donor trajectory identifiers for the swap. The generation scripts still reference original workspace paths, and the calibration file `results/e36_dgv/scores.json` is not packaged. The supplied responses and labels support statistical replay, but the README's distinction between replay and regeneration should also appear in §7, with costs updated to include the new inference and judging runs.

3. **Keep benchmark judging uncertainty visible.** Identical retained responses receive different categories across the two end-50 conditions in 20 original-reader, 16 GRPO, and 13 three-layer pairs. The problem bootstrap conditions on these labels; it does not include uncertainty from repeated judging. This does not overturn the large reported changes, but merits a brief qualification. The official judge also assesses fit to the problem rather than independently certifying the represented reasoning state.

4. **Keep §4.3 easy to scan.** A compact table for the official-label comparison would communicate the error–correctness trade-off more clearly than the current numerical paragraph. Detailed calibration and per-run records belong in the supplement. The present overall argument remains clear enough for acceptance.
