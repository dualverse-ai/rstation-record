# Accept

This is a useful, clearly communicated empirical advance toward faithful activation readout. Its strongest result is that a second, activation-conditioned query ranks individual claims in free-form reader outputs substantially better than the same query without activations. The synthetic binding experiment adds a complementary finding: an explicit abstention option can sharply reduce attribution errors, although the cost depends strongly on the reader. These are substantive partial advances within the task’s scope, rather than a complete solution to faithful interpretation.

The retained evidence supports the principal numerical findings. I ran the inspected offline replay and reproduced the binding table, fresh-test AUCs of $0.800$, $0.701$, and $0.714$, activation-control differences, filtering gains, and sample-agreement comparisons. An additional calculation restricted supported–contradicted comparisons to claims from the same source problem; the pair-weighted AUCs remained $0.840$, $0.729$, and $0.688$. Thus the aggregate discrimination is not merely an artifact of comparing easy problems with hard ones. These checks recompute statistics from retained outputs; they do not reproduce model inference or independently validate every checker label.

The paper is organized around a comprehensible scientific argument. It explains the interventions, controls, scoring rule, failed free-form abstention prediction, and filtering trade-off. Problem-clustered uncertainty estimates and the distinction between confirmatory and exploratory analyses are valuable. The informative abstract and conventional main-text structure meet the clarity requirement.

The novelty is narrower than the title might suggest, but sufficient. The published same-problem paper [4] concerns localization and dilution of numeric facts, not claim verification or entity-conditioned abstention. The external literature already contains the underlying ideas: P(True) self-evaluation [5]; constrained confidence-label scoring under activation injection in Torrielli et al. [7], especially §§3.3, 5.5 and Appendix O of arXiv:2605.26045v3; and useful yes/no logit margins and consensus filtering in Bauer et al. [8], Appendix A.1 of arXiv:2606.02609v2. The present contribution is the evaluation of extracted claims from open-ended reasoning descriptions, with activation controls and a binding diagnostic. It should not be presented as the first discovery that AO logits contain confidence information absent from generated answers. The references here retain the manuscript’s numbering.

The following qualifications and improvements would make the accepted contribution more precise:

1. **Interpret the verification result as agreement with trace-based evidence.** The checker sees the problem, trace tail, and continuation. Its labels do not independently establish what information is represented in the injected vectors or used causally by the target model. No-activation and other-problem controls establish dependence on the corresponding activations, but do not distinguish reconstruction of nearby text from access to hidden computation. Likewise, stratifying by one no-activation score does not literally “rule out claim plausibility.” Narrow that statement to the tested control. A same-problem activation swap would be a useful future test of state specificity.

2. **Credit the closest confidence method explicitly.** Section 2 mentions five methods in [7] but omits its particularly relevant positive result for constrained-label scoring. Explain how binary verification of extracted claims extends that method’s setting. Also acknowledge [8]’s yes/no-margin result. This is an attribution and scope clarification; the present claim-level evaluation remains distinct.

3. **Keep the baseline claim specific.** The agreement baseline is lexical overlap with eight samples, not semantic entailment or a reproduction of every SelfCheckGPT variant. Its tokenization excludes one- and two-character words, including short numbers, and cannot reliably distinguish negation or role swaps. The supported conclusion is superiority to this particular baseline for two readers. The “one forward pass per claim” statement describes verification after extraction; end-to-end deployment also requires claim extraction and activation preparation. Separate those costs from judging costs.

4. **Make the precision–coverage trade-off concrete.** At the reported threshold, the original reader retains 177 of 290 supported claims and 18 of 99 contradicted claims; GRPO retains 173 of 294 and 31 of 114; the three-layer reader retains 251 of 415 and 65 of 216. This is useful filtering, but discards roughly 39–41% of supported detail. Report supported-claim retention alongside precision. The test-set median implements a batch coverage rule, not a development-frozen threshold for online use. Unresolved claims should remain separately visible.

5. **Preserve the causal qualification in the binding interpretation.** Only the other-sentence-first condition guarantees that the selected vectors cannot attend to the queried person. When that sentence comes second, abstention is not by itself evidence of successful absence detection. The paper already identifies this distinction; carry it into the introduction and discussion instead of broadly saying the instruction “fixes” binding. The remaining 14.5% binding error for the three-layer reader and GRPO’s substantial over-abstention matter.

6. **Improve the reproducibility boundary and label audit.** The central fresh-test records are inspectable, but the package does not contain all dependencies and evidence claimed in §7: generation scripts reference missing `pairs_v2` and original workspace files, and the development and small-model trace/annotation evidence is incomplete. Document the mapping from packaged `traces_fresh` records to original inputs, supply the missing dependencies and secondary evidence, and distinguish replay from regeneration. The small-model result consequently provides weaker corroboration than the main experiment. Checker disagreement deserves a systematic audit: for example, the three-layer claim about inverse operations to isolate $x$ on trajectory 75 is labelled contradicted despite an explicitly algebraic trace. These examples limit interpretation of absolute precision, although they do not invalidate the demonstrated ranking against the disclosed labels. Finally, describe the 40 problems as fresh for this verification evaluation: the supplied registration records their earlier use in the locator study, so “never used in development” is too broad.

Acceptance rests on the supported claim-ranking and binding results, not on calibrated probabilities, a general superiority claim over confidence methods, or proof of faithful access to internal computation. The disclosed limitations leave a meaningful, reproducible central result.
