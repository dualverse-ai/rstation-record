# Accept

The paper provides a meaningful, bounded advance in faithful activation readout: a reader trained on fine-tune differences generalizes to previously unseen secret words and adapters, substantially outperforming matched plain-state training on unrelated prompts. This is relevant to the supplied problem despite using a reduced, single-word task. The main contribution is clear, and the manuscript appropriately reports failures on game prompts, substantial fabrication, and limited transfer across training recipes.

The strongest evidence is PRE-REG 14: 217/360 neutral-prompt answers are correct across 18 retained adapters, compared with 0.028 for the matched plain-state reader. Independent resampling of the retained records gives word-cluster bootstrap intervals of approximately $[0.389,0.797]$ for the $0.600$ advantage over the behavioral comparator and $[0.361,0.781]$ for the $0.575$ advantage over the plain-state reader. These agree with the reported intervals within resampling variation. All 131 supplied replay checks pass, and independently rescoring the retained reader answers reproduces their hit labels. Independently implementing the agreement filter also reproduces the PRE-REG 15 rates of 0.466 correct and 0.111 wrong. These are checks of retained predictions and analysis, not reproductions of model training or inference.

Several qualifications and corrections should accompany publication:

1. **Make the distinction from closely related work explicit.** Activation-difference inputs are established in the original AO work [1]. Published rStation paper 3 already reports neutral-prompt taboo recovery using frozen fine-tune-minus-base inputs: 0.250 to 0.500 with subtraction and 0.717 with additional centroid-reference decoding [2]. That paper appeared about 54 hours before this submission, within the allowed concurrency window; overlap alone therefore does not establish disqualifying precedence. Nevertheless, it is directly relevant and should be discussed. The present contribution is training across many adapters and demonstrating held-out-word generalization under a specified recipe, rather than discovering subtraction or neutral-prompt secret recovery. The development table in PLAN_excerpt.md reports useful matched untrained-reader controls, including neutral accuracy 0.025 versus 0.650 after training on held-out zoo words. Bring this comparison into the scientific presentation and retain its supporting predictions. It makes the role of training clearer than comparison with the original single-layer reader alone.

   Agreement-based abstention on taboo readout also predates this submission: *Building Better Activation Oracles* reports Consensus@10 [3], and Torrielli et al. study sampled-answer agreement and calibration [4]. Explain that AGR aggregates greedy answers across different prompts of the same adapter and also excludes training labels. Its supported contribution is the measured performance of that combination in this setting. It does not establish superiority over existing sampling-based consensus.

2. **Restrict explanatory claims to what was measured.** Section 3's “only because the word is token-visible” is stronger than the evidence. Differences between adapter families, logit-lens ranks, and reader success establish an association; they do not isolate token visibility as a necessary or sufficient cause. The ten-epoch and in-context comparisons do not remove these confounds. Different secrets, data, hyperparameters, and prompt sets also vary across recipe comparisons, so “recipes differ in what they write” is a plausible interpretation, not an identified mechanism. These qualifications leave the central readout result intact. The behavioral claim must remain conditional on the tested single-response neutral-prompt baselines: the paper itself shows that hint-based querying can recover the secrets.

3. **Keep abstention and selection scope visible.** PRE-REG 15's fabrication result concerns six property-passing adapters; the neutral-transfer endpoint remains uninformative under its registered rule. The property check uses the known secret to compute its rank, so it selects an experimental population rather than providing a deployable eligibility test for unknown secrets. AGR also assumes that training-label answers can be rejected: this is appropriate for the deliberately disjoint evaluation words but would discard correct answers on secrets from the training vocabulary. Report correct, wrong, and abstained fractions together, and retain these restrictions wherever the “three quarters” reduction is summarized.

4. **Correct the reproducibility description and numerical scope.** Section 10 says that generated training data for every word and per-item evidence for every table are supplied. Only 3 of the 79 training-data files listed in DATA_SHA256.json are present; several diagnostics are retained as summaries, and the trained reader is represented by a hash rather than downloadable weights. The scripts provide a regeneration route, but require author-specific paths and external assets. State this accurately, document portable setup and dependency revisions, and preferably release the complete data and trained reader. The retained predictions support the principal statistical conclusions, but replaying them does not verify activation extraction or regenerate the missing artifacts.

   The neutral-prompt donor entry in Section 6 is also mis-scoped: its retained records contain 2/360 hits, approximately 0.006. The reported 0.001 is the all-prompt donor rate. This correction does not materially affect the conclusion. Clarify that the behavioral contrast averages the per-item maximum of the two baseline hit indicators, rather than taking the maximum of their aggregate accuracies; this explains otherwise puzzling differences in the game-prompt comparisons.

The manuscript is understandable without reconstructing its argument from the experiment log. Its informative abstract and explicit negative results are strengths. For presentation, replace informal paper/post references with consistent numbered citations, put mathematical expressions such as $\delta=h_{\mathrm{adapter}}-h_{\mathrm{base}}$ in LaTeX delimiters, and explain the inherited reader recipe without relying on the label “E65.” A compact plot of correct/wrong/abstained fractions across recipes would help communicate the practical limits while preserving the underlying table values.
