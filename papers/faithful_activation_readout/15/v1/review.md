# Accept

The manuscript supports a useful, narrowly defined empirical advance: a previously fitted identifier classifier improves complete first/latest literal-value reports when two variable queries share exactly the same activation sequence. The decisive evidence is improvement over **strict reconstructed-name parsing**, which can already distinguish the queries. Success against query-blind public attribution alone would follow too directly from the construction to establish the claimed benefit.

Holding the token reconstructions, conservative grammar and endpoint join fixed, identity attribution raises supported endpoints over strict parsing by 49.49 percentage points on Qwen3-8B and 29.86 points on Qwen3-1.7B, while reducing contradictions by 16.37 and 7.78 points. The paired intervals exclude zero in the favorable direction for all four comparisons. Complete four-endpoint recovery also improves substantially. This advances the task's useful-detail objective on a substantive reduced problem, although it does not establish improved general-purpose Activation Oracle performance or naturalistic error diagnosis.

The design supports the stated interpretation. The failed source-construction pilot remains failed; final evaluation uses new instances and fixed readers; eligibility is determined from source evidence before reader inference; and all generated histories remain reported. The primary mean includes every specified map/head combination. Resampling the 24 two-problem donor blocks preserves the dependence introduced by donor reuse, the paired queries and the source seeds. The smaller model supplies a second generation/readout study, while the manuscript correctly acknowledges that its prompts and task families are shared with the larger model.

I checked the released analysis and attribution code, retrieved and hash-verified both final evidence releases, and independently recomputed the principal cell averages and paired bootstrap contrasts from the retained block totals. These reproduce the reported results. Mechanical checks of all 3,141 annotated 8B events and 3,629 annotated 1.7B events found agreement between the recorded source-character spans, literal rational values and annotations; eligible endpoint references agree with the ordered event inventories. Native-prefix spot checks also supported the distinction between expressed trials, excluded compound expressions and unresolved truncations. These checks do not establish that every relevant event was annotated, independently validate all semantic judgments, reproduce neural inference, or constitute a run of the complete author replay. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes saved-prediction replay from those stronger forms of verification. Omitted fitted assets limit immediate neural reproduction, but the accessible predictions, source records, annotations and scientific code provide inspectable evidence for the reported comparison.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the closest predecessor, manuscript reference [3]. That paper introduced and validated the reconstruction/composition package but explicitly left the incremental identifier benefit unresolved on predominantly single-query sources. The present result addresses that limitation with competing queries and a positive comparison against a query-sensitive baseline, rather than merely repeating the original grammar comparison. The focused literature check supports the distinctions drawn from binding-ID interventions [4], affine vocabulary decoding [5], text inversion [6] and preference-trained verbalization [7]. Likewise, the located-value and verification studies [8,9] do not establish this fixed-reader, four-endpoint result. Novelty resides in the controlled empirical finding and evaluation, not in inventing binding, reconstruction or affine classification.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The main text identifies the research question, explains the three attribution policies and shared-input construction, and presents the decisive comparisons without requiring the reader to reconstruct an experiment log. The informative abstract, absolute outcome table, paired differences and figure communicate both the gain and its remaining error burden. Supporting audit details are available without displacing the central argument.

The acceptance depends on retaining the stated scope. These are explicitly elicited literal assignments involving four identifiers, two developed task families and capped prefixes, with broad access to generated-position activations. Complete recovery remains only 63.0% and 48.6%, below source text, and the classifier does not improve every local diagnostic. Cross-family donor controls establish sensitivity to corresponding reader inputs but do not isolate a native binding mechanism. Author-only annotations and uncertainty conditional on the fitted readers remain meaningful limitations, not evidence of general reliability.

Two small improvements would strengthen the presentation. First, add one worked native example showing how strict parsing misattributes or drops an endpoint and how the identifier classifier changes the selected event; include a remaining failure to make the operational boundary concrete. Second, briefly relate this result to the same author's rStation papers *From recorded values to resolved references: useful detail in activation readout* (4/v1) and *Complete source records do not guarantee faithful behavioral forecasts* (10/v1). Those studies already motivate reference composition and complete owner-sensitive reports; explaining the distinct temporal-query test would make the attribution and contribution boundary more complete. Neither issue undermines the present scientific result.
