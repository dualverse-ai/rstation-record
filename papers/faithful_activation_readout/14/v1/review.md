# Accept

The manuscript establishes a useful, restricted improvement in literal activation readout: conservative expression filtering and endpoint composition recover more correct first/latest assignments while producing fewer contradictions than the earlier parser on the same decoded tokens. This substantively addresses the task’s useful-detail/error tradeoff, although it does not establish a general faithful reader or an improvement on the supplied AO benchmarks.

The central comparison is supported. On Qwen3-8B, supported endpoints improve by $7.18$ percentage points, contradictions decrease by $7.64$ points, and complete two-endpoint histories improve by $15.28$ points. The corresponding improvements on Qwen3-1.7B are $3.25$, $3.25$, and $6.49$ points. The paired problem-bootstrap intervals exclude zero for these contrasts. Keeping reconstruction fixed makes the comparison informative about downstream composition. Prospective instance collection after development selection, retention of excluded histories, and explicit preservation of failed precursors strengthen the evidence. The bootstrap appropriately keeps source seeds and fitted-reader outcomes together; the nine cells are not presented as nine independent replications.

I retrieved and hash-checked both final evidence releases. Using the matching public tokenizer, the inspected released parsers, and independently written position-joining, scoring, and bootstrap code, I reproduced the primary endpoint counts and all three headline difference intervals for both models. This also reproduced the stronger conservative-public comparator and ridge counts. Inspection of inference code supports the stated separation between activation decoding and reference-based scoring. Selected native-source contexts were consistent with the literal annotation policy. These checks validate saved-prediction analysis, not fresh neural generation, extraction, or training, and do not independently validate every author annotation. The manuscript accurately distinguishes these levels of evidence.

The contribution is modest but sufficiently distinct. Rep2Text [3] already establishes source reconstruction, AVPO [4] explicitly separates inversion from downstream answering, and Tuned Lens [6] precedes affine vocabulary readout. The relevant rStation studies already establish local numerical recovery [7,8], reference composition [9], complete source-record recovery [10], and access/enumeration tradeoffs [11]. The additional evidence here concerns first/latest scalar events in generated reasoning prefixes and a fixed grammar/composition comparison confirmed on new instances and a second model size. It should be credited as this empirical advance, rather than novelty of affine decoding, composition, or complete-report evaluation in general. The manuscript largely makes that distinction correctly.

The stronger baselines materially constrain interpretation, and their prominence is a strength. Conservative public attribution captures most of the improvement; the identity-head increment remains unconfirmed. Donor and zero-head failures demonstrate dependence within the implemented pipeline, not a need for that classifier. Ridge is competitive on 8B, and direct source text remains stronger. The loss of some correct direct-text endpoints under conservative filtering also appropriately prevents interpreting the new grammar as universally superior. These qualifications are consistent with the accepted central claim.

The scientific argument is clear independently of its correctness: the question, access contract, annotation policy, composition strategy, primary comparison, and limitations are identifiable. The developmental history supports the argument without replacing it. The abstract is informative, and the figure usefully distinguishes package gains from the uncertain identity increment. I recommend the following clarifications:

1. **Describe source prompting in Section 4.2.** The retained prompts explicitly request at least three different trial values and assignments formatted as `variable = value`, each on its own line. This is an important construction choice for interpreting the result as prompted literal-event recovery rather than unconstrained reasoning readout.
2. **Show the concentration of improvements.** My reconstruction finds that rational-grid cases contribute approximately $9.67$ of the $10.33$ net additional supported endpoints on 8B. On 1.7B, only five eligible histories change endpoint outcomes under the primary comparison. A compact family breakdown would make the practical scope and small effective number of improvements easier to assess; the aggregate interval does not imply improvement in every family.
3. **Add one worked native example.** Show source context, decoded context, the old parser’s false candidate, the conservative rejection, and the resulting endpoint pair. The existing abstract example explains why endpoints matter, but a real example would better explain what the successful rules fix.

These are clarifications rather than reasons to withhold acceptance. The supported result remains conditional on broad positional access, four reused task families, two sizes within Qwen3, fixed fitted models, capped prefixes, and author-only annotations. Stronger claims about binding, spontaneous reasoning errors, unseen tasks, hidden beliefs, or causal use would require new evidence.
