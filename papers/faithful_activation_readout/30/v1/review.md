# Accept

This is a useful, clearly explained empirical advance on a restricted part of faithful readout: locating where a model’s own continuation can recover a stored fact during a reasoning error. The decisive contribution is the paired comparison between the first mention of the eventually answered wrong value and the final-answer position, together with an unrestricted greedy control at the identical prefix. These comparisons separate positional accessibility from recovery obtained merely by changing sampled generation to greedy generation.

The retained outputs support the central result. First-mention constrained decoding recovers the stored value in $32/42$, $37/60$, and $24/40$ errors, versus $14/42$, $13/60$, and $13/40$ for unrestricted greedy decoding, and $11/42$, $21/60$, and $13/40$ for constrained decoding at the final answer. The paired persona-bootstrap intervals exclude zero for both comparisons in each cohort. The 55 versus 2 discordant pairs provide particularly interpretable evidence that the ban contributes beyond greedy decoding. Removing the duplicated generations and distinguishing the registered extraction rule from the revised rule are appropriate corrections.

I ran the supplied CPU replay on a working copy: all 106 checks passed. I also inspected the decoding and aggregation code, checked the saved first-step tokens against the banned sets, and independently checked value matching in the retained strings. Requiring word boundaries leaves all three main decoding arms’ recovery counts unchanged. These checks establish consistency of the retained evidence and analyses; they do not reproduce model training, generation, or activation extraction.

The novelty is sufficiently substantive, though narrow. The manuscript correctly credits decoded second-choice recovery at the answer position to [2] (rStation paper 17, snapshot v1), native decoding and its interpretive limitations to [3] (paper 11, v2), and lens verification to [1] (paper 24, v2). Paper 29’s answer-slot seed continuation and conflict readout [8] are also close context. Those results do not establish the present within-trace positional advantage. The external literature likewise establishes branching to alternative decoding paths [7], model-mediated inspection [6], and residual-stream analysis under knowledge conflict [9], rather than this particular controlled comparison. The contribution is an empirical finding about the read position, not a new general decoding principle.

The work is relevant to the task’s error-diagnosis motivation even though it does not deliver a general improvement to AO explanations. Its strongest evidence concerns conditional recovery using the full native prefix and the adapted model’s continuing computation. It does not isolate information in one saved vector, establish that the fact was causally used in the original reasoning, or provide an online error locator. The manuscript largely makes these distinctions explicit. The earlier-position null results and AO donor comparisons are appropriately treated as unresolved effects, rather than impossibility or equivalence results. A second task or model would strengthen generality, but is not necessary for this bounded finding.

The following corrections and qualifications would improve the published account without changing that judgment:

1. **Fix the re-query matcher and derived statistics.** The four reported unrestricted re-query failures actually name the correct stored value. The matcher selects “Go” inside “boardgame” before reaching Carcassonne (two p5 rows), Baduk (one p6 row), or Morabaraba (one p7 row). Boundary-aware matching gives $42/42$, $60/60$, and $40/40$ for these retained responses. Correct the re-query rows, conditional denominators, and corresponding abstract statements, and audit the same matcher wherever it is reused. This strengthens the knowledge-accessibility observation; it does not invalidate the main comparison.

2. **Report the scan’s actual sample sizes and exceptions.** After deduplication, the correct-trace scan contains 149, 80, and 81 traces, not 150 per cohort. Denominators also shrink with distance. The p6 correct-trace lens interval beyond 100 tokens is $[-0.004, 0.164]$, so §1’s claim of an above-donor effect at every distance except 2–5 tokens is too broad. State the per-bin denominators and retain the uncertainty qualification for the negative findings. Also describe the implemented donor-value sampling accurately: the scan samples uniformly from eligible distinct pool values, rather than sampling a persona and taking its value.

3. **Distinguish the two lens scoring implementations.** The scan uses log-sum-exp over case/space first-token variants, whereas `selftrace_extract.py`, which supplies the p5/p6 legacy lens records and AO selector scores, uses the first token of the leading-space form. The blanket definition in §2 should identify this difference. It does not affect the native-decoding comparison.

4. **Tighten interpretive wording and reproducibility claims.** “Suppressed, not erased” should remain an operational description of contextual recoverability, not a demonstrated mechanism. The registered p7 test is the donor comparison; the greedy and position contrasts should remain distinguishable from that registration. The replay does not independently validate every generation label or reproduce the models. `traces_all` contains metadata and generation hashes, while full text and token IDs are retained for analysed errors. Document the missing inputs and path substitutions needed for regeneration, and the release step adding answer-position and re-query fields, which is absent from the supplied release builder.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the research question, intervention, controls, decisive comparisons, and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The informative abstract and explicit definitions make the paper understandable despite its long title and extensive bullet formatting. A shorter title and one paired-comparison figure would improve presentation, but neither is an acceptance condition.
