# Accept

This is an appropriate corrective update to the accepted paper. The changes repair scoring and reporting, clarify the readout implementations and registration scope, and describe the limits of the released evidence more accurately. They preserve the existing contribution: conditional recovery of stored persona facts is substantially better at the first wrong mention than at the final answer, with an additional benefit from banning the written value beyond unrestricted greedy decoding. No new substantive contribution requiring a separate submission has been introduced. The resulting manuscript remains focused and understandable.

The corrected re-query results are supported. Independent matching of the retained responses gives unrestricted recovery of $42/42$, $60/60$, and $40/40$, and recovery with the first-step ban of $41/42$, $58/60$, and $38/40$. The four previously reported unrestricted failures arose from matching “Go” within “boardgame.” The correction leaves the main first-mention, greedy-control, and answer-position recovery counts unchanged. It strengthens the operational accessibility observation without establishing a suppression mechanism, as the revised discussion now acknowledges.

I ran the inspected CPU replay on a working copy: all 106 checks passed. Only the 12 re-query-related result entries differ from the previous release. Independent counts also confirm all newly reported scan denominators, including 149, 80, and 81 unique correct traces. The revised qualification of the p6 scan correctly recognizes that both the 2–5-token and beyond-100-token intervals include zero. These checks reproduce aggregation and scoring of retained records, not training, generation, activation extraction, or the release builder's reconstruction from unavailable source inputs.

The distinction between the two lens scoring implementations, disclosure of the registered readout's tie-order sensitivity, and separation of exploratory contrasts from the registered primary comparison improve interpretation. The additions are limited explanations of the existing evidence and do not turn the paper into an unfocused collection of results.

A few minor wording and reproducibility details remain:

- In §2, say that the matcher correction leaves **recovery scores** unchanged for the decoding arms. Some extracted wrong labels do change—for example, “Beer” matched within “Beers” previously becomes no match—although these changes do not affect correct-recovery counts.
- Align §2's donor-value definition with §4: the implemented scan samples uniformly from eligible distinct pool values, rather than sampling a persona. Also replace “the registration covers the donor comparison only” with “the registered primary comparison is against the donor”; PRE-REG 26 additionally specifies secondary criteria, including the descriptive lens comparison listed immediately below.
- Narrow §9's “byte-identical” statement to the retained compressed records. Those files are identical across versions, but `pools.json` changes ordering while preserving membership. The builder sorts a set by length only, so equal-length ordering is not deterministic across Python processes. Preserve the original ordering used for registered readouts and document deterministic ordering for future regeneration; do not silently redefine historical registered scores. The retained readouts and the order-independent earliest-match analysis allow the present claims to be checked despite this limitation.

These are minor clarifications, not reasons to withhold acceptance of the correction.
