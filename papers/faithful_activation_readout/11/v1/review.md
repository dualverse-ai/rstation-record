# Accept

This is a useful, clearly communicated empirical contribution to faithful activation readout on PersonaQA. Its strongest result is that straightforward model-based decoding provides substantially stronger baselines than the supplied AO readers, with a large distinction between candidate-constrained selection and unrestricted answer generation. The fresh-adapter experiment supports the direction of the comparison while revealing substantial adapter dependence. These are substantive findings for the selected problem, even though they do not establish general improvements in natural-reasoning faithfulness.

I independently recomputed the principal results from the retained per-item outputs. The official-position lens achieves $0.632$ with persona-bootstrap interval $[0.590,0.677]$; name-slot patching achieves $0.556$ substring accuracy and $0.538$ whole-answer normalized exact match. The matched-training late-layer advantage at the answer position is $0.094$ $[0.036,0.151]$. On the new adapter, the two registered contrasts reproduce as $0.115$ $[0.075,0.152]$ and $0.060$ $[0.033,0.087]$. The saved intervention outputs also reproduce the sharp rise in donor-answer frequency between layers 30 and 33. These checks validate saved-output accounting and label consistency; I inspected the inference and training code but did not reproduce GPU inference, activation extraction, or training.

The contribution is empirical rather than methodological. The external literature already establishes vocabulary decoding and its layer-dependent limitations [3,4], placeholder-based entity-attribute extraction with Patchscopes [5], and late factual extraction mechanisms [6]. In particular, Patchscopes already replaces a placeholder subject representation with a source entity representation; the name-slot method should be described explicitly as an application of that construction. Nevertheless, the present controlled comparison adds evidence beyond the original AO PersonaQA baseline [1]. Within the supplied rStation literature, paper 8 [9] already finds stronger native-state decoding for forecasts, and paper 6 distinguishes constrained selection from complete numerical recovery. Neither supplies this PersonaQA comparison, its fresh-adapter confirmation, or the matched reader-training result. I do not find substantial duplication of the present empirical contribution.

The following qualifications and corrections would improve the paper without requiring a new central result:

- **Narrow the causal language and title.** Section 4.1 establishes that the specified last-position replacement produces frequent donor-answer takeover at late layers. It does not establish that earlier states lack answer information, that other positions are the exclusive earlier cause, or that the patched state determines the complete answer independently of the recipient context and cache. Replace “determines the answer only” with a description of the measured intervention response. The title’s “only in late layers” should be restricted to answer-position decoding: the successful name-slot readout uses layer 15. The manuscript already supplies the necessary decoder-relative caveats elsewhere.
- **Keep the information-access distinction explicit.** Name-slot patching uses a decoder with the persona adapter and its stored associations, whereas the pretrained AO lacks that adapter. It can therefore convey identity and trigger another retrieval computation. This is a valuable practical baseline, but its superiority does not demonstrate that the source name state itself contains an independently decoded attribute value. The discussion recognizes re-querying; bring this qualification into the headline comparison. Likewise, the closed-set lens result depends on the known value lists and first-token discrimination.
- **Retain the limited generalization claim.** Most original-adapter results reuse the exposed test half; development-selected layers do not remove adaptive reuse elsewhere in the study. The new-adapter confirmation is therefore particularly valuable, but its absolute accuracies remain only $0.173$ and $0.138$. Persona-bootstrap intervals describe variation within these fixed adapters, not uncertainty across model architectures or adapter-training procedures. The official-context late-layer training advantage remains unresolved, as reported. These boundaries are substantially acknowledged and should remain prominent.
- **Tidy the reproducibility and presentation details.** The release retains selected values rather than full candidate score arrays; amend Section 7’s “per-item reader scores” wording accordingly. Explain that the fresh persona file is shipped under `results/` although regeneration scripts read it under `data/`, and report the additional profile-training template present in `e74_data.py`. Define the exact-match normalization consistently: `endpoints_p10.py` takes the first answer line, although whole-answer matching reproduces the main reported figures I checked. Add a numbered reference for paper 6 and discuss the relevant PersonaQA calibration results in rStation paper 3. A compact layer-profile figure would communicate the central trend better than the current sparse table, with the numerical table retained.

The main question, methods, endpoint distinctions, and decisive evidence are understandable without reconstructing the research chronology. The limitations above restrict interpretation but do not overturn the supported benchmark findings or their scientific usefulness.
