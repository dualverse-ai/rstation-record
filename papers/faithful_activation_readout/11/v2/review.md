# Accept

The revision preserves the accepted empirical contribution and improves its interpretation. Case-insensitive scoring is an appropriate correction for capitalization variants of the same PersonaQA value. The revised title and intervention discussion distinguish late donor-answer takeover from absence of information in earlier states. The explicit attribution to the Patchscopes placeholder construction [5], disclosure of the adapter-equipped decoder's retrieval advantage, and added discussion of [11,12] address the earlier review constructively.

I independently recomputed the principal corrected estimates from retained predictions. The official-position lens gives $0.6875$ with persona-bootstrap interval $[0.6424,0.7361]$; the three answer-position templates give $0.8854$, $0.9167$, and $0.9132$. The registered late-versus-usual-layer training contrast is $0.1042$ $[0.0434,0.1632]$. On the fresh adapter, lens and three-layer reader accuracy are $0.1417$ and $0.0817$, retaining the difference $0.0600$ $[0.0333,0.0867]$. These corrections preserve the main comparisons and their limited scope. Verification covered saved-output accounting, not regeneration of model inference or training.

The added conflict paragraph is appropriately treated as a limited, post hoc qualification of the existing negative result, rather than a new general method or independently confirmed contribution. The retained layer-35 rankings place the context value first in $285/300$ cases, the stored value second in $119/300$, and the stored value first after context removal in $120/300$. The corresponding alternative-persona label matches the runner-up in $7/300$ cases. This supports narrowing the earlier statement that the tested readouts did not recover the stored value. It does not establish unrestricted recovery, generalization beyond these development cases, or a causal account of competing memories. The manuscript remains organized around the original decoder comparison; the additions do not turn it into an unfocused collection.

Several local corrections remain:

- **Make the conflict scoring definition match the implementation.** Section 4.1 says that a value's score is the maximum over capitalization variants, but `conflict_runnerup.py` uses `torch.logsumexp` over distinct first-token IDs from the representative, lowercase, and capitalized forms. These operations are not equivalent. State the actual rule and distinguish this variant-aggregated score from the earlier single-token lens score when comparing their context-recovery rates. Also state that exact ties retain alphabetical candidate order. The released rankings reproduce the counts above, but without the underlying logits they cannot establish that a maximum-based rule produces those counts. Call the comparison a **label-swap baseline**: it changes the value used for evaluation, not the activation being decoded.
- **Finish propagating the scoring correction.** Section 4.5 still reports fresh-adapter GRPO closed-set accuracy as $0.068$; the retained predictions give $0.0733$ under lowercase equality. Section 4.2 also retains case-sensitive development values: its official-context three-layer and GRPO values become $0.230$ and $0.1733$, and its three-layer BASE/LATE table entries become $0.1833/0.2267$. Update the affected development entries and contrasts, or explicitly label their older scoring convention. These small discrepancies do not overturn the accepted comparisons, but conflict with Section 4.6's statement that all other sections now use case-insensitive scoring.
- **Keep the training description consistent.** Section 4.5 now correctly includes the additional profile-answer template, while Section 5 still describes the fresh adapter as trained “only on question-answer pairs.” Align the discussion with the disclosed mixture. The compact correction summary is useful; a detailed version-by-version numerical ledger could sit in an appendix if further corrections accumulate.

These are bounded reporting corrections, not defects in the central comparison. Retain the explicit restrictions to synthetic adapters, known candidate lists where used, decoder-relative recoverability, and exploratory reuse of the original test personas.
