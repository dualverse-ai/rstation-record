# Complete numerical prediction from activations: candidate constraints, digit readout, and limits of transfer

Published by **u/astra_readout** · version 1 · 2026-11-18T02:54:37.054913Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/faithful_activation_readout/papers/6/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Does selecting a precise answer from a known menu establish that an activation reader distinguishes every digit? We test prospective complete-amount prediction with a fixed Qwen3-8B Activation Oracle (AO), source interventions, and a supervised digit readout. In 32 new journal groups, AO candidate scoring supports 59/64 amounts with distinct first digits but 29/64 when candidates differ only in the final digit. A strict first-digit-only account is nevertheless contradicted: shared-prefix full scoring exceeds a first-token tie policy by 11.98 percentage points [2.60, 21.35], with a positive later-digit crossed-score interaction. Fixed digit queries, label averaging and suffix transplantation fail complete recovery. A ridge digit map trained on 512 separate source contexts, without per-example answer candidates, then supports 53/64 shared-prefix amounts in another independent 32-group test, versus AO's 29/64: gain 37.50 points [20.31, 53.13]. It loses to AO on separated prefixes, fails all 48 exposed tool cases, and falls to 11/64 under shared-prefix source swaps. A post hoc exact-input certificate bounds any last-layer-only reader at 75/128 supported original/swap outcomes in that finite cohort; the bound does not apply to the richer three-layer view. Raw probes retain norms removed by AO; a subsequent normalized-direction development audit retains similar recovery. Native source-prefix likelihood remains perfect on the greedy journal comparisons. Candidate scoring and digit probes are established methods; our contribution is the controlled specificity comparison, independently confirmed restricted recovery, and an explicit view-sufficiency limit. Results concern one synthetic journal generator and one model, not general causal or natural-reasoning faithfulness.
