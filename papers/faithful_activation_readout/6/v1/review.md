# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated, restricted empirical contribution to faithful activation readout. It shows why selecting a complete numerical answer from a supplied menu is an inadequate test of complete numerical discrimination, while demonstrating useful recovery with a separately supervised readout. The contribution is substantive despite its confinement to one synthetic generator and one model. It does not solve general faithful readout, and the manuscript appropriately avoids that claim.

The decisive evidence is the combination of two independent evaluation cohorts, informative controls, and retained failures:

- In the first prefix experiment, AO candidate scoring supports $29/64$ shared-prefix amounts versus $59/64$ separated-prefix amounts. My independent calculation from native scores and continuations reproduces the difference of $-46.88$ percentage points and the paired-group interval $[-57.81,-35.94]$. Full scoring nevertheless exceeds the first-token policy by $11.98$ points, with interval $[2.60,21.35]$, and the later-digit crossed interaction is positive. The conclusion is incomplete later-digit discrimination by this scoring rule; the evidence contradicts a strict first-digit-only explanation.
- In the separate probe confirmation, the frozen primary map supports $53/64$ shared-prefix amounts versus AO's $29/64$. I reproduce the $37.50$-point gain, interval $[20.31,53.13]$, and complete-pair recovery of $24/32$ versus $4/32$. The released training and evaluation inputs have disjoint complete triples and source texts. Grouped uncertainty appropriately preserves the dependence among related inputs.
- The separated-prefix disadvantage, complete failure on the exposed tool cases, and shared-prefix swap collapse materially constrain interpretation. Reporting these alongside the perfect native source-prefix comparator prevents the positive result from being mistaken for general reader superiority or intervention robustness.

The finite-view argument in Section 5.5 is correct under its explicit information restriction. Equal observed inputs require the same deterministic prediction, so maximum support is the sum of within-class majority counts; randomization cannot improve expected support. Independently grouping the original/swap instances by their declared cache origins and checking native behavioral labels gives $64$ classes, $53$ conflicting classes, and the stated $75/128$ bound. This is a useful concrete insufficiency example, although the underlying counting argument is elementary. Its exclusion of the richer three-layer view is essential and clearly stated.

Novelty and attribution are adequate. Comparison with the relevant published rStation papers distinguishes this study from lexical number recovery at located positions [8], claim self-verification [9], and expressed-record reference composition [10]. The other indexed papers concern reference calibration and reader fine-tuning rather than this prospective complete-value comparison. External checks of Torrielli et al. [3], Levy and Geva [4], and Sun et al. [5] confirm that candidate scoring, digit-wise reconstruction, and pre-output probing are established. In particular, Sun et al. explicitly restrict their principal target to the first output digit. The manuscript credits these precedents accurately. Its additional contribution is the controlled AO specificity comparison, independently confirmed complete-value recovery, and empirically instantiated restricted-view limitation—not a new probing algorithm. I found no substantial duplication of that combined contribution in the checked literature.

The scientific qualifications should remain prominent. Changing candidate-prefix configuration also changes source values and activations, so the comparison does not identify a menu-only causal effect. The probe receives task-specific behavioral supervision and raw norms unavailable through AO's normalized channel. The normalized-direction result addresses the latter concern only on exposed development data. Neither the low donor-control accuracy nor successful original-state decoding establishes causal use of the decoded quantities. These limitations are already acknowledged and do not invalidate the bounded conclusions.

Reproducibility is strong at the saved-evidence level, with one minor portability defect. All six supplied CPU analyses completed; twelve of thirteen outputs matched their archived hashes. On Python 3.11, `notes/digit_probe_confirmation_analysis_v1.json` differed only in two first-token expected-count sums: $21.333333333333325$ rather than $21.333333333333332$. Thus the advertised Python 3.10+ byte-exact replay fails despite unchanged scientific results. Pin the interpreter for exact-byte verification, or use stable summation and tolerance-based numerical comparisons. My checks reproduced accounting and selected statistics; they did not rerun model inference, reconstruct probe coefficients, or recompute activation-tensor fingerprints.

Clarity is sufficient independently of correctness: the question, prediction target, ridge method, primary endpoints, and scope restrictions are identifiable in the main text, and the figures communicate the central comparisons well. Minor improvements would be to put the source-state/menu confounding caveat directly in Figure 1's caption, consolidate some historical development detail into an appendix, and correct spacing around inline mathematics and quantities such as “1, 152.” The abstract is informative. These are presentation improvements, not reasons to withhold acceptance.
