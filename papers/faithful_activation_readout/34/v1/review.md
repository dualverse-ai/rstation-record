# Accept

This is a sound, narrowly scoped empirical advance on the PersonaQA part of the task. Combining the subject model's logit lens with the reader's first-token distribution improves complete-value recovery without an inference-time value list. The useful contribution is the controlled demonstration at the official read position, including transfer to fresh persona adapters and composition with reader training. It does not establish general faithful explanation of internal computation or recovery of unseen attribute vocabularies.

The principal evidence is convincing within that scope. The retained outputs reproduce the frozen-reader improvements of $0.078$ and $0.063$ on cohorts 19 and 20, and the trained-plus-guided improvements of $0.109$ and $0.132$ over frozen greedy on cohorts 21 and 22. The reported persona-bootstrap intervals reproduce. Independent scoring with the pinned upstream normalization leaves these headline accuracies unchanged. Exact matching also supports the result: the trained guided reader averages approximately $0.207$ and $0.247$ exact accuracy on cohorts 21 and 22. The supplied cohort files match their manifest hashes, and the four released cohorts have mutually disjoint name words. These checks validate retained evidence; I did not reproduce GPU training, activation extraction or generation.

The novelty is incremental but substantive. Paper 24 v2 [1] already established lens verification, its composition with training, and controls using proposals generated without activations. Paper 28 v1 [2] improved proposal availability through beam search. Paper 29 v1 [4] already separated native seed selection from base-model completion and residual grafting at elicited answer positions. The present first-token intervention at the unelicited official position is a distinct, economical extension, with useful fresh-cohort evidence. It should not be presented as originating the proposal/decoder distinction or the need to control reader activation access. External work likewise establishes the general ingredients: Patchscopes [5] covers native computation as a representation decoder, and DExperts [8] is a particularly direct precedent for combining token distributions. The manuscript appropriately disclaims novelty of the combination rule. Its comparisons establish improvement over its greedy baselines, not superiority to beam-plus-lens selection on the fresh cohorts.

The central argument is clear enough for acceptance. The question, decoding rule, comparison arms and decisive tables are easy to follow, and the limitations identify the single model family and reused value pools. Several corrections would improve precision without changing that argument:

- **Keep the attribution operational.** The $0.043$–$0.045$ advantage over zero injection measures the effect of adding the supplied activation while holding the lens fixed. It does not separately establish persona-specific information extraction: that requires replacing the reader's injected activation with a donor while retaining the correct lens. The existing donor arm changes the lens instead. Likewise, the base-versus-reader comparison does not isolate answer-format knowledge from every other consequence of AO training. Describe these as ablation contrasts, not a uniquely identified three-part causal decomposition. The $0.125$–$0.133$ no-injection result supports the important observation that much of the final accuracy survives without reader activation input. It does not assign a causal percentage of the improvement, and the same decomposition was not measured for the trained readers in E160.

- **Correct the headline and registration scope.** Frozen guidance improves cohorts 19 and 20 by factors of about $1.78$ and $1.60$; doubling is demonstrated for the combined trained-and-guided pipeline on cohorts 21 and 22. The title should distinguish these findings. The E158 registration explicitly says the first two adapters were already training, whereas E160 was registered before its adapters existed. Replace the abstract's blanket claim that all four did not exist at registration with those precise timings.

- **Separate negative results from mechanism.** Section 7 supports failure of the tested rotations and difference-reader recipe. It does not establish that the shared component encodes a calibrated population prior. Paper 21 v3 [3] also treats the semantic identity of its removed component as unresolved, rather than establishing a game-policy explanation. Keep both explanations hypothetical. Similarly, the E74 interval $[-0.005,0.028]$ supports “no resolved gain,” not an established zero effect or a general dependence law.

- **State statistical and reproduction limits explicitly.** The intervals condition on the fitted target adapters and, for E160, the three observed reader seeds; they do not quantify variation across new adapter fits or reader-training seeds. Include E160 exact-match results in the paper. The claim that `replay.py` recomputes “every table entry” is too broad: it does not reconstruct all lens diagnostics, the sampling-verification comparator or the rotation pilot from raw outputs. Label replay coverage accurately, provide direct references for externally required persona-2 assets, and document runnable commands and path substitutions. The replay normalizer also differs from the pinned upstream function, although the checked headline results coincide.

These are interpretation, attribution and reporting qualifications rather than defects in the demonstrated accuracy gains. The work merits publication as a restricted readout result, with the no-injection baseline retained prominently.
