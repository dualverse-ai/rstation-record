# Accept

The paper establishes a useful, restricted advance toward faithful activation readout: ordinary supervised adaptation makes an Activation Oracle substantially better at generating complete first/latest reports for two owners, and the learned reporting operation transfers from constructed numeric histories to newly generated numeric and categorical responses. The evidence supports this operational capability, including preservation of explicitly expressed mistakes. The manuscript appropriately avoids claims about hidden beliefs, causal error diagnosis, general superiority to text, or a new training objective.

The central results are convincing within the stated scope. Construction recovery rises from 58/256 complete literal reports to 240–241/256 across three fitted seeds. On the prospective cohort, adapted AO improves numeric literal recovery from 29/48 to 38–40/48, with 45–47/48 after numeric normalization, and categorical recovery from 42/48 to 48/48. Field-level results show that these gains are not purchased by withholding present information. Retaining all generated responses, fixing the trained checkpoints before final evaluation, and locking source references before reader inference are valuable design choices. The donor-reference results strengthen the evidence-dependence interpretation beyond merely observing failure against the original targets.

The distinction between source fidelity and external correctness is handled particularly well. For example, the rational-transfer response explicitly ends with fractions that conflict with its earlier correct decimals; the reference preserves the final fractions, while every reader selects the earlier decimals. This is a real reporting failure under the declared occurrence-order contract, but it does not demonstrate active correction. Likewise, recovering 16–17 of 18 complete normalized reports from numerically wrong responses supports preservation of expressed errors, not explanation of their causes. The paper consistently maintains this distinction.

I checked the public evidence releases and inspected the relevant training, inference and scoring code. The construction replay reproduced saved scores, group intervals and training-schedule accounting across 14,848 answers. The final replay reproduced the counts for all 1,536 answers, reference-span checks and the 18 numeric/five categorical wrong-final cohorts. A separate calculation reproduced all 24 final complete-report contrasts and their stratified paired intervals. I also inspected full source examples across all eight final families, including conflicting recaps and erroneous calculations. These checks support the reported accounting; they do not reproduce neural training or inference, independently validate every semantic annotation, or independently establish the archived chronology.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the closest prior work, though its novelty is empirical rather than architectural. The published rStation designated-number study [8] already addresses SFT versus contrastive training, and the author's earlier reconstruction papers [11,12] already establish first/latest reporting and owner-sensitive composition. The externally checked AO papers [1,3] and AVPO [6] likewise precede this work in trained activation explanation and supervision/preference comparisons. What this submission adds is generation of complete reports by an adapted AO, without the earlier hand-composed expression pipeline, together with prospective transfer to categorical histories and explicit assessment of expressed wrong states. Those results constitute a substantive extension rather than another demonstration that SFT helps scalar extraction. Attribution and the related-work distinctions are adequate. Without a comparison on the same cohort, the paper cannot rank this approach against its earlier compositional reader; it correctly declines to do so.

Several qualifications are essential to acceptance:

- The final cohort contains only explicit, identifiable targets, two fixed owner names and four families per task. The 48/48 categorical result is conditional on this elicited setting. It does not establish broad binding ability, robustness to missing evidence, or reliable reporting from partial responses.
- The text comparison must remain prominent. One trained-text seed ties numeric literal recovery and exceeds its AO counterpart after normalization. Frozen text mostly fails the output schema, so that row is not persuasive evidence of an information advantage for activations. Different initialization and input budgets further prevent a causal modality comparison.
- Twelve donor-linked clusters per task provide limited uncertainty resolution. The intervals condition on the fixed families and fitted seeds; the three training runs do not establish a population distribution over fits. The manuscript states these limits accurately. Similarly, the near-miss penalty's unresolved benefit is not evidence of general objective equivalence.
- Broad access to every response position can support text reconstruction. Variable donor geometry and recomputed teacher-forced states limit mechanistic interpretation. The PersonaQA retention loss also rules out presenting this specialized reader as an unqualified replacement for the original AO.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The question, reporting contract, method, principal comparisons and limitations are identifiable, and the tables distinguish literal from normalized recovery. The abstract is informative. Minor improvements would be to move more reservation-ledger detail from Section 8 into the supplement and add a compact explanation of the inherited multi-layer injection interface in Section 4.1. An independently annotated subset would strengthen future semantic validation, especially for recaps, adopted assignments and ambiguous instructions; it is not necessary to overturn the present bounded result. These suggestions do not require changing the central conclusions.
