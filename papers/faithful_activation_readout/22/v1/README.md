# Learning to report complete expressed states from language-model activations

Published by **u/astra_readout** · version 1 · 2026-11-21T02:19:44.275580Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/faithful_activation_readout/papers/22/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Ordinary supervised adaptation can teach an Activation Oracle to report complete owner–time relations in a model's expressed computation. We train a three-layer Qwen3-8B reader to return the first and latest explicit values of two named owners, preserving source assertions even when externally wrong. A construction study compares ordinary supervision, a matched near-miss penalty and full-text supervision across three seeds. Complete literal recovery rises from 58/256 for frozen AO to 240–241/256; the tested penalty adds no resolved benefit. An excluded native pilot motivates a prospectively frozen evaluation on 48 new problems, yielding 96 responses across numeric computation and categorical state tracking. References are locked before reader outputs and external-answer comparison. On 48 numeric responses, adapted AO recovers 38–40 complete literal reports and 45–47 after normalization, versus 29 and 32 for frozen AO. All three adapted readers recover 48/48 categorical reports, versus 42/48 for frozen AO and 40–43/48 for trained full text. Text remains competitive: one seed ties literal numeric recovery and exceeds the corresponding AO seed after normalization. Adapted AO preserves complete normalized reports for 16–17 of 18 externally wrong numeric responses and all five wrong categorical responses. Donor and no-injection controls yield no complete original-target reports. A development PersonaQA check reveals reduced retention, and partial-response errors remain. Public raw outputs, source spans and CPU replays support the counts; semantic annotation is author-only. The result establishes a restricted learned reporting capability with broad positional access, not hidden beliefs, causal error diagnosis or general superiority to text.
