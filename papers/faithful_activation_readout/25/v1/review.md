# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical contribution to faithful activation readout. The central result is that accurate recovery of a fine-tune's assigned disposition does not establish that the fine-tune expresses that disposition in its tested replies. The comparison across matched specifications makes this distinction concrete: the neutral-prompt lens decodes all 24 TD and all 24 SDF units, although their measured behaviour differs substantially. The topic-minus-neutral variant also decodes all 24 SDF units. The result survives using the base reference: R1 recovers 23/24 TD and 24/24 SDF dispositions. Thus the principal observation is not dependent solely on the shared ensemble reference.

The calibrated text-reader contrast is consistent with this interpretation: TD accuracy is $16/24$, SDF accuracy is $7/24$, and the difference of recipe gaps is $-0.375$. This is a comparison of assigned-label recovery across recipes, not a demonstration that text is generally superior to activations or that activations lack behavioural information. The manuscript makes these distinctions, discloses the degenerate original comparator, and does not conceal the missed point prediction or the absence of resolved within-cell behaviour tracking. These qualifications are essential to acceptance.

The contribution is relevant to the supplied problem because it tests what an apparently accurate activation-derived claim actually warrants. It does not deliver improved hallucination–specificity performance on AObench or PersonaQA, but it supplies a substantive evaluation control. The main question, methods, evidence and limitations are sufficiently clear, despite some unnecessary prominence given to experiment identifiers.

Novelty is modest but sufficient. The published rStation papers [1, 2] recover taboo content without separating training descriptions from enacted behaviour. Minder et al. [5], *Narrow Finetuning Leaves Clearly Readable Traces in Activation Differences* (arXiv:2510.13900v3), already establish readable training-domain traces and warn about narrow-finetuning artifacts. Szablewski et al. [4], *The Model Organism Lottery* (arXiv:2607.01033), establish substantial recipe effects at matched behavioural strength. Neither general observation should be presented as new here. The additional evidence is the explicit low-expression SDF comparison over matched specifications, together with the calibrated reply-reader comparison and the limits of behaviour-strength prediction. This is a distinct, useful diagnostic rather than a new readout or calibration method. Section 2 should explain the connection to [5] more fully than its current one-line attribution.

Verification supports the reported arithmetic. After inspecting the relevant code, I ran the CPU replay: all 11 checks matched, including the nested 18-check replay. Independently applying the stated regular expressions to all 1,536 retained fine-tuned-model replies reproduced their saved hit labels. I also checked the base-reference decoding counts and retrieved both cited corpus attachments; their hashes and aggregate example counts agree with the manuscript. These checks reproduce analysis of retained outputs, not training, activation extraction or model generation. I inspected the ridge-probe code and summary but did not reproduce that experiment.

Several qualifications and small corrections would improve the paper:

- Keep behavioural absence explicitly operational. Six prompts, 120-token continuations and a keyword rubric cannot establish general absence. The source-blinded author-agent audit is helpful, but it does not establish the rubric's sensitivity across TD, SDF and students. The narrower conclusion about low measured expression is supported; stronger behavioural or causal conclusions would need broader semantic evaluation and fresh units.
- The statistical intervals and sign-test values should remain conditional summaries of this reused, designed grid. The fixed-grid qualification does not itself establish exchangeability for exact population inference. The observed paired counts carry the main conclusion; registration after cohort exposure does not supply independent confirmation.
- Distinguish a recipe comparison from an isolated intervention on description versus behaviour. TD and SDF also differ in chat formatting, loss masking and data content. Moreover, the SDF generator explicitly requests examples of things the assistant said, and the released documents contain quoted demonstrations. “Describe-only” should mean document-format training whose resulting model shows little measured expression, not training text devoid of behavioural examples. The failed E132 gate leaves the format explanation unresolved.
- Correct the reconstruction claim in §7. The cited archives contain concatenated `*_all.jsonl.xz` corpora and filename lists, but no per-unit record boundaries or identifiers. Variable TD/student dataset sizes prevent unambiguous splitting from those lists alone. Release per-unit files or a manifest with counts, offsets and hashes, and identify the missing inference helpers. The retained response and score evidence remains inspectable, so this packaging gap does not invalidate the finite-cohort result, but exact adapter reconstruction is not presently demonstrated.
- Clarify that R3 uses layers 24/30/33; §3's shared “layers 27–35” wording applies to the lens. In §6, describe E132 as reference-dependent decodability: success after subtracting one common reference does not by itself show that the reference manufactured unit-specific information. Also add the requested breakdown of training, extraction, generation and evaluation costs.

Acceptance is for the documented counterexample to interpreting disposition decoding as evidence of expressed behaviour, with the stated finite-cohort and measurement limits. It is not a claim of a general behavioural detector or a causal explanation of what these representations encode.
