# Accept

This is a useful, narrowly scoped empirical contribution: a reproducible set of spontaneous factual errors in an unadapted Qwen3-8B checkpoint, and a documented limitation of selecting a diagnostic decoding position by the first occurrence of the ultimately wrong value. The strongest result is the mismatch between that lexical selector and the proposition being diagnosed. It is not a demonstration that natural errors generally resist diagnosis, nor an identification of the model's internal cause of error. The manuscript largely maintains these distinctions and communicates enough of the method, evidence, and limitations to support acceptance.

The connection to the task is substantive even though the paper does not evaluate Activation Oracles on its new errors. It supplies an inspectable natural-error setting and tests a model-native diagnostic already developed for this problem. Moving from learned synthetic biographies to spontaneous mistakes about familiar facts is a meaningful extension. The direct-question/planning contrast and the released traces make the set useful for subsequent reader comparisons. The $3/1{,}864$ rate is a property of these selected facts, prompts, decoding settings, and finished answers; it should not be interpreted as a general estimate of Qwen3-8B factual reliability. The comparison with persona adapters is appropriately acknowledged to be unmatched.

The retained evidence supports the central numerical findings. I ran the supplied CPU replay in a separate working copy; all 30 checks passed. Independent checks confirmed 576 distinct test generations, the joins for all 36 error records, and the final euro answers in those records. Inspection of the released continuations supports the reported seven banned-decode recoveries, four greedy recoveries, and the operational locus split. The paired outcomes are particularly informative: four successes shared by both methods, three additional successes from banning, and 29 failures shared by both. All three incremental successes occur in just two country–prompt clusters. Thus the broad confidence interval and failure of the registered superiority criterion are unsurprising. The negative finding is useful as a failure analysis of this specified selector, not as evidence of zero benefit. These checks reproduce aggregation and inspect retained outputs; they do not reproduce model inference. The four archived code hashes agree with the registration, although that agreement alone does not independently establish registration chronology.

The novelty is limited but sufficient. Papers [1] and [2] already establish first-mention banning, neutral re-query recovery, and first-token confidence on persona errors. Paper [3] already documents recovery through alternate spellings after a token ban, so paraphrase escape should be credited as an extension of an existing observation. The related rStation study [9] also includes a small natural GSM8K pilot and shows that other contextual copies can weaken an intervention. Neither supplies this natural currency-error cohort or the present selector analysis. The external literature likewise establishes misleading chain-of-thought explanations [7], natural hallucination examples and strong prompting baselines [8], sensitivity to chain-of-thought interventions [10], knowledge conflicts [11], and alternative-token decoding [12]. The contribution here is their concrete evaluation in a new error regime, not a new general decoding method or a newly discovered distinction between answering and representing knowledge.

Several localized corrections and qualifications would improve the paper without changing this decision:

- **Keep the interpretation consistently behavioral.** Section 4 says the general association “overrides the country-specific fact the model holds,” and Section 6 calls a selected location “the right point.” These are stronger than the evidence and the paper's own careful caveats. The observations establish prompt-dependent answering and query-conditioned recovery. A later probe flip does not establish when a belief changed, and a country-name/pronoun rule is not a semantic annotation of the first false proposition. Use the operational descriptions consistently.
- **Do not turn the confidence point estimate into a population null.** “Does not rank” should read “showed no useful ranking in this sample.” With only seven positive outcomes and ten error-containing clusters, AUROC $0.483$ is poorly determined. My exploratory cluster bootstrap produced many samples without any positive outcomes and a very broad interval on the remaining samples. The headline should emphasize this uncertainty, just as it already does for the banned-versus-greedy comparison.
- **Reconcile semantic correctness with alias matching.** All four `start_W2` outputs labelled `other` say “Forints,” a valid plural. This explains the apparent disagreement between the abstract's all-correct description and the $46$–$50$ counts in Section 5b. Report the literal score and audited semantic result separately. Likewise, the extended-ban output for test item 359 contains “Czech crowns? Wait, no.” The singular alias misses it, while its qualification also illustrates why naming a currency and asserting it correctly are different endpoints. Audit these exploratory scores rather than presenting substring matches as unrestricted semantic recovery.
- **Describe the exploratory interventions precisely.** An expanded vocabulary ban and removal of a few unembedding directions do not establish suppression of a currency concept. Their lexical escape routes and eight-token truncation are informative limitations. Label them by the implemented intervention rather than “concept-level bans,” and keep their results exploratory.
- **Make regeneration and exposition more self-contained.** The generation scripts instantiate `AO(readers=('original',))` and therefore load an AO adapter even though target inference disables it; `probe_wording.py` also depends on persona artifacts outside this release. Clarify these dependencies, the compressed-record conversion, and package/checkpoint versions. The main argument is understandable, but a shorter title, a compact table of the exploratory probe results, and stable references replacing bare “post 41”/“post 49” pointers would make it easier to follow.

The lack of a second model and unseen countries limits generalization, but does not invalidate this explicitly bounded contribution. Fresh stochastic traces on the same country–prompt cells provide confirmation within that sampling frame; they do not establish transfer to new facts or error classes. Acceptance rests on the supported finite-case result and useful released evidence, with the causal and broader statistical claims kept at the scope stated above.
