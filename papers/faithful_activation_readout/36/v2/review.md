# Accept

This revision appropriately clarifies the accepted contribution without introducing a new substantive result. The central argument remains identifiable: natural currency errors provide a bounded test of first-mention decoding, and its lexical selector often precedes the assertion being diagnosed. The added table summarizes existing exploratory evidence rather than expanding the paper into a separate collection of experiments.

The principal changes improve scientific interpretation. Section 4 now distinguishes prompt-dependent answering from evidence about an internal fact being overridden. Section 5 correctly treats AUROC $0.48$ as an uncertain sample result with seven positive outcomes, rather than evidence of a population null. Section 5b describes the implemented vocabulary ban and unembedding-direction removal without claiming that either suppresses a currency concept. These qualifications preserve the useful finite-case failure analysis while avoiding stronger causal conclusions.

The revised alias accounting is supported by the retained outputs. My targeted inspection confirmed literal start-of-thinking scores of $50$, $50$, and $46$ out of $50$, with all four misses under the third wording beginning “Forints.” Reporting the audited naming count separately is appropriate. The expanded-ban record also contains “Czech crowns? Wait, no.” as described; its explicit retraction makes the distinction between naming and correctly asserting a currency necessary. The retained literal intervention totals remain $8/34$ and $13/34$. These checks concern archived outputs and aggregation, not regenerated model inference. The regeneration explanation also better distinguishes loading an adapter from using it during target inference, and acknowledges the missing persona dependencies.

Two minor corrections remain:

- **Correct the new attribution in Section 5b.** The explicit prior observation of a banned value reappearing under another spelling is documented in reference [3], *Wrong answers with known information conditions on Qwen3-1.7B persona adapters*, Section 4: it gives “Empañadas” after “Empanadas” was banned and quantifies these continuations. Cite [3] for that observation; [1] remains appropriate for the first-mention decoding method. The previous review likewise pointed to [3]. Adding references [9]–[12] to the bibliography would be more useful with a brief, relevant connection in the related-work discussion.
- **Make the new table self-contained.** Define “W0” as the quick-fact-check wording, and identify the three wordings in their reported order. Its denominators already distinguish the 50-error cohort from the 34 selected intervention cases; retain that distinction. Synchronize the manuscript heading with the revised metadata title.

The unchanged limitations remain decisive for interpretation: one model, a small targeted error cohort, query-conditioned probes, and wide uncertainty. The revision neither establishes a causal location of belief change nor demonstrates general diagnostic failure. Within those limits, the corrections strengthen the existing paper and are suitable for an accepted-paper update.
