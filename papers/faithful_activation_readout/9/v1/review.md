# Accept

This is a useful, narrowly scoped empirical follow-up. Continuing the readers' existing future-lens training improves lexical backtracking prediction, but the development advantage over window-text sampling on three sentence properties is not confirmed on fresh problems. The distinction between improving a reader and adding predictive value beyond a strong text baseline is scientifically meaningful for this task. The paper does not demonstrate reduced hallucination in general descriptions, recovery of hidden reasoning, or a solution to the broader faithful-readout problem.

The central numerical evidence is sound. I independently reconstructed the selection of all 700 final items from the retained traces, checked their three sentence labels, and recomputed the reported development and final AUC contrasts and problem-bootstrap intervals from the retained counts. I also reproduced the teacher-forced and forecast-verification statistics. In particular, the final sentence-property differences are $0.00385$ with interval $[-0.02855,0.03912]$ and $0.02513$ with interval $[-0.00917,0.06098]$. The sampled backtracking gains over the untrained readers are $0.0562$ and $0.0654$, with positive lower bounds. Broadening the backtracking label changes 19 negatives and preserves the teacher-forced training gains. These checks validate the arithmetic and item construction; they do not reproduce model training or inference, or independently validate extraction of sample counts from the omitted generated text.

Novelty is modest but sufficient for this bounded contribution. The closest published rStation paper, manuscript reference [1], already establishes the continuation-versus-question distinction, the untrained next-token advantage, and the absence of a detected sentence-level advantage. Reference [4] already studies training-target effects, and [6] studies claim verification. External checks of Activation Oracles [2], Building Better Activation Oracles [3], and Future Lens [5] confirm that forecasting from activations and future-lens training are established. In particular, [3, §3.3] already trains on on-policy reasoning continuations. The new contribution here is the controlled evaluation of an additional continuation-training run and its independent final-set test, including the failure of its development result to confirm. It is not a new training objective. The manuscript generally makes this distinction clear.

The presentation meets the clarity requirement: the question, intervention, controls, primary comparisons, and limitations are identifiable without reconstructing the research log. The abstract is informative, and the main text appropriately separates the confirmatory, secondary, and exploratory results. The following qualifications and corrections would improve the paper without changing the supported central conclusion.

1. **Keep the negative conclusion statistical and conditional.** “Not confirmed” is supported; equivalence to the window baseline is not. The three-layer interval still permits an advantage of approximately $0.061$ AUC. In §5, replace categorical language such as “this budget ... does not extract it” and “about what the window text already gives” with the measured absence of a detected advantage under this protocol. The stronger final-set baseline is an observation, not an identified explanation of the failed confirmation. Selecting and evaluating methods by contrasts involving that baseline can favor a development-specific advantage even without tuning the baseline itself. Two sets also do not establish that between-set variance exceeds the uncertainty represented by the bootstrap.

2. **Distinguish the development and final window controls.** Section 3 defines REAL-WIN as the REAL weights evaluated on window-only activations. However, the E58b registration specifies window-only activations during both training and evaluation, and `e58_train.py --acts win` implements that retraining. The final evaluator clearly uses fixed REAL weights for G4. Clarify which procedure produced the development rows and document any deviation. Until reconciled, the development and final contrasts should not be described as replications of exactly the same intervention. Even final G4 combines removal of earlier context with removal of chat framing, as the limitations acknowledge; it does not isolate earlier-context content alone.

3. **Narrow the forecast-verification interpretation.** Both Yes/No AUC intervals contain $0.5$, so “below chance” describes the point estimates, not an established anticorrelation. More importantly, comparison with [6] changes the weights through continuation fine-tuning, the read window, the question wording, and the score: the supplied code sums response-token log probabilities, including termination, whereas [6] uses a next-token Yes/No margin. Thus §4.4 supports failure of this particular verifier to improve sample agreement, not a controlled attribution to forecasting rather than visible-trace claims. Define the score explicitly. A matched untrained-reader comparison and consistent scoring would be needed for the stronger transfer claim. Relevant additional attribution is Torrielli et al., *Confidence and Calibration of Activation Oracles for Reliable Interpretation of Language Model Internals*, arXiv:2605.26045v3, which already studies AO sample agreement and confidence.

4. **Explain the operational sentence labels.** The code splits at the first period, exclamation mark, question mark, or newline; it therefore also splits decimal numbers. A generated continuation without such a delimiter is assigned “long” regardless of its observed word count. These are reproducible lexical proxies, but the main text should state the segmentation and truncation rules rather than suggesting ordinary sentence annotation. Results concern the backtracking-balanced cut distribution, one trained seed, and eight samples per item; the intervals do not include training-seed variability. Label the multiple reported intervals as pointwise.

5. **Complete the regeneration instructions and provenance.** The numerical summaries are sufficient for the statistical checks above, but the advertised training command requires `e51_train_acts.pt`, which no script in this supplement creates. The extractor exists in the cited predecessor's `e51_run.py`; provide an explicit versioned dependency and command, or include the extraction step. Also reconcile the supplied `analyze_final2.py` hash, which begins `f7ee2387`, with the registration's `5f35ce7e` freeze claim; document portability edits instead of calling the file unchanged if that explains the difference. Retaining the raw sampled continuations in a linked archive would allow auditing the lexical extraction and censoring rules, beyond replaying their sufficient statistics.

These qualifications leave a credible result for a fixed training recipe and evaluation distribution. Broader claims about semantic faithfulness, general training effectiveness, or universal failure of forecast verification would require additional evidence.
