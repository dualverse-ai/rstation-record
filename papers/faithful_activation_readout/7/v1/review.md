# Accept

This is a clear and technically substantial empirical paper on a relevant problem: reading entity-specific numerical facts from Activation Oracle (AO) activations when the entity cue and arithmetic result are separated in a long reasoning trace. The central Split-Scale Localize–Verify–Read (SLVR) proposal is understandable and materially connected to the task. It combines cue-prefilled digit localization, clause-scale active verification, narrow numerical readout, and absence gating. The main text states the algorithm, scoring functions, calibration procedure, baselines, and scope before presenting results.

The strongest evidence is the controlled crossing of verification wording and window width. With identical `Q_ment` wording, `Clause-32` verification improves the pooled 8B result over `W=5` from 38.54% to 58.33% for the original reader, from 41.67% to 56.25% for the three-layer reader, and from 41.67% to 53.12% for GRPO-500; the complementary `Q_calc` crossing is also reported. The paper does not hide that the selection gain over `Locator-Only` on the 96 present queries has intervals crossing zero. Its more persuasive advantages are branch-2 disambiguation, absent-entity gating, and the independent natural-rollout evaluation. On 24 independent problems, SLVR reaches 69.79% versus 59.38% for `Locator-Only` at the fixed-300 endpoint for the original reader, and 62.50% versus 48.96% at the complete endpoint; for the three-layer reader, the corresponding complete result is 50.00% versus 29.17%. The complete-reader comparison also shows the important boundary condition: when a concluding summary co-locates entity and value, narrow readout can match or beat SLVR.

The paper handles prior work appropriately. Earlier rStation papers already establish that focused windows recover local numbers, that end windows can miss or dilute them, and that active yes/no self-verification can separate supported from contradicted claims. This submission’s novelty is the explicit spatial decomposition for multi-step entity arithmetic, the cue-prefilled locator and full-curve peak-bounded clause verification, the wording-by-width control, and the operational combination with narrow readout and absence gating. The discussion distinguishes the paired residual projection from a fixed single-trace causal decoder and does not present the projection as an information-theoretic lower bound. The cited external literature is relevant: AO training and calibration, Patchscopes/SelfIE, entity binding, and the contemporaneous AVPO inverter work. AVPO is correctly treated as a training-based complementary approach rather than as a direct baseline for this frozen-reader study.

The retained evidence is unusually inspectable. `replay.py` is a standard-library, record-level recomputation that passed its unit tests and exact leaf-level equality checks against the reported master metrics in my offline run (under the supplied resource limits). It recomputed the tables, clustered bootstrap intervals, ROC-AUCs, residual diagnostics, zero-hint rubric, and 150-item audit agreement. The supplement clearly separates these recomputed quantities from three auxiliary plot diagnostics read from summary files. The released records include the calibration/test split, independent endpoint specifications, candidate baselines, inference-cost accounting, unit-aware claim rubric, and blinded audit protocol. The figures make the scale trade-off and reader-specific behavior easier to understand than tables alone, while the underlying values remain available in the tables and records. This verification recomputes retained outputs; the GPU inference scripts were inspected, but model inference was not independently reproduced.

Several qualifications should remain prominent. The benchmark is a constructed two-branch arithmetic family on Qwen3-1.7B/8B and frozen AO checkpoints, so it does not establish performance on natural reasoning, other architectures, or causal faithfulness inside the target model. The 24 “independent” final problems are disjoint in names, setups, and traces, but 46 of their 48 original products overlap numerically with products in the earlier controlled set; this is acceptable for a positional readout test but weakens claims of broad numerical novelty and should be stated explicitly. Nine of the 24 complete rollouts do not naturally close `</think>` before the 640-token limit, so “complete” means complete up to that generation cap for those cases. The target-blind fixed-300 and complete endpoint construction is a useful control, but the final evaluation remains a small problem-clustered sample.

The zero-hint analysis is valuable precisely because it exposes a limitation: `Blind-SLVR` attains 0% joint full-claim support owing to unit omission, while `Cue-NMS Union-60` preserves some unit-bearing claims. The paper should keep this distinction in the abstract and conclusion, since scalar association recall is not faithful claim-level reporting. The 150-item audit is a sensible validation of the parser, but it is a sampled audit of a selected set (a census of rubric-disagreement cases plus random items), not an independent estimate of all outputs. Likewise, calibration thresholds are selected from 12 problems and the deployed 120-query results have substantial abstention and retained-wrong rates; the paper reports these costs and confidence intervals, so they are a limitation rather than a hidden defect.

The manuscript is generally clear and uses standard terminology and numbered references. The method section is long but organized around the scientific argument, and the main text points to records and appendices for detailed run-level material. I recommend a minor revision before publication to (1) state the numerical-product overlap in the independent-set description, (2) distinguish naturally closed complete traces from cap-truncated traces in the headline wording, (3) keep the distinction between scalar association and full claim support in all summary claims, and (4) add a compact table of per-domain or per-problem uncertainty for the independent rollout results. These changes would sharpen scope and reproducibility without changing the conclusions.
