# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical contribution to faithful activation readout. The strongest results are improved structured recovery through reference-contrasted decoding and the demonstration that sentence-level CR-LLR pruning offers little beyond simple positional truncation. Neither constitutes a general solution to faithful interpretation, but both substantively advance the supplied task. The main questions, decoding rule, controls, evidence and limitations are sufficiently clear for publication.

The structured results have meaningful supporting controls. PersonaQA includes substring and exact-match accuracy, persona-disjoint calibration/test sets, and no-activation, shuffled-dimension and donor interventions. The fresh-profile experiment provides an independent setting, including a locked transfer coefficient. I independently recovered the three-layer reader's improvement from $10.00\%$ to $17.14\%$ on FreshProfiles, with a profile-clustered paired interval of $[1.43,12.86]$ percentage points. The conditional Taboo result usefully shows that input subtraction behaves differently across tasks. The synthetic-number experiment establishes preservation of a deliberately inserted local number, as the manuscript now explicitly acknowledges; it does not establish recovery of spontaneous reasoning errors.

The free-form controls are particularly valuable. The $72/72$ fresh-case agreement between CR-LLR pruning and first-sentence truncation, together with the AObench length controls, prevents an apparent improvement from being mistakenly attributed to activation-sensitive claim selection. The prefix-reset experiment supports dependence on conditioning history, while its wide AUC intervals appropriately limit conclusions about discrimination. The retained prefixes make the distinction between benchmark plausibility and agreement with expressed reasoning inspectable.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the published same-problem papers. Paper [10] studies localization and dilution of numbers, including earlier donor-based contrastive decoding; paper [11] studies binding, abstention and explicit claim self-verification. This submission contributes the centroid/null/donor comparison across readout regimes and the conditioning-history/position analysis. A focused external check of contrastive decoding [6], context-aware decoding [7], and AO confidence estimation [9] supports treating the method as an empirical adaptation of established contrastive principles, rather than a new decoding principle. Reference [9], including its steering-strength sensitivity experiment in Appendix N, deserves a more specific comparison. Its author names should also be corrected to Federico Torrielli, Peter Schneider-Kamp and Lukas Galke Poech.

I ran the supplied offline replay successfully and independently checked FreshProfiles predictions against all 4,000 retained hit entries, the retained PersonaQA greedy/CRD/donor predictions, synthetic-number prediction matching, selective precision, and several paired intervals. These checks validate arithmetic on retained evidence, not GPU inference, judge regeneration, or the correctness of manual annotations. The following qualifications and corrections should accompany the results:

1. **Reference superiority remains preliminary.** Improvement over greedy decoding is better established than superiority over alternative contrastive references. My paired profile bootstrap gives CRD minus Donor-CD at $\beta=0.50$ as $1.43$ percentage points $[-5.71,8.57]$ for the three-layer reader and $8.57$ $[0.00,18.57]$ for GRPO. CRD minus Null-CD is $1.43$ $[-7.14,10.00]$ for each. Present “outperforms” as an observed point-estimate comparison and retain the manuscript's restrictions on generalization. A zero reference coefficient removes the hook in the reference branch, not the target branch; the synthetic-error discussion should make this explicit and avoid inferring a unique causal mechanism from comparisons that also change reference content and sometimes $\beta$.

2. **Several audit details disagree with the retained annotations.** Among the 33 benchmark-correct answers, the supplied sentence labels aggregate to 13 supported, 17 unresolved and 3 contradicted, not 13 supported, 12 unresolved and 8 contradicted. Thus the headline $20/33$ not fully supported is reproduced, but its stated decomposition is not. Single-sentence $S_2$ has 17 supported, 21 unresolved and 17 contradicted annotations, rather than 17/22/16. The example identifying item 43 as supported $S_2$ followed by contradicted $S_3$ is also incorrect: item 43 has one sentence; item 14 has supported $S_2$ followed by unresolved $S_3$. These corrections preserve the principal support-rate and truncation findings.

3. **Treat the prefix audit as an operational, manually annotated measure.** Substring membership verifies that an evidence span exists; it does not verify entailment or contradiction. For example, item 35 labels “probabilities” versus Huffman symbol frequencies as contradicted, although the frequencies can be normalized into probabilities; item 55's mention of half-cell reduction potentials is not directly contradicted merely because the prefix supplies a cell potential. Review such cases and distinguish factual conflict from an unreached explanation. The $64$-token cutoff also cannot establish that an unexpressed fact is absent from the internal representation. Sentence-level units can contain multiple claims and should not be conflated with independently extracted atomic facts.

4. **Narrow the reproducibility claim.** The replay does not recompute every table cell and confidence interval as Section 7 claims: for example, it does not recompute the PersonaQA CR-KL selective precision or the routing-versus-mixture bootstrap intervals. Some arms retain hit indicators without their original prediction strings. State exactly what is recomputed, and provide portable paths, upstream revisions, model-weight revisions and judge prompts for full regeneration. Configuration hashes alone do not pin model weights. The existing records are sufficient to inspect the principal findings, but full regeneration was not demonstrated here.

The manuscript is organized around scientific questions rather than experiment chronology, and its equations and comparison figures communicate the main argument. The abstract and opening contribution bullets remain crowded with numbers and internal labels; shortening them would improve accessibility. These presentation issues and the localized numerical corrections above do not overturn the supported empirical advances.
