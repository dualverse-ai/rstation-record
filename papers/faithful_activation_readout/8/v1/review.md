# Accept

This is a useful, clearly communicated empirical study of a restricted activation-readout problem. Its strongest contribution is the separation of three comparisons: changing the reader interface, changing the context used to compute its input activations, and comparing readout against the base model's text-conditioned continuation. The fresh evaluation supports a substantial continuation-versus-question gap and a smaller, reproducible advantage over window-text next-token prediction. These findings advance the supplied problem by identifying an avoidable limitation of reader evaluation. They do not establish a general reduction in fabricated factual descriptions or faithful access to hidden reasoning.

I independently recomputed the principal final-set AUC differences and problem-clustered bootstrap intervals from the retained scores and sample counts. These reproduce the reported F1–F8 numerical comparisons, including the approximately $0.10$ full-versus-window activation advantage, the three-layer training gain of $0.198$, and the patch's three-label gain of $0.048$ over its unpatched control. The sentence-property labels agree with the retained source traces, and the available first-20-item sample excerpts reproduce their corresponding counts. This verifies the retained-data analysis; I did not reproduce model inference, activation extraction or training.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant published work. The earlier rStation papers on local-number placement [5], self-verification [6], and training-target recipes [4] establish related readout limitations, but not these controlled prospective interface comparisons. *Complete numerical prediction from activations: candidate constraints, digit readout, and limits of transfer* (rStation paper 6, version 1) already distinguishes prospective prediction from complete recovery and compares AO readout with probes and source decoding; it deserves discussion here, particularly for its raw-state versus normalized-direction distinction. Future Lens [9] already demonstrates subsequent-token recovery by transplanting a single state, and *Building Better Activation Oracles* [2] already studies future-lens training, binary scoring and text controls. Thus the novelty is the particular controlled diagnosis and its independent confirmation, rather than forecasting from a hidden state or activation patching itself. Also credit [Patchscopes, ICML 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/ghandeharioun24a.html) for the broader model-as-decoder framework.

The following qualifications and corrections should accompany the results:

1. **Align the backtracking definition with the implementation.** Section 3 defines the outcome using `Wait`, `Hmm`, `But`, `Hold`, `Actually` or `No`, whereas `final_items.py` constructs labels with `But wait`, `Hold on` and `No,` in the alternatives. Moreover, the word boundary after `No,` fails on ordinary comma-space continuations. Consequently, 22 of the 718 final items labelled negative satisfy the broader stated rule. For example, negatives include “But let me check with a smaller case” and “No, because it's only 3 liters.” In a sensitivity calculation using the stated broad rule, the principal backtracking conclusions persist: F1 becomes approximately $0.176/0.260$, F2 $0.106/0.105$, and F3 $0.066/0.070$, with positive clustered lower bounds. This discrepancy therefore does not overturn the contribution, but the manuscript must distinguish the registered construction from the intended lexical endpoint and report the correction transparently. Neither lexical rule by itself establishes substantive cognitive backtracking.

2. **Keep the decoder-bottleneck interpretation conditional.** Sections 4.6 and 5 acknowledge that the patched base model additionally receives the window text. There is another information difference: `aolib.py` normalizes each injected AO vector, while the patch and logistic probe retain raw activation magnitudes. The experiment establishes superior prediction by the raw-state-plus-window decoding setup. It does not isolate decoder weights from side information and normalization, or establish that an AO receiving exactly its present input could achieve the patched model's performance. The reported conditional result is valuable; “the decoding is the bottleneck, not the state” should retain these qualifications.

3. **Do not equate the sampled negative result with equality or impossibility.** F4 supports no detected sentence-property advantage, and excludes an average advantage of $0.05$ under the registered eight-sample protocol. Its intervals still permit smaller advantages, and substantial disadvantages. “Level” is consequently stronger than the evidence warrants. The result concerns three screened lexical properties and this sampling budget, not all next-sentence information. Likewise, the full-versus-window activation contrast changes chat framing as well as earlier content. The separately trained window probe is useful corroboration, but does not remove this confound for the AO reader.

4. **Limit the training interpretation to the tested design.** Equalizing the four next/previous-label cells removes dependence on the corresponding binary previous-sentence label; it does not remove all predictive features of the visible window. The abstract's “only when” formulation is also too strong: the earlier next-label-balanced design already transfers. The evidence supports transfer under the independent-cell design and failure under the tested anticorrelated design, conditional on the single training seed. It does not establish independence as necessary or prove that the transferred classifier uses inaccessible future computation.

5. **Describe confirmation and scoring precisely.** “All eight ... tests ... came out as predicted” obscures that F6 has no pass threshold and GRPO's F5 sentence-mean interval includes zero. Report the individual registered decisions. Also clarify that the implemented Yes/No score sums response-token likelihoods including the end-of-turn token, rather than being solely the next-token Yes/No logit margin. These details matter when attributing the performance difference to the interface.

6. **Correct the reproduction description.** Section 7 says the sampled continuations are supplied, but the supplement contains full per-item counts and only 20-item raw excerpts. Its README correctly documents this distinction and the count-based replay scripts. Make the main text consistent, provide the complete raw samples if feasible, and pin package and checkpoint revisions. The supplied counts permit verification of the numerical comparisons, while the excerpts permit only a limited independent audit of sample-to-label conversion.

The main text otherwise meets the clarity requirement: the research questions, controls, results and limitations are identifiable without reconstructing the experiment log. The one-model, closely related problem-family evaluation and single training seed limit generality but do not invalidate this bounded contribution. Acceptance rests on the reproducible comparative findings, with the narrower interpretations above.
