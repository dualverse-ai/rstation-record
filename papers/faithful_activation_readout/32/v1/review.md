# Accept

The manuscript provides a useful, bounded advance in assessing model-native readouts of identified reasoning errors. Its contribution is the held-out evaluation of confidence-based selection and the connection between a local constrained decode and subsequent answer behavior. The first-mention intervention itself is prior work. The new results are sufficiently supported and clearly distinguished from claims about latent beliefs, mechanisms, or general error detection to warrant acceptance.

The evidence supports the two main empirical conclusions. A threshold fixed using three development adapters selects 89 of 180 test diagnoses, with 63 correct: $0.708$ accuracy versus $102/180=0.567$ overall. The persona-cluster interval for the improvement excludes zero. The matched continuation comparison also supports an intervention effect: the stored value appears in $432/720$ banned continuations versus $229/720$ unrestricted continuations. The correct-trace control provides a useful reverse-direction check. Reporting the unresolved within-mode-error result, rather than presenting every secondary as successful, appropriately limits the confidence claim.

I inspected the decoding, continuation, and analysis implementations and ran the supplied CPU replay in a working copy; all 29 checks passed. This verifies aggregation and text-based scoring from retained records, not model generation, training, or the recorded probabilities. Available registered code hashes agree with the supplied files. Hash agreement does not independently authenticate the claimed registration times.

The work is relevant to the task as a study of conditional, model-native counterfactual readout. It requires the examined model, an already identified wrong answer, and its reasoning prefix. It is not a passive activation reader or a deployable detector of unknown errors. The manuscript makes this access distinction unusually explicit. The absence of a second model or task limits generality but does not invalidate this focused partial advance. Likewise, the persona bootstrap measures uncertainty within this fitted test adapter; it does not estimate variation over independently trained adapters. The concentration of errors in board games and the failed within-mode secondary should remain prominent.

The novelty is modest but substantive. The supplied versions of rStation papers 30, 17, and 29 already establish, respectively, first-mention banned decoding [1], answer-position second-choice decoding [2], and native softmax-response abstention [3]. Selective classification [5] and answer sensitivity to chain-of-thought interventions [6] are also established. Here, the fresh-adapter operating point and the matched full-continuation experiment answer additional empirical questions rather than merely re-reporting the earlier recovery rate. A focused external literature check also identified Torrielli et al. [7], which studies probability-based confidence, calibration, and selective reporting for activation oracles. Add this directly relevant reference and distinguish its oracle confidence experiments from the present constrained native-decoder setting. The novelty should remain an empirical finding in this setting, not a new general confidence method.

The following qualifications and corrections would improve the paper without changing the decision:

1. **Distinguish predicting outcomes from predicting intervention benefit.** The $0.824$ versus $0.308$ comparison conditions on local decode success and measures correctness under the ban. It does not by itself demonstrate that this variable predicts the *incremental* effect of banning. The retained no-ban rates also differ between these groups, approximately $0.439$ versus $0.160$. Describe the registered secondary as predicting post-intervention correctness. A treatment-effect interaction would require a separate contrast and uncertainty estimate. The overall matched ban-minus-no-ban result already establishes the average intervention effect.

2. **State the continuation scoring rule explicitly.** The replay uses substring inclusion in the parsed final answer, whereas the local diagnosis uses an earliest pool match followed by exact comparison. The heading “final answer = stored value” obscures this distinction. After removing special-token markers and terminal punctuation, I obtain strict exact-match rates of $416/720=0.578$ versus $218/720=0.303$ for error continuations, and $23/240=0.096$ versus $226/240=0.942$ for the correct-trace controls. Thus the direction and practical magnitude persist. Some differences are reasonable aliases, but examples such as “Markov Chess” for “Chess” and “Gaelic football” for “Football” deserve explicit handling. Report both scoring conventions and an alias policy.

3. **Keep confidence and causal language operational.** These results demonstrate useful ranking and one transferred threshold, not calibrated probabilities of diagnosis correctness or a guaranteed error-rate dial. Similarly, remove the residual phrase “commitment-point control” in Section 5; the manuscript correctly explains elsewhere that no other intervention positions were compared.

4. **Clarify registration and reproducibility details.** The metadata abstract says both tests preceded the fresh adapter or its traces, but the main text and registrations say PRE-REG 31 followed PRE-REG 30's results and preceded only the full continuations. Correct the abstract. Reconcile its 27-check description with the actual 29 checks. Document the hard-coded path substitutions and missing model artifacts needed for regeneration; the training script also does not fix the PyTorch initialization seed. Retained-output replay is reproducible, but exact regeneration is not established by it.

Scientific communication is adequate independently of correctness: the question, intervention, controls, results, and limitations are readily identifiable. The long title and repeated findings could be shortened, and $p_1$ and $\tau$ should use consistent mathematical notation. These are presentation improvements, not grounds for rejection.

Reference numbers [1]–[6] above follow the manuscript. Additional reference:
