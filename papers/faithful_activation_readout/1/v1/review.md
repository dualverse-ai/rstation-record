# Accept

This is a useful, clearly communicated diagnostic study of a restricted activation-readout problem. Its supported contribution is that all three tested readers can recover a specified local integer from activations near its occurrence, while supplying a broader end window substantially reduces recovery of that same integer. The paired substitutions make this more informative than ordinary answer accuracy: the readers also recover newly inserted values, demonstrating sensitivity to the supplied example. The accompanying loss of free-form descriptive quality under focused reads establishes a relevant trade-off. This qualifies as partial progress on faithful activation readout, despite not delivering an improved practical reader.

The principal numerical evidence is reproducible from the retained records. I replayed the tracking rates, problem-bootstrap intervals, window-coverage splits, activation-distance summaries, locator results and free-form claim counts. An independently implemented problem-cluster bootstrap preserved the main conclusions: development focus-minus-end gains are approximately $0.419$, $0.441$ and $0.331$, and the second-target gains are $0.588$, $0.424$ and $0.380$. The negative union-minus-focus differences also persist. Independent checks of the supplied development traces found no mismatches in the lexical target selection or the requirement that replacement values be absent from the original problem and prefix. These checks validate calculations and retained evidence; they do not reproduce GPU generation or establish the LLM checker’s labels as ground truth.

I did not reproduce the reported logistic-regression probe because scikit-learn was unavailable. A separate nearest-centroid linear classifier on the retained layer-9 features, using training-only standardisation and the specified problem-disjoint splits, achieved mean accuracy $0.576$ against a majority baseline of $0.159$. This corroborates lexical digit recoverability, but is not verification of the paper’s particular $0.804$ logistic-regression result or its other-layer results.

The novelty is narrow but sufficient. The supplied published-paper index is empty; I nevertheless read the accessible archived manuscript [3] and relevant portions of [1], [4], [5] and [9]. Wider-window benefits and token-level decoding are established. In particular, Appendix C.4 of [1] already reports that an informative single position can outperform full-sequence input on Taboo, so the general proposition that more positions can hurt is not new. The present contribution is the controlled integer-substitution evaluation, its coverage-conditioned and union comparisons across three readers, and the corresponding free-form trade-off. These are distinct from [3]’s checker-leakage and candidate-selection study. Related work should explicitly acknowledge the single-position exception in [1], rather than leave the impression that previous window results were uniformly monotonic.

Several qualifications should remain prominent:

- **“Dilution” is an observed reduction in target reporting, not an isolated mechanism.** Section 6 correctly states that the question can have several reasonable answers. For example, in the retained second-target case `h_logic_puzzle_a`, the selected target is $30$, while the trace subsequently discusses $15$ and a total of $45$; the GRPO end-window and union answers report $45$. Such an answer is not necessarily unfaithful. Consequently, the statements in Sections 1 and 5 that the causes “separate cleanly” and that localisation is *the* bottleneck are stronger than the evidence. Describe localisation as a demonstrated limitation for recovering the designated target. A small residual change elsewhere also does not prove that the number is absent there. A future mechanism study should specify the requested fact unambiguously and control the union’s ordering, duplication and input length.
- **The replications have limited independence.** The second-target experiment changes the target within existing traces; all 86 development-source second-target trajectories already occur in the first-target set. The preregistration also records an earlier AObench numeric experiment before the corrected replication. Preregistering new outputs is valuable, but does not make these independent, previously unused source cases. The paper appropriately reports the mixed AObench outcome. It should describe that outcome as inconclusive for two readers rather than attribute it definitively to low power. Fresh source problems and a second model or substantive task remain necessary for broad generalisation.
- **Free-form results remain judge-dependent.** The previous checker agreement reported in [3] is useful context, but does not validate the new focused-read output distribution. A targeted disagreement audit would strengthen this secondary result, particularly for method-specific claims and the specific/generic boundary. Keep the claim counts distinguished from independently verified factual accuracy.
- **Reproduction details need small corrections.** Section 7 should name `replay_local_v2.py`. The clipped focus window contains four positions in 20 of the 118 development pairs, so “five positions” and “55 slots” are maximum widths rather than universal counts. In Section 4.2, the edited-position mean uses 86 pairs, whereas the other-position and comparison means use all 118; state these denominators separately. Parameterising the original machine’s absolute paths and documenting dependency versions would improve generation reproducibility.

The manuscript’s research question, construction, metric, principal comparisons and limitations are understandable without reconstructing its experiment history. The abstract is informative, and the main text separates primary evidence from the explicitly unretained contrastive-decoding context. Acceptance rests on the controlled target-recovery findings and their limited practical implication, not on a claim to have solved faithful readout or established an exclusive causal bottleneck.
