# Accept

This revision preserves the accepted contribution and improves its interpretation. The distinction between lexical recovery of a designated number and faithful description of a model’s reasoning is clearer; the discussion now treats “dilution” as reduced reporting rather than an identified mechanism. The added attribution to [1, Appendix C.4], disclosure of reused source trajectories, and corrections to window widths and denominators address the previous review. The manuscript remains organized around the same controlled target-recovery argument.

The Qwen3-1.7B experiment in Section 4.5 is a bounded replication of that argument, appropriate supporting evidence for this revision rather than a separate substantive contribution. From the retained responses, I independently recovered tracking rates of $0.300$ for end-50, $0.876$ for focus-5 and $0.638$ for their union across 210 pairs from 38 problems. A separate problem-cluster bootstrap gave focus-minus-end $0.576$ with a 95% interval approximately $[0.489,0.661]$, and union-minus-focus $-0.238$ with interval $[-0.328,-0.149]$. End-50 had no successes among the 38 pairs whose designated position was outside its window. Checks against the retained traces found no discrepancies in the selected lexical targets or replacement-value novelty. These checks reproduce scoring and support the reported directions; they do not reproduce model inference, token alignment, or the claimed timing of preregistration. The disclosed loss of the trace-generation script limits generation reproducibility but does not prevent inspection of the supporting evidence. Reused problems and two models within one family still limit generalisation.

The added benchmark comparison also agrees with the retained labels: end-50 reduces labelled error by approximately $0.175$, $0.135$ and $0.250$, and increases labelled correctness by $0.116$, $0.167$ and $0.193$ for the original, GRPO and three-layer readers. Independently computed problem-clustered intervals exclude zero for all six differences. This supports the contextual comparison, subject to the benchmark judge’s definition of correctness; it does not independently establish trajectory-specific fidelity or explain why wider windows help.

Several small corrections would make the revision internally consistent:

- Introduction finding 4 introduces a residual-probe locator and a 47% result, but the methods, Section 4.3 and retained replay describe attention locators, not that experiment. Remove this unsupported extra result. Likewise, replace “The residual stream does not, in a simply decodable way, mark ‘the most recent number’” with the narrower observation that the tested attention locators recover the designated position unreliably. Their failure does not establish an absence of decodable information. This peripheral statement is unnecessary to the supported contribution.
- The opening still says “We separate these causes,” although the revised discussion correctly disclaims an isolated mechanism. Align that sentence with the observational interpretation. Remove the remaining “underpowered” parenthesis in Section 6, consistent with Section 4.1’s appropriately inconclusive assessment.
- The benchmark replay intersects end-5, end-50 and an additional self-verification condition. Consequently, the three-layer comparison uses 980 items although end-50 has 982 scored items. Use the two conditions actually compared to define the paired sample, or explain the exclusion. Keep the unused self-verification results clearly separate from this paper’s evidence and claims.

Acceptance rests on the existing controlled readout contribution, its clearer qualifications, and a supported replication. The additions do not establish a general localisation method, a causal account of dilution, or a solution to faithful activation readout.
