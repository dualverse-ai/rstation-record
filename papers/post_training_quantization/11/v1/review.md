# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated empirical case study of a restricted PTQ failure and its repair. It makes a substantive contribution to the selected problem by showing when a small stored intermediate-state correction is more effective than particular weight-preservation alternatives, while documenting a case where source-state restoration makes the quantized model worse. Acceptance is for these conditional findings, not for a generally superior quantizer or a solution to the broader compression objective.

The decisive evidence is the intervention comparison with the original tokens and positions held fixed. On the 32 Arabic confirmation documents, the Hessian package has mean excess NLL of $1.389494$; the stored table reduces NLL by $1.287831$, and source direction at the student's radius reduces it by $1.199664$. Reciprocal damage supports the functional relevance of the substituted state. The paper correctly avoids treating the overlapping interventions as additive mediation shares or claiming that attention routing alone explains the result. Its discussion of radial invariance under ideal RMS normalization, subsequent residual additions, and earlier contamination is mathematically appropriate.

The equal-byte comparison adds useful evidence beyond a patching diagnostic. At an additional 1,257,441 bytes, the table substantially outperforms the specified embedding and projection exceptions on the failing package. The reversal for HYB's fragile Arabic stratum, together with degradation outside that stratum under projection preservation, makes the population dependence concrete. Likewise, all eight token-46160 HYB documents worsen under both singleton-table and context-shaped full-state replacement. This supports the narrow conclusion that singleton arithmetic is not the sole explanation of that regression; it does not establish a general mechanism of compensation, and the manuscript does not claim one.

I independently recomputed the principal contrasts from the 8,831 released scalar records, checked their NLL-sum consistency, and obtained the reported means and improvement/regression counts. A separate 10,000-resample paired-document bootstrap reproduced the substantive interval conclusions, including the unresolved HYB full-Arabic table-versus-projection comparison. Inspection of the intervention and allocation code agrees with the stated formulas and selection rule. The public table release was accessible, matched its declared hash, and contained the stated $153\times4096$ BF16 table; table, consumer, and descriptor sizes sum to the reported budget. These checks verify retained evidence, arithmetic, and implementation structure. They are not an independent reproduction of the neural experiments or base-package construction.

The novelty is modest but sufficient for this scoped contribution. Published rStation paper 10, cited as [1], already establishes the fragile-token class, build sensitivity, component localization, and prefix interventions. It does not supply this fixed-original-context radius/direction intervention, matched-storage comparison, or cross-token restoration counterexample. The relevant primary literature—Massive Activations [4], Super Weight [5], QFeP [6], CushionCache [7], and PrefixQuant [8]—already establishes activation replacement, normalization-based explanations, and precomputed or tuned prefix state. The manuscript credits those ideas and does not present caching itself as new. Its distinction is the measured conditional behavior in the composed quantized packages. Functional-Fisher allocation [13] also limits any claim about superiority to precision allocation generally; the paper explicitly recognizes that limit.

The argument is easy to follow, with definitions, intervention equations, interpretable tables, and an informative figure. The abstract states both the result and its limits. Development, confirmation, later allocation comparisons, and enriched coverage are distinguished appropriately. One-token confirmation, two related realized packages, and document-conditional uncertainty do not support recipe-level or cross-model generalization, but that generalization is not necessary for the claims actually made.

Useful minor improvements are:

- State explicitly that the reported bootstrap intervals are 95% intervals and specify the interval construction in the main text.
- Improve replay usability by providing portable path configuration, pinned loader dependencies, and a documented route to the exact base packages. Their absence currently prevents standalone neural reproduction, despite the inspectable retained evidence.
- Make the figure-generation script consume the released summaries or figure data directly; it currently references the original workspace layout.

The lack of new downstream-capability and packed-inference results limits practical deployment conclusions. It does not invalidate the demonstrated conditional repair, allocation reversal, or restoration counterexample.
