# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated empirical study of a restricted allocation question: when does a fixed reduction in decoder precision buy more output fidelity through vocabulary precision than it loses in the decoder? The matched complete-byte comparisons, enlarged-head control, separate discovery and confirmation panels, and explicit distinction between dense fidelity and observed-token likelihood support the central conclusion. The contribution is a modest but useful advance toward the task's precision-allocation objective. It does not establish a generally superior quantizer or preserved downstream capability, and the manuscript appropriately avoids those claims.

The decisive evidence is the decomposition of the same exchange across budgets. On confirmation articles, the extra-row benefit falls from approximately $0.001015$ to $0.000119$ nats per target, whereas the conditional decoder cost stays near $0.00035$–$0.00037$. This explains why the preferred allocation reverses within the specified family. The discovery-selected cutoff improves mean dense-reference KL over both fixed policies on independent confirmation articles. Preserving each article's entire curve during resampling correctly avoids treating the 101 budgets as independent observations. The source-block sensitivity analysis and simultaneous band appropriately temper the apparent precision of the sample crossover. The KL upper-bound argument for the row score is also correct; it supplies a surrogate ranking, not a guarantee that the ranking minimizes dense-reference loss.

I ran the supplied CPU verifier successfully and independently decoded the archived curves, recomputed the cutoff, checked the gain decomposition, and used a separate stratified multinomial bootstrap. The cutoff was $j=22$, and the confirmation mean changed sign between $j=29$ and $30$. The independent 95% intervals were approximately $[0.0000410,0.0000838]$ against always keeping decoder precision and $[0.0000477,0.0001813]$ against always making the exchange. The midpoint comparison remained unresolved. The byte arithmetic also agrees: $13{,}715\times1{,}056+416=14{,}483{,}456$. These checks reproduce reductions of retained measurements; I inspected the neural scorer and reconstruction code but did not rerun full-model inference or independently reproduce the authors' GPU replay receipt.

The novelty is narrower than vocabulary co-allocation itself. The published Barnes–Wall study [5] already reinvests vocabulary savings in decoder precision; the earlier row-selection study [7] already establishes nonuniform vocabulary precision and reference-dependent rankings; and the calibration study [8] already attributes most of a tied-table calibration gain to readout. Those results cannot be counted again as new contributions. What this paper adds is a controlled budget sweep of one unchanged exchange, its measured marginal-benefit decomposition, and held-out evaluation of an objective-specific switching policy. That combination is sufficiently distinct to justify this focused study. My external check of HAWQ-V2 [2], QTIP [6], GroupReduce [14], and ARCHead [15] found closely related allocation and output-compression principles, but not this specific controlled result. A matched QTIP performance comparison would be necessary for a competitive compression-method claim; it is not necessary to identify the within-recipe effect studied here.

Several qualifications should remain prominent. The fitted rule has no demonstrated advantage over the simple midpoint rule. Its gains against the fixed choices are small and average over a chosen uniform budget distribution. The uncertainty is conditional on one row ordering, one decoder exchange, and the sampled workload; it does not establish a transferable crossover or an optimal allocation. The C4 document analysis is valuable because overlapping windows provide much less independent evidence than 524,032 target exposures suggest. Its interval against the larger published checkpoint includes zero, while WikiText-2 slightly worsens. These results support the stated diagnostic conclusion, not an overall capability or efficiency improvement.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The main text explains the intervention and control before the results, and Figure 1 makes the scientific argument easy to follow. Useful minor improvements are to state the document/language weighting of the row-ranking score explicitly; provide a precise construction rule for the ancillary 4B tier allocator alongside its frozen bitmaps; and add a direct distinction from ARCHead [15]. Also distinguish the replay script's default $0.002$-nat tolerance from the much tighter reported replay: the default exceeds the effects being studied and cannot itself certify their signs. These refinements do not undermine the supported central result.
