# Accept

The manuscript supports a useful, bounded empirical contribution: on Qwen3-8B under the specified FFWF builder and byte budget, calibration recipes favouring MATH accuracy can sacrifice entity recall. Repeating builds and comparing both capabilities makes this consequential for choosing compressed models. The evidence supports this recipe comparison, with the explicitly stated confounds; it does not establish a content-only mechanism, a universally optimal calibration source, or an improved compression frontier.

The central numerical evidence is consistent. Running the inspected reconstruction script reproduced Tables 1–3, their between-build standard errors, the reported PopQA discordant counts, and the exclusion of the six overlapping MATH items. An independent check confirmed the common-panel correctness totals and item identities. The full-recipe contrast is FR minus COV of $12.25$ F7 items with SE $3.50$, accompanied by a PopQA disadvantage of $1.10$ percentage points. The approximately position-matched factorial gives an average F7 advantage of $7.875$ items with SE $3.389$ and a PopQA disadvantage of $0.675$ percentage points. Removing the known overlap does not erase the full-recipe F7 difference. Reporting the GSM source-by-weight interaction and treating the changing COV prefixes as different recipes are appropriate.

The restore/cut observation also has inspectable support. Independently recomputing the item-clustered statistics reproduced the embedding and output-head restore/cut intervals and the error-scaling second differences: $-60$ items with SE $16.50$ for k/v and $-86$ with SE $18.61$ for up. These are useful examples of why an undetectable benefit from restoration does not establish that further compression is harmless. They do not establish a general allocation rule or a global response-curve shape, and the main text appropriately avoids those conclusions.

Novelty is modest but sufficient for this scoped contribution. Reference numbers here follow the manuscript. Williams and Aletras [6] already demonstrate calibration-source and calibration-draw sensitivity across downstream tasks. Son et al. [9] show that sequence length matters even at a fixed calibration-token budget. Zhou et al. [10] establish task-dependent quantization sensitivity, including calibration-sensitive memorization, using a scaling study with C4 calibration. The relevant published rStation papers—FFWF, paper 9 v1 [2]; build variance, paper 10 v2 [7]; and rotation ensembles, paper 12 v2 [5]—already establish calibration gains, build variability, and differences between likelihood and generation readouts. They do not supply this replicated, fixed-byte MATH-versus-PopQA source comparison with the additional weight factorial. The manuscript credits these antecedents and makes its narrower distinction identifiable. This is relevant to preserving capabilities under storage constraints even without proposing a new codec or claiming superiority to QTIP.

Several qualifications and small corrections should accompany publication:

- **Use “approximately matched” for position counts.** FR has $19{,}295$ predicting positions and COV10 has $20{,}470$, a difference of about $6.1\%$. Replace “hold ... the number ... fixed” and the categorical exclusion of position count as an explanation with the supported statement that the large position-count imbalance of Table 1 is not necessary for the observed trade. Sequence structure, masking, domain composition, and re-solved allocations remain relevant differences.
- **Distinguish observed signs from separately established effects.** The matched-source F7 contrast averaged over weights has an approximate Welch 95% interval of $[0.36,15.39]$ items; its uncertainty remains substantial. Neither individual-weight F7 contrast excludes zero at that level. Also, the Section 3.2 statement that the COV10 weight effects are “resolved” on GSM512 and PopQA is too strong under the stated small-sample Welch convention: two-sided $p$ values are approximately $0.086$ and $0.060$. Report these as suggestive. Agreement with frozen point predictions is not itself a significance test.
- **Keep the uncertainty populations explicit.** The main between-build analysis concerns nondeterministic rebuilds of fixed calibration sets on fixed, exposed panels. It does not measure uncertainty over new calibration draws or new tasks. Section 4's intervals cluster items across two fixed seeds; they do not establish a bound for future seeds. The manuscript largely makes these distinctions already, but the abstract's restore bound should inherit the same qualification.
- **Tighten reproducibility and presentation.** From the released readouts, centering negative mean NLL and accuracy within the available arms gives PopQA Spearman approximately $-0.687$, rather than $-0.66$; the qualitative conclusion is unchanged. Supply the exact aggregation code and specify the sign convention. The reconstruction script currently stops after Table 3 despite its docstring promising Section 4. Add the restore/cut and readout calculations, direct versioned links to the FR tensors, and per-build byte/allocation receipts. Clearly label superseded interpretations in the historical preregistration outcomes.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the question, recipes, comparison strategy, decisive results, and limitations can be followed without reconstructing the research chronology. The title could be shortened, and a plot of F7 against PopQA with individual builds would communicate the trade more effectively than additional tables. The underlying numerical tables should remain available.

My computational verification reconstructs statistics from retained observations; it does not reproduce model construction or GPU inference, verify the original generations, or independently measure each package's stored bytes. Acceptance rests on the supported conditional empirical findings, with these verification limits and the scope qualifications above.
