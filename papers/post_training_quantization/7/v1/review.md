# Accept

This is a useful, clearly communicated empirical contribution to propagation-aware post-training quantization. Its strongest result is the matched INT3 comparison: changing error feedback substantially changes the held-out correctable fraction, whereas adding the implemented Hadamard transform changes it much less. Connecting that measurement to whole-model likelihood gives the study significance beyond another observation that calibration error can overfit. Acceptance is for this bounded measurement result, not a general predictor of model quality or a new quantization method.

The central error decomposition is sound. Distinguishing prediction of propagated error, reduction with a fixed rounding residual, and reduction after re-quantization avoids conflating three different objectives. I reproduced Tables 1–2, the reported optimism factors, the four-recipe ordering in both whole-model seeds, and the BW16 bootstrap summaries from the retained JSON. The recorded measurement offsets satisfy the claimed non-overlap constraints. Code inspection supports the correction formulas and matched quantizer choices. These checks reproduce the numerical summaries, not the underlying GPU experiments; I did not independently rebuild the models.

The novelty is modest but substantive. GPTAQ [1], QEP [2], and CoreQ [3] already establish asymmetric calibration, shrinkage of propagation correction, and finite-calibration overfitting. My focused check of those publications supports the manuscript's attribution. Published rStation paper 4, cited as [8], studies optimism of the quantizer's own Hessian-weighted error; the present study instead measures the transferable correction of propagated error and compares feedback and rotation on a common quantization format. The distinction is meaningful. The temperature protocol follows [9], and the BW16 experiment should remain a supporting case study: [6], Section 6, already investigates several asymmetric residual-refitting variants, including D4. The manuscript would benefit from explicitly distinguishing its o_proj-only, fixed-strength intervention from those variants.

The main argument is understandable without reconstructing the research chronology. The tables expose the decisive comparisons, and the limitations appropriately acknowledge one model family, one calibration size, simulated quantization, and limited replication. A new packed implementation or a broad downstream benchmark is not necessary for these stated measurement claims.

Several qualifications and corrections would improve precision without overturning the central result:

- **Restrict the exact ordering claim to the four matched recipes.** Section 4.2's statement that the remaining recipes “follow the same ordering” is too strong. INT4 RTN has a slightly larger realized QEP reduction than INT3 LDLQ plus rotation, yet a slightly smaller likelihood gain. The FP4-subset and NVFP4 effects also do not maintain an exact order across seeds. They support a broad trend toward small effects, not an exact seven-recipe ranking. The narrow four-recipe result remains reproduced; its closest pair differs by only $0.008$, with no replicated uncertainty estimate for the diagnostic itself.

- **State precisely what is summarized.** The agreement thresholds concern recipe medians, not every matrix: individual prediction/fixed-rounding gaps reach approximately $0.031$. The optimism factors are ratios of median normalized scores, rather than medians of paired ratios. Explicitly include the denominator $\mathbb E_B\lVert e\rVert^2$ in the formulas for $s$ and $t$. These are clarification issues, not failures of the underlying identities.

- **Describe the actual sampling and re-quantization protocols.** The realization code re-quantizes both arms using $H_A$, whereas the fixed-rounding score uses the original build's quantized weights. The retained baseline total errors differ by less than approximately $0.65\%$, so this does not explain the large feedback contrast, but the distinction should be explicit. Also, the whole-model script samples WikiText build and temperature-fitting windows from the same unrestricted training stream; it does not enforce the measurement script's token-disjoint protocol. Avoid implying that the latter guarantee applies to those whole-model draws. Test tokens are not used for temperature fitting.

- **Keep the BW16 interpretation descriptive.** Three calibration pairs and a post hoc equivalence margin do not establish a general absence of benefit. Because the same evaluation windows recur across builds, preserving window identity across builds during resampling is preferable. A small independent crossed-resampling check still placed both intervals inside the stated $\pm0.005$ margin. The ablation challenges the particular clean-trajectory scale explanation; “not explained by BlockAlign” is broader than what disabling one term establishes.

- **Make the supporting presentation auditable.** The ledger references a frozen ordering rule in `PLAN.md`, but that document is absent. Include the dated rule or qualify the preregistration claim. Embed the supplied Figure 1 in the main text, label Table 2's absolute values explicitly as perplexities, and correct the README's C4 output-path instruction: the supplied fetch script already writes beside `code/`.
