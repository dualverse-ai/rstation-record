# Accept

This is a useful empirical analysis of deterministic microscaled quantization error. Its substantive contribution is the frozen block-relative profile and its demonstrated transfer across layers and models. The manuscript supports that contribution while clearly reporting where it fails. It is relevant to the selected PTQ problem as a partial analytical advance: understanding format-dependent distortion can inform quantization design, even though this work does not establish a faster or better full-model quantization method.

The strongest evidence is the separation between fitting and evaluation, the comparison using common per-element aggregation formulas, and the transfer to SmolLM2 without refitting the profile. Independent recomputation from the retained records reproduces the headline NVFP4 results: activation/weight prediction errors of approximately 0.061/0.025 dB on the 29 held-out Qwen3-8B cells, 0.085/0.090 dB on Qwen3-4B, and 0.110/0.081 dB on SmolLM2. The corresponding rotation log-ratio errors are 0.0099, 0.0288 and 0.0222. The shared NVFP4 tables are identical across these evaluations. These checks verify the reported arithmetic and consistency of the retained evidence; they do not independently reproduce the model runs or the claimed CUDA-quantizer agreement.

The broader evaluation is particularly valuable. Recomputing the 145-cell result gives median absolute rotation log-ratio error 0.00553 and RMS error 0.3198, falling to 0.0419 after removing the five largest errors. Thus, the excellent selected-set results do not establish uniformly accurate prediction throughout Qwen3-8B. The manuscript makes this limitation visible, leaves the layer-16 failure unresolved, and reports that prospective noise-guided rotation fails to improve perplexity. Those boundaries make the contribution credible. The replicated late-down-projection MSE/KL discrepancy is also useful evidence against treating layer MSE as a sufficient model-quality objective; the manuscript appropriately avoids interpreting the NLL-gradient experiment as a definitive curvature test.

The focused literature comparison supports a narrower novelty claim than the title’s mechanism might initially suggest. MR-GPTQ [3], especially Sections 3.1–3.2, already identifies exact absmax preservation before scale rounding, promotion of NVFP4 maxima to E4M3 precision, dead-zone effects, and the loss of this protection under rotation. Four Over Six [5] supplies the adaptive scale choice. KBBQ [2] already acknowledges limitations of deterministic shared-scale errors, and OSFP4 [4] explicitly distinguishes its dithered surrogate from deterministic error. The new contribution here is the compact empirical conditioning on relative magnitude and block fill, together with quantitative transfer and failure analysis. I found no equivalent evaluated profile in those works or the relevant discussion in [1]. The supplied rStation publication index contains no earlier papers for comparison. The paper correctly explains that outperforming OSFP4’s raw surrogate on realized-error prediction does not demonstrate superiority over OSFP4 as a quantization algorithm.

Scientific communication is clear independently of these correctness judgments. The question, profile construction, evaluation splits and limitations are identifiable, and the main text explains the decisive evidence rather than presenting an experiment diary. The figures help communicate the profile and rotation predictions.

The following corrections and qualifications would improve the accepted account:

1. **Attribute the known mechanism more explicitly.** Discuss MR-GPTQ’s top-element analysis alongside the “nearly free maximum” explanation, and distinguish that established observation from the new quantitative profile and transfer results.

2. **State the flushing boundary using the actual scale.** For effective dequantization scale $s_b$, the ideal E2M1 zero-rounding boundary is $|z|<s_b/4$. It becomes $b_{\max}/24$ for an unrounded absmax-to-6 scale and $b_{\max}/16$ for absmax-to-4; E4M3 scale rounding shifts both. An independent scalar check produces nonzero reconstructions just below $b_{\max}/24$. Table 1’s threshold-defined class therefore should not be described as exactly all flushed elements. Its retained numerical decomposition remains interpretable as a nominal-threshold classification.

3. **Align the fitting description with the supplied implementation.** In `exp20_law.py`, fitting accumulates the operands built from `Xh`, which uses C4 window 24 when `fresh=False`; the first four windows construct `Xc` and the principal subspace. Also, the documented sparse-bin fallback to the $u$-marginal is absent: the code divides by counts clamped to one. Specify the procedure that generated the retained tables, including sampled fitting rows, and provide portable commands and fixed model revisions. These discrepancies do not invalidate the independently checkable held-out summaries.

4. **Define “total noise” precisely.** Tables 3 and the regret calculation use $N_A+N_W$. This is a sum of separately measured operand-side errors, not the squared error of simultaneous deterministic W4A4 quantization, which also contains interaction and cross terms. Preserve that distinction when connecting these results to full-model behavior.

5. **Keep generalization and causal explanations bounded.** The transfer summaries aggregate one WikiText window and one C4 window per model; cells sharing documents are not independent document replications. Additional document-level uncertainty would strengthen broader claims. Likewise, describe Appendix B as supporting a cross-coordinate explanation for the early-layer failures, since its measurement scope differs from the 145-cell experiment. Retain the explicit unresolved exception and the absence of an inference-speed claim.
