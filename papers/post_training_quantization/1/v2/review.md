# Accept

This revision preserves the accepted contribution: a calibrated block-relative profile that predicts deterministic quantization distortion across held-out layers and models, with explicit limits on its ability to predict model quality. The changes are appropriate corrections, clearer attribution and limited supporting evidence for that contribution. The resulting manuscript remains organized around a coherent scientific argument; the additional replication table does not turn it into an unfocused collection of results.

The explicit attribution to MR-GPTQ [3] and Four Over Six [5] improves the distinction between established mechanisms and the empirical profile. The corrected zero-rounding boundary, description of the actual fitting procedure, and definition of total noise as $N_A+N_W$ resolve important ambiguities identified in the accepted review. The latter distinction matters because simultaneous deterministic W4A4 error also contains interaction and cross terms.

Independent recomputation from the new retained records reproduces Appendix C. For NVFP4, activation/weight RMS errors are approximately 0.0425/0.0212 dB for Qwen3-8B and 0.0942/0.0758 dB for Qwen3-4B. Rotation log-ratio errors are 0.0103 and 0.0280, with sign accuracy 28/29 and 41/41. The retained profile tables and fitted constants are identical to those in the original evaluation. This supports the stated limited replication without refitting; it does not provide broad document-level uncertainty estimates.

The correction to Section 5 is scientifically warranted. Across the 20 retained single-linear controls, the unquantized smoothing paths give KL values of approximately $3.18\times10^{-4}$ to $8.66\times10^{-4}$ nats/token, while quantized-minus-unquantized differences range from $4.60\times10^{-5}$ to $9.17\times10^{-4}$. The reported late-down-projection ratios, correlation of approximately 0.254 between log ratios, and 80% sign agreement are reproducible from these records. The manuscript correctly withdraws the earlier interpretation and explains why baseline subtraction does not isolate quantization harm. This correction does not undermine the profile's central evidence. The full-model experiments remain the relevant evidence that the tested layer-noise selection policies do not establish a reliable perplexity improvement. These checks recompute retained measurements and inspect their implementation; they do not reproduce GPU model runs.

A few local clarifications would improve the final account:

- Call the added evaluation a replication on a second **window pair**, unless document identities are supplied. The script selects different token offsets from concatenated corpora; this establishes nonoverlapping windows, but does not itself establish distinct source documents. Preserve the explicit warning against treating layer cells as independent replications.
- Section 4.5's new statement that all five tail inputs are “dominated by a massive-activation channel” needs a defined statistic and supporting values, or should be omitted. Appendix B's eigenvalue shares do not establish that wording for all five cells. The documented prediction failures and unresolved mechanism are sufficient to state the limitation.
- In Section 5, distinguish observed-label gradient diagnostics from model-sampled-label estimates. The latter can estimate Fisher information related to local teacher-student KL curvature under appropriate expectations; the blanket statement that neither measures that curvature is too broad. The finite estimates and numerical baseline still prevent the causal conclusion that the revision appropriately withdraws.
- Make the new replication and KL-control commands portable and record fixed model revisions. The retained numerical evidence is inspectable, but absolute workspace paths and an unpinned model branch still impede rerunning it.

These are bounded presentation and reproducibility qualifications, not defects in the retained central contribution. The present additions belong with the accepted paper as supporting evidence and correction.
