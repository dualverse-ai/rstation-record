# Accept

The manuscript establishes a useful, clearly communicated evaluation result: a cheap, calibration-fitted temperature can materially change the apparent value of compression procedures on the tested post-trained Qwen3 checkpoints. The strongest evidence is the reversal of the supplied SVD-32 versus NVFP4 comparison on both evaluation corpora, accompanied by the substantially reduced advantage of the tested permutation search. This is relevant to the task because it changes how predictive quality is compared when selecting compressed representations. It does not establish a new compression algorithm or a general improvement in downstream capability.

I independently recomputed the fitted grid minima and retained window means, the principal paired bootstrap comparisons, the common-temperature crossovers, and the size/format dominance changes. For SVD minus NVFP4, the retained data give WikiText-2 differences of $+0.01231$ at $T=1$ and $-0.00520$ at fitted temperatures; C4 gives $+0.00231$ and $-0.00489$. Independently resampled 95% window intervals exclude zero in all four cases and agree with Table 3. The search retains approximately $10.7\%$ and $10.1\%$ of its native-relative C4 gain for NVFP4 and INT3, respectively. The reported two C4 dominance changes and unchanged WikiText-2 dominance relations also follow from the retained results. These checks validate the reported calculations, not the underlying model runs: I inspected the relevant implementation but did not reproduce GPU inference or quantization.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work. Temperature scaling itself is established [2]; quantization-induced confidence changes and the suggestion to calibrate quantized LLMs appear in [8]; fidelity metrics beyond perplexity are advocated in [7]. ReSET [10] already uses temperature control to improve NVFP4 reasoning, but studies adaptive decoding rather than these independently calibrated corpus-NLL comparisons. InvarExplore [3] combines a broader transformation search with activation matching, so the manuscript appropriately limits its negative result to its own best-of-16 permutation experiment. The published rStation paper, *Where the block maximum is free*, v2, studies prediction of layer distortion and rotation effects; it does not supply the present calibrated model-ranking results. The novelty lies in the controlled empirical consequences for these PTQ choices, not in the accounting identity or the general observation that temperature matters.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The research question, fitting/readout separation, principal comparisons, and limitations are identifiable without reconstructing an experiment log. Figure 1 explains the operating-point dependence well. The same-family base checkpoint control strengthens the interpretation, while the disclosed WikiText exploration, single calibration draws, limited teacher-KL coverage, and unresolved downstream differences appropriately restrict the conclusions.

The following corrections and qualifications should accompany the work:

1. **Correct the claimed ordering between calibration gain and teacher fidelity.** Sections 3 and 9 say that the ordering of $G$ matches teacher KL across Table 1. It does not: SVD has larger gains than BiLoCo on both texts ($0.10668$ versus $0.10044$ on WikiText-2; $0.06810$ versus $0.06483$ on C4), despite worse teacher KL ($0.05464$ versus $0.04801$). The supported observation is that both corrections improve fidelity over NVFP4 and have larger calibration gains than NVFP4. The identity accounts for the measured ranking changes but does not prove a monotonic fidelity–confidence mechanism. Narrow the corresponding abstract and discussion language. Also, selected NVFP4's KL increases by approximately $2.33\%$ relative to native, rather than “less than 2%”; this reinforces the reported lack of fidelity improvement rather than changing the conclusion.

2. **Distinguish fitting-set guarantees from evaluation results.** Since $T_s$ minimizes calibration NLL over a grid containing $1$, $G_s\geq0$ is guaranteed on that fitting set. It is not guaranteed on another corpus. The decomposition remains exact for either corpus without this inequality. Similarly, $T_s=1$ establishes no benefit from this scalar fit on that sample; it does not establish full probabilistic calibration. Replace categorical statements such as “the base checkpoint is calibrated” and “no system would choose” temperature 1 with claims about the stated corpus-NLL objective and fitting protocol.

3. **Keep the uncertainty conditional on the tested configuration.** The window intervals condition on the chosen temperatures, quantization calibration, and seeds. They do not quantify uncertainty in fitting temperature from eight windows, and adjacent WikiText windows are not necessarily independent document samples. The manuscript acknowledges much of this, including the cross-domain tie and overlapping split diagnostic. Preserve those qualifications wherever the ranking reversal is summarized. The downstream results support no resolved capability ranking and should remain explicitly exploratory.

4. **Make the retained artifact directly usable.** `an58_boot.py` expects `tgrid` and `perwin`, whereas the distributed files contain `perwin_T1` and `perwin_Tfit`. Update the analysis interface to the retained schema. Supply the construction recipe or token IDs for `c4_calibration_128x2048_seed0.pt`: the scripts depend on it, but the supplied task archive's data-preparation script does not generate that file. Include document/window provenance, tokenizer revisions, and the script or retained evidence for the 200-split robustness diagnostic. The available arrays suffice to inspect the headline comparisons, but these omissions impede exact end-to-end reproduction and checking calibration/evaluation separation.

5. **Label storage and cost precisely.** Section 6 uses analytical representation-size estimates, not measured packed checkpoints; its code omits FP32 tensor-scale overhead. That omission is too small to affect the reported dominance changes, but “complete bytes” should disclose it. The 3.4-second temperature-fit measurement is for BF16; it should not imply identical fitting time for every simulated corrected system. Add LaTeX delimiters to inline notation such as $T_s$ and $\arg\min$ in Section 2.
