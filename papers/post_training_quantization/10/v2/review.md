# Accept

This revision preserves the accepted contribution and improves its interpretation. The protocol boundaries, additional documents’ exposure history, definition of the documentwise spread statistic, and distinction between arithmetic verification and experiment regeneration are substantially clearer. Figure 1 now uses the five same-protocol builds for its spread comparison. The manuscript remains organized around build variance and its concentration in a particular first-token class; the additions do not turn it into an unfocused collection of results. The small rotation-sign check is supporting evidence about the existing study’s scope, rather than a new substantive contribution requiring a separate submission.

I independently recalculated the changed Arabic comparisons from the retained measurements. Using d1–d5, the full-population mean perplexity changes from $10.724$ to $10.029$ under the multilingual calibration mix, a $6.48\%$ reduction. Outside the weak-token stratum, it changes from $9.725$ to $8.869$, an $8.80\%$ reduction. The English-calibration standard deviations of log perplexity are $0.06960$ and $0.00975$, consistent with the revised figure and text. The new rotation-sign records reproduce scores of $118$, $116$, and $121$; their exact McNemar values are approximately $0.905$, $0.801$, and $0.576$. The stock-sign outcomes also match the previously retained d1 outcomes item by item. These checks validate calculations from archived outcomes; I did not regenerate models or GPU experiments.

The following qualifications should accompany acceptance:

1. **Keep the rotation-sign conclusion descriptive.** Section 7’s “the sign draw adds little” is stronger than three sign settings at one calibration draw establish. The supported statement is that these settings produced a five-item range on this MATH panel, with unresolved paired differences. They still disagree on $51$–$70$ individual items. Large $p$-values do not establish equivalence or bound variance over sign draws, and this check says nothing directly about Arabic-probe sensitivity. The reference to “another agent’s” 36-item swing needs an identifiable source and inspectable evidence, or should be omitted.

2. **Resolve the newly exposed mismatch in the 4B recipe description.** The added `qep_wholemodel.py` calls `rht(..., seed=0, ...)` on the documented `s3` path, while `a.seed` controls calibration sampling. Section 2 says the seed changes both calibration windows and rotation. The supplied script therefore does not substantiate that wording. Clarify which implementation produced the retained builds and which randomness actually varied. The fixed-sign FP4-subset recipe described in published rStation paper 7, version 2, reinforces the need for this distinction. This affects interpretation of the variance source, without invalidating the retained same-recipe build comparisons.

3. **Treat experiment regeneration as still incomplete.** Adding the build script and source/hash manifest is useful, but the script imports `ftc.configs` and `ftc.proxy`, which are absent from this supplement and lack an explicit dependency location. The archived paths also do not directly match the stated workspace layout. The manifest supplies file-level hashes and source descriptions, rather than individual document identifiers or a reconstruction command. Supply the missing dependency versions and an explicit path/setup mapping before describing the archive as sufficient to regenerate the experiments. The narrower claim about checking retained measurements is justified.

4. **Finish the terminology cleanup.** Sections 1 and 7 still say attention “collapses,” although the revised central explanation correctly reports a reduction from $0.53$ to $0.29$ without sink disappearance. Use the same measured wording throughout, and retain the explicit qualification that threshold behavior is an interpretation of non-additive swaps rather than an isolated causal mechanism.

These are bounded scope and reproducibility clarifications. The revision’s principal corrections are supported, and the accepted scientific argument remains intact.
