# Accept

This is a useful, narrowly scoped empirical contribution to PTQ evaluation. Its strongest result is the identification of a BF16-defined first-token class that concentrates large differences between quantized Qwen3-8B builds on Arabic documents. Component swaps, document-level losses, activation measurements, attention measurements, and evaluation on additional documents provide complementary evidence. The 32-build Qwen3-4B study also gives a concrete demonstration that item-paired comparisons of two realized models do not establish differences between quantization recipes. These findings are substantively relevant to judging capability retention at fixed compression, despite not introducing a better quantizer.

The statistical accounting is sound. I recomputed the supplied tables and independently checked the count-variance decomposition, panel disjointness, activation-based stratum membership, and additional-document spread statistic. The 4B count variances decompose as approximately $53.0=27.5+25.5$ and $81.2=33.5+47.7$; the reported McNemar rejection counts and approximately $0.30$ cross-panel correlation reproduce. The manuscript appropriately avoids treating the 496 overlapping pairs as independent trials or calling McNemar miscalibrated. At 8B, distinguishing d0's protocol from d1–d5 is essential and is handled explicitly in the main text.

The first-token result is more informative than a generic warning about seeds. On the original Arabic documents, the retained measurements reproduce excess NLL of $0.727$ versus $0.110$ and $0.118$ for the weak-activation stratum, with substantially smaller differences elsewhere. The additional-document statistic reproduces as $0.655$ versus $0.0333$ nats/token. This statistic is the **mean of documentwise standard deviations across d0–d2**, rather than the standard deviation of pooled corpus NLL. The evidence supports an input-dependent vulnerability of these particular builds. It does not establish a universal token class, a failure probability over calibration draws, or an isolated causal mediator. The stated limitations largely respect those boundaries.

The focused literature comparison supports a distinct contribution. Williams and Aletras [11] already establish substantial calibration-data effects; general benchmark variance [9, 10] is also established. Massive activations [2], persistence of attention sinks after super-weight removal [4], PrefixQuant [6], and Son et al.'s prefix-cache method [7] anticipate the broad activation/prefix motivation. They do not subsume the specific weak-activation token classification, its interaction with these weight-only builds, and its predictive value on additional Arabic documents. Published rStation work on the BW16 codec [1], temperature fitting [3], and calibration-Hessian optimism [8] addresses different central questions. The unsuccessful aggregate-error predictor is appropriately secondary, rather than presented as a new general impossibility result.

The paper is clear enough for acceptance: the questions, comparison units, principal evidence, and limitations are identifiable, and Figure 1 makes the concentration effect understandable. The following corrections would improve precision without changing the supported contribution:

1. **Keep protocol boundaries consistent throughout.** The abstract attributes the corpus range to five same-protocol builds, but the retained corpus measurements cover only d0–d2. Figure 1(a) pools six builds from two protocols; label it accordingly or use d1–d5. The $20\times$ additional-document contrast likewise concerns the fixed, heterogeneous d0–d2 trio. The multilingual comparison pools d0–d2 as its English baseline; using d1–d5 instead still gives descriptive improvements of about $6.5\%$ overall and $8.8\%$ outside the weak-token stratum.

2. **Describe attention reduction without claiming sink disappearance.** Attention mass $0.29$ on one position can still represent strong concentration. Replace “it is no longer one” with the measured reduction, and treat “threshold” and “all-or-nothing” as interpretations of the observed non-additivity. Update Figure 1's older “weak-sink,” “strong-sink,” and “sink-strength intervention” labels to match the main text's more careful terminology.

3. **Clarify the additional documents' exposure history.** Section 4 says their training half was “never used anywhere,” but `draw_errmap.py` uses the Arabic training half B for earlier local-error measurements. This does not itself show that the frozen token criterion was selected using these outcomes, but the stronger untouched-data claim is unsupported by the supplied scripts. State precisely what was previously measured and what was frozen before the new evaluation.

4. **Separate arithmetic reproducibility from experiment regeneration.** The retained outcomes permit meaningful checking, but the 4B build script is absent and the 8B scripts require workspace-specific paths, imports, and externally supplied probe texts. Archive the missing build implementation, provide document identifiers/hashes and explicit setup commands, and narrow “recomputes every number” where evidence is only summarized. My verification reproduced calculations from retained measurements; it did not regenerate quantized models or GPU experiments.

5. **Keep recommendations proportionate to the evidence.** Two builds per arm are a minimum replication check, not a generally sufficient basis for method-level inference. The predictor correlations $0.38$, $-0.36$, and $0.33$ show similar observed ranking performance; without uncertainty on their differences, “no better” should mean “no demonstrated improvement.”
