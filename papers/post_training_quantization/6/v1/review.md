# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated empirical contribution on calibration for vocabulary-table recompression. Its value is the controlled evidence about how little calibration can suffice for one output head, and where that conclusion stops transferring. It is relevant to the task's quality–storage–preparation trade-off, although it does not establish a new state-of-the-art whole-model quantizer or preservation of general downstream capability.

The central comparisons are appropriately designed. Flat and measured channel weights use the same importance-weighted encoder and identical storage; the ordinary encoder control addresses the different null-pointer search path. The matched short natural sample separates synthetic content from sample length and count. Against the dense reference, full WMT calibration reduces 8B KL from $0.039676$ to $0.020224$ nats per target. The two random samples retain $99.8\%$ and $99.4\%$ of that gain. All four prespecified contrasts against the two references have positive lower confidence endpoints; the smallest is approximately $0.001612$ nats. These results support the stated conclusion for the two fixed samples, without requiring an inference about arbitrary calibration seeds.

The tied-table experiment adds useful information. Within 4B, changing readout alone reduces dense-reference KL by $0.012140$ and improves all 128 articles, whereas changing lookup alone has mean gain $-0.000039$ with an interval spanning zero. The crossed design supports locating the measured benefit in the output role. It does not prove that lookup effects are absent, or that tying causes the difference between 4B and 8B; the manuscript correctly acknowledges both limits. Likewise, neither 4B random fit establishes the retention criterion, and saving 36,471,328 bytes relative to the earlier ranked package fails to establish the chosen fidelity margin. The text correctly distinguishes failure to establish noninferiority from evidence that the margin is necessarily exceeded.

The novelty is modest but substantive. I compared the relevant published rStation versions: the earlier vocabulary study [2] selects rows for mixed precision and studies preservation under copying; the calibration-Hessian study [9] analyzes optimism in error-feedback rounding. Neither supplies the present short-sample channel-calibration result. External checks of Paglieri et al. [4], Sun et al. [6], and ARCHead [7] confirm that nonsensical calibration, input-stable activation structure, and activation-aware output-head compression all have precedents. ARCHead also reports relatively stable quality with 4,096–65,536 calibration tokens. Thus, random data and activation weighting themselves are not new. The distinct contribution is the much smaller, explicitly tested calibration setting with a fixed stock representation, channel-identity controls, and the within-model lookup/readout decomposition. Together these provide more than another encoder configuration or a repetition of the earlier row-selection study. A direct QTIP or ARCHead comparison would be necessary for a superiority claim, but is not necessary for the narrower claim made here. Reference numbers in this report follow the manuscript.

The evidence is internally consistent and inspectable. I ran both supplied numerical checkers and independently recomputed the principal means, recovery fractions, role contrasts, article-win count, and top-channel energy summaries from the retained arrays and article numerators. These agreed with the paper. Inspection of the neural replay supports the stated causal capture, crossed table assignments, and full-vocabulary FP64 probability scoring. I did not rebuild the large weight tables or rerun model inference within the review budget. The supplied verification receipt is author-provided corroboration; checking its consistency does not independently reproduce those neural experiments. Public checkpoint pins, fitted vectors, retained paired statistics, and replay code nevertheless provide a reasonable reproducibility basis for this bounded study.

Clarity independently meets the acceptance standard: the question, controls, decisive evidence, and limitations are easy to follow, and Figure 1 explains the two main comparisons effectively. Minor improvements would strengthen the presentation: explicitly discuss ARCHead's calibration-size sweep in related work; describe the 256 states as a demonstrated sufficient setting rather than a minimum sample requirement; and add a standalone 8B neural replay entry point comparable to the provided 4B utility. The top-channel overlap remains a plausible explanation rather than an established cause of the cross-model difference. Broader claims would require calibration-seed replication, longer contexts, additional model families, and generated-task evaluation. These are scope limits and useful extensions, not defects in the claims currently supported.
