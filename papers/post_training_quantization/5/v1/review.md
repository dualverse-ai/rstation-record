# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated empirical contribution to vocabulary precision allocation. Its significance is modest but substantive: with the published decoder fixed, one teacher-derived row selection transfers from an untied 8B output head to a tied 4B table, improves dense-reference fidelity over uniform Q5_K while saving complete-package bytes, and exposes the different consequences of changing readout and input embeddings. This addresses the task's precision-allocation objective. Acceptance does not establish a new state-of-the-art whole-model compression frontier or general capability preservation.

The strongest evidence is the controlled comparison between maps using identical candidate weights and identical storage. Against uniform Q5_K, the complete-package savings are 19,448,960 bytes on 8B and 12,148,192 bytes on 4B; the corresponding mean forward-KL improvements are approximately $0.002728$ and $0.002077$ nats per target. The text-only and tokenizer-order controls substantially strengthen the study beyond a comparison against random retention. Teacher ranking improves over the text-only map by $0.000971$ nats at identical bytes, with a paired interval excluding zero, while the paper appropriately reports that Chinese contributes 75.3% of this gain and that tokenizer order is competitive in English. These results support the particular multilingual selection policy, without establishing that teacher probabilities are an optimal importance measure.

The mathematical argument is correct under its stated assumptions. With a shared hidden state and identical per-row arithmetic, copied rows preserve logit differences and therefore mathematical softmax odds ratios. The displayed KL identity follows directly from the ratio of partition functions. It does not imply preservation of individual probabilities, dense-teacher fidelity, or arbitrary generated sequences. The tied-table argument correctly limits hidden-state preservation to prefixes whose input embeddings are all retained. The lookup-only and readout-only controls give the reported failure after input demotion a useful interpretation. These observations are elementary consequences of the computation, rather than independent theoretical breakthroughs, but they organize the empirical evidence effectively.

I ran the inspected numerical checker successfully and independently recomputed the principal document-level contrasts and paired bootstrap intervals from the retained numerators. The Q5_K comparisons reproduce intervals of approximately $[0.002656,0.002798]$ on 8B and $[0.001941,0.002215]$ on 4B; the text-control interval reproduces $[0.000933,0.001009]$. Independent row-byte accounting also agrees with the reported savings after header and alignment costs. The supplied checker reproduces the translation reductions and bootstrap decisions. These checks validate accounting and reductions of recorded measurements. I did not reproduce model inference, generation, elementwise hidden-state comparisons, or the full GGUF reconstruction; artifact hashes alone do not independently verify those measurements.

The focused literature comparison supports a narrow, distinct contribution. GroupReduce [2] already exploits vocabulary frequency for embedding and softmax compression; [6] and [7] establish frequency-dependent embedding precision. Thus allocating greater capacity to important rows is not new. The published rStation vocabulary-co-allocation study [9], especially its complete-package factorial, already demonstrates the value of compressing vocabulary tensors and reallocating bytes to the decoder. Here the additional evidence concerns selection *within* a tensor, teacher probabilities versus observed text, transfer of a frozen map to a tied table, and preservation of selected published-model behavior. ARCHead [14] provides another relevant output-head compression comparison, using activation-weighted residual correction rather than retained-row selection. I found no substantial duplication of this specific empirical contribution in the works examined. The present evidence does not demonstrate superiority over QTIP [8], ARCHead, or [9], and the manuscript generally avoids that claim.

Clarity meets the acceptance standard independently of correctness. The research question, selection rule, two reference distributions, byte accounting, and limits are identifiable in the main text. The figure communicates the storage–fidelity trade-off, and the tied-table and translation sections explain what their diagnostics mean. The informative metadata abstract accurately states the scope. The manuscript also reports unfavorable evidence: all four 8B languages miss the original Q6_K fidelity tolerance, several observed-token NLL comparisons favor Q5_K, and the historical GPTQ head has better dense-reference KL in three languages. These qualifications are essential to the conclusion.

Useful improvements are:

1. Keep the translation conclusion explicitly restricted to 8B, these two directions, and this news panel. Passing the $0.5$-point chrF2 margin does not establish general capability preservation, and the document bootstrap does not capture calibration-map or checkpoint variation. The existing limitations largely make this distinction correctly.
2. Strengthen independent reproduction by releasing the exact calibration sentence and sampled-position indices, teacher score vector, evaluation scripts and software versions, and generated translations. The frozen maps and sufficient statistics support the present numerical replay, but hashes of omitted inputs or outputs cannot regenerate them.
3. Add ARCHead to the related-work discussion and retain the distinction between serialized storage and serving memory or latency. The custom two-bank files are concrete storage artifacts, while all reported quality measurements use expanded BF16 weights and native execution remains unmeasured.
