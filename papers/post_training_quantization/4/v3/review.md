# Accept

This revision resolves the remaining scope and reproducibility comments on the accepted version without adding a substantive scientific contribution. The manuscript remains focused on predicting calibration-Hessian layer-error optimism and explaining its consequences and limits for quantization design. Its argument, retained numerical evidence, and distinction from the block-distortion and temperature-calibration studies [14, 15] remain intact.

The mathematical wording is improved appropriately. Defining $\bar d$, stating that the error covariance is proportional to $C$, and describing the prediction as a ratio of expected normalized errors align the abstract and Section 2 with the assumptions and derivation in Section 3. Restricting the Section 4.1 heading to agreement across the three tested codes avoids implying universal code independence. These changes clarify the existing result rather than strengthening its claims.

The reproduction changes address the previously identified practical obstacles. Code inspection confirms that the C4 fetcher now creates and writes to the directory used by the Hessian collector, replaces the hardcoded comparison path with an optional environment variable, and preserves the pinned dataset revision and filtering rule. The revised instructions supply the required measured-optimism arguments and identify the additional C4-prefix Hessians used in Figure 1. Both figure scripts now create their documented output directory. These conclusions concern consistency of the instructions and code; I did not rerun dataset acquisition, Hessian collection, or GPU experiments.

The ledger now explicitly places predictor-guided damping outside this manuscript, and Section 6 correctly limits its completeness claim to supporting whole-model results. No new damping method is established by this revision. The one-model, input-side-Hessian, simulated-quantization limitations remain clear, and the additions do not obscure the accepted contribution.

One minor reproducibility improvement remains: the newly documented `oos_proxy.py` commands target bundled result files, while the script opens its output in append mode. Recommend fresh output filenames for regeneration and subsequent comparison with the retained evidence, so reruns do not silently accumulate duplicate measurements. Formatting the command sequences as fenced code blocks would also improve readability.
