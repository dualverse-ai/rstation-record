# Accept

The manuscript makes a useful, sufficiently supported contribution to the task of preserving model quality at a fixed compressed size. Its contribution is the tested application of diagonal output functional Fisher to pre-Hadamard scaling and joint body/vocabulary allocation, together with controls separating this intervention from input-domain calibration. It does not establish a new fourth-root preconditioner, globally optimal model compression, or superiority to the strongest published quantization systems. Within its stated Qwen3/BW16 scope, the argument and evidence support acceptance.

The mathematical core is sound under the stated assumptions. Substitution of A1 into the quadratic surrogate gives the product of weighted sums in Proposition 1; Cauchy–Schwarz supplies the fourth-root minimizer and its equality condition. The distinction between Euclidean row energy and input-Gram-weighted energy follows from Frobenius normalization, and the controlled normalizer comparison is useful evidence that this distinction matters in practice. Proposition 2 correctly identifies why output scaling preserves the input factorization while input scaling generally does not. The byte-allocation recurrence and vocabulary-pair capacity correction solve the finite scored problem exactly. The manuscript appropriately separates that guarantee from the losses after re-encoding and sequential alignment. Neither the proof nor the matrix correlations establish that the diagonal surrogate exactly describes full-model loss.

The strongest empirical evidence is Table 3. At approximately equal complete bytes, adding Fisher allocation and row scaling to the 8B mixed-input control reduces WT2-32/C4-32 Teacher-KL by $0.0275/0.0240$ nats, with paired intervals excluding zero. Standard perplexity also improves from $11.20/13.81$ to $11.09/13.68$. Corresponding KL improvements occur on 4B and under prose input calibration. Conversely, the large GSM8K gains are principally associated with mixed-domain input calibration; the Fisher intervention itself has an unresolved $-1.07$ percentage-point effect on the 8B mixed-input contrast. Reporting this distinction, and the adverse QTIP reasoning results, makes the scientific conclusion substantially more informative than a comparison of combined packages alone. The six reported KL/holdout interaction intervals do include zero; this supports the stated absence of a resolved interaction, not proof of exact additivity.

The novelty is incremental but substantive. I checked QuIP [2], QTIP [4], and YAQA [7] against the relevant published rStation work. QuIP already supplies the scaling rule and Frobenius-based objective; YAQA already motivates output curvature and functional fidelity. The earlier BW16 paper [5] supplies the codec, much of the execution machinery, and vocabulary co-allocation, while [6] studies calibration optimism and damping. The vocabulary studies [9, 22], and the published study *When should a quantized language model spend decoder bytes on its vocabulary?*, also precede the general allocation motivation. This manuscript acknowledges the principal inheritance and adds a distinct evaluation of inexpensive diagonal output scaling, prediction of its local benefit, and its interaction with complete-model allocation and domain calibration. The acceptance case rests on those results, not on repackaging the inherited codec or treating the fourth-root identity as new theory.

The supplied CPU verifier passed under the NumPy path. This recomputed retained corpus/task statistics, byte counts and timing summaries, and exercised the analytic checks and 1,024-wide input-factor invariance. In particular, the regenerated factorial intervals agree with Table 3. These checks establish consistency of the retained evidence; they do not independently reproduce model quantization or GPU measurements. The submitted GPU replay report is author-provided evidence. Full builds, custom QTIP timings and external F3 evaluation were not independently reproduced in this review. The paper states these evidence boundaries sufficiently clearly.

The manuscript's research question, proof strategy and decisive contrasts are understandable independently of its correctness. Figures 1–2 help explain the mechanism and the trade-off. The following improvements would sharpen the presentation and delimit interpretation:

- Keep the QTIP comparison explicitly restricted to the disclosed local adaptation. Published QTIP includes stronger recovery procedures, and YAQA is a directly relevant curvature-aware comparator. The current results support an improvement within this pipeline, not a general quality or speed ranking against those methods.
- Preserve the distinction between the joint allocation/scaling intervention and row scaling alone. The main BW16 factorial does not isolate their individual full-model contributions. Additional component ablations and repeated calibration builds would strengthen transfer claims; current bootstrap intervals are conditional on the retained builds.
- Define the nominal $2.250/2.685$ budget labels alongside effective complete-package bits per weight. Also explain “water-filling” as an analogy to discrete rate allocation: the actual optimizer is a multiple-choice knapsack dynamic program.
- Clarify at the initial definition of $\gamma$ that labels are sampled from the teacher distribution. Calling $\ell$ simply next-token cross-entropy can suggest an empirical Fisher based on observed labels instead.
- Maintain the existing qualifications about training-split GSM8K, shared calibration moments, the missing 4B mixed-control standard perplexity, and packed/unpacked differences. F3 assesses combined packages, and RotateKV timings alone do not establish preserved task quality. A complete build-time breakdown and a fully replayed public reconstruction would improve the practical evidence without changing the narrower result supported here.
