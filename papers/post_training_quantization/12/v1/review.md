# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical study of how rotation-induced quantization errors combine at the output of an LLM. Its contribution is the controlled finite-ensemble comparison across likelihood, teacher fidelity, token decisions and generation, together with a test of low-ensemble-size extrapolation. The manuscript clearly disclaims a new quantizer, a storage improvement and identification of an irreducible error component. On that scope, the results are scientifically meaningful and substantively relevant to preserving capability under post-training quantization.

The central evidence supports the stated descriptive conclusions. Holding calibration and codec settings fixed while varying rotation seeds isolates a useful source of variation. The larger pools show substantial likelihood recovery without equivalent restoration of teacher fidelity or token accuracy. The generation results provide a useful caution about transferring teacher-forced recovery fractions to downstream capability, although they remain exploratory. The distinction between original-four-seed and within-pool extrapolation, including the corrected account of the 8B preregistered comparison, makes the interpretation assessable.

I independently reconstructed the single-package values, fitted intercepts and two-package recovery fractions in Table 1 from the retained per-window arrays. I also ran the supplied NumPy refits for all four enlarged pools and recomputed the MATH pair statistics from the item records. These agree with the reported numbers to the displayed precision. In particular, the MATH recovery mean is approximately $0.198$, with the reported across-pair SE of $0.108$. The retained full-vocabulary audit summaries reproduce the approximately $0.073$–$0.074$ increase in NLL recovery and the near-constant KL-minus-NLL difference. These checks reproduce analysis of retained measurements; they do not independently reproduce model construction, inference or the full-vocabulary dumps.

The mathematical identity in Section 2 is correct. Recognizing that normalized geometric averaging gives KL and excess NLL the same ensemble-normalization term removes an otherwise misleading interpretation of their similar fitted slopes. Likewise, treating $B$ as a finite-range empirical extrapolation, rather than a measured shared-error component, is appropriate. The small but systematic KL discrepancies in Table 2 are reported rather than hidden behind high goodness-of-fit values.

The novelty is sufficiently distinct from the closest literature. SpinQuant [13] establishes variation across random rotations and learns better rotations; it does not supply these controlled ensemble-size recovery curves. Wood et al. [14], including Corollary 9, supplies the relevant ensemble theory, which is appropriately credited rather than claimed as new. Published same-problem work already establishes confidence-related discrepancies between NLL and fidelity [4], calibration-build variation [12], and differing responses of fidelity and reasoning to calibration choices [15]. This paper adds a different intervention—averaging packages whose rotations alone vary—and measures its finite-size response across those readouts. The contribution should continue to be presented on that basis, rather than as a new general theory of quantization damage.

Several qualifications and improvements would strengthen the presentation without changing this decision:

- **Keep the full-vocabulary conclusion local.** Section 3.3 supports increased NLL recovery at two unrepaired operating points and the sampled positions. “Every NLL statement … is conservative” is broader than the audit establishes, particularly for repaired packages and large ensembles. State the measured direction at those two points, and replace “bounds the effect” in Section 5 with “measures the effect.” Also distinguish the exact same-population KL/NLL identity from its approximate application when NLL alone filters targets.
- **Clarify subset sampling and uncertainty.** The curve builder combines randomly sampled whole-pool subsets with subsets retained for original-group and half-pool diagnostics, and the refit averages their union. This is not simply a uniform random sample of subsets at each size. Document this design and its counts; ideally retain separate masks for the whole-pool estimator and diagnostic groups. The window-bootstrap errors condition on both packages and the retained subsets and do not quantify subset-sampling uncertainty. This matters most for interpreting the small extrapolation discrepancies.
- **Keep generation inference descriptive.** The four pairs reuse the same seed pairs across two rates and all use one item panel. The across-pair SE is a useful spread summary, but “about 3 SE” should not be read as a calibrated significance test for a general NLL-versus-generation difference. The explicit limitations and negative first-pair result are valuable.
- **Correct the calibration-count description.** Section 2 describes 128 calibration windows for the smaller-model builds, whereas the supplied commands use `--windows 32` and the builder rescales the allocation to that count. State the actual count per pipeline and distinguish allocation proportions from realized window counts. Label the experiments explicitly as weight-only, BF16-expanded evaluation.
- **Improve access and visualization.** Publish the omitted log-probability files through a durable archive when possible, and preserve the fitted repair parameters if exact replay is intended. A compact plot of recovery against ensemble size, retaining the numerical tables, would make the main comparison considerably easier to follow.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the research question, ensemble operation, readout definitions, central results and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The abstract is informative. The peripheral massive-row and entropy observations could move to an appendix to sharpen the argument, but they do not obscure it. Acceptance is for the finite-ensemble diagnostic findings; it does not establish a universal recovery law, an infinite-ensemble error floor, or a practical compression advantage.
