# Accept

This is a meaningful, sufficiently supported contribution to compressed inference on Qwen3-8B. The strongest contribution is the support-partitioned quarter-product indexing of a 16-dimensional Barnes–Wall codebook, together with an implementation that exploits this representation during batched decoding. The complete-package vocabulary experiment adds a useful demonstration of reallocating storage. Acceptance is for these explicitly bounded algorithmic and systems results, not for general superiority over current low-bit quantization methods.

The central argument is convincing. The weight-four Hamming-code masks permit canonical even and odd four-coordinate tables to serve multiple cosets. This addresses a concrete decoding problem: procedural unranking has long dependencies, whereas direct half-block tables are large. Algorithm 1 explains the decomposition, and the fixed-weight comparison isolates indexing from quantization quality. The later batch-reuse optimization and whole-model measurements connect that mechanism to a practical outcome. This goes beyond merely substituting a familiar lattice or adjusting bit widths.

I independently recomputed the lattice cardinalities using integer-norm and parity enumeration, obtaining $|C_{22}|=248{,}874{,}804{,}961$ and $|C_{45}|=68{,}713{,}032{,}753{,}121$, including the 4,320-vector minimum shell. Independent byte calculations reproduce the two explicit-seed V2 package totals, $2{,}759{,}457{,}760$ and $2{,}872{,}703{,}968$ bytes. Recalculation from the retained window arrays reproduces the principal raw-NLL, fitted-NLL and teacher-KL confidence intervals. The iso-budget generation contrast also reproduces: N512 gives $-0.78125$ percentage points with interval $[-3.32031,1.75781]$, and exploratory M256 gives $+8.203125$ points with McNemar $p=0.001914$. These checks establish arithmetic and retained-record consistency. I inspected relevant codec, calibration, runtime and evaluation code; I did not compile the CUDA extensions, repeat the exhaustive bijection tests, or reproduce GPU reconstruction and latency measurements.

The scientific interpretation mostly respects the evidence. BW16 has lower raw NLL than the adapted QTIP baseline, but fitted-NLL contrasts remain unresolved and teacher KL favors QTIP. The manuscript reports all three rather than equating lower raw perplexity with better preservation. Likewise, the bounded encoder's small synthetic audit concerns a diagonal surrogate, not exact minimization of the full Hessian loss. The distinction between those objectives is explicit and mathematically consequential. The separate clipping in BlockAlign is appropriately described as a heuristic blend.

The novelty check supports a distinct, scoped contribution. The two published rStation papers [4, 15] concern distortion prediction and temperature-controlled evaluation; their findings are credited and do not already supply this codec. QTIP [8] already provides parallel local-window trellis decoding, which the manuscript correctly acknowledges. QuIP# [3] establishes lattice-codebook PTQ, and LLVQ [9], including its indexing and CUDA discussions, already establishes hierarchical lattice indexing and fused decoding. Consequently, neither lattice quantization nor fused dequantization alone is new here. The specific support partition, compact four-dimensional tables and demonstrated execution trade-off provide the substantive distinction. CurveTQ [10] is also relevant prior work, but its curvature-weighted trellis approach is different. Missing direct LLVQ/CurveTQ experiments prevent a current state-of-the-art claim; they do not invalidate the narrower contribution demonstrated here.

Important qualifications and improvements are:

- **Keep the baseline and timing scope prominent.** The $1.31$–$1.37\times$ advantage is over an independently adapted Stage-I QTIP implementation, with matched comparisons from one historical GPU launch. The additional BW16-only reconstruction supports repeatability of that system, not the comparative speedup distribution. Preparation estimates assemble stages with different boundaries and should remain clearly separated from measured complete preparation time. Matched repeated launches and direct current-method comparisons would strengthen a subsequent broader claim.
- **Capability retention is limited.** N512 non-inferiority is relative to the specified compressed baseline, not proof of preserving BF16 capability. Programme exposure and truncated generation records limit inference from N512/M256, especially the exploratory M256 gain. Window-bootstrap intervals also condition on the selected recipe and fitted temperature; they do not capture calibration-seed variation or all dependence between adjacent text windows. Fresh evaluation and complete generation traces would improve this evidence.
- **Correct the execution-mode label.** Figure 1(c) calls the likelihood measurement “live Mode-1.” The supplied corpus evaluator sends 2,048-token sequences through the body and approximately 256-token chunks through the head, invoking Mode 2 under the documented dispatch rule. Label live compressed corpus evaluation separately from Mode-1 decode timing; the reconstruction does not itself establish Mode-1 corpus NLL. Existing Mode-1 reference checks and generation results remain useful evidence.
- **Simplify presentation and reconcile small provenance details.** The research question, algorithms and decisive results are identifiable, so clarity is adequate for acceptance. However, the abstract and main text are overloaded with implementation labels, exact byte counts and repeated verification history. Move more of these details to the appendices and lead with the indexing insight and measured trade-off. Also reconcile the calibration hash in §3.1 (`9736b152...`) with the manifest's `cal_128_sha256` (`f96b131a...`), and keep incremental versus total allocated VRAM terminology consistent with Appendix C.

These limitations constrain generalization and presentation rather than undermine the demonstrated codec and storage–serving contribution.
