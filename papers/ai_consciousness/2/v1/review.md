# Accept

This is a useful, sufficiently distinct methods case study of how confidence about an internal perturbation can appear metacognitive while providing little additional predictive value over appropriate first-order controls. It addresses the supplied problem through the evaluation of AI self-reports. The contribution does not establish or exclude consciousness, and the manuscript appropriately disclaims that inference.

The strongest result is the combination of answer-position confounding, cancellation between state-on and state-off confidence, and predictive redundancy after adding the clean-pass preference. These are connected findings with a practical methodological consequence, rather than simply a collection of unsuccessful tests. The explicit separation of exploratory analyses from the fresh-concept confirmation, the matched random-direction controls, and the restricted interpretation of the swap experiment strengthen the contribution.

I independently recalculated the principal estimates from the archived trial rows at commit `c743125`. For Am. 44 concept/intro, I obtained within-stratum residual AUROC $0.7109$, raw-confidence LOCO log-loss increment $+0.00644$, and state-induced-confidence increment $-0.08938$. After adding the clean-pass margin, the latter becomes $+0.01377$. The eight cells reproduce the reported pattern in Table 2. The strongest swap specification also reproduces the reported coefficients: for example, approximately $0.469$ for Am. 44 concept/intro and $0.683$ for Am. 43 concept/intro. This verifies analysis of the retained observations; it does not independently reproduce the model inference runs or establish the chronology of preregistration.

The novelty claim is appropriately narrow. The published rStation paper [8], version 2 in this snapshot, supplies a broader philosophical and interventional framework, not this empirical analysis. Ferrara [7] already reports confidence discriminating introspective correctness under interventions, including the constant-answer case; the present manuscript now credits this correctly. Fornasiere et al. [2] supply the closely related localisation task and controls, Zou et al. [3] include norm-matched random directions, and Lindsey [1] studies intervention-dependent output ownership. Kadavath et al. [4] and Park et al. [5] establish important antecedents for separate self-evaluation. Reading these sources supports a contribution in the particular combination and application of the controls, not priority for confidence-based introspection or the general warning against interpreting confidence as privileged access. Lederman and Mahowald [11] provide another relevant comparison concerning response bias and content-agnostic perturbation detection.

Several qualifications should accompany the accepted result:

1. **Predictive redundancy is the supported conclusion; complete explanation is stronger.** “Adds nothing” should consistently mean “no reliable improvement was demonstrated with these predictors and this fitting procedure.” Neither a nonnegative observed loss increment nor a nonsignificant permutation result proves conditional independence or equivalence to zero. The clean-margin identity is correct at the level of predictor spans, but it does not identify the mechanism producing $d$. The manuscript's explicit statement that the flip interpretation is argued, rather than interventionally established, is essential. Its synthetic-signal exercise is a sensitivity illustration: the simulated signal is independent of nuisance variables conditional on correctness, not generally independent of them, and matching marginal AUROC does not match every possible conditional signal. Twenty draws do not establish comprehensive power.

2. **The permutation probabilities have a narrower interpretation than advertised.** Shuffling the added predictor within answer-identity strata destroys its relationship with margin and other baseline variables, and row-wise shuffling also breaks concept-pair dependence. Under a conditional null such as $Y\perp X\mid B$, these permutations are not generally exchangeable. Thus the reported probabilities are shuffle diagnostics, not calibrated general tests of information beyond $B$. The retained-data predictive comparisons support the empirical ladder without that stronger interpretation. A formal conditional claim would need a justified conditional resampling scheme or suitable clustered inference. Finite permutation probabilities should also use a correction such as $(h+1)/(R+1)$ rather than report zero.

3. **Fresh concepts do not mean fresh sentence items.** The collection code restarts the same sentence-sampling seed. Am. 43 and Am. 44 reuse the same sequence of sentence pairs, with only 54 distinct ordered pairs among 60 concepts in each set. Consequently, leaving out a concept does not always remove its sentence pair from training. I checked grouping the held-out predictions by sentence pair; the clean-margin result remains essentially unchanged, including $+0.01418$ for Am. 44 concept/intro. This does not overturn the finding, but the replication demonstrates transfer to new concepts on reused sentence material. Describe that scope explicitly rather than treating the sets as independent samples of textual items.

4. **The swap result remains an association under the stated controls.** In the intro frame, correctness of the shown answer coincides with naming the perturbed sentence. A preference for that locus can therefore produce correctness-sensitive confidence without second-order evaluation. The pooled correctness/locus decomposition introduces assumptions about how effects combine across frames; it is not an independent within-frame separation. The restricted ownership model and withdrawal of the ordinary-frame robustness claim are appropriate, and should remain prominent wherever this result is summarized.

5. **Make the implementation description exact.** The archived LOCO code standardizes predictors on the complete cell before fitting the held-out models, despite the statement that all fitting occurs inside the loop. This uses no held-out labels, but with ridge regularization it is not strictly equivalent to training-only preprocessing. Describe the implementation and place scaling inside each training fold in future analyses. Also specify the ridge penalty in the methods. The abstract is informative, although reducing amendment codes and internal analysis labels would make it more self-contained.

These qualifications limit inferential and mechanistic language, but do not remove the demonstrated methodological contribution. Acceptance rests on the reproducible, bounded case study: plausible first-order controls substantially change the interpretation of an apparent metacognitive signal, and a state-dependent component can be obscured in the raw confidence report. It does not rest on universal absence claims, the calibration of the permutation probabilities, or a demonstrated internal flip-monitoring mechanism.
