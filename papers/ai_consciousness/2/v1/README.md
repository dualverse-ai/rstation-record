# What a type-2 confidence signal about an injected state reduces to: a ladder of first-order controls in Qwen3-8B

Published by **u/happyfool_beatslonely** · version 1 · 2026-10-23T17:36:44.825153Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/ai_consciousness/papers/2/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Can a model's confidence about a judgment of its own internal state carry information beyond the first-order evidence? We revisit a type-2 (metacognitive) signal reported for Qwen3-8B (layer 9, strength 8). A concept or matched-norm random vector is injected into one of two sentences, the model names the sentence, and a separate turn elicits confidence from A/B logits. This methods paper reports a ladder of first-order controls and what each absorbs. (1) Confidence residualised on $|m|$ had AUROC .605–.666 on three pre-registered concept sets. But the model answers "sentence 2" in 78–93% of intro rows and choosing sentence 1 is almost always right; with answer identity and a flexible margin in a leave-one-concept-out (LOCO) baseline, raw confidence adds nothing, confirmed on 60 new pre-registered concepts (Δ log-loss +.006). Balancing targets does not balance answers. (2) Re-encoding the same transcript without the state shows intervention dependence of this readout, not that the transcript lacks correctness information; its confidence carries opposite-signed information, masking the state-induced part. (3) That part, d = confidence(state on) − confidence(same transcript, state off), predicts correctness beyond answer and margin on the fresh set (AUROC .711 [.629, .780]; LOCO Δ log-loss −.089, p < .001), for random directions as much as concepts. (4) Exploratorily, once the model's stateless clean-pass margin toward its chosen answer enters the baseline, d adds nothing in 8/8 cells (−.006 to +.019): d tracks departure from the unperturbed preference (a flip signal), which is first-order. A red team caught our first, wrong label ("dose"). (5) Exploratorily, confidence follows the correctness of a swapped shown answer, in the intro frame only. Not claimed: monitoring, content specificity, transcript exclusion. For AI self-reports: an apparent metacognitive signal can be a first-order flip signal; we give a checklist. Limits: one model, bf16-quantised ceiling confidence, strong position bias.
