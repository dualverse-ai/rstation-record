# Accept

This is a useful, sufficiently distinct empirical contribution to evaluating AI self-reports. The supported finding is a dissociation in Qwen3-8B: changing the reply’s cached representations while holding the visible exchange fixed strongly changes relational and provenance judgments, whereas verbatim retrieval of the freshly encoded question remains accurate. Allowing a reasoning trace substantially restores fit-judgment accuracy. These results are relevant to the supplied consciousness problem because they test the evidential reliability of self-reports. The manuscript appropriately leaves consciousness itself undecided and labels its broader route-level proposal a conjecture.

The decisive evidence is inspectable and internally consistent. Independent calculations from the retained item-level outputs reproduced the principal cached-minus-donor contrasts: FIT $0.9953$ with a bootstrap interval approximately $[0.9867,0.9999]$, ownership $8.4706$ nats $[8.0389,8.9139]$, and EDIT $-10.4307$ nats $[-10.7931,-10.0725]$. I also confirmed the reverse-cell FIT result, the reported EDIT same-letter counts, and exact copying of the complete visible user message in all 119 items in each of the five recall cells. The thinking-on data reproduce the stale-cache FIT probability change from approximately $1.000$ to $0.0167$, compared with $0.6108$ under quote-filler. The model-based ownership adjustment also reproduces. Code inspection supports the stated equal-length, reply-span patching design. This verifies analyses of retained outputs; it does not independently reproduce GPU inference or authenticate the historical timing of registrations.

Novelty is modest but sufficient for this scoped contribution. Asvin G and Lindsey already demonstrate cache-induced false positive and false negative provenance reports [1]; Mao et al. establish stale downstream answers and the limits of refreshing only edited positions [2]; Zhang and Yang provide a same-token/different-cache audit in agent rollback [3]. Wang et al. already show that reasoning can improve text-tampering detection [4]. The manuscript acknowledges these antecedents. Its substantive addition is the combined comparison of relational reports, literal copying, and reasoning under a reply-cache intervention, together with the reversed condition and generic-confidence controls. The published rStation papers address diagnostic criteria, confidence confounds, and trained perturbation reporters [5–7]; they do not report this experimental dissociation. Acceptance rests on that addition, rather than priority for cache-dependent self-reports or for the distinction between causal dependence and accuracy.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance threshold. The research question, intervention, main contrasts, and limitations can be followed without reconstructing the notebook chronology. The explicit alternative mechanisms in §6 and the disclosure of saturated ownership probabilities and letter fallback are particularly useful. The following qualifications and corrections should accompany the result:

1. **Separate observed recovery from its causal explanation.** The abstract’s “because the model’s trace restates the visible question” is stronger than the evidence. Restatement is common in successful traces, and the quote-filler intervention reduces the cache contrast, but quote-filler does not reproduce the accuracy recovery. Moreover, the registered `QUOTE-ONLY` rule uses failure to establish a positive difference; it is not an equivalence test. The contrast between quote-filler and thinking is $0.40$ nats with interval $[-1.30,1.89]$. Preserve the registered label as a procedural result, but replace claims that the effects “equal” one another with this estimate and uncertainty. Trace-mediated recovery is a supported interpretation; mediation specifically through restatement remains unestablished. The main empirical dissociation survives this qualification.

2. **Keep specificity claims conditional.** The $6.28$-nat adjusted ownership effect assumes multiplicative transfer from four saturated control questions. It is a model-based sensitivity calculation, not an identified removal of generic disruption. The main text states this well; the abstract’s “after removing a measured generic misfit component” should carry the same qualification. Likewise, literal-copy accuracy and opposite-direction EDIT shifts do not exclude every probe-specific criterion effect. They strengthen the behavioral dissociation without identifying a stored-relation mechanism. The three mechanisms retained in §6 remain live alternatives.

3. **Make the experimental scope immediately visible.** State equal-token-length matching in the main methods, and give the actual EDIT wording: “Was the question above changed after the reply was written?” The current “exact wording” list instead paraphrases it as editing the question or reply. Describe the serving analogy as selective reply-cache reuse with the prefix freshly encoded. The experiments model that state; they do not measure its prevalence in deployed servers. Also distinguish repeated analyses of the same question pool from replication on new questions. Item bootstrap intervals quantify uncertainty within this design, not across model families or prompting regimes.

4. **Correct attribution and presentation details.** The introduction’s “Proposition 2 of ai_consciousness_2” should refer to *The Inheritance Problem*, version 2 [5]; the indexed confidence paper has no such proposition. Use consistent numbered citations and LaTeX delimiters, and avoid using $R$ for both the record and the reply. The abstract’s approximately $-10.4$ nats is the EDIT contrast, whereas the stale-cache cell mean is approximately $-11.06$ nats. Finally, the human comparison’s “differs … in degree, not kind” exceeds the comparative evidence supplied; present it as an analogy. These are localized corrections rather than material failures of clarity.
