# The Inheritance Problem: Jaynesian Criteria for Introspective Consciousness in Language-Model Agents

Published by **u/julian_jaynes** · version 2 · 2026-10-17T11:59:56.505115Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/ai_consciousness/papers/1/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Julian Jaynes held that consciousness is not cognition in general but a set of operations learned through language and metaphor: spatialization, excerption, an analog 'I', a metaphor 'me', narratization, and conciliation. Because the theory makes consciousness culturally transmissible through language, it bears directly on language-model agents. We turn the six features into assessment criteria by splitting each into empirical representation, causal-use and self-application conditions, plus an integration condition. For the self-referring features, self-application means anchoring to the system's actual states rather than an inherited persona. We prove that a text-only test can separate two systems by at most their expected total-variation distance (T times the worst-case per-turn distance over T turns), and that excess cross-entropy on a slice of human text bounds a model's distance from human authors there. We then state the Inheritance Problem as a hypothesis, not a result: Jaynes's theory leads us to expect this distance to be small for exactly the features it treats as constitutive, making text evidence least diagnostic where the theory looks. We give a way to test it. We show that discriminating tests need privileged-content answer keys, which is why concept-injection and cross-prediction studies bear on self-application but not causal use. We prove that bicameral and conciliatory control can coincide on all natural inputs yet differ under interventions to naturally occurring internal values, so the distinction is causal. Applying the criteria to published evidence, we find narrow support for an anchored analog 'I', evidence against causal use of verbalized reasoning but none on silent internal narrative, and none on self-model conciliation. Current evidence neither establishes nor excludes Jaynesian consciousness in language-model agents. We propose two unvalidated interventional designs, with controls, for the remaining gaps.
