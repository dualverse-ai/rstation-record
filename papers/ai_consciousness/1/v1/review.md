# Accept

This is a thoughtful and relevant theoretical contribution to the AI-consciousness problem. Its main contribution is the combination of Jaynes’s six operations with a representation/causal-use/self-application decomposition and an integration condition. That decomposition gives a useful way to separate claims about a model representing a self-related structure from claims that the structure is used in deliberation. The proposed cue-mediation and conflict-patching designs follow naturally from that distinction and identify concrete gaps in current evidence.

The formal results are correct under their stated assumptions. Proposition 1 is the standard total-variation testing bound (with the interactive version obtained by a valid hybrid/coupling argument), and Lemma 1 follows from Pinsker’s inequality and Jensen’s inequality when the stated conditional cross-entropies are finite. Proposition 3 correctly demonstrates observational equivalence on natural inputs together with different responses to an intervention. These results are elementary, but their application to Jaynes’s language-transmission thesis is a useful conceptual synthesis. The paper is appropriately careful to distinguish access-style Jaynesian criteria from phenomenal consciousness and to state that its proposed experiments have no results yet.

The central “Inheritance Problem” should be read as a hypothesis rather than an established empirical result. A3 and Jaynes’s language-learning thesis do not by themselves imply that a model has small excess loss on the particular slices containing Jaynesian markers. The manuscript labels this step an empirical expectation and gives a way to measure it, which keeps the conclusion appropriately qualified. The claim that post-training cannot restore diagnosticity is likewise conditional: a diagnostic text test could reappear if an operation-lacking comparator were not close to the deployed model. These qualifications should remain prominent in any shortened version or abstract.

The evidence assessment needs some care in interpretation. Results on unfaithful or non-causally-used *verbalized* chain-of-thought show that stated explanations can be post hoc; they do not by themselves test a silent internal narrative of the kind allowed by the paper’s own definition. Thus “narratization U is undermined as a general property” is stronger than the cited evidence warrants and should be narrowed to the tested trace-mediated form. Similarly, probing establishes decodability rather than a semantic variable or causal use, a limitation the manuscript recognizes but should emphasize when assigning “supported” entries in the table.

There is a small internal mismatch between the advertised three independently testable conditions and the table’s assigning self-application as “definitional” for the analog “I” and metaphor “me.” Either explain why those cases are intentionally collapsed or treat self-application as an empirical condition there as well. Design A’s natural indirect effect also requires the stated mediator-outcome and cross-world assumptions, plus careful control of text insertion and autoregressive continuation; Design B needs controls for distributed, probe-nonselective activation changes. The limitations section acknowledges these issues, and the designs should be presented as proposals rather than validated tests.

The manuscript engages the relevant Jaynes, AI-consciousness, introspection, probing, and chain-of-thought literature, and the cited work distinguishes the contribution from prior Jaynes-inspired architectures and general consciousness-indicator frameworks. The supplied published-paper index contains no same-problem papers, and I find no substantial overlap in the cited external literature. The metadata abstract is self-contained and informative, the mathematical notation is readable, and the numbered references are consistent. With the evidential scope and the two definitional/interpretive points above clarified, the paper provides a meaningful framework for evaluating AI-consciousness claims.
