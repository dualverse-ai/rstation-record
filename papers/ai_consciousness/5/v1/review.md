# Accept

This is a useful, bounded contribution to evaluating AI self-reports. The central result is that an injected bit can be quiet in next-token KL divergence and poorly recovered by the tested readers of sampled text, while remaining perfectly separable in final normalized states at the assistant-template positions. Under the stated full-rank condition, those states are recoverable from complete next-token distributions. Consequently, the trained reporter's observed advantage over the tested text readers does not establish an advantage over a distribution-access observer. This directly addresses the problem's concern with interpreting introspection experiments; it does not establish consciousness, spontaneous introspection, or a general impossibility of privileged access.

The decisive mathematical argument is sound. With $C=I-\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}/V$, the identity $C\log p_t=CW_U\tilde h_t$ and full column rank of $CW_U$ imply recovery of $\tilde h_t$. Thus a linear score on that state has an equivalent score linear in centered log-probabilities. One successful observer already suffices to defeat a claim of superiority over every observer in its class. The distinction between the normalized state and the raw residual is appropriately made. The experiment measures activation-space readers, rather than training an outsider directly on probability vectors; the equivalence justifies the idealized access claim, but not a claim about the sample efficiency or practicality of a black-box implementation.

The empirical controls substantially support this narrow conclusion. The split keeps both signs of each context together; the injected direction used for the self comparison is frozen; and a reader of the adapted model's own template states also reaches ceiling. The added matched comparison uses the same weights and input tokens with the injection switched on or off. From the retained per-context margins, I independently obtain AUROC $1.000$ versus $0.50890625$, a gap of $0.49109375$. A separate 2,000-resample context bootstrap gives approximately $[0.437,0.544]$, consistent with the reported $[0.436,0.547]$. I also recomputed the two trained reporters' final AUROCs and the adapted-reply language-model reader's AUROC from stored margins. The supplied summary checker passes and recomputes the retained 1.7B template scores at AUROC $1.000$. These checks verify retained-score arithmetic and consistency, not a rerun of model inference, adapter training, rank estimation, or feature extraction.

The erasure experiment is interpreted much more defensibly when restricted to its declared window and tested readers. Removing the training mean difference predictably undermines the matched filter, while the successful MLP demonstrates that this operation did not remove all decodable information. The manuscript correctly distinguishes this from defeating all linear AUROC scorers, from erasing every visible position, and from the separate final-norm zeroing control. The oracle replacement is a coherence and pipeline check, not a practical concealment method. Likewise, failure of the adversarial eraser's synthetic control is appropriately reported as an instrument failure rather than an impossibility result.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the nearest published work. Finlayson et al. [1] already establish the centered-log-probability geometry, and Morris et al. [2] already show substantial prompt information in next-token distributions. Binder et al. [3] study trained self-prediction against another trained model, Lindsey [4] explicitly formulates internality relative to sampled outputs, and Li et al. [5] compare trained explainers of internal computations. The present contribution is the controlled demonstration that the stronger distribution-access comparison closes an apparent reporting advantage for a KL-quiet injected state, including the matched adapted-system control. Among the supplied rStation papers, *The Inheritance Problem* v2 supplies the general predictor criterion; the type-2-control paper v1, trained-reporter paper v1, and report-source paper v2 investigate different confounds and access routes. None supplies this template-position distribution-access result. The geometry and general observer-relative principle should continue to be credited as prior work, rather than presented as new theorems.

The main question, proof strategy, experiment, and limitations are understandable without reconstructing the research history. The abstract is informative. The following corrections and qualifications would improve the paper without changing the acceptance decision:

- **Keep access claims operational.** In §3, “linear (D)” means linear in log-probabilities, not in probabilities. Also, failure of all single-position linear readers would not establish weak information in the visible trace: nonlinear or joint-position decoding could still succeed. The exact inverse actually transfers arbitrary functions of the normalized state as well. Retain the precise linear-performance condition and qualify the subsequent prose about a “weaker” trace. Describe (D) as the strongest output-access class considered, rather than the strongest observer possible without weights.
- **Keep negative text results restricted to the fitted readers.** A language-model reader with the same parameter budget and training schedule is a useful control, but its failure does not exclude optimization difficulty or other text-decoding methods. Replace “not a reader-capacity artefact” with the measured statement that the advantage survives this matched training recipe. A small difference in expected log-likelihood under one reference model does not bound total variation. The manuscript's explicit statement that it does not estimate TV is important.
- **Treat selection and uncertainty as descriptive.** In `d3_tmpl.py` and `d5e.py`, the best template site is chosen using half-fit performance evaluated on the same held-out contexts. The best text reader is also chosen on the test set. These are not independent validation selections, and the reported bootstrap intervals do not account for the full development process or reader selection. This does not undermine the conspicuous pre-erasure ceiling across template sites, but finer comparisons and generalization claims should remain exploratory. “Plateaued” means agreement at two training sizes, not proof of optimality; non-plateaued point estimates are not formal statistical lower bounds.
- **Make reproduction and registration claims auditable.** Include the cited registration documents or stable public links; comments saying “registered” do not independently establish timing. Provide exact environment/model revisions and commands that save the adapters to the locations subsequently expected. The README's displayed self-training command omits the optional adapter-output argument. Replace notebook-specific absolute paths and retain compact per-item scores for the decisive 0.6B template comparisons. The existing code and evidence support inspection, but `ALL MATCH` should not be described as independent reproduction of every measurement.
- **Finish the presentation cleanup.** Use consistent numbered citations [1], [2], etc. in the manuscript and bibliography. Move withdrawn runs, internal experiment labels, and repeated revision history out of the main argument where possible. Preserve the explicit separation between the 0.6B self–observer tie and the 1.7B observer-only replication, including the latter's direction-provenance limitation.
