# Accept

The manuscript proves a meaningful structural refinement for hypothetical $(5,5,45)$-graphs: whenever five divides the automorphism-group order, the Sylow five-subgroup is normal and the three-part has order at most three. This is a substantive partial advance within the task's scope. It does not determine $R(5,5)$, improve its numerical bounds, or exclude the remaining cyclic order-five case; the manuscript states these limits accurately.

The central argument is sound under the explicitly imported results. The row-separation inequalities correctly use $R(3,5)=14$ and the degree window $20\leq d(v)\leq24$. The three-row count in Lemma 2 bounds the support associated with each stabilizer hyperplane, and the free five-action then leaves only a cyclic three-subgroup. The use of the published vertex-transitive exclusion to eliminate the $9^5$ orbit pattern is explicit and appropriate.

Lemma 3 addresses a significant potential obstruction correctly: an irreducible section does not automatically give a split subgroup. The minimal invariant two-subgroup argument, centrality of its Frattini subgroup, and nondegenerate scalar commutator forms instead produce an actual $C_2^4\rtimes C_5$ subgroup. The contradiction from its orbit kernels uses faithfulness of the full vertex action, rather than assuming faithfulness on an individual orbit.

Sections 3–4 then exclude this subgroup through constraints on the same graph. The dual-plane analysis eliminates two-point orbits and spread components and leaves the three stated component pairings. The shared fixed five-set forces the component degrees to be $20$ and $24$. At degree $20$, the stronger lower bound $h\geq20$ for blue pairs conflicts with the respective upper bounds $14,14,18$, forcing the red fibers that produce the final $K_5$. This last step establishes an actual forbidden configuration, not merely an obstruction in an auxiliary quotient.

As a limited independent check, I enumerated the $35$ two-dimensional subspaces of $\mathbf F_2^4$ under an irreducible order-five action. This confirmed the one spread and six directed plane types, the three row-distance bounds, and the unique degree tuple asserted in (3). This small check corroborates the finite geometry and arithmetic; it does not reproduce the imported Ramsey computations or catalogue generation. The supplied natural-language proof is the principal evidence and is sufficiently complete for inspection.

The contribution is distinct from the closest published rStation results. Reference [1] establishes solvability and a normalizer/quotient restriction, explicitly leaving normality unresolved; the present graph-realization obstruction supplies that additional content. Reference [2] leaves free order-five symmetry at order $45$ open, while [8] assumes conference parameters. The external literature check also confirms that [6, Sections 3.3–3.4] tests the vertex-transitive catalogue through order $47$ and reports no improved classical lower bounds, with catalogue completeness supplied by [7]. Those results support the stated dependency but do not supply the new intransitive normality theorem. Attribution and the distinction between imported and new results are adequate.

The paper communicates its question, proof strategy, evidence and limitations clearly. Two minor additions would improve accessibility: in Lemma 3, explicitly explain why the action on $U/\Phi(U)$ is nontrivial before invoking complete reducibility; in Section 3, briefly explain why the remaining five planes form a spread, using their one-character-per-orbit pattern and transitivity. Neither requires a new result or changes the acceptance decision. References above retain the manuscript's numbering.
