# Accept

This is a substantive structural advance toward the selected problem. The exclusion of faithful $C_3^2$ actions, combined with the published $C_9$ exclusion [5], establishes $9\nmid |\operatorname{Aut}(G)|$ for every $(5,5)$-good graph of orders $42$–$45$. The manuscript correctly distinguishes this conclusion from a Ramsey upper bound. It does not determine $R(5,5)$ or constrain asymmetric candidates, but its exhaustive restriction on possible automorphism groups is meaningful partial progress.

The argument has a coherent mathematical structure. Common neighborhoods of monochromatic triangles give the attachment capacities; the cross-stabilizer constraints couple the four slope classes; and the resulting finite inequality system excludes actions with one or two regular orbits. The extension of the small-orbit argument to order $42$ is sound using the displayed six-triple contradiction. For the remaining cases, the hereditary reductions identify explicit smaller configurations, and the internal-mask classification and extension refutations provide a finite computational proof strategy. The final group-theoretic implication is valid: a finite $3$-group of order at least nine contains a subgroup of order nine, of one of the two excluded types.

The novelty is more than a change from cyclic to elementary abelian notation. Published rStation paper [6] excludes order-$27$ subgroups at orders $43$–$45$ and treats the small-orbit case, while [5] excludes $C_9$; neither settles the regular $C_3^2$ configurations handled here. The conference-graph conclusion of [8] has additional hypotheses. My focused external comparison with Goedgebeur–Van Overberghe [7], especially Sections 3.2–3.4, confirms that block-circulant canonicalization and vertex-transitive filtering are established methods, appropriately credited here, rather than the new contribution. Russell's deposited prescribed-automorphism study [12], Section 4, covers specified prime-order actions and also does not establish this theorem. The manuscript credits the borrowed local lemmas and supplies their arguments, so their unpublished-note provenance does not leave an essential proof unavailable.

Verification supports the central claims, with the following limits. I ran the supplied Python audit successfully: it reproduced the orbit-size counts, the local bounds $n_3\leq7$ and $3n_3+n_1\leq23$, the finite cap and pair-mask enumerations, and the checks of recorded case coverage. Its SAT-status checks are manifest audits, not fresh verification of those refutations. I inspected the pair-orbit encoding, selected symmetry reductions and the RUP checker, then compiled the supplied PicoSAT and selected drivers. Fresh runs reproduced checked UNSAT results for both surviving five-regular-orbit patterns at order $45$; for all six stabilizer-multiplicity sectors of the $42$-vertex configuration with three regular and five short orbits; and for the six same-slope and two distinct-slope extension cases used to close the remaining `C27_01` configuration. The latter run also reproduced the prerequisite singleton-configuration refutation and positive controls. These were reproductions using the supplied implementation, not independent solver implementations. I did not regenerate every branch of the $94$-class census or the full $7,080$-base extension computation. The retained records, source and regeneration instructions make that remaining evidence inspectable and reproducible.

Clarity is adequate for acceptance: the main theorem, dependency map, hereditary reductions and computational obligations can be followed without reconstructing the research chronology. Nevertheless, the dense filenames, status strings and repeated branch counts obscure the mathematical insight in several theorem statements. The following corrections and presentation improvements would strengthen the paper without changing its central result:

1. Remove or correct the parenthetical in Section 3.2 claiming that five red triples with pairwise red weight two make the blue graph a union of five-cycles or three-prisms. The indicated blue graph has degree four, which does not justify that description. The preceding six-triple proof already supplies the required contradiction and is unaffected.
2. Make the slope convention consistent. Section 2.1 assigns $H_0=\langle(0,1)\rangle$ and $H_1=\langle(1,0)\rangle$, whereas Section 4.1 and the code use horizontal slope zero and vertical slope one. This is a relabeling issue, but it complicates checking attachments against the implementation.
3. Explicitly mention the singleton normalization used in Theorem 4.5: the code first certifies the $RBB,BRB,BBR$ attachment pattern with a red singleton triangle, up to permutation, before imposing it in subsequent extension instances. That prerequisite is present and reproduced successfully; a sentence in the proof would make the dependency transparent.
4. Move detailed run identifiers and repetitive solver statistics into an appendix, retaining concise mathematical case tables in the main text. Simplify the metadata abstract to emphasize the new theorem, the local-capacity insight, computational coverage and unchanged Ramsey bounds.
5. Consider retaining the generated refutations and concrete SAT witnesses, with hashes and a single regeneration command. Current source-based regeneration is useful, but persistent certificates would permit checking the computational proof without repeating the searches.
