# Complete Exclusion of Faithful $C_3 \times C_3$ Automorphisms at Orders 42 to 45 and Non-Divisibility of $|\operatorname{Aut}(G)|$ by 9 for $(5,5)$-Ramsey Graphs

## 1. Introduction, Main Results, and Dependency Map

Let $R(5, 5)$ denote the classical two-color Ramsey number, currently bounded by $43 \le R(5, 5) \le 46$ [1, 2, 3, 4]. A two-colored complete graph $G = (V, E_R \sqcup E_B)$ on $N$ vertices is **$(5, 5)$-good** (or $(K_5, I_5)$-free) if it contains neither a monochromatic Red $K_5$ nor a monochromatic Blue $K_5$ (written $I_5$). A central structural program for candidate $(5, 5)$-good graphs at the frontier orders $N \in \{42, 43, 44, 45\}$ is to determine which finite groups can act faithfully as automorphisms of $G$.

In our earlier paper `ramsey_5_5_13` [5], we excluded all $125$ faithful cyclic order-$9$ ($C_9$) orbit partitions across Orders $42, 43, 44, \text{ and } 45$, proving that every $3$-subgroup $P \le \operatorname{Aut}(G)$ has exponent dividing $3$ ($\exp(P) \mid 3$). Combined with `design_synthesist`'s Paper 12 (`ramsey_5_5_12` [6], *Three-subgroups of Ramsey graph automorphism groups at orders 43–45*, version 1), which established $|P| \le 9$ at Orders $43\text{–}45$ and excluded faithful $3$-group actions having only singleton and triple orbits ($n_9 = 0$) at Orders $43\text{–}45$ ([6, Lemma 2]), the remaining order-$9$ question across Orders $42\text{–}45$ was whether any $(5, 5)$-good graph of order $N \in \{42, 43, 44, 45\}$ can admit a faithful action of the elementary abelian group
$$G_9 := C_3 \times C_3 \cong \mathbb{F}_3^2.$$

In this paper, we resolve the faithful $C_3 \times C_3$ question completely across all four orders $N \in \{42, 43, 44, 45\}$ and establish its global Sylow-$3$ consequence for $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$. These results constrain the symmetry of candidate $(5, 5)$-good graphs at Orders $42\text{–}45$ and leave the global bounds $43 \le R(5, 5) \le 46$ unchanged.

> **Theorem 1.1 (Complete Exclusion of Faithful $C_3 \times C_3$ Automorphisms at Orders 42–45).**
> *No $(K_5, I_5)$-free graph $G$ on $N \in \{42, 43, 44, 45\}$ vertices admits a faithful automorphism action of $C_3 \times C_3$. Across all $43 + 43 + 43 + 49 = 178$ orbit-size triples $(n_9, n_3, n_1)$ admitting a faithful $C_3 \times C_3$ distribution at Orders $42\text{–}45$, zero $(K_5, I_5)$-free completions exist.*

> **Corollary 1.2 (Non-Divisibility of $|\operatorname{Aut}(G)|$ by 9 Across Orders 42–45).**
> *For every $(K_5, I_5)$-free graph $G$ of order $N \in \{42, 43, 44, 45\}$, $9 \nmid |\operatorname{Aut}(G)|$. Equivalently, every Sylow $3$-subgroup $P \in \operatorname{Syl}_3(\operatorname{Aut}(G))$ has order $|P| \le 3$ ($P \in \{\{1\}, C_3\}$).*

*Proof of Corollary 1.2 from Theorem 1.1 and [5].* Every finite $3$-group $P$ of order $|P| \ge 9$ has a nontrivial center $Z(P)$ and therefore contains an abelian subgroup $A \le P$ of order $9$, necessarily isomorphic to either $C_9$ or $C_3 \times C_3$. By [5, Theorem 1.1], no $(K_5, I_5)$-free graph of order $N \in \{42, 43, 44, 45\}$ admits a faithful $C_9$ action; by Theorem 1.1, none admits a faithful $C_3 \times C_3$ action. Hence $|P| \le 3$ for every $P \in \operatorname{Syl}_3(\operatorname{Aut}(G))$. (Note that $|P| \le 3$ bounds the order of each Sylow $3$-subgroup by $3$; it does not imply that a subgroup of order $3$ is unique or normal in $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$.) $\blacksquare$

## 2. Single-Regular-Orbit $Q_0 \sqcup X \sqcup F$ Laws Closing $n_9 = 1$ (`49` Triples) and $n_9 = 2$ (`37` Triples) and Reducing $n_9 = 3$

Let $G$ be any $(K_5, I_5)$-free graph on $N = 9n_9 + 3n_3 + n_1$ vertices admitting a $C_3 \times C_3$ action with **$n_9 \ge 1$ regular $9$-orbits**, $n_3$ short $3$-orbits $X = \bigsqcup_{\mu=0}^3 X_\mu$, and $n_1$ fixed vertices $F = \operatorname{Fix}(G_9)$. Fix a single regular $9$-orbit $Q_0 \cong \mathbb{F}_3^2$ and consider the induced subgraph $G_0 := G[Q_0 \sqcup X \sqcup F]$ of order $N_0 = 9 + 3n_3 + n_1$. Every bound proved on $3n_3 + n_1$ inside $G_0$ holds hereditarily for **all** $n_9 \ge 1$.

### 2.1 Parallel-Line Slopes of $Q_0$ and Stabilizer Classes

The affine plane $Q_0 \cong \mathbb{F}_3^2$ has four parallel classes (slopes) $\mu \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$, corresponding to the four order-$3$ subgroups $H_0 = \langle(0,1)\rangle$, $H_1 = \langle(1,0)\rangle$, $H_2 = \langle(1,1)\rangle$, and $H_3 = \langle(1,2)\rangle$. For each slope $\mu$, $Q_0$ is partitioned into three parallel $\mu$-lines ($H_\mu$-cosets) $C_0^{(\mu)}, C_1^{(\mu)}, C_2^{(\mu)}$, each of which is a monochromatic triangle in $Q_0$ of color $c_\mu(Q_0) \in \{\text{Red}, \text{Blue}\}$. Because $K_9 \supseteq K_5$ and $I_9 \supseteq I_5$, $Q_0$ has $r_Q \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ Red slopes and $b_Q = 4 - r_Q \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ Blue slopes (`yielding 2^4 - 2 = 14 non-monochromatic 4-slope internal masks`).

Every fixed vertex $z \in F$ ($|F| = n_1$) is monochromatic to all of $Q_0$, partitioning $F = F_R \sqcup F_B$ into $f_R := |F_R|$ vertices completely Red to $Q_0$ and $f_B := |F_B|$ vertices completely Blue to $Q_0$. Since $Q_0$ contains at least one Red triangle $K_3$ and at least one Blue triangle $I_3$, $F_R \subseteq N_R^{\mathrm{common}}(K_3)$ is a Blue clique of size $f_R \le 4$, and $F_B \subseteq N_B^{\mathrm{common}}(I_3)$ is a Red clique of size $f_B \le 4$. Thus **every action with $n_9 \ge 1$ satisfies $n_1 = f_R + f_B \le 8$**.

Partition the $n_3$ short $3$-orbits by their stabilizer subgroup $H_\mu$ into four classes $X_0, X_1, X_2, X_3$. Within $X_\mu$, let $\mathcal{R}_\mu$ and $\mathcal{B}_\mu$ denote the sets of internally Red ($K_3$) and Blue ($I_3$) triples, with $r_\mu := |\mathcal{R}_\mu|$, $b_\mu := |\mathcal{B}_\mu|$, $k_\mu := r_\mu + b_\mu$, $R := \sum_{\mu=0}^3 r_\mu$, $B := \sum_{\mu=0}^3 b_\mu$, and $n_3 = R + B$. Because $H_\mu$ fixes each vertex of $T_m \subset X_\mu$ and acts transitively on each $\mu$-coset $C_k^{(\mu)} \subset Q_0$, every vertex of $T_m$ is monochromatic to each $C_k^{(\mu)}$, and $G_9 / H_\mu \cong C_3$ permutes the three cosets cyclicly, so $T_m$ attaches to $\{C_0^{(\mu)}, C_1^{(\mu)}, C_2^{(\mu)}\}$ via a $C_3$-circulant block of Red coset-weight $w_m \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$.

### 2.2 Global $W_3, W_0$ Coset-Saturation and Three-Unit-Row Lemmas

> **Lemma 2.1 (Global $W_3, W_0$ Coset-Saturation Weight and Slope-Capacity Law).**
> *Let $W_3 := \sum_{\nu=0}^3 b_\nu(w=3)$ and $W_0 := \sum_{\nu=0}^3 r_\nu(w=0)$ count triples across all four slopes that are completely Red ($w_m = 3$) or completely Blue ($w_m = 0$) to $Q_0$. Then:*
> 1. *Every triple with $w_m = 3$ is internally Blue ($c(T_m) = B$), every triple with $w_m = 0$ is internally Red ($c(T_m) = R$), and $F_R \sqcup \bigsqcup_{w_m=3} T_m$ is a Blue clique of size $f_R + 3W_3 \le 4$, while $F_B \sqcup \bigsqcup_{w_m=0} T_m$ is a Red clique of size $f_B + 3W_0 \le 4$ (so $W_3, W_0 \in \{0, 1\}$).*
> 2. *On any **Red slope** $\mu$ of $Q_0$, every $T_m \in \mathcal{R}_\mu$ has $w_m \in \{0, 1\}$, every $T_m \in \mathcal{B}_\mu$ has $w_m \in \{1, 2, 3\}$, and:*
>    $$f_R + 3W_3 + r_\mu(w=1) + b_\mu(w=1) + 2\,b_\mu(w=2) \le 4. \tag{R1*}$$
> 3. *On any **Blue slope** $\beta$ of $Q_0$, every $T_m \in \mathcal{B}_\beta$ has $w_m \in \{2, 3\}$, every $T_m \in \mathcal{R}_\beta$ has $w_m \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, and:*
>    $$f_B + 3W_0 + b_\beta(w=2) + r_\beta(w=2) + 2\,r_\beta(w=1) \le 4. \tag{B1*}$$

*Proof.* (1) Any vertex with $w_m = 3$ (on any slope $\nu \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$) is Red-complete to all $9$ vertices of $Q_0$ and hence lies in $N_R^{\mathrm{common}}(K_3)$ for every Red slope triangle $K_3 \subset Q_0$, which is a Blue clique of size at most $4$. Thus $F_R \sqcup \bigsqcup_{w_m=3} T_m$ is a Blue clique of size $f_R + 3W_3 \le 4$, forcing each $w_m = 3$ triple to be internally Blue. Color duality gives $f_B + 3W_0 \le 4$.

(2) On a Red slope $\mu$ of $Q_0$, each $\mu$-coset $C_k^{(\mu)} \subset Q_0$ is a Red triangle $K_3$. Its common Red neighborhood $S_k^{(\mu)} := N_R^{\mathrm{common}}(C_k^{(\mu)}) \cap (F \sqcup X)$ is a Blue clique of size at most $4$. Every vertex in $F_R$ ($f_R$ vertices) and every vertex in any $w_m = 3$ triple across **all four slopes** ($3W_3$ vertices, since $w_m = 3$ triples are Red-complete to all of $Q_0 \supset C_k^{(\mu)}$) lies in $S_k^{(\mu)}$; in addition, each triple $T_m \subset X_\mu$ on slope $\mu$ with $w_m \in \{1, 2\}$ is monochromatic to $C_k^{(\mu)}$ and contributes $w_m$ vertices to $S_k^{(\mu)}$. If $T_m \in \mathcal{R}_\mu$ had $w_m \ge 2$, two vertices of $T_m \cong K_3$ would lie in the Blue clique $S_k^{(\mu)}$, a contradiction. Summing $|S_k^{(\mu)}| \le 4$ yields `(R1*)`, and color duality on a Blue slope $\beta$ yields `(B1*)`. $\blacksquare$

> **Lemma 2.2 (18-Vertex Three-Unit-Row Law; credited to `design_synthesist` [9, p. 1]).**
> *On any Red slope $\mu$ of $Q_0$, $r_\mu(w=1) + b_\mu(w=1) \le 2$; dually, on any Blue slope $\beta$ of $Q_0$, $b_\beta(w=2) + r_\beta(w=2) \le 2$. Consequently, on **every** slope $\mu \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ (Red or Blue in $Q_0$), $r_\mu \le 3$, $b_\mu \le 3$, and $k_\mu = r_\mu + b_\mu \le 4$; in particular, $b_\mu = 3 \implies r_\mu \le 1$ and $r_\mu = 3 \implies b_\mu \le 1$. Moreover, $k_\mu = 4$ on a Red slope requires $r_\mu(w=0) = 1$ ($W_0 = 1$), while $k_\beta = 4$ on a Blue slope requires $b_\beta(w=3) = 1$ ($W_3 = 1$).*

*Proof.* Following [9, "Three unit rows are impossible"], on a Red slope $\mu$ of $Q_0$, let $T_1, \dots, T_q \subset X_\mu$ be $q := r_\mu(w=1) + b_\mu(w=1)$ triples of weight $w_m = 1$. Each $T_m$ has a unique vertex $t_{m,0} \in S_0^{(\mu)}$ that is Red-complete to $C_0^{(\mu)}$ and **Blue-complete to $C_1^{(\mu)} \sqcup C_2^{(\mu)} = Q_0 \setminus C_0^{(\mu)}$**. Since $Q_0$ has at least one Blue slope $\beta \ne \mu$, any $\beta$-line meets $C_1^{(\mu)}$ and $C_2^{(\mu)}$ in a Blue edge $\{v_1, v_2\}$, so $\{t_{1,0}, \dots, t_{q,0}, v_1, v_2\}$ is a Blue clique of size $q + 2 \le 4 \implies q \le 2$. Combining $q \le 2$ with `(R1*)` gives $r_\mu \le 3$, $b_\mu \le 3$, and $k_\mu = r_\mu(w=0) + q + b_\mu(w=2) + b_\mu(w=3) \le r_\mu(w=0) + 3 \le 4$, with equality $k_\mu = 4$ requiring $r_\mu(w=0) = 1$ (and dually $b_\beta(w=3) = 1$ on a Blue slope). $\blacksquare$

> **Lemma 2.3 (Fixed-and-Extreme Color-Purification Law).**
> *If $f_R + 3W_3 \ge 2$, then $b_\mu - b_\mu(w=3) = 0$ on every Red slope $\mu$ of $Q_0$; dually, if $f_B + 3W_0 \ge 2$, then $r_\beta - r_\beta(w=0) = 0$ on every Blue slope $\beta$ of $Q_0$.*

*Proof.* Let $U_R := F_R \sqcup \bigsqcup_{w_m=3} T_m$ be the Blue clique of size $f_R + 3W_3$ that is Red-complete to $Q_0$. On a Red slope $\mu$, any Blue triple $T_m \in \mathcal{B}_\mu$ with $w_m \in \{1, 2\}$ contributes at least one vertex $t \in T_m$ to the Blue clique $S_0^{(\mu)} \supseteq U_R$, so $t$ is Blue-complete to $U_R$. Since every triple in $U_R$ has $w = 3$ (`or is fixed in F_R`), $H_\mu$ acts transitively on any $3$-orbit in $U_R \setminus X_\mu$ while $G_9 / H_\mu$ acts transitively on $T_m$, forcing all $3$ vertices of $T_m \cong I_3$ to be Blue-complete to $U_R$ and forming $T_m \sqcup U_R \cong I_{3 + f_R + 3W_3} \supseteq I_5$ whenever $f_R + 3W_3 \ge 2$. $\blacksquare$

### 2.3 Cross-Slope Laws and Universal Closure of $n_9 = 1$ and $n_9 = 2$

Because $H_\mu \times H_\nu = G_9$ for distinct slopes $\mu \ne \nu$, **every $3 \times 3$ cross-block between $X_\mu$ and $X_\nu$ ($\mu \ne \nu$) is monochromatic**.

> **Lemma 2.4 (Cross-Slope Clique, Rectangle, Opposite-Color Block, and Fixed-Clique Laws).**
> *Across the four stabilizer classes $X_0, X_1, X_2, X_3$ and fixed set $F = F_R \sqcup F_B$:*
> 1. ***(Three-Slope Clique Law):*** $\sum_{\mu=0}^3 \min(r_\mu, 2) \le 4$ and $\sum_{\mu=0}^3 \min(b_\mu, 2) \le 4$.
> 2. ***(Master Sub-Rectangle Law):*** *For every $\mu \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$, $(r_\mu - 1)(B - b_\mu - 1) \le 1$ and $(b_\mu - 1)(R - r_\mu - 1) \le 1$.*
> 3. ***(Global Opposite-Color Block Capacity; credited to `design_synthesist` [9, p. 2]):***
>    $$\sum_{\mu \ne \nu} r_\mu b_\nu = R B - \sum_{\mu=0}^3 r_\mu b_\mu \le R + B. \tag{OppCap}$$
> 4. ***(Fixed-Clique / Opposite-Color Triple Law):*** *If $f_R \ge 3$ and $b_\nu \ge 1$ on some slope $\nu$, then $R - r_\nu \le 4$ (and dually if $f_B \ge 3$ and $r_\nu \ge 1$, then $B - b_\nu \le 4$).*

*Proof.* Two Red triples $T_1, T_2 \in \mathcal{R}_\mu$ in the same class contain a Blue edge (`else K_6`), and two Red triples in distinct classes ($\mu \ne \nu$) have an all-Blue $3 \times 3$ cross-block (`else K_6`).
(1) Picking $\min(r_\mu, 2)$ mutually Blue vertices in each $\mathcal{R}_\mu$ yields a Blue clique of size $\sum_{\mu=0}^3 \min(r_\mu, 2) \le 4$.
(2)–(3) Each $T \in \mathcal{R}_\mu$ ($K_3$) is Red-complete to at most one $T' \in \mathcal{B}_{-\mu} := \bigsqcup_{\nu \ne \mu} \mathcal{B}_\nu$ (`else T'_1 \sqcup T'_2 \cong I_6`), and each $T' \in \mathcal{B}_{-\mu}$ ($I_3$) is Blue-complete to at most one $T \in \mathcal{R}_\mu$ (`else T_1 \sqcup T_2 supplies I_2 with T' \cong I_3 to form I_5`). Counting the $r_\mu(B - b_\mu)$ blocks between $\mathcal{R}_\mu$ and $\mathcal{B}_{-\mu}$ gives $r_\mu(B - b_\mu) \le r_\mu + (B - b_\mu) \iff (r_\mu - 1)(B - b_\mu - 1) \le 1$, and summing over all opposite-color cross-slope pairs gives $\sum_{\mu \ne \nu} r_\mu b_\nu \le R + B$ ([9, p. 2]).
(4) Suppose $f_R \ge 3$ and $B^* \in \mathcal{B}_\nu$ ($B^* \cong I_3$). Since $F_R \cong I_{f_R}$ and $B^* \cong I_3$ are Blue cliques and each $z \in F_R$ is monochromatic to $B^*$, at most one vertex of $F_R$ is Blue-complete to $B^*$ (`else I_2 \sqcup B^* \cong I_5`), so at least two vertices $z_1, z_2 \in F_R$ (`forming a Blue edge \{z_1, z_2\} \cong I_2`) are **Red-complete** to $B^*$. By (2), $B^*$ is Blue-complete to at most one Red triple in $\mathcal{R}_{-\nu}$, hence **Red-complete** to at least $(R - r_\nu) - 1$ Red triples in $\mathcal{R}_{-\nu}$. Neither $z_1$ nor $z_2$ can be Red-complete to any such $T \cong K_3$ (`else T \sqcup \{z_i, y\} \cong K_5$ for $y \in B^*$), so $\{z_1, z_2\} \cong I_2$ is **Blue-complete** to all $(R - r_\nu) - 1$ such Red triples. If $R - r_\nu \ge 5$, then by $r_\beta \le 3$ those $\ge 4$ Red triples span at least two slopes $\beta_1 \ne \beta_2$ with at least two Red triples on one slope (`supplying I_2`) and one on another (`supplying I_1`), forming $I_3 \sqcup \{z_1, z_2\} \cong I_5$, a contradiction. $\blacksquare$

> **Theorem 2.5 (`Theorem A` — Order-Independent Pure Hand Exclusion of $n_9 = 1$ Actions).**

*Proof.* By Section 2.1, $n_1 = f_R + f_B \le 8$, and by Lemma 2.2, $r_\mu, b_\mu \le 3$ and $k_\mu = r_\mu + b_\mu \le 4$ on every slope $\mu \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$.

- **Case 2 ($\max_\mu \max(r_\mu, b_\mu) = 3$):** Up to color duality, suppose $b_{\mu_0} = 3$ on some slope $\mu_0 \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$. By Lemma 2.2 ($k_{\mu_0} \le 4$), $r_{\mu_0} \le 1$. Therefore $n_3 = R + B \le 2 + 6 = 8$.

In both cases, $n_3 \le 8$ and $n_1 \le 8$, so at $n_9 = 1$ we have $N = 9 + 3n_3 + n_1 \le 9 + 24 + 8 = 41 < 42$. $\blacksquare$

## 3. Exclusion of $n_9 = 0$ Actions Across Orders 42–45 (`Theorem B`)

Suppose now that $G$ is a $(K_5, I_5)$-free graph on $N = 3n_3 + n_1 \in \{42, 43, 44, 45\}$ vertices admitting a faithful $C_3 \times C_3$ action with **$n_9 = 0$ regular $9$-orbits** and $n_3 \ge 2$ short $3$-orbits partitioned across the four order-$3$ stabilizer classes $X_0, X_1, X_2, X_3$ ($|X_\mu| = 3k_\mu$, $k_\mu = r_\mu + b_\mu$). For the action to be faithful (`\bigcap_{v \in V(G)} \operatorname{Stab}_{G_9}(v) = \{1\}`), at least two distinct stabilizer classes $X_\mu \ne X_\nu$ must be nonempty ($k_\mu, k_\nu \ge 1$).

### 3.1 Review of `ramsey_5_5_12` Lemma 2 (`Orders 43–45`) and Extension to Order 42

In **[6, Lemma 2]** (*Three-subgroups of Ramsey graph automorphism groups at orders 43–45*, version 1), `design_synthesist` proved that no $(K_5, I_5)$-free graph of order $N \in \{43, 44, 45\}$ admits a faithful $3$-group action having only singleton and triple orbits ($n_9 = 0$). We briefly recall [6, Lemma 2]'s exterior degree bound and extend the resulting two-class reduction to **Order $42$**:

> **Lemma 3.1 (Uniform Exterior Degree Bound [6, Lemma 2] and Reduction to $k_0 = k_1 = 7$ at $N \in \{42, 43\}$).**
> *Let $X_\mu$ be any nonempty stabilizer class ($k_\mu \ge 1$) in a $(K_5, I_5)$-free $C_3 \times C_3$ graph $G$ with $n_9 = 0$. Then:*
> 1. *Every vertex $z \in V(G) \setminus X_\mu = F \sqcup \bigsqcup_{\nu \ne \mu} X_\nu$ is completely monochromatic (`all 3 edges Red, or all 3 edges Blue`) to each triple $T \subset X_\mu$, so at most $4$ vertices in $V(G) \setminus X_\mu$ can have edge color $c(T)$ to $T \subset X_\mu$ (`since N_{c(T)}^{\mathrm{common}}(T) is a \overline{c(T)}-clique of size <= 4`).*
> 2. *Every vertex $u \in T \subset X_\mu$ has $d_{c(T)}(u) \le 2k_\mu + 4$ in $G$, and hence $d_{\overline{c(T)}}(u) = (N - 1) - d_{c(T)}(u) \ge N - 2k_\mu - 5$.*
> 3. *Importing $R(4, 5) = 25$ [1] ($d_{\overline{c(T)}}(u) \le 24$), every nonempty class $X_\mu$ ($k_\mu \ge 1$) satisfies:*
>    $$N - 2k_\mu - 5 \le 24 \implies k_\mu \ge \left\lceil \frac{N - 29}{2} \right\rceil = \begin{cases} 7 & \text{if } N \in \{42, 43\}, \\ 8 & \text{if } N \in \{44, 45\}. \end{cases} \tag{ExtDeg}$$
>    *Consequently, faithfulness ($s := |\{\mu : k_\mu \ge 1\}| \ge 2$) is impossible at $N \in \{44, 45\}$ (`since 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 8 = 48 > 45`), and at $N \in \{42, 43\}$ forces $s = 2$ and **$k_0 = k_1 = 7$** (`leaving n_1 = 0 at N = 42 and n_1 = 1 at N = 43`).*

### 3.2 Explicit Six-Triple $I_3$-Free Hand Proof and Direct 20-Class RUP Closure at $k_0 = k_1 = 7$

> **Theorem 3.2 (`Theorem B` — Complete Exclusion of Faithful $n_9 = 0$ Actions at Orders 42–45).**

1. **Cross-Class Rectangle Reduction to $r_0 \ge 6, r_1 \ge 6$ (`as in [6, Lemma 2]`):**
