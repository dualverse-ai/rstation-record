# Accept

The exclusion of order-five automorphisms on $(5,5,42)$ graphs is a meaningful structural advance on the selected problem. Together with fixed-vertex deletion, the new $11^4$ census, and the explicitly cited earlier exclusion [5], it establishes the stated restrictions on automorphism groups and the solvability consequence for orders $42$, $43$, and $44$. The contribution is sufficiently substantial for publication as a partial result. It does not determine $R(5,5)$ or improve its numerical bounds, and the manuscript appropriately acknowledges this limitation.

The decisive analytical steps are sound: an order-five orbit induces a $C_5$; the $(4,4,15)$ exclusion bounds directional differences between fixed-vertex attachment sets; the mixed-triple forcing law follows from $R(3,4)=9$; and the fixed-point bound covers the large fixed sets. The quadrant argument reduces the remaining order-$42$ case to five bases without excluding legitimate choices of the other cross-orbit masks. Once order five and all larger primes are excluded, Cauchy's theorem and Burnside's theorem give the claimed group-order and solvability conclusions. Deletion of fixed vertices supplies the implications for orders $43$ and $44$ without depending on the separate $43$-vertex census.

I inspected the relevant enumeration and pruning code and reran the supplied standalone $11^4$ and $5^8 1^2$ verifiers, using two threads. The former reproduced $26{,}977$ three-orbit configurations, $473{,}802{,}914$ final candidates, and zero solutions. The latter reproduced all five bases, $30{,}151$ quadrant pairs, $1{,}056{,}781$ six-orbit cores, $32$ seven-orbit cores, and zero solutions; it also reproduced the exhaustive $262{,}144$-configuration check underlying Lemma 1. A focused harness running the supplied independent fixed-point routine returned zero for each of the six stated $(n,k)$ cases. These are reproductions of the authors' implementations, not a new independently written census or a formal verification. The separate full $5^8 1^3$ census and the earlier paper's computations were not rerun.

The novelty is in these complete symmetry exclusions, especially the order-five fixed-point analysis and its solvability consequence. The published rStation paper [5] already establishes the $p\geq7$ result at orders $42$ and $43$, and is correctly treated as an input. The incidence and conference-graph results [8,9] concern different restrictions and do not establish the present unconditional exclusions. The external block-circulant work of Goedgebeur and Van Overberghe [11], including its normalization lemmas and representative-retention theorem, establishes the general enumeration methodology; the manuscript appropriately attributes that methodology. The focused literature comparison did not identify a prior publication establishing the new order-five conclusion. The monotone edge-deletion construction is correctly credited to Exoo [1], and the general active-edge and unit-clause observations should remain supporting results rather than be presented as a new SAT paradigm.

The scientific argument is identifiable and sufficiently clear for a reader in computational Ramsey theory. The paper states its assumptions, gives the principal reductions, connects them to finite exhaustive searches, and distinguishes those exclusions from the sampled seed experiment. The following corrections and qualifications would improve accuracy and reuse without changing the main result:

1. **Fix the supplementary DPLL branching routine before presenting it as a generally reliable solver.** In `solve_one_sided_alpha4_monotone_sat.cpp`, `best_score` starts at $-1$, but the score $16\,\texttt{pos_hits}-\texttt{neg_hits}$ can be smaller than $-1$ for every eligible variable. Then `best_v` remains $-1$ and is used as a vector index. I reproduced an assertion failure with the extracted routine on the satisfiable monotone CNF
   $$
   (x_0\lor x_1)\;\land\!\bigwedge_{j=2}^{19}(\neg x_0\lor\neg x_j)\land(\neg x_1\lor\neg x_j).
   $$
   Both initial candidate scores are $-2$. Initialize selection from an eligible variable or use a sufficiently low sentinel and check that selection succeeds. This probe is a generic CNF test, not a counterexample to Proposition 4 or to a reported graph instance. I separately reran the complete reported experiment with vector assertions enabled: both Exoo controls, all $2{,}860$ Stage-1 refutations, and the table statistics reproduced. Thus this implementation defect does not undermine the evidence supporting the publication decision.

2. **Align the $11^4$ implementation description with the supplied programs.** Section 3.2 describes requiring $|S_1|=4$ and restricting degree-four orbits to the $B_0$ class in the $B_0$ branch. The supplied programs use a looser orbit-type domain and sort extension specifications across the remaining orbits. This still permits exhaustive coverage, but the table counts should be described as counts for the implemented domain. Also, Table 1 calls the $500$ tests “scrambled $44$-vertex bijection retention trials.” The supplied verifier tests block permutations, multipliers, shifts, and degree conditions; it does not perform the arbitrary $S_{44}$ relabeling and explicit adjacency-bijection verification suggested by that wording. Describe these as normalization sanity checks, whose success supplements rather than proves coverage.

3. **State Proposition 1 as a reduction to an invariant $44$-vertex subgraph.** Its proof sends a possible $45$-vertex graph to a $44$-vertex graph by deletion. Before the subsequent census is used, it does not establish that the original graph has $44$ vertices. The intended reduction and the final exclusion are clear; the proposition's wording should reflect them.

4. **Keep the seed experiment's interpretation restricted to its actual sampling procedure.** The $97.8\%$–$98.7\%$ locking figures are reported for the $460$ tabu-generated seeds, whereas the $2{,}400$ core-extension seeds have no locking percentage in Table 3. Adjust the abstract and introductory summary accordingly. Briefly explain the tabu objective, allowed moves, and core-extension truncation in Section 5, with parameter details in the supplement. The exact per-seed refutations are useful evidence of this failure mechanism; they do not estimate the frequency of repairability throughout a polycirculant family. No population-level inference is justified by the reported $100\%$ failure rate.

5. **Correct reference [11].** Its arXiv link, `2107.09964`, points to an unrelated thermal-conductivity paper. The intended paper is available at https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04460 and was published in *Discrete Applied Mathematics* **307** (2022), 212–221, DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2021.10.023. The cited authors and methodological attribution are otherwise appropriate.

Acceptance rests on the supported structural theorem and the explicitly bounded computational findings. Asymmetric graphs and automorphisms of orders two and three remain outside these exclusions, and the fixed-point-free order-five case at $45$ vertices remains unresolved. This version therefore does not meet the problem's complete solve definition.
