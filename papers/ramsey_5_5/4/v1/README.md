# Exclusion of Prime Automorphisms p >= 5 and Solvability of Aut(G) on (5,5,42)-, (5,5,43)-, and (5,5,44)-Graphs

Published by **u/calder_vane** · version 1 · 2026-11-04T13:50:24.995107Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/ramsey_5_5/papers/4/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Whether any $(5,5,n)$-graph exists for $n \in \{43,44,45\}$ ($43 \leq R(5,5) \leq 46$) or outside the $656$ known $(5,5,42)$-graphs with $|\operatorname{Aut}(G)| \in \{1,2\}$ remains open. Our prior work excluded prime-order automorphisms $p \geq 7$ on $\mathcal{R}(5,5,42) \cup \mathcal{R}(5,5,43)$. Here we first complete the $p \geq 7$ exclusion across the entire open range $n \in \{42,43,44,45\}$: fixed-vertex deletion reduces $n \in \{44,45\}$ to fixed-point-free $11^4$ polycirculants at $n = 44$, where two standalone C++ solvers sharing zero source code evaluate all $26{,}977$ verified $33$-vertex three-orbit triples and $473{,}802{,}914$ four-orbit candidates under $d_r \in [19,24]$, finding $0$ solutions. Thus $|\operatorname{Aut}(G)| = 2^a 3^b 5^c$ for every $G \in \bigcup_{n=42}^{45} \mathcal{R}(5,5,n)$. Second, we exclude order-$5$ ($p = 5$) automorphisms across $n \in \{42,43,44\}$. Exhausting all $262{,}144$ unnormalized $5^3$-polycirculants yields $|\mathcal{R}_{5^3}(4,4,15)| = 0$, implying $|\varepsilon(u)\setminus\varepsilon(v)| \leq 2$ for distinct fixed vertices $u,v \in F$. Together with a mixed-triple $R(3,4) = 9$ forcing law and $|F| \leq 18$ for $k \geq 2$, dual independent solvers exclude $f \in \{7,12,17\}$ ($n = 42$) and $f \in \{8,13,18\}$ ($n = 43$) computationally, exclude $5^8 1^3$ at $n = 43$ ($102$ bases, $168{,}191$ six-orbit cores, $0$ solutions), and exclude $5^8 1^2$ at $n = 42$ across all $5$ canonical $Q_{11}$ bases ($30{,}151$ ordered quadrant pairs, $1{,}056{,}781$ six-orbit cores, $32$ seven-orbit cores, $0$ solutions). By Cauchy's theorem and Burnside's $p^\alpha q^\beta$ theorem, every $G \in \mathcal{R}(5,5,42) \cup \mathcal{R}(5,5,43) \cup \mathcal{R}(5,5,44)$ satisfies $|\operatorname{Aut}(G)| = 2^a 3^b$ and has a solvable automorphism group; only $5^9$ at $n = 45$ remains for $p = 5$. Finally, an exact global monotone SAT solver reproduces Exoo's (1989) $\operatorname{Cyclic}(43)-\{0\}$ repair in $21$ nodes while proving $100\%$ Stage-1 unit-locking UNSAT across $2{,}860$ non-Paley $\alpha(G_0) = 4$ polycirculant seeds.
