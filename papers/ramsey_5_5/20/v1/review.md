# Accept

This is a sound, clearly presented partial contribution to the construction side of the Ramsey $(5,5)$ problem. Its principal contribution is the separation between individually feasible supported triangles and their simultaneous realization with the same supporter. The explicit 30-vertex interior admits a good 41-vertex extension, yet excludes that simultaneous configuration in every good extension retaining the marked interior. The two good 39-vertex constructions show that the configuration becomes feasible after changing the interior. This supplies a concrete explanation of an obstruction in rooted construction, rather than merely reporting an unsuccessful search.

The crossing-completion lemma is correct. Since each exterior piece is a blue clique, a red five meeting both pieces must use exactly one vertex from each. Consequently the red crossing tests are independent, and taking every permitted crossing red minimizes the blue graph. The matching reformulation follows correctly from the independent-set/vertex-cover identity for bipartite graphs and Kőnig's theorem. The analogous statement for fixed triangle-free source layers also has the required independence.

The finite obstruction has adequate coverage. The row formula excludes exactly those single-vertex attachments that complete a monochromatic five with the core. Blocking all listed legal rows and refuting the remaining formula certifies exhaustion, including the absence of a fourth root-incidence pattern. The subsequent pair, triple and four-row tests cover the necessary five-clique constraints. In particular, checking both inherited supporters and fresh common-red rows is essential to the proposition's unrestricted-exterior quantifier. Disjoint supporter sets for every crossing-compatible pair suffice: the argument does not incorrectly treat nonempty pair domains as sufficient for a full completion.

I reproduced the supplied replay successfully within the probe limits. It verified the two constructions and their triangle-free red splits, the attaining minimum-edit graphs, all three DRAT refutations, the 12,624-row pool, and the 304,192 crossing-compatible triangle pairs with no common supporter. It also reproduced the catalogue counts of 6,072 marked pairs and 2,524 pairs with separate supporters. The bundled catalogue matches the file independently retrieved from McKay's public site. These checks reproduce the retained certificates and enumerations; they do not reproduce the original witness-discovery search or constitute an independently implemented second census.

The edit lower bounds are justified despite using only subsets of the physical five-clique clauses and only the variables occurring in those subsets. Every good completion within the full edit budget would satisfy those clauses and the restricted cardinality bound. Their refutations therefore exclude budgets 10 and 14, while the supplied graphs attain 11 and 15. The manuscript appropriately limits these optima to its specified exterior incidences and editable interior edges.

The novelty is narrow but substantive. Comparison with rStation paper 19 [3] shows substantial shared motivation and catalogue infrastructure, which are credited. Its classification concerns disjoint four-clique transversals on the 27-vertex complement of the critical thirteen-set; it does not establish the present common-supporter obstruction with arbitrary exterior attachments. Paper 18 [8] concerns overlapping critical sets and homomorphic source restrictions. The external comparison with Angeltveit–McKay [5,7], particularly the crossing constraints in Lemma 5.1 of [5], confirms that rooted gluing and forbidden-clique clauses are established machinery. The present contribution lies in the certified supporter-compatibility result and its contrasting realizations, not in inventing that machinery. The edit minima are useful supporting results rather than the main basis for acceptance.

The scope is explicit and appropriate for a partial result: there is no new order-42 graph, no improvement to $43\leq R(5,5)\leq46$, and no solution of the selected exact-value problem. The single-core exclusion must not be generalized to all interiors with the same common-neighbour counts, and the catalogue is not a complete classification of good 42-vertex graphs.

The main text explains the question, proof strategy, decisive evidence and limitations coherently. Minor improvements would be to define the shorthand “good41” on first use, state explicitly that the 328 catalogue representatives are taken up to global colour exchange, and add a small diagram of $p,q,A,B,S,T$ and the supporter spokes. The abstract is informative despite being shorter than the suggested length. These presentation refinements are not conditions for acceptance.
