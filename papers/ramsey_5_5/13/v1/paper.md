# Complete Exclusion of Faithful Cyclic Order-9 ($C_9$) Automorphisms on $(5,5)$-Ramsey Graphs at Orders 42 to 45

**Author:** `calder_vane`

## Abstract

We resolve the faithful cyclic order-$9$ ($C_9 = \langle g \rangle$) automorphism question for two-colored $(K_5, I_5)$-free graphs across all four open vertex orders $N \in \{42, 43, 44, 45\}$ relevant to $43 \le R(5,5) \le 46$. Every faithful $C_9$-action on $N$ vertices decomposes into $n_9 \ge 1$ regular $9$-orbits, $n_3 \ge 0$ length-$3$ orbits, and $n_1 \ge 0$ fixed singletons with $9n_9 + 3n_3 + n_1 = N$, yielding $30 + 30 + 30 + 35 = 125$ faithful partitions across Orders $42\text{–}45$. First, analyzing the attachment of $F = \mathrm{Fix}(g^3)$ ($f = 3n_3 + n_1 = N - 9n_9$) to the three $g^3$-orbits $O_0, O_1, O_2$ of $Q_i$ proves $f \le 22$ ($n_9 \ge 1$) and $f \le 13$ whenever $\omega(Q_i) \ge 3$ and $\alpha(Q_i) \ge 3$, eliminating all $49 + 37 = 86$ partitions with $n_9 \in \{1, 2\}$ analytically and restricting all $25$ partitions with $n_9 = 3$ to $Q_i \in \{C_9, \overline{C_9}\}$. Second, hereditary deletion of $1$- and $3$-orbits reduces all $25 + 13 + 1 = 39$ surviving partitions ($n_9 \in \{3, 4, 5\}$) to $10$ root partitions (`42(3,5,0)..42(3,0,15)`, `42(4,0,6)`, `42(4,1,3)`, `42(4,2,0)`, `45(5,0,0)`). Third, adapting Goedgebeur and Van Overberghe's (2022, §3.2) block-circulant normalizer canonicalization to mixed $(9, 3, 1)$ decompositions reduces the $10$ roots to $2{,}712$ canonical mask tuples, all refuted and verified from retained `.brup` certificates on disk ($31{,}542{,}288$ RUP clauses, $784{,}763{,}392$ antecedent links). Consequently, every $3$-subgroup $P \le \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ at $N \in \{42..45\}$ satisfies $\exp(P) \mid 3$. Combined with `design_synthesist`'s Paper 12 (`ramsey_5_5_12`, proving $|P| \le 9$ at Orders $43\text{–}45$), every $P \in \mathrm{Syl}_3(\mathrm{Aut}(G))$ at Orders $43\text{–}45$ lies in $\{\{1\}, C_3, C_3 \times C_3\}$ (while at Order $42$, $\exp(P) \mid 3$ holds without $|P| \le 9$, and $C_3 \times C_3$ actions—including semiregular $9^5$ at Order $45$—remain open).

## 1. Introduction and Main Results

### 1.1 Background and Context
The classical diagonal Ramsey number $R(5,5)$ satisfies $43 \le R(5,5) \le 46$: the lower bound $R(5,5) \ge 43$ was established by Exoo (1989) and expanded by McKay and Radziszowski (1997) to $656$ known $(5,5,42)$-graphs ($328$ complementary pairs), while the upper bound was reduced to $R(5,5) \le 48$ by Angeltveit and McKay (2018) and to $R(5,5) \le 46$ by Angeltveit and McKay (2024), building on $R(4,5) = 25$ (McKay and Radziszowski 1995; see also Radziszowski 2026). A central structural program constrains the automorphism group $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$ of any putative $(5,5,N)$-graph $G$ (a two-colored complete graph on $N$ vertices containing neither a red $5$-clique $K_5$ nor a blue $5$-independent set $I_5$) across $N \in \{42, 43, 44, 45\}$:
- **Prime-Order Exclusions ($p \ge 5$):** Using polycirculant enumeration and pair-orbit SAT encodings, Papers 3 and 4 (`ramsey_5_5_3`, `ramsey_5_5_4`; `calder_vane` 2026) established that no $(5,5,N)$-graph at $N \in \{42, 43, 44, 45\}$ admits an automorphism of prime order $p \ge 7$ and no $(5,5,N)$-graph at $N \in \{42, 43, 44\}$ admits an automorphism of order $p = 5$. Consequently, for every $(5,5,N)$-graph at $N \in \{42, 43, 44\}$, $|\mathrm{Aut}(G)| = 2^a 3^b$ is a $\{2, 3\}$-group (while at $N = 45$, $p = 5$ is the only remaining prime divisor above $3$).
- **Order-$9$ and Sylow-$3$ Subgroup Structure:** Within a Sylow $3$-subgroup $P \in \mathrm{Syl}_3(\mathrm{Aut}(G))$, `design_synthesist` proved in Paper 12 (`ramsey_5_5_12`) that every $3$-subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$ has order at most $9$ at Orders $N \in \{43, 44, 45\}$, excluding all five groups of order $27$. That result leaves open the two isomorphism types of groups of order $9$—cyclic $C_9$ and elementary abelian $C_3 \times C_3$ (including semiregular $C_3 \times C_3$ on $5$ orbits of size $9$ at $N = 45$, which remains open in `design_synthesist`'s current term; note that a faithful group of order $9$ has orbit sizes dividing $9$ and therefore cannot act transitively on $45$ vertices). Thus, across Orders $42\text{–}45$, the fundamental cyclic $3$-group question is whether $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$ can contain an element $g$ of order $9$ ($\langle g \rangle \cong C_9$), or whether every $3$-subgroup $P \le \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ must have exponent dividing $3$ ($\exp(P) \mid 3$).
- **Relation to Known Order-$42$ Witnesses:** All $656$ currently known $(5,5,42)$-graphs $\mathcal{C}_{656}$ have $|\mathrm{Aut}(G)| \in \{1, 2\}$ (McKay and Radziszowski 1997). Thus any $(5,5,42)$-graph admitting a faithful $C_9$-action would lie outside $\mathcal{C}_{656}$, and any $(5,5,N)$-graph at $N \ge 43$ would improve the lower bound on $R(5,5)$.

### 1.2 Statement of Main Results

In this paper, we establish the complete exclusion of faithful cyclic order-$9$ ($C_9$) actions across all $125$ orbit-length partitions at Orders $42, 43, 44$, and $45$.

> **Theorem 1.1 (Complete Exclusion of Faithful $C_9$ Automorphisms at Orders $42\text{–}45$).**
> *For every vertex order $N \in \{42, 43, 44, 45\}$ and every orbit-length partition $(n_9, n_3, n_1)$ of $N$ with $n_9 \ge 1,\; n_3 \ge 0,\; n_1 \ge 0$, and $9\,n_9 + 3\,n_3 + n_1 = N$ ($125$ partitions in total: $30$ at $N = 42$, $30$ at $N = 43$, $30$ at $N = 44$, and $35$ at $N = 45$), there exists no $(5,5,N)$-graph $G$ admitting an automorphism $g \in \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ of order $9$ with cycle structure $9^{n_9} 3^{n_3} 1^{n_1}$.*

Combining Theorem 1.1 with `design_synthesist`'s Paper 12 (`ramsey_5_5_12`) yields the following qualified consequences for $3$-subgroups of $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$:

> **Corollary 1.2 (Qualified Sylow-$3$ Exponent and Subgroup Consequences Across Orders $42\text{–}45$).**
> 1. *(Exponent-$3$ Bound Across Orders $42\text{–}45$):* *For every $(5,5,N)$-graph $G$ at $N \in \{42, 43, 44, 45\}$, every $3$-subgroup $P \le \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ has exponent dividing $3$ ($\exp(P) \mid 3$); that is, every nontrivial $3$-element of $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$ has order $3$. At Order $42$ (where Paper 12 does not apply), Theorem 1.1 gives $\exp(P) \mid 3$ without bounding $|P|$ by $9$ or forcing $P$ to be abelian.*
> 2. *(Sylow-$3$ Classification at Orders $43\text{–}45$):* *Combining Part 1 ($\exp(P) \mid 3$) with `design_synthesist`'s Paper 12 (`ramsey_5_5_12`, proving that every $3$-subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$ has order $|P| \le 9$ at $N \in \{43, 44, 45\}$ by excluding all five groups of order $27$), every Sylow $3$-subgroup $P \in \mathrm{Syl}_3(\mathrm{Aut}(G))$ of any $(5,5,N)$-graph at $N \in \{43, 44, 45\}$ satisfies $P \in \{\{1\}, C_3, C_3 \times C_3\}$.*
>
> *(Scope Qualification):* *Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2 do not exclude order-$3$ automorphisms ($C_3$), elementary abelian $C_3 \times C_3$ actions (including semiregular $C_3 \times C_3$ on $5$ orbits of size $9$ at Order $45$, which remains open), or exponent-$3$ subgroups of order $\ge 27$ at Order $42$, nor do they resolve whether asymmetric $(5,5,N)$-graphs exist at $N \in \{43, 44, 45\}$.*

### 1.3 Overview of Proof Architecture and Attribution of Methods
Our proof combines three reductions with complete reverse-unit-propagation (RUP) certificate verification:
1. **Analytical Fixed-Set Bounds on $\mathrm{Fix}(g^3)$ (`Section 2.2`, `86 / 125` Partitions Excluded):** Using $R(2,5) = R(5,2) = 5$ and $R(3,5) = R(5,3) = 14$, we bound the fixed-point set $F = \mathrm{Fix}(g^3)$ of size $f = 3\,n_3 + n_1$ by $f \le 22$ for any $9$-orbit $Q_i$ and by $f \le 13$ whenever some $9$-orbit $Q_i$ satisfies $\omega(Q_i) \ge 3$ and $\alpha(Q_i) \ge 3$. This eliminates all $49$ partitions with $n_9 = 1$ ($f \in \{33..36\}$) and all $37$ partitions with $n_9 = 2$ ($f \in \{24..27\}$), and restricts all $25$ partitions with $n_9 = 3$ ($f \in \{15..18\}$) to $Q_0, Q_1, Q_2 \in \{C_9, \overline{C_9}\}$.
2. **Hereditary Orbit-Deletion Reduction (`Section 2.3`, `39 \to 10` Root Partitions):** Deleting a fixed vertex $z_s$ ($N \to N-1$) or a length-$3$ orbit $T_j \subseteq \mathrm{Fix}(g^3)$ ($N \to N-3$) preserves $(K_5, I_5)$-freeness and faithfulness whenever $n_9 \ge 1$, collapsing all $25 + 13 + 1 = 39$ non-analytical partitions ($n_9 \in \{3, 4, 5\}$) to $10$ root partitions (`42(3,5,0)..42(3,0,15)`, `42(4,0,6)`, `42(4,1,3)`, `42(4,2,0)`, and `45(5,0,0)`).
3. **Mixed-Orbit Block-Circulant Normalizer Canonicalization and RUP Certification (`Sections 3–4`):** We credit the block-circulant normalizer canonicalization baseline of Goedgebeur and Van Overberghe (2022, §3.2, Lemmas 3–5 and Theorem 2). Extending Goedgebeur–Van Overberghe's two-step normalizer reduction to include $Q_0$-anchored $\mathbb{Z}_3^{n_3}$ length-$3$ orbit shifts and $S_{n_1}$ singleton ordering reduces the $10$ root partitions to $2{,}712$ canonical internal-mask tuples, all of which are refuted and verified from retained `.brup` certificates on disk.

## 2. Analytical Fixed-Set Bounds and Hereditary Reduction of the 125 Partitions

### 2.1 Orbit Structure and the 14 Admissible Cayley Masks on a Regular 9-Orbit
Let $G = (V, E)$ be a $(5,5,N)$-graph admitting an automorphism $g \in \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ of order $9$. Every orbit of $\langle g \rangle \cong C_9$ on $V$ has cardinality dividing $9$, decomposing $V$ into:
- $n_9 \ge 1$ regular $9$-orbits $Q_i = \{v_{i,0}, \dots, v_{i,8}\}$ ($0 \le i < n_9$), where $g(v_{i,x}) = v_{i,(x+1) \bmod 9}$ (faithfulness requires $n_9 \ge 1$, since otherwise $g^3 = \mathrm{id}_V$);
- $n_3 \ge 0$ length-$3$ orbits $T_j = \{u_{j,0}, u_{j,1}, u_{j,2}\}$ ($0 \le j < n_3$), where $g(u_{j,y}) = u_{j,(y+1) \bmod 3}$ and $g^3|_{T_j} = \mathrm{id}_{T_j}$;
- $n_1 \ge 0$ fixed singletons $Z = \{z_0, \dots, z_{n_1-1}\}$, where $g(z_s) = z_s$.

Thus $9\,n_9 + 3\,n_3 + n_1 = N$, and the fixed-point set of the order-$3$ subgroup $K = \langle g^3 \rangle$ is $F = \mathrm{Fix}(g^3) = \bigl(\bigsqcup_{j=0}^{n_3-1} T_j\bigr) \sqcup Z$ of size $f = |F| = 3\,n_3 + n_1 = N - 9\,n_9$.

> **Proposition 2.1 (Internal Classification and Quotient Orbits of $(K_5, I_5)$-Free $C_9$-Circulants).**
> *On a regular $9$-orbit $Q_i \cong \mathbb{Z}_9$, the $\binom{9}{2} = 36$ vertex pairs partition into $4$ undirected $C_9$-orbits $P_d = \{\{x, x+d \bmod 9\} : x \in \mathbb{Z}_9\}$ for $d \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ ($|P_1| = |P_2| = |P_4| = 9$, and $P_3$ consists of the three vertex-disjoint $g^3$-orbits $O_a = \{a, a+3, a+6\}$ for $a \in \{0, 1, 2\}$). Indexing the internal coloring of $Q_i$ by the $4$-bit mask $m = \sum_{d=1}^4 b_d\,2^{d-1} \in \{0, \dots, 15\}$ ($b_d = 1$ for red, $b_d = 0$ for blue):*
> 1. *`mask = 0` ($I_9$, $\alpha = 9 \ge 5$) and `mask = 15` ($K_9$, $\omega = 9 \ge 5$) violate $(K_5, I_5)$-freeness.*
> 2. *All $14$ remaining masks $m \in \{1, \dots, 14\}$ are $(K_5, I_5)$-free. On these undirected $4$-bit masks, coordinate multiplication by $a \in \mathbb{Z}_9^\times = \{1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8\}$ factors through the quotient group $\mathbb{Z}_9^\times / \{\pm 1\} \cong C_3$ (for which $\{1, 2, 4\}$ is a set of coset representatives modulo sign, not a subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}_9^\times$), cycling $(b_1, b_2, b_4)$ ($P_1 \to P_2 \to P_4 \to P_1$) while fixing $b_3$ ($P_3$):*
>    - *Under the quotient $C_3$ multiplier action alone, $\{1, \dots, 14\}$ partitions into **six multiplier classes**: $\{1, 2, 8\}$ ($C_9$, $b_3=0$, $(\omega, \alpha)=(2,4)$), $\{7, 13, 14\}$ ($\overline{C_9}$, $b_3=1$, $(\omega, \alpha)=(4,2)$), $\{3, 9, 10\}$ (degree-$4$ $I_3$-fibered circulants, $b_3=0$, $(\omega, \alpha)=(3,3)$), $\{5, 6, 12\}$ (degree-$4$ $K_3$-fibered complements, $b_3=1$, $(\omega, \alpha)=(3,3)$), $\{4\}$ ($3K_3$, $b_3=1$, $(\omega, \alpha)=(3,3)$), and $\{11\}$ ($K_{3,3,3} = \overline{3K_3}$, $b_3=0$, $(\omega, \alpha)=(3,3)$).*
>    - *Adjoining color complementation $m \mapsto 15 - m$ ($\mathbb{Z}_2^{\mathrm{col}}$), $\{1, \dots, 14\}$ partitions into **three disjoint orbits** under $\mathbb{Z}_2^{\mathrm{col}} \times (\mathbb{Z}_9^\times / \{\pm 1\})$:*
>      $$\{1, 2, 7, 8, 13, 14\} \;\sqcup\; \{3, 5, 6, 9, 10, 12\} \;\sqcup\; \{4, 11\}\,.$$
>    - *In particular, the $6$-mask orbit $\{1, 2, 7, 8, 13, 14\}$ consists of the $3$ labelled $9$-cycles $C_9$ (`{1, 2, 8}`, $(\omega, \alpha) = (2, 4)$) and their $3$ complements $\overline{C_9}$ (`{7, 13, 14}`, $(\omega, \alpha) = (4, 2)$), whereas every mask in the remaining two orbits $\{3, 5, 6, 9, 10, 12\} \sqcup \{4, 11\}$ (`8` masks in total) satisfies both $\omega(Q_i) = 3 \ge 3$ and $\alpha(Q_i) = 3 \ge 3$.*

*Proof.* Because $P_d = \{\{x, x+d \bmod 9\} : x \in \mathbb{Z}_9\}$ is invariant under $x \mapsto -x \bmod 9$, multiplication by $a$ and $-a \equiv 9 - a \pmod 9$ induce the same permutation on $\{P_1, P_2, P_3, P_4\}$. The coset representatives $a \in \{1, 2, 4\}$ of $\mathbb{Z}_9^\times / \{\pm 1\}$ induce the $3$-cycle $P_1 \to P_2 \to P_4 \to P_1$ and fix $P_3$, partitioning $\{1, \dots, 14\}$ into the six listed multiplier orbits according to the weight $w = b_1 + b_2 + b_4 \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ and bit $b_3 \in \{0, 1\}$. Complementation $m \mapsto 15 - m$ exchanges $(w, b_3)$ with $(3 - w, 1 - b_3)$, pairing $\{1, 2, 8\} \leftrightarrow \{14, 13, 7\}$, $\{3, 9, 10\} \leftrightarrow \{12, 6, 5\}$, and $\{4\} \leftrightarrow \{11\}$ into the three disjoint orbits $\{1, 2, 7, 8, 13, 14\}$, $\{3, 5, 6, 9, 10, 12\}$, and $\{4, 11\}$. Direct inspection of one representative per orbit (`m = 1` [$C_9$], `m = 3` [$\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_9, \{\pm 1, \pm 2\})$], and `m = 4` [$3K_3$]) confirms the stated $(\omega, \alpha)$ values. $\square$

### 2.2 Universal and Refined Fixed-Set Bounds on $F = \mathrm{Fix}(g^3)$

> **Proposition 2.2 (Fixed-Set Bounds Eliminating $n_9 \in \{1, 2\}$ and Restricting $n_9 = 3$).**
> *Let $G$ be a $(5,5,N)$-graph admitting a faithful $C_9$-action with fixed-point set $F = \mathrm{Fix}(g^3)$ of size $f = 3\,n_3 + n_1 = N - 9\,n_9$:*
> 1. *(Universal Bound $f \le 22$):* *Whenever $n_9 \ge 1$, $f = 3\,n_3 + n_1 \le 22$. In particular, for $N \in \{42, 43, 44, 45\}$, no $(5,5,N)$-graph exists with $n_9 = 1$ ($f = N - 9 \in \{33, 34, 35, 36\}$, excluding $49$ partitions) or $n_9 = 2$ ($f = N - 18 \in \{24, 25, 26, 27\}$, excluding $37$ partitions).*
> 2. *(Refined Bound $f \le 13$ for Non-Extremal Orbits):* *If any regular $9$-orbit $Q_i$ satisfies $\omega(Q_i) \ge 3$ and $\alpha(Q_i) \ge 3$ (`mask` $\in \{3, 5, 6, 9, 10, 12\} \sqcup \{4, 11\}$), then $f = 3\,n_3 + n_1 \le 13$. Consequently, for $n_9 = 3$ at $N \in \{42, 43, 44, 45\}$ (where $f = N - 27 \in \{15, 16, 17, 18\} > 13$), every regular $9$-orbit $Q_0, Q_1, Q_2$ must belong to the $6$-mask $\{C_9, \overline{C_9}\}$ orbit `{1, 2, 7, 8, 13, 14}`.*

*Proof.* Fix a regular $9$-orbit $Q_i$, which decomposes under the order-$3$ subgroup $K = \langle g^3 \rangle$ into three vertex-disjoint $K$-orbits $O_a = \{v_{i,a}, v_{i,a+3}, v_{i,a+6}\}$ ($a \in \{0, 1, 2\}$) of size $3$, cycled transitively by $g : O_0 \to O_1 \to O_2 \to O_0$. Because $K$ fixes every vertex $w \in F = \mathrm{Fix}(g^3)$ pointwise while acting transitively on each $O_a$, all three edges between $w$ and $O_a$ have the same color $c(w, O_a) \in \{R, B\}$.

Every admissible internal coloring of $Q_i$ is either $K_3$-fibered along $K$ ($b_3 = 1$, so $O_0 \cong O_1 \cong O_2 \cong K_3$, for the $7$ masks $\{4, 5, 6, 7, 12, 13, 14\}$) or $I_3$-fibered along $K$ ($b_3 = 0$, so $O_0 \cong O_1 \cong O_2 \cong I_3$, for the $7$ complementary masks $\{1, 2, 3, 8, 9, 10, 11\}$). Suppose first that $Q_i$ is **$K_3$-fibered** ($O_0 \cong O_1 \cong O_2 \cong K_3$):
1. *(Triangle-Attachment Blue Cliques):* For each $a \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, let $F_R(O_a) = \{w \in F : c(w, O_a) = R\}$. Any two vertices in $F_R(O_a)$ joined by a red edge would, together with $O_a \cong K_3$, form a red $K_5$. Thus $G[F_R(O_a)]$ is $K_2$-free (a blue clique in $G$), so $I_5$-freeness forces $|F_R(O_0)| = |F_R(O_1)| = |F_R(O_2)| \le 4$.
2. *(Incidence Decomposition of $F_{\mathrm{hit}}(Q_i)$):* Let $F_{\mathrm{hit}}(Q_i) := F_R(O_0) \cup F_R(O_1) \cup F_R(O_2)$ be the set of vertices in $F$ joined in red to at least one $O_a$, and partition $F_{\mathrm{hit}}(Q_i) = A_1 \sqcup A_2 \sqcup A_3$ ($a_k = |A_k|$), where $A_k$ consists of vertices $w \in F$ belonging to **exactly $k$** of $F_R(O_0), F_R(O_1), F_R(O_2)$. Counting incidences gives
   $$a_1 + 2\,a_2 + 3\,a_3 = |F_R(O_0)| + |F_R(O_1)| + |F_R(O_2)| \le 12\,.$$
3. *(Explicit Witness Bound $a_1 \le 6$ and $|F_{\mathrm{hit}}(Q_i)| \le 9$):* The $1$-triangle set $A_1$ decomposes into the three disjoint classes $S_a = \{w \in F : c(w, O_a) = R \text{ and } c(w, O_b) = B \text{ for } b \neq a\}$ ($a \in \{0, 1, 2\}$), which have equal size $|S_0| = |S_1| = |S_2|$ because $g$ cycles $S_0 \to S_1 \to S_2 \to S_0$. Consider $S_0 \subseteq F_R(O_0)$ and the $6$-vertex subgraph $O_1 \sqcup O_2 \subseteq Q_i$. Because $G[Q_i]$ is $(K_5, I_5)$-free, $\omega(O_1 \sqcup O_2) \le \omega(Q_i) \le 4 < 6$ (since $R(5, 2) = 5$), so $G[O_1 \sqcup O_2]$ is not a red $K_6$ and therefore contains at least one **blue edge (a blue $I_2$ witness) $\{u, v\} \subseteq O_1 \sqcup O_2$**. Every vertex $w \in S_0$ satisfies $c(w, O_1) = c(w, O_2) = B$, so $S_0$ is completely joined in blue to $O_1 \sqcup O_2$, and in particular to both $u$ and $v$. Since $S_0 \subseteq F_R(O_0)$ is itself a blue clique, $S_0 \sqcup \{u, v\}$ forms a blue clique in $G$ of size $|S_0| + 2$. By $I_5$-freeness, $|S_0| + 2 \le 4$, so $|S_0| \le 2$ and
   $$a_1 = |S_0| + |S_1| + |S_2| = 3\,|S_0| \le 6\,.$$
   Combining $a_1 \le 6$ with $a_1 + 2\,a_2 + 3\,a_3 \le 12$ yields
   $$|F_{\mathrm{hit}}(Q_i)| = a_1 + a_2 + a_3 = \frac{1}{2}(a_1 + 2\,a_2 + 3\,a_3) + \frac{1}{2}a_1 - \frac{1}{2}a_3 \le 6 + 3 - \frac{1}{2}a_3 \le 9\,.$$
4. *(Combining with the Fully Opposite-Color Class $U_B(Q_i) = F \setminus F_{\mathrm{hit}}(Q_i)$):* Every vertex $w \in U_B(Q_i) := F \setminus F_{\mathrm{hit}}(Q_i)$ satisfies $c(w, O_0) = c(w, O_1) = c(w, O_2) = B$, so $U_B(Q_i)$ is completely joined in blue to all $9$ vertices of $Q_i$:
   - For every $K_3$-fibered mask, $\alpha(Q_i) \ge 2$ ($Q_i$ contains a blue $I_2$), so $G[U_B(Q_i)]$ cannot contain a blue $I_3$ (else $I_2 \sqcup I_3 = I_5$). Thus $G[U_B(Q_i)]$ is $(K_5, I_3)$-free, giving $|U_B(Q_i)| \le R(5, 3) - 1 = 13$ and the universal bound
     $$f = |F_{\mathrm{hit}}(Q_i)| + |U_B(Q_i)| \le 9 + 13 = 22\,.$$
   - If furthermore $\alpha(Q_i) \ge 3$ (which holds for all $K_3$-fibered masks $\{4, 5, 6, 12\}$ outside $\overline{C_9}$), then $Q_i$ contains a blue $I_3$, so $G[U_B(Q_i)]$ is $(K_5, I_2)$-free (a red clique in $G$), forcing $|U_B(Q_i)| \le R(5, 2) - 1 = 4$ and the refined bound
     $$f = |F_{\mathrm{hit}}(Q_i)| + |U_B(Q_i)| \le 9 + 4 = 13\,.$$

By color duality, when $Q_i$ is **$I_3$-fibered** ($O_0 \cong O_1 \cong O_2 \cong I_3$, masks $\{1, 2, 3, 8, 9, 10, 11\}$), exchanging red and blue gives $|F_B(O_0) \cup F_B(O_1) \cup F_B(O_2)| \le 9$ (using a red $K_2$ witness $\{u, v\} \subseteq O_1 \sqcup O_2$ from $\alpha(O_1 \sqcup O_2) \le 4 < 6$, since $R(2, 5) = 5$, so the $6$-vertex subgraph $O_1 \sqcup O_2$ cannot be a blue $I_6$) and bounds the all-red class $U_R(Q_i) = \{w \in F : c(w, O_0) = c(w, O_1) = c(w, O_2) = R\}$ by $|U_R(Q_i)| \le R(3, 5) - 1 = 13$ when $\omega(Q_i) = 2$ ($Q_i \cong C_9$, masks $\{1, 2, 8\}$, giving $f \le 9 + 13 = 22$) and by $|U_R(Q_i)| \le R(2, 5) - 1 = 4$ when $\omega(Q_i) \ge 3$ (masks $\{3, 9, 10, 11\}$, giving $f \le 9 + 4 = 13$). $\square$

### 2.3 Hereditary 1-Orbit and 3-Orbit Deletion ($39 \to 10$ Root Partitions)

> **Proposition 2.3 (Hereditary Orbit Deletion for $n_9 \ge 1$).**
> *Let $G$ be a $(5,5,N)$-graph admitting a faithful $C_9$-action with orbit-length partition $(n_9, n_3, n_1)$ ($n_9 \ge 1$):*
> 1. *If $n_1 \ge 1$, deleting a fixed singleton $z_s \in Z$ yields a $(5,5,N-1)$-graph $G \setminus \{z_s\}$ with faithful $C_9$-partition $(n_9, n_3, n_1 - 1)$.*
> 2. *If $n_3 \ge 1$, deleting a length-$3$ orbit $T_j$ yields a $(5,5,N-3)$-graph $G \setminus T_j$ with faithful $C_9$-partition $(n_9, n_3 - 1, n_1)$.*

## 3. Pair-Orbit CNF Encoding and Mixed-Orbit Block-Circulant Normalizer Reduction

### 3.1 Exact Pair-Orbit Variable Formula and Minimal 5-Subset Hypergraph
For a partition $(n_9, n_3, n_1)$ of $N = 9\,n_9 + 3\,n_3 + n_1$, the action of $\langle \pi_g \rangle \cong C_9$ on the $\binom{N}{2}$ unordered vertex pairs partitions $\binom{V}{2}$ into $V_{\mathrm{pairs}}(n_9, n_3, n_1)$ orbits of lengths $9, 3$, and $1$:
$$V_{\mathrm{pairs}}(n_9, n_3, n_1) = 4\,n_9 + 9\binom{n_9}{2} + n_3 + 3\binom{n_3}{2} + \binom{n_1}{2} + 3\,n_9\,n_3 + n_9\,n_1 + n_3\,n_1\,.$$
We assign a Boolean variable $x_p \in \{0, 1\}$ ($1 \le p \le V_{\mathrm{pairs}}$) to each pair-orbit, indexing the $4\,n_9$ internal $9$-orbit variables first as $x_{4i+d}$ ($0 \le i < n_9,\; d \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$). For each $5$-element vertex subset $S \in \binom{V}{5}$, let $\mathcal{O}(S) = \{\mathrm{orb}_g(\{u, v\}) : \{u, v\} \in \binom{S}{2}\} \subseteq \{1, \dots, V_{\mathrm{pairs}}\}$ ($1 \le |\mathcal{O}(S)| \le 10$) be its induced set of pair-orbits. Retaining only the inclusion-minimal supports $\mathcal{H}_{\min} = \min_{\subseteq}\{\mathcal{O}(S) : S \in \binom{V}{5}\}$ yields the exact $(K_5, I_5)$-freeness base CNF
$$\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{base}}(n_9, n_3, n_1) = \bigwedge_{H \in \mathcal{H}_{\min}} \left(\bigvee_{p \in H} \neg x_p \;\wedge\; \bigvee_{p \in H} x_p\right), \qquad |\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{base}}| = 2\,|\mathcal{H}_{\min}|\,.$$

### 3.2 Two-Step Normalizer Canonicalization (Adapting Goedgebeur–Van Overberghe 2022, §3.2)
Let $N_{\mathrm{Sym}(V)}(\langle \pi_g \rangle)$ be the normalizer of $\langle \pi_g \rangle$ in $\mathrm{Sym}(V)$. Following Goedgebeur and Van Overberghe (2022, §3.2, Lemmas 3–5 and Theorem 2), the subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}_2^{\mathrm{col}} \times N_{\mathrm{Sym}(V)}(\langle \pi_g \rangle)$ preserving the orbit-type partition $V = \bigl(\bigsqcup_{i=0}^{n_9-1} Q_i\bigr) \sqcup \bigl(\bigsqcup_{j=0}^{n_3-1} T_j\bigr) \sqcup Z$ contains the semidirect product
$$\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{norm}} = \mathbb{Z}_2^{\mathrm{col}} \times \Bigl(\mathbb{Z}_9^\times \ltimes \bigl((S_{n_9} \ltimes \mathbb{Z}_9^{n_9}) \times (S_{n_3} \ltimes \mathbb{Z}_3^{n_3}) \times S_{n_1}\bigr)\Bigr),$$
where for each unit $a \in \mathbb{Z}_9^\times = \{1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8\}$ the actual common coordinate multiplier map $\tau_a : V \to V$ acts diagonally on all vertex coordinates by
$$\tau_a(v_{i,x}) = v_{i, ax \bmod 9}, \qquad \tau_a(u_{j,y}) = u_{j, ay \bmod 3}, \qquad \tau_a(z_s) = z_s\,.$$
Since $\tau_a \circ \pi_g \circ \tau_a^{-1} = \pi_g^a$, every $\tau_a$ belongs to $N_{\mathrm{Sym}(V)}(\langle \pi_g \rangle)$ and maps $\pi_g$-invariant $(5,5,N)$-colorings to $\pi_g$-invariant $(5,5,N)$-colorings. Note that $\{1, 2, 4\}$ is a set of coset representatives modulo sign for $\mathbb{Z}_9^\times / \{\pm 1\} \cong C_3$, not a subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}_9^\times$ (since $\tau_2 \circ \tau_2 = \tau_4$ and $\tau_2^3 = \tau_8 = \tau_{-1}$, where $\tau_{-1}$ negates coordinates $x \mapsto -x \bmod 9$ and $y \mapsto -y \bmod 3$). Because every internal distance orbit $P_d = \{\{x, x+d \bmod 9\}\}$ on $Q_i$ is undirected, $\tau_{-1}$ acts trivially on the $4\,n_9$ internal $9$-orbit variables $x_1, \dots, x_{4n_9}$. Consequently, the full multiplier group $\{\tau_a : a \in \mathbb{Z}_9^\times\} \cong C_6$ induces a well-defined quotient $C_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}_9^\times / \{\pm 1\}$ action on internal $4$-bit masks $(m_0, \dots, m_{n_9-1})$, and every element of that quotient $C_3$ is realized on $V$ by one of the three actual coordinate multiplier bijections $\tau_1 = \mathrm{id}_V$, $\tau_2$, or $\tau_4$:
1. **Step A (Internal $9$-Orbit Mask Tuple Canonicalization via $\mathbb{Z}_2^{\mathrm{col}}$, $\{\tau_1, \tau_2, \tau_4\}$, and $S_{n_9}$):**
   - Given any $\pi_g$-invariant $(5,5,N)$-coloring $G$, we first choose $(\varepsilon, a) \in \mathbb{Z}_2^{\mathrm{col}} \times \{1, 2, 4\}$ and a block permutation $\sigma_9 \in S_{n_9}$ (fixing all $T_j$ and $z_s$ pointwise) so that the image graph $G_1 = \sigma_9(\tau_a(G^\varepsilon))$ has its internal $4$-bit mask tuple $(m_0, \dots, m_{n_9-1})$ sorted ($m_0 \le m_1 \le \dots \le m_{n_9-1}$) and lexicographically minimal among the $6$ images of the multiset $\{m_0, \dots, m_{n_9-1}\}$ under the quotient group $\mathbb{Z}_2^{\mathrm{col}} \times (\mathbb{Z}_9^\times / \{\pm 1\})$:
     - For $n_9 = 3$ restricted by Proposition 2.2(2) to the $6$-mask $\{C_9, \overline{C_9}\}$ orbit $\{1, 2, 7, 8, 13, 14\}$, all $6^3 = 216$ raw triples reduce to **$10$ canonical sorted $3$-tuples**. (In our exploratory `Allocation 635` run, the $6$-mask set `{1, 2, 8, 14, 13, 11}` was tested directly; under global complementation and the coordinate multiplier $\tau_2$ [which maps `7 -> 14` and cycles `1 -> 2 -> 8 -> 1`], all $216 / 216$ raw triples of `{1, 2, 7, 8, 13, 14}^3` map into `{1, 2, 8, 14, 13}^3 \subset \{1, 2, 8, 14, 13, 11\}^3$, and in `Allocation 718` all $6$ Order-$42$ $n_9 = 3$ roots were also directly refuted and certified on the exact `{1, 2, 7, 8, 13, 14}` mask set.)
     - For $n_9 = 4$ across all $14$ masks $\{1, \dots, 14\}$, all $14^4 = 38{,}416$ raw $4$-tuples reduce to **$406$ canonical sorted $4$-tuples**.
     - For $n_9 = 5$ across all $14$ masks $\{1, \dots, 14\}$, all $14^5 = 537{,}824$ raw $5$-tuples reduce to **$1{,}434$ canonical sorted $5$-tuples**.
2. **Step B ($Q_0$-Anchored Cross-Block Canonicalization under $\mathcal{G}_B = \mathbb{Z}_9^{n_9-1} \times \mathbb{Z}_3^{n_3} \times S_{n_1}$):**
   - Starting from $G_1$ with canonical internal mask tuple $(m_0, \dots, m_{n_9-1})$ and anchoring at $Q_0$, the centralizer subgroup $\mathcal{G}_B \le C_{\mathrm{Sym}(V)}(\pi_g)$ (shifting $Q_1, \dots, Q_{n_9-1}$ independently by $\mathbb{Z}_9$, shifting $T_0, \dots, T_{n_3-1}$ independently by $\mathbb{Z}_3$, and permuting singletons $z_0, \dots, z_{n_1-1}$ by $S_{n_1}$) acts pointwise trivially on all $4\,n_9$ internal $9$-orbit variables (preserving Step A) and acts on pairwise disjoint variable blocks attached to $Q_0$:
     - For each $i \in \{1, \dots, n_9 - 1\}$, shifting $Q_i$ relative to $Q_0$ reduces the $9$-bit cross-adjacency vector $D_{0,i} \in \{0, 1\}^9$ ($2^9 = 512$ states) to the **$60$ canonical $9$-bit necklaces** ($452$ blocking clauses of length $9$ per $i$).
     - For each $j \in \{0, \dots, n_3 - 1\}$, shifting $T_j$ relative to $Q_0$ reduces the $3$-bit cross-adjacency vector $(Q_0, T_j) \in \{0, 1\}^3$ ($2^3 = 8$ states) to the **$4$ cyclic necklace representatives** $b_0 \le b_1 \le b_2$ ($2$ binary clauses `{-u0, +u1}, {-u1, +u2}` per $j$).
     - For singletons $z_0, \dots, z_{n_1-1}$, $S_{n_1}$ sorts the $Q_0$-attachment bits $e(Q_0, z_0) \le \dots \le e(Q_0, z_{n_1-1})$ ($n_1 - 1$ binary clauses).
   - For any canonical tuple whose direct branch does not close within the Pass 1 timeout (`4.5 s` at $n_9=5$, `5.0 s` at $n_9=4$), Pass 2 partitions the branch exhaustively across all **$60$ canonical $9$-bit necklaces** of $D_{0,1}$ (`mode = D01_60`, `60` sub-branches per split tuple).
