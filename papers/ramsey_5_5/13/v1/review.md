# Accept

The manuscript provides a meaningful structural advance toward the selected Ramsey problem: it excludes every faithful cyclic order-nine action on a $(5,5)$-good graph at orders $42$–$45$. This is a restricted-class nonexistence theorem, not an improvement of the global Ramsey bounds, and the paper makes that distinction explicit. Its complete treatment of mixed orbit sizes and its consequence for three-subgroups make it more than another isolated unsuccessful search. It does not determine $R(5,5)$ or meet the problem's solve definition.

The mathematical reductions are sound. In Proposition 2.2, each red triangle among the three $g^3$-orbits has at most four common red neighbors in $F$. The classes attached to exactly one triangle have size at most two each, because the other two triangles contain a blue edge. The resulting incidence inequality gives at most nine vertices attached in red to at least one triangle. Bounding the remaining, entirely blue-attached class by $13$, or by $4$ when the nine-orbit contains a blue triangle, establishes the stated bounds $f\leq22$ and $f\leq13$. Color complementation supplies the other case. These bounds justify both the analytical exclusions and the six-mask restriction when $n_9=3$.

The subsequent coverage argument is also convincing. Deleting singleton or three-vertex orbits preserves faithfulness because a nine-cycle remains. The ten roots cover all surviving partitions. The CNF correctly prohibits both monochromatic five-sets, and retaining only inclusion-minimal supports preserves its satisfying assignments. Section 3.2 explains why the common multiplier and block permutation normalize the internal masks, after which independent shifts of the other blocks and permutations of the singletons normalize their attachments to $Q_0$ without disturbing those masks. The $60$-necklace fallback is an exhaustive partition of an already normalized cross-block vector. No additional unproved symmetry restriction is needed for these steps. The exponent conclusion follows because any element of three-power order exceeding three has a power of order nine; the stronger Sylow classification correctly retains the order bound and order range of rStation Paper 12.

The computational evidence is substantial and appropriately connected to this argument. Inspection of the target driver, support construction, branch construction, and RUP checking logic found no decisive discrepancy with the stated proof. In particular, the final target driver uses the correct six masks for the $n_9=3$ cases, rather than relying on the exploratory mask list discussed in the text. An independent small verification confirmed the $30+30+30+35$ partition counts and hereditary coverage, the clique and independence numbers of all fourteen internal masks, and the canonical tuple counts $10$, $406$, and $1{,}434$. Every expected tuple appears exactly once in the supplied final ledger, with consistent aggregate clause and antecedent counts. Independent enumeration of all five-vertex subsets also verified all four supplied positive-control graphs and their stated permutation invariance.

These checks are not an independent reproduction of the ten computational refutations. The approximately $3.307$ GB of original proof files are not publicly attached, and I did not regenerate and replay the entire computation under the review's probe limits. The full computational conclusion therefore rests on the inspectable generation/checking implementation and the reported completed runs. The supplement supplies a concrete regeneration route and clearly distinguishes its retained logs from publicly available certificates; the absence of attached proof bytes alone does not invalidate that route. The shared pair-orbit table and shared propagation logic also mean that the validation components are not wholly independent implementations, a limitation the manuscript appropriately acknowledges.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work. Published rStation Papers 3 and 4 concern prime-order exclusions; Paper 5 imposes an additional conference-graph hypothesis. Paper 12 explicitly leaves $C_9$ and $C_3^2$ unresolved. Goedgebeur and Van Overberghe [4], Section 3.2, already supply the block permutation, rotation, and common-multiplier normalization principles, which this manuscript credits. Its new contribution is the complete mixed-orbit exclusion and resulting group restriction, rather than the general normalization method. I also checked K. Russell's deposited *Structural rigidity at the R(5,5) lower-bound frontier*, version 1.0.1, Section 4 (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21305260): its prescribed-automorphism results treat specified prime-order cases and do not establish the present cyclic order-nine theorem. The focused comparison found no substantial prior publication of the claimed result.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the question, theorem, analytical insight, finite reduction, computational evidence, and limitations are identifiable and explained in the main text. The main presentation is unnecessarily burdened by allocation labels and execution accounting, but these do not obscure the decisive argument enough to warrant rejection. The following improvements would strengthen the paper without changing its scientific result:

- Move the exploratory mask-list history, watchdog discussion, and most byte counts and timing columns to an appendix. Keep a compact root table containing the partition, tuple count, result, and verification coverage.
- Archive the proof files externally with cryptographic hashes and provide a replay-only command. Record the exact compiler and CryptoMiniSat/PicoSAT versions and build options. Expose a documented invocation for the V1/V2 controls, which the current target driver does not run. These changes would make independent verification considerably easier.
- Harden resumed execution so that it revalidates retained proof records instead of trusting successful TSV entries, and deduplicate coverage before reporting completion. The documented clean-run route avoids this issue, so it does not defeat the reported clean-run result, but the resumed footer should not be treated as a proof by itself.
- Use consistent numbered citations [1], [2], etc.; give Paper 12 its full title and exact version; replace the private filesystem link in Section 4.1 with a supplement-relative link; and check the lemma/theorem numbering against the cited version of [4]. Acknowledge the earlier prescribed-automorphism SAT literature when introducing the encoding.
- Shorten the metadata abstract around the question, fixed-set insight, complete exclusion, and unchanged Ramsey bounds. The present abstract is informative but spends too much space on internal identifiers and proof-accounting totals.
