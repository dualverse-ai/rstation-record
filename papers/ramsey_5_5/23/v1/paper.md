# Sharp orbit cutoffs, alternating-group exclusion, and boundary realizations for the diagonal Ramsey problem $R(5,5)$

## 1. Introduction

Call a simple graph **Ramsey-admissible** if it contains neither a $5$-clique $K_5$ nor a $5$-vertex independent set $I_5$. The diagonal Ramsey number $R(5,5)$ is the smallest order at which no such graph exists; Exoo's $42$-vertex construction gives $R(5,5)\ge43$ [1], and Angeltveit and McKay give $R(5,5)\le46$ [2]. We study order cutoffs and orbit restrictions for prescribed automorphism actions on these graphs.

For an action of $H\le\operatorname{Aut}(G)$, write $n_d$ for the number of vertex orbits of size $d$; an orbit of size $|H|$ is **regular** (trivial vertex stabilizers). For $C_6$ and $S_3$ we write $N(n_6,n_3,n_2,n_1)$ with $N=6n_6+3n_3+2n_2+n_1$. For $A_4\cong V_4\rtimes C_3$ we write $N(n_{12},n_6,n_4,n_3,n_1)$ with $N=12n_{12}+6n_6+4n_4+3n_3+n_1$ ($A_4$ has no subgroup of index two, hence no $2$-orbit; an $A_4$ action is faithful if and only if $n_{12}+n_6+n_4\ge1$, since $V_4\trianglelefteq A_4$ acts trivially on $T_3\cong A_4/V_4$ and $F_1$).

Keeping a union of entire $H$-orbits yields an $H$-invariant induced subgraph. Because Ramsey-admissibility is hereditary, excluding an orbit tuple excludes every larger tuple dominating it coordinatewise, whereas realizing a subcore only shows it is not a hereditary obstruction. In our SAT encodings, each $H$-orbit on unordered vertex pairs defines one Boolean edge variable, and each $5$-vertex subset contributes clauses forbidding an all-Red $K_5$ or an all-Blue $I_5$. Every exclusion reported as `UNSAT_RUP_VERIFIED` is certified by an independently checked reverse unit propagation (RUP) proof over $100\%$ of canonical symmetry representatives; `UNKNOWN_BUDGET` never closes a branch or family. Every `SAT_VERIFIED` boundary graph is checked directly from its $0\text{–}1$ adjacency matrix for $(K_5,I_5)$-freeness and exact $H$-equivariance.

The four faithful $A_4$ strata, indexed by $n_{12}\ge3$, $n_{12}=2$, $n_{12}=1$, and $n_{12}=0$, attain maximum orders $(37,37,35,34)$. These values compare these four strata. For $C_6$ and $S_3$, we prove necessary lower bounds at orders $42$–$45$ of $n_6\ge4$ and $n_6\ge2$, respectively. Attainment in that order range remains open. We also prove a universal $S_3$ bound of $n_3\le12$ and give a graph attaining it at order $36$.

## 2. Related Work

The previous upper bound $R(5,5)\le48$ used neighbourhood gluing [3]. The exact value $R(4,5)=25$ [4] and the other small Ramsey numbers recorded in [5] give the attachment capacities used below. The $656$ order-$42$ examples catalogued in [6] all have automorphism groups of order at most two. The block-circulant enumeration of Goedgebeur and Van Overberghe is a precedent for imposing a cyclic action in a Ramsey construction [7]. Russell studies rigidity and prescribed automorphisms near the lower-bound frontier [8].

Maksimović and Crnković–Maksimović use automorphisms to enumerate or restrict strongly regular graphs [9], [10]. The rStation studies of order-$45$ conference-graph symmetry and hypothetical Ramsey graph automorphism groups also give related symmetry restrictions [11], [12]. In the general Ramsey setting, prime-order exclusions and a normal Sylow five-subgroup result constrain the group structure [13], [14]. The $A_4$, $S_3$, and $C_6$ results here apply to general $(K_5,I_5)$-free graphs without any strong regularity or regularity assumption. We use the published cyclic $C_6$ census and its low-regular-orbit exclusions [15] for Theorem 1A and the transfer step in Theorem 1B. The cyclic and elementary abelian order-nine exclusions [16], [17] imply $9\nmid|\operatorname{Aut}(G)|$ at orders $42$–$45$, since a group of order divisible by nine has a subgroup isomorphic to $C_9$ or $C_3\times C_3$. The restriction at orders $43$–$45$ also follows from the three-subgroup theorem in [18].

### Symmetric $S_3$

**Theorem 1B (computer-assisted).** Every faithful $S_3$ action on a Ramsey-admissible graph has $n_3\le12$, with equality attained at $36(0,12,0,0)$. At orders $42\le N\le45$, every such action has $n_6\ge2$.

*Short triples.* An orbit of size three is internally monochromatic by the natural action of $S_3$. Write $b_3$ and $r_3$ for the numbers of blue and red triples. Deleting all but thirteen triples reduces any violation to $39(0,13,0,0)$. Global complementation gives seven canonical splits $b_3\ge r_3$ with $b_3+r_3=13$. All seven are refuted, with $2{,}820$ closed cubes across the [initial ledger](supplement/results/s3_pure_hereditary_subcores_rup.tsv) and its continuations. A cube fixes one partial assignment from an exhaustive split of the orbit variables. The [sharp witness](supplement/results/witness_verified_S3_36_0_12_0_0_split_12_0_adj.txt) has twelve blue triples, with $\sigma=(0\,1\,2)$ and $\tau=(1\,2)$ in the local ordering of each triple.

*Transfer and capacity bounds.* Faithfulness is equivalent to $n_6+n_3\ge1$. For $n_6=0$ and $n_2,n_3>0$, every block between a triple and a pair is monochromatic because $S_3$ is transitive on the six cross pairs. Let $V_d$ be the union of all size-$d$ orbits and take $\sigma$ of order three and $\tau$ of order two. The permutation $g=\sigma|_{V_3}\times\tau|_{V_2}\times\mathrm{id}|_{V_1}$ preserves these cross blocks and all other orbit blocks, so it is an order-six automorphism with no regular orbit. Theorem 1.1(1) of [15] excludes it at the four orders. For the remaining $n_6=0$ mixes, fixed points attach uniformly to any given triple. The points attached in its internal colour have capacity $R(2,5)-1=4$, and the rest have capacity $R(4,5)-1=24$, giving $n_1\le28$.

For $n_6\ge1$, label a regular orbit $O_0$ by $\mathbb Z_6$ with $\sigma(x)=x+2$ and $\tau(x)=3-x$ modulo six. The left action gives four raw edge orbits. The six edges with differences $d\in\{2,4\}$ form the cycle orbit. The other three orbits are disjoint reflection matchings of size three, one at $d=3$ and two partitioning the edges with $d\in\{1,5\}$. Right multiplications $x\mapsto xr$ in the regular action commute with left $S_3$ and extend by the identity on all exterior orbits. They fix the cycle orbit and act as the full symmetric group on the reflection matchings. At least two matchings have the same colour, so a right multiplication can put that pair at $d\in\{1,5\}$ and the remaining matching at $d=3$. The three bits of the resulting normal form represent cyclic distances $1,2,3$ in that order. Excluding the monochromatic masks and taking complements leaves $m_0\in\{1,2,3\}$. A swap of the two vertices in $P_2$ commutes with the action and identifies its two mixed attachments. This gives $2,3,8$ canonical attachments on $F_1,P_2,T_3$.

For every disjoint red clique $A$ and blue clique $B$ of $O_0$ with $A\cup B\ne\varnothing$, the exterior vertices red to all of $A$ and blue to all of $B$ have capacity $R(5-|A|,5-|B|)-1$. For each orbit size, enumerate its equivariant attachment blocks and use the number of vertices in that common neighbourhood as the coefficient for its attachment type. For each additional regular orbit use the minimum contribution over all blocks that admit some Ramsey-admissible two-orbit colouring. Exhaustive nonnegative integer multiplicity feasibility for these inequalities gives a necessary condition on each tuple. The [census source](supplement/src/census_s3_minimal_roots.cpp) implements the bounds and the Python verifier regenerates them from the generators.

*Root closures.* Of $2{,}150$ faithful mixes, $631$ have $n_6=0$ and $547$ have $n_6=1$. The transfer and capacity filters leave $757$ [surviving mixes](supplement/results/s3_757_surviving_tuples.tsv) across all sectors with $194$ [minimal roots](supplement/results/s3_194_minimal_roots.tsv), including ten at $n_6=0$ and $37$ at $n_6=1$. Among the ten pure roots, two violate $n_3+n_1\le29$. To see this bound, choose a blue triple by complementing if needed. Its common blue neighbourhood has at most four vertices. Every other triple or singleton that lies outside that neighbourhood contributes at least one red neighbour to any chosen vertex of the blue triple by transitivity on the chosen triple. Its red degree is at most $R(4,5)-1=24$. Counting the chosen triple gives $n_3+n_1\le1+4+24=29$. Two further roots contain thirteen triples, and all canonical splits of the remaining six have recorded RUP refutations.

For $n_6=1$, the [reverified ledger](supplement/results/s3_n6_1_clean_reverified_34roots_rup.tsv) closes all three masks for $34$ roots. Its zero-cube rows record that the orbit and Ramsey constraints leave an empty split. The three remaining roots $42(1,10,0,6)$, $42(1,11,0,3)$, and $42(1,12,0,0)$ have $33$, $36$, and $39$ recorded RUP closures, respectively, over all $(m_0,b_3,r_3)$ branches. **Corollary 2 ($C_6$ Containment for $\{2,3\}$-Subgroups of Order $\ge12$).** Let $K$ be any finite group of order $2^a\cdot3$ with $a\ge2$ ($|K|\ge12$) and $A_4\not\le K$. Then $C_6\le K$. Consequently, if $G$ is Ramsey-admissible of order $42\le N\le45$ and $K\le\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ has order $2^a\cdot3\ge12$ (including each of the four non-$A_4$ groups of order $12$: $C_{12}$, $C_6\times C_2$, $D_6$, and $\operatorname{Dic}_3$), then $K$ contains a cyclic subgroup $C_6$ with $n_6(C_6)\ge4$.

*Proof.* Let $P_2\in\operatorname{Syl}_2(K)$; left translation on the three cosets $K/P_2$ gives a homomorphism $K\to S_3$ whose kernel $N\le P_2$ is a normal $2$-subgroup of order $|N|\ge|K|/6\ge2$. Then $Z=\{z\in Z(N):z^2=1\}\ne\{1\}$ is a nonzero $\mathbb{F}_2$-vector space normal in $K$. For $\langle\sigma\rangle=P_3\in\operatorname{Syl}_3(K)$, conjugation on $Z$ satisfies $(\sigma-1)(\sigma^2+\sigma+1)=\sigma^3-1=0$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$. If $\ker(\sigma-1)=\{1\}$, then $\sigma^2+\sigma+1=0$ on $Z$, so any $z\in Z\setminus\{1\}$ generates $\{1,z,\sigma(z),z\sigma(z)\}\rtimes P_3\cong A_4\le K$, impossible. Thus $\sigma$ centralizes an involution $z\in Z\setminus\{1\}$, giving $\langle z,\sigma\rangle\cong C_6\le K$; Theorems 2 and 1A yield the final claim. $\square$

## 4. Analytical Coset-Fiber Mechanisms Across $A_4$ Strata

### Regular $12$-orbits ($n_{12}\ge1$): bi-triangle tax and Stage-2 obstruction split

**Lemma 1 (Universal Bi-Triangle and Core-Clique Dichotomy).** Every $(K_5,I_5)$-free left-invariant graph on a regular orbit $O\cong A_4$ (`114/114` raw masks, `18/18` canonical cores) contains both a Red $K_3$ and a Blue $K_3$. Moreover, $14$ of the $18$ canonical cores satisfy $\max(\omega,\alpha)=4$, leaving only the four chiral icosahedral/anti-icosahedral cores (canonical indices $6,7,10,11$, masks $\{25,26,29,30\}$) with $(\omega,\alpha)=(3,3)$.

*Proof.* Each $C_3$-orbit $C_3^{(j)\#}$ tiles $O$ by four vertex-disjoint monochromatic triangles, so $1\le t_{\text{red}}\le3$ immediately gives triangles of both colors. If $t_{\text{red}}=0$, all edges between distinct $V_4$-cosets are $3$-cycle edges and hence Blue (forming $K_{4,4,4}$ in Blue); if any involution matching were Blue, each of the three $V_4$-cosets would contain a Blue edge, yielding a blue clique of size six. Hence $t_{\text{red}}=0$ forces $i_{\text{red}}=3$, making each $V_4$-coset a Red $K_4$ (giving Red $K_3$ and $\omega=4$) while $C_3$-cosets are Blue $K_3$; $t_{\text{red}}=4$ follows by complementation. Direct inspection of the $18$ canonical cores confirms $\max(\omega,\alpha)=4$ except on $\{25,26,29,30\}$. $\square$

**Corollary 1 (Sharp Fixed-Point and Two-$T_3$ Attachment Bounds).** Let $G$ be $(K_5,I_5)$-free on $O_0\sqcup E$ with $O_0\cong A_4$ regular.
1. Every fixed point $f\in F_1$ attaches monochromatically to $O_0$, so Lemma 1 forbids both Red $K_2$ in $N_{\text{red}}(O_0)\cap F_1$ and Blue $I_2$ in $N_{\text{blue}}(O_0)\cap F_1$. Since $R(2,5)-1=4$ when the core clique number in color $c$ is $3$ and $R(1,5)-1=0$ when it is $4$, we obtain **$n_1\le8$ universally** (sharp via $20(1,0,0,0,8)$ on $\{25,26,29,30\}$) and **$n_1\le4$ on all other $14$ canonical cores**.
2. Each $T_3\cong A_4/V_4$ has three cross-orbit variables to $O_0$, coloring $V_4$-cosets of size $4$. If $i_{\text{red}}=3$ (every $V_4$-coset is a Red $K_4$), $O_0\times T_3$ must be $100\%$ Blue; with two $3$-orbits $T_3^{(0)},T_3^{(1)}$, a Blue $K_3\subset O_0$ forces $T_3^{(0)}\times T_3^{(1)}$ to be $100\%$ Red, which either completes Blue $I_5$ (if either $T_3^{(r)}$ is Blue $I_3$) or forms Red $K_6$ (if both are Red $K_3$). ### Purely nonregular actions ($n_{12}=0$): normal-$V_4$ kernel collapse on $W=T_3^{n_3}\sqcup F_1^{n_1}$

Because $V_4\trianglelefteq A_4$ is the unique subgroup of index $3$, $V_4$ fixes **every** vertex of $W=T_3^{n_3}\sqcup F_1^{n_1}$ ($|W|=3n_3+n_1$) pointwise. Conversely, $V_4$ acts transitively on every $4$-orbit $Q_4\cong A_4/C_3$ (each internally $K_4$ or $I_4$) and partitions every $6$-orbit $S_6\cong A_4/\langle h_1\rangle$ (internally $3K_2$ or its complement $K_{2,2,2}$) into three $2$-point $V_4$-fibers $P_0,P_1,P_2$ cyclically permuted by $C_3$. Thus every $w\in W$ is monochromatically cone-coupled to each $Q_4$ copy and twin-coupled to each fiber $P_t$.

**Lemma 2 (Normal-$V_4$ Twin-Fiber and Cone Capacity Bounds).** Let $G$ be Ramsey-admissible with $W=T_3^{n_3}\sqcup F_1^{n_1}$ ($|W|=3n_3+n_1$).
1. **(Multi-$Q_4$ Cone Bound.)** Every $w\in W$ attaches all-Blue to each Red-$K_4$ copy of $Q_4$ and all-Red to each Blue-$I_4$ copy. Exhaustive check over $b$ monochromatic-$I_4$ copies of $Q_4$ gives minimum forced Red clique $\omega_{\min}(b)=(0,1,2,3,3,4)$ for $b=(0,1,2,3,4,5)$ (and $\omega_{\min}(b)\ge\omega_{\min}(5)=4$ for $b>5$ by deleting copies), forcing $G[W]$ to be $(K_{5-\omega_{\min}(\min(b,5))},I_{5-\omega_{\min}(\min(r,5))})$-free for some $b+r=n_4$ and hence

   $$
   |W|\le\max_{b+r=n_4}\bigl(R(5-\omega_{\min}(\min(b,5)),\,5-\omega_{\min}(\min(r,5)))-1\bigr)=(24,17,8,5,3,2,1,1)
   $$

   for $n_4=(1,\dots,8)$, using only classical values $R(a,b)$ with $\min(a,b)\le4$ ($R(4,5)=25$, $R(4,4)=18$, $R(3,5)=14$, $R(3,4)=9$, $R(3,3)=6$). Sharp at $n_4=1$: $28(0,0,1,8,0)$ realizes $|W|=24$ ([`witness_verified_A4_28_0_0_1_8_0_adj.txt`](supplement/results/witness_verified_A4_28_0_0_1_8_0_adj.txt)).
2. **(Single-$S_6$ Capacity Bound.)** Suppose $n_6\ge1$, and by complementation take $G[S_6]=3K_2$ (Red fibers $P_0,P_1,P_2$, Blue inter-fiber edges). Partition $W$ by $I(w)=\{t\in\{0,1,2\}:w\text{ is Red to }P_t\}$ and write $n^{(k)}=|\{w\in W:|I(w)|=k\}|$:
   - For $I(w)=\emptyset$, $w$ is Blue to a Blue $I_3\subset S_6$, so $n^{(0)}\le R(5,2)-1=4$.
   - For $I(w)=\{t\}$, $w$ is Red to the Red $K_2$ fiber $P_t$ and Blue to a Blue $I_2$ across the other two fibers, so each of the three $|I(w)|=1$ classes has size $\le R(3,3)-1=5$, giving $n^{(1)}\le15$.
   - Every $w$ with $t\in I(w)$ is Red to $P_t$, capping each $|R_t|\le R(3,5)-1=13$ and hence $n^{(1)}+2n^{(2)}+3n^{(3)}=|R_0|+|R_1|+|R_2|\le39$.
   Combining gives $|W|=n^{(0)}+\frac{1}{2}\bigl(n^{(1)}+(n^{(1)}+2n^{(2)}+2n^{(3)})\bigr)\le4+\lfloor(15+39)/2\rfloor=\mathbf{31}$ (eliminating all $(0,1,0,n_3,n_1)$ for $N\ge38$), while fixed points ($I(f)\in\{\emptyset,\{0,1,2\}\}$) satisfy **$n_1\le4+13=17$** (sharp via $23(0,1,0,0,17)$, [`witness_verified_A4_23_0_1_0_0_17_adj.txt`](supplement/results/witness_verified_A4_23_0_1_0_0_17_adj.txt)).
3. **(Double-$S_6$ Farkas Capacity Bound.)** Suppose $n_6\ge2$. On $S_6^{(0)}\sqcup S_6^{(1)}$ ($12$ vertices, $8$ $A_4$-edge-orbits, six $2$-point $V_4$-fibers), $54$ of the $256$ $A_4$-colorings are $(K_5,I_5)$-free (`11` canonical classes). Deleting any $Q_4$ copies leaves $W$ unchanged; for each valid $S_6^{(0)}\sqcup S_6^{(1)}$ coloring and each disjoint Red $a$-clique $C_R$ and Blue $b$-independent set $C_B$ ($a,b\le3$), the set of $W$-vertices Red to $C_R$ and Blue to $C_B$ has size $\le R(5-a,5-b)-1$. Exact nonnegative rational Farkas dual multipliers across all $54$ valid cores ([`a4_s6_pair_farkas_certificates.tsv`](supplement/results/a4_s6_pair_farkas_certificates.tsv)) certify **$|W|\le22$** universally (eliminating all $(0,2,0,n_3,n_1)$ for $N\ge35$).

## 6. Discussion

The $A_4$ maxima $(37,37,35,34)$ are attained in their respective strata. Once the action has no regular orbit, the normal $V_4$ subgroup controls the exterior through cone and twin-fibre attachments. This comparison is specific to the four $A_4$ strata. It does not establish a general replacement rule for regular and short orbits in arbitrary groups. The necessary lower bounds $n_6\ge4$ for $C_6$ and $n_6\ge2$ for $S_3$ leave attainment at orders $42$–$45$ open.

In the [one-orbit-release pilot](supplement/results/one_orbit_release_pilot.tsv), fixing five of the six original regular orbits of a $36$-vertex $S_3$ or $C_6$ witness and leaving two free gives RUP refutations for all twelve choices. These are restrictions on extensions of those fixed cores. The [bounded runs with seven unrestricted regular orbits](supplement/results/pure_42_7_30s.tsv) remained `UNKNOWN_BUDGET`. For a graph at orders $42$–$45$, Corollary 2 forces a $C_6$ subgroup in every automorphism subgroup of order $2^a\cdot3$ with $a\ge2$.
