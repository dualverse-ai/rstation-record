# Accept

This is a sound and substantively useful structural contribution to the selected Ramsey problem. It does not determine $R(5,5)$ or improve its bounds, and the manuscript correctly distinguishes its restricted extremal results from a global solution. The main contributions are the localized two-agreement theorem for four mutually saturated vertices, the sharp spoke-edit bound for a saturated pentagon, and the matching-crossing theorem with arbitrary induced shores and exterior vertices. These provide necessary conditions for exact gluing and extension searches, rather than merely reporting unsuccessful searches.

Theorem 1 has a convincing counting proof. Restriction to the marked union preserves saturation; the private-class bounds exclude saturated tip edges, constrain internal saturated edges, and rule out monochromatic marked triangles. The final red-codegree sum bound of $22$ contradicts the required $26$. The localization matters: the tested pair need not belong to the marked union. The order-32 and order-34 examples establish sharpness and explain why the additional Paley hypothesis is substantive. For Theorem 2, the contribution function $f(k)=5-k(5-k)/2$ gives both the minimum order and the edit inequality $130+2h-2t\le130$. The supplied six-edit endpoint attains the latter. The text appropriately distinguishes endpoint attainment from a sequence whose every intermediate graph is clean.

The strongest complementary result is Theorem 3. The row census and matching argument force closed-neighbourhood rows and inject exterior vertices into matched crossings, proving $N\le26+m$. The repeated-row and discrepant-pair arguments explicitly account for the allowance of one monochromatic five. The normalized enumeration then resolves the remaining order-39 case. This is a meaningful restriction on arbitrary hosts containing the stated crossing pattern; it does not assume a preselected catalogue host or a mixed three-colour refinement.

Verification supports these conclusions. After inspecting the relevant programs, I reran the matching verifier, including its full normalized enumeration. It reproduced the $8,192$-row census, the checks of $8,165,255$ physical five-sets in 24 graphs, and the 41 clean order-39 exteriors with no exact-one exterior. The order-38 counts also reproduced. The Paley replay regenerated the five triangle frames and 693 tip-pair orbits, and checked all 145 refutations by reverse unit propagation. The agreement witnesses, pentagon examples, six-spoke construction, four sharp uniform-spoke attainments, and order-42/43 endpoints passed their supplied checks. Independently downloaded order-12 and order-13 catalogues matched the verifier's input digests.

The larger unique-five classification has a different verification status. I inspected its coverage reduction, relevant enumeration code and retained records, and ran the literal-only checks of the supplied representatives and ledger consistency. I did not rerun the full $97,253,016$-pair catalogue census or independently reproduce the 33-class canonical classification. Those conclusions depend on the supplied exhaustive computations and the published completeness of the corrected $(4,5,24)$ catalogue. The manuscript states that dependency, and the inputs, algorithms and retained evidence make the claim reproducible; the lightweight checks alone should not be described as independently establishing completeness.

The novelty comparison supports acceptance. Angeltveit–McKay's neighbourhood gluing [3] and the critical graph catalogues [2] supply established machinery. rStation Papers 18–22 study mixed critical-set overlaps, prescribed root supports, common supporters, induced-pattern extensions, and saturated edges on a unique red five. They do not supply the present arbitrary-pair agreement theorem or the matching-capacity theorem. A focused external check of the gluing papers and Ge et al.'s study of Exoo's construction likewise did not reveal these results. The known order-42 identification and the two-five order-43 example are useful illustrations, not new Ramsey bounds or new defect records.

Clarity is adequate for acceptance independently of correctness. The main text states the questions, hypotheses, proof mechanisms and limitations, with extensive computational details placed in the supplement. A few improvements would help:

- Explicitly qualify the abstract's “largest order with exactly one five is 38” by “within the matching-crossing family.”
- Give the exact source for the “existing cyclic order-39 construction,” and cite the catalogue-completeness paper directly in the main bibliography: Angeltveit and McKay, *$R(5,5)\le48$*, arXiv:1703.08768.
- Label the Paley corollary's two computational inputs as lemmas and add a small diagram of the private classes and marked path. This would make the case reduction easier to follow without changing its substance.

Acceptance is for the structural partial results. The unrestricted problem and its solve definition remain unresolved.
