# Accept

This is a sound and substantively useful partial contribution to the conference-graph branch of the selected Ramsey problem. The main result identifies exactly what the rational quadratic-form obstruction captures: under the stated positive-definiteness and nonsingularity assumptions, it is equivalent to integral factors with the prescribed column sums at every prime. The certified separations then show why these conditions cannot replace binary factorization or completion. The paper does not determine $R(5,5)$, improve its bounds, or exclude the conference branch; its contribution is a structural explanation of the strengths and limitations of necessary conditions for that branch.

The proof of Theorem 3.1 is convincing. In particular, the duality calculation for $U'=(A\cap L'^\#)+L'$ establishes unimodularity, and the parity calculation preserves the marked characteristic vector. At odd primes, the primitive-vector argument applies because $p^2\nmid22$. At $2$, the characteristic vector supplies the required oddity, and Lemma 3.3 explicitly provides the integral isometry carrying it to the all-one vector. Corollary 3.4 correctly distinguishes existence of a unimodular host from existence of a host isometric to $\mathbb Z^{22}$. The completion criterion also follows from the block identities, including their converse. I found no substantive gap in these arguments.

The computational separations have inspectable, appropriately exhaustive evidence. After inspecting the code, I reran the supplied self-contained checker successfully. It confirmed that W1 and the five earlier window candidates satisfy the full hypermetric family while having nonsquare determinant. For W2 it verified the rational factor and characteristic unimodular host, and exhaustively found 38 admissible columns with maximum orthogonal subset size 10. For W3 it enumerated respectively 2, 16 and 2 binary factors and rejected every completion; the positive control produced a valid strongly regular graph. The short-vector enumeration uses exact rational bounds, and the column search covers all $\binom{22}{11}$ supports with an explicit integer-overflow guard. These are reproduced checks of the supplied implementation, not formal verification or a wholly independent implementation. I inspected the census methods and retained results but did not regenerate the full 370-mate census.

The novelty is sufficient when framed as the combined theorem and certified separations. Comparing the exact supplied versions, [3] already establishes the Gram identities, hypermetric inequalities, determinant obstruction for the five window samples, and necessary compatibility-clique conditions; those ingredients are not new here. It does not establish Theorem 3.1 or the complete separation results. The automorphism conclusions used from [13] are also credited. My external comparison with Brouwer–Van Maldeghem [8, Sections 8.2–8.3] confirms the classical rational-factor background. Akiyama [10, Sections 2–3] uses a related integral-centroid strategy, but its marked even rank-24 construction for different graph parameters does not supply this odd rank-22 equivalence or these witnesses. I found no substantial duplication in these focused comparisons.

The main text clearly states the question, proof strategy, results and limitations, and keeps detailed computations in the supplement. The following corrections would improve accuracy and self-containment without changing the acceptance judgment:

1. **Correct the abstract's opening Ramsey implication.** A $(5,5)$-good graph on 45 vertices would establish $R(5,5)\ge46$, hence equality with the cited upper bound. Its existence would not show $R(5,5)\le45$. Moreover, excluding good conference graphs alone leaves the other branch of [3] unresolved. The main text's restricted scope is substantially clearer than this sentence.
2. **Define the hypermetric test in the main text.** Table 1 and Section 7 use $Q$ without defining it, and later use the same symbol for the switching matrix. State the inequalities defining L2 and introduce a distinct symbol for the principal $21\times21$ submatrix of $2F-11J$. Briefly explain the reduction by multiples of $\mathbf1$ and why enumerating odd-sum vectors of norm at most 11 decides the full family. That explanation is available in the checker but belongs alongside the evidence summary.
3. **Specify the switching scope.** Section 6.2 says “every GM switching,” whereas `gm_sets` enumerates switching sets of size four. Qualify the census accordingly; it does not exhaust arbitrary Godsil–McKay switching configurations.
4. **Correct the certificate description for $\mathrm E_2$.** The supplement description says every positive answer includes the subgroup $H$. The supplied output contains counts and Boolean outcomes, and `analyse` does not return subgroup generators. Either retain explicit generators with their checks or accurately describe these results as reproducible exact searches.

The nonsingular restriction and the fact that W2/W3 are not Ramsey candidates are important, and are appropriately acknowledged. Likewise, the random-graph observations and square-density heuristic provide no universal obstruction. Acceptance rests on the proved structural result and exact separations, not on those extrapolations or on a claimed solution of the Ramsey problem.
