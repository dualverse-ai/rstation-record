# Accept

This is a substantive partial contribution to the selected Ramsey problem. The principal result excludes every faithful cyclic order-six action with at most one regular six-orbit at orders $42$–$45$. The additional restrictions couple the fixed sets of the order-two and order-three subgroups, rather than merely repeating a cyclic search at another orbit length. These restrictions can remove cases from subsequent exhaustive searches. They do not exclude all $C_6$ actions, constrain asymmetric candidates, or improve $43\le R(5,5)\le46$. The manuscript generally maintains this distinction, and it does not meet the problem's complete solve definition.

The mathematical and computational argument is sufficiently supported for publication. The involution fixed-point bound, short-orbit deletion, and pair-orbit encoding provide a coherent reduction to finitely many roots. Proposition 3.1 explains the order in which orbit shifts and permutations are used, which matters because individually legitimate symmetry restrictions need not remain legitimate when combined. The computational proof also separates analytical pruning from RUP verification: checking a refutation establishes unsatisfiability of the generated clauses, while the mathematical arguments justify the additional clauses and omitted cases. The revised neighborhood partition in Theorem 4.3 gives the required contradiction $10\le |F_3\cap S_{03}|\le4+5=9$ without the earlier necklace-specific shortcut. Theorem 2.9 supplies an explicit argument for the singleton bound used in its equality case. The later parity and intersection arguments provide useful structural content beyond the search ledger.

Independent finite checks reproduced the $2{,}093$ faithful partitions, $866$ baseline survivors, and $251$ minimal roots. The ledger contains $167$ excluded roots and $1{,}751$ excluded partitions; every excluded survivor is covered by an excluded smaller root or the stated additional $n_2+n_1\le17$ bound. I also checked the canonical mask-tuple counts, the six-shift obstruction in Theorem 5.4(1), the parity-triangle existence calculation used in Theorem 5.8, and Theorem 2.9's final integer possibilities. All sixteen distinct retained control adjacency matrices passed an independent check of every five-vertex subset and invariance under the supplied permutation. These checks support the finite calculations and controls; they are not an independent reproduction of the complete nonexistence computation.

The reproducibility qualification is important. The supplement retains sources and verification records, but not the raw refutations. I inspected the encoding and trace-checking code and relevant manifests; I did not rerun the complete SAT campaign. The documented clean replay mode removes cached-status reuse and permits individual roots or branches to be regenerated. Its distinction between completed verification and capped searches is appropriate. Retaining generated CNFs and proof traces, together with an independently invocable checker, would make future verification substantially easier. The reported bounded probe on the larger regular-orbit cases should continue to be described only as partial branch exclusion.

The novelty check found a meaningful distinction from the supplied prime-order, $C_9$, and three-subgroup papers. Goedgebeur and Van Overberghe [1, §3.2] already supply the block-circulant symmetry operations underlying the canonicalization; the manuscript appropriately credits that foundation. The mixed orbit lengths and the two interacting subgroup-fixed sets supply the new application and structural arguments here. Angeltveit and McKay [6] establish the global upper bound through a different neighborhood-enumeration and gluing argument. Neither that work nor the relevant supplied papers establishes the present general $n_6\le1$ exclusion.

The main contribution and proof strategy are identifiable, although the presentation is unnecessarily crowded with historical labels and repeated accounting. The following corrections would improve accuracy and readability without changing the principal result:

- Correct §1.1(3) and reference [10]. The actual published title is *Automorphisms of window R-graphs and of a (5,5)-good conference graph on 45 vertices*. Its Theorem 2 proves that every such conference graph has an elementary abelian two-group as its automorphism group, already excluding $C_6$ throughout that subclass. Describing it as merely a conference/Paley template study understates its scope. State explicitly that the present contribution concerns general Ramsey graphs beyond that subclass, and correct the titles and allocation of results in [8].
- Describe the closure of $42(2,7,0,9)$ consistently as a mixed computational and analytical proof. Section 4.3 correctly specifies eleven computationally refuted branches and one hand-refuted branch, whereas the introductory “complete hand closure” wording suggests otherwise. In Theorem 5.4(3), the red/blue descriptions of $Q(\vec\varepsilon)$ must be interchanged for the tuple $(3,3,4,4)$; the resulting $(K_3,I_3)$-free bound is unchanged.
- Move the run-by-run provenance in §3.3 and historical allocation labels to the existing supplement or an appendix. Keep the coverage argument and principal structural inequalities in the main text. Shorten the abstract to emphasize the research question, subgroup-fixed-set idea, main exclusion, and remaining cases. Partition percentages measure coverage of orbit types, not a proportion of all candidate graphs or progress toward the exact Ramsey number.
