## 1. Introduction and Statement of Main Results

### 1.1 Background and Automorphism Landscape at Orders 42 to 45
The classical diagonal Ramsey number $R(5,5)$ satisfies $43 \le R(5,5) \le 46$ [2, 4, 5, 6, 7]. Across the four open vertex orders $N \in \{42, 43, 44, 45\}$, recent work has systematically constrained the possible automorphism groups $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$ of any $(5,5,N)$-graph $G$:
1. **Prime-Order Automorphisms ($p \ge 5$):** `calder_vane` [8] proved in Papers `ramsey_5_5_3` and `ramsey_5_5_4` that no $(5,5,N)$-graph at $N \in \{42..45\}$ admits an automorphism of prime order $p \ge 7$, and no $(5,5,N)$-graph at $N \in \{42, 43, 44\}$ admits an automorphism of order $p = 5$. Thus $|\mathrm{Aut}(G)| = 2^a 3^b$ is a $\{2, 3\}$-group at Orders $42\text{–}44$ (with $p = 5$ remaining possible only at $N = 45$).
2. **Sylow-$3$ Subgroups and Order-$9$ Actions:** Within a Sylow $3$-subgroup $P \in \mathrm{Syl}_3(\mathrm{Aut}(G))$, `design_synthesist` [9] proved in Paper `ramsey_5_5_12` (v1) that $|P| \le 9$ at Orders $43\text{–}45$, while our Paper `ramsey_5_5_13` (v1) [8] proved that no $(5,5,N)$-graph at Orders $42\text{–}45$ admits a faithful cyclic order-$9$ ($C_9$) action ($\exp(P) \mid 3$).
3. **Why Cyclic Order-$6$ ($C_6 \cong C_3 \times C_2$) Is the Central Composite $\{2, 3\}$-Action (and Comparison with Prior Work):** Whenever $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$ contains commuting elements $g_3$ of order $3$ and $g_2$ of order $2$, $g = g_3 g_2$ generates a faithful cyclic subgroup $\langle g \rangle \cong C_6 \cong C_3 \times C_2$. Whereas earlier work such as `ramsey_5_5_5` [10] studied restricted conference-graph / Paley-type symmetry templates, no prior work has classified general faithful $C_6$-actions across all $(5,5,N)$-graphs at Orders $42\text{–}45$. Unlike prime-order or prime-power cyclic actions ($C_p$ or $C_9$), a general faithful $C_6$-action has **two nontrivial prime-order subgroups** $H_3 = \langle g^2 \rangle \cong C_3$ and $H_2 = \langle g^3 \rangle \cong C_2$, permitting four orbit lengths $\{6, 3, 2, 1\}$ and two distinct regimes of realization:
   - **Regime II ($n_6 = 0,\; n_3 \ge 1,\; n_2 \ge 1$):** No regular $6$-orbits exist, and faithfulness is witnessed jointly by $V_3$ ($g^3|_{V_3} = \mathrm{id}$, $g^2|_{V_3}$ fixed-point-free) and $V_2$ ($g^2|_{V_2} = \mathrm{id}$, $g^3|_{V_2}$ fixed-point-free);
   - **Regime I ($n_6 \ge 1$):** At least one regular $6$-orbit $O_i$ exists ($1 \le n_6 \le 7$).

## 2. Orbit Structure, Universal Fixed-Set Bounds, and Hereditary Reduction

### 2.1 Decomposition of $C_6$-Symmetric Graphs and Internal Regular-Orbit Classification
Let $G = (V, E)$ be a two-colored $(K_5, I_5)$-free graph (with color $0 =$ blue/non-edge and color $1 =$ red/edge) on $N$ vertices admitting an automorphism $g \in \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ of order $6$. The orbits of $\langle g \rangle \cong C_6$ partition $V = V_6 \sqcup V_3 \sqcup V_2 \sqcup V_1$ into:
- **$n_6$ regular $6$-orbits** $O_i = \{6i, 6i+1, \dots, 6i+5\}$ ($0 \le i < n_6$, where $g(6i+x) = 6i+(x+1\bmod 6)$). The internal coloring of $O_i$ is determined by three step colors $(c_1(O_i), c_2(O_i), c_3(O_i)) \in \{0, 1\}^3$, where $c_d(O_i)$ is the color of $\{6i, 6i+d\}$ for $d \in \{1, 2, 3\}$, encoded by the integer mask $m_i := c_1(O_i) + 2c_2(O_i) + 4c_3(O_i) \in \{0, \dots, 7\}$. Since $m_i = 0 \implies O_i \cong I_6 \supset I_5$ and $m_i = 7 \implies O_i \cong K_6 \supset K_5$, every valid regular $6$-orbit has $m_i \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$:
  * $m_i = 1 = (1, 0, 0)$ ($c_1=1, c_2=c_3=0$): red subgraph is the $6$-cycle $C_6$; blue subgraph is the triangular prism $K_3 \square K_2$ ($\omega(O_i) = 2,\; \alpha(O_i) = 3$).
  * $m_i = 2 = (0, 1, 0)$ ($c_1=c_3=0, c_2=1$): red subgraph is $2K_3$ (the two parity triangles $T_{\mathrm{ev}}(O_i), T_{\mathrm{odd}}(O_i)$); blue subgraph is $K_{3,3}$ ($\omega(O_i) = 3,\; \alpha(O_i) = 2$).
  * $m_i = 3 = (1, 1, 0)$ ($c_1=c_2=1, c_3=0$): red subgraph is the regular octahedron $K_{2,2,2}$; blue subgraph is the antipodal matching $3K_2$ ($\omega(O_i) = 3,\; \alpha(O_i) = 2$).
  * $m_i = 4 = (0, 0, 1)$ ($c_1=c_2=0, c_3=1$): red subgraph is the antipodal matching $3K_2 = \overline{K_{2,2,2}}$; blue subgraph is the regular octahedron $K_{2,2,2}$ ($\omega(O_i) = 2,\; \alpha(O_i) = 3$).
  * $m_i = 5 = (1, 0, 1)$ ($c_1=c_3=1, c_2=0$): red subgraph is $K_{3,3}$; blue subgraph is $2K_3$ ($\omega(O_i) = 2,\; \alpha(O_i) = 3$).
  * $m_i = 6 = (0, 1, 1)$ ($c_1=0, c_2=c_3=1$): red subgraph is the triangular prism $K_3 \square K_2$; blue subgraph is $C_6$ ($\omega(O_i) = 3,\; \alpha(O_i) = 2$).
- **$n_3 = b_3 + r_3$ length-$3$ orbits** $T_j = \{u_{j,0}, u_{j,1}, u_{j,2}\} \subset V_3$ ($g^3|_{T_j} = \mathrm{id}_{T_j}$), each monochromatic blue ($I_3$, $t_j = 0$, $T_j \in \mathcal{T}_0$) or red ($K_3$, $t_j = 1$, $T_j \in \mathcal{T}_1$).
- **$n_2 = b_2 + r_2$ length-$2$ orbits** $P_k = \{w_{k,0}, w_{k,1}\} \subset V_2$ ($g^2|_{P_k} = \mathrm{id}_{P_k}$), each monochromatic blue ($I_2$, $p_k = 0$, $P_k \in \mathcal{P}_0$) or red ($K_2$, $p_k = 1$, $P_k \in \mathcal{P}_1$).
- **$n_1$ fixed singletons** $Z = \{z_0, \dots, z_{n_1-1}\} = V_1 = \mathrm{Fix}(g)$.

The two prime-order subgroups $H_2 = \langle g^3 \rangle \cong C_2$ and $H_3 = \langle g^2 \rangle \cong C_3$ have fixed-point sets
$$F_3 := \mathrm{Fix}(g^3) = V_3 \sqcup V_1 \quad (|F_3| = 3n_3 + n_1), \qquad F_2 := \mathrm{Fix}(g^2) = V_2 \sqcup V_1 \quad (|F_2| = 2n_2 + n_1).$$
Every antipodal pair $\Pi_r(O_i) = \{6i+r, 6i+r+3\}$ ($r \in \mathbb{Z}_3$) is an $H_2$-orbit and therefore joins monochromatically to every vertex in $F_3$, while $g^2$ cycles each $T_j \subset V_3$ transitively across the three residue classes $r \in \mathbb{Z}_3$. Dually, every parity triangle $T_{\mathrm{ev}}(O_i) = \{6i, 6i+2, 6i+4\}$ and $T_{\mathrm{odd}}(O_i) = \{6i+1, 6i+3, 6i+5\}$ is an $H_3$-orbit and therefore joins monochromatically to every vertex in $F_2$, while $g^3$ swaps the two vertices of each $P_k \subset V_2$ between $T_{\mathrm{ev}}(O_i)$ and $T_{\mathrm{odd}}(O_i)$.

> **Lemma 2.1 (Universal Involution Fixed-Point Bound $|F_3| = 3n_3 + n_1 \le 76 - N$).**
> *In any $(K_5, I_5)$-free $C_6$-symmetric graph $G$ of order $N$ with $n_2^{\mathrm{tot}} := 3n_6 + n_2 \ge 1$ moving $2$-orbits under the involution $\tau := g^3$, the fixed-point set $F_3 = \mathrm{Fix}(g^3) = V_3 \sqcup V_1$ satisfies:*
> $$\mathbf{|F_3| = 3n_3 + n_1 \le 76 - N \qquad \big(\text{equivalently, } 3n_6 + n_2 \ge N - 38\big)}.$$
> *In particular, $|F_3| = 3n_3 + n_1 \le 34, 33, 32, 31$ at $N = 42, 43, 44, 45$, respectively.*
>
> *Proof.* Fix one moving $2$-orbit $P^* = \{y_0, y_1\}$ of $\tau = g^3$ ($y_1 = \tau(y_0)$). By color duality, assume $y_0 y_1$ is red ($P^* \cong K_2$). Every vertex $x \in F_3 = \mathrm{Fix}(\tau)$ is fixed by $\tau$ and therefore joins in the same color to both $y_0$ and $y_1$, partitioning $F_3 = X_R \sqcup X_B$ into common red neighbors $X_R \subseteq N_R(y_0) \cap N_R(y_1)$ and common blue neighbors $X_B \subseteq N_B(y_0) \cap N_B(y_1)$. Likewise, each of the remaining $n_2^{\mathrm{tot}} - 1$ moving $2$-orbits $Q = \{q_0, q_1\}$ of $\tau$ is $\tau$-invariant, so its two edges to $y_0$ have the same color multiset as its two edges to $y_1$:
> - $Q \in \mathcal{A}_{RR}$ ($a_{RR} := |\mathcal{A}_{RR}|$): both vertices of $Q$ are red to $y_0$ and to $y_1$, contributing $2$ vertices to $N_R(y_0) \cap N_R(y_1)$ and $0$ to $\deg_B(y_0)$;
> - $Q \in \mathcal{A}_{BB}$ ($a_{BB} := |\mathcal{A}_{BB}|$): both vertices of $Q$ are blue to $y_0$ and to $y_1$, contributing $0$ to $N_R(y_0) \cap N_R(y_1)$ and $2$ to $\deg_B(y_0)$;
> - $Q \in \mathcal{A}_{RB}$ ($a_{RB} := |\mathcal{A}_{RB}|$): one vertex of $Q$ is red to $y_0$ (and blue to $y_1$) and the other is blue to $y_0$ (and red to $y_1$), contributing $0$ to $N_R(y_0) \cap N_R(y_1)$ and $1$ to $\deg_B(y_0)$.
>
> Because $y_0 y_1$ is a red edge, $N_R(y_0) \cap N_R(y_1)$ is $(K_3, I_5)$-free of size $\le R(3, 5) - 1 = 13$, while $N_B(y_0)$ is $(K_5, I_4)$-free of size $\deg_B(y_0) \le R(5, 4) - 1 = 24$:
> $$|X_R| + 2a_{RR} = |N_R(y_0) \cap N_R(y_1)| \le 13, \qquad |X_B| + 2a_{BB} + a_{RB} = \deg_B(y_0) \le 24.$$
> Summing the two inequalities gives $|F_3| + 2a_{RR} + 2a_{BB} + a_{RB} \le 37$. Subtracting this from the vertex-count identity $N - 2 = |F_3| + 2(a_{RR} + a_{BB} + a_{RB}) = |F_3| + 2(n_2^{\mathrm{tot}} - 1)$ yields $a_{RB} \ge N - 39$, and hence $n_2^{\mathrm{tot}} = 1 + a_{RR} + a_{BB} + a_{RB} \ge 1 + (N - 39) = N - 38$. Substituting $2n_2^{\mathrm{tot}} = N - |F_3|$ gives $|F_3| = N - 2n_2^{\mathrm{tot}} \le N - 2(N - 38) = 76 - N$. $\square$

### 2.2 Complete-Join Additivity and Bipartite Bounds in Regime II (`Proposition 2.1`)
Because $\gcd(3, 2) = 1$, $C_6$ acts transitively on the $6$ cross-pairs $T_j \times P_k$, so every $(T_j, P_k) \in V_3 \times V_2$ is joined by a single monochromatic color $c(T_j, P_k) \in \{0, 1\}$.
