# Accept

This is a sound structural advance for the selected Ramsey problem. Subject to the explicitly identified published inputs, the manuscript proves that every hypothetical $(5,5)$-good graph on $45$ vertices has solvable automorphism group, with order $2^a3^b5^\epsilon$, $\epsilon\leq1$, and determines the quotient by $O_{5'}(A)$ when five divides the order. It does not determine $R(5,5)$ or improve its bounds. Its significance is the exclusion of all nonsolvable symmetry, including intransitive actions and nonsplit extensions, rather than just another prescribed cycle type.

The central proof is convincing. Fixed-point deletion and the order-$44$ exclusion make every five-element fixed-point-free and force Sylow five-subgroups to have order at most five. Passing to a minimal nonsolvable subgroup correctly produces a perfect group with nilpotent Frattini kernel and quotient $A_5$; it does not assume that an $A_5$ section splits. The odd-orbit reduction to sizes $5$, $15$ and $45$ is exhaustive. The coordinate-projection arguments handle nontrivial kernels on the smaller orbits and on blocks of three. In the blocks-of-nine case, the degree restrictions force cross-degrees four or five; these force faithful kernel action on each block. The subsequent bound on the Frattini quotient, exclusion of order-five automorphisms of the kernel, and use of the nongenerator property correctly yield the central-action contradiction. Section 5 also justifies the quotient conclusion: the odd number of pentagon orbits rules out multipliers two and three, and the Frattini argument transfers that restriction to $A/O_{5'}(A)$.

I independently checked the fifteen-point orbital calculation with a small exact permutation enumeration. The unordered pair orbits have sizes $15,30,30,30$; all six displayed products agree with the table, and every one of the $16$ invariant two-colorings has one of the stated monochromatic five-sets. The four displayed degree triples also check. These checks support the explicit finite calculations, while the extension arguments were assessed as mathematical proofs. I did not rerun the imported Ramsey computations or rederive Herzog's classification. The classification list is stated in the cited Lima–Pereira source [6], and the two Ramsey exclusions used here match the statements in Calder [3]. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes those dependencies from its new proof.

The novelty is adequately supported by the closest comparisons. Calder [3] establishes solvability at orders $42$–$44$, but leaves the order-$45$ three-prime case. Goedgebeur–Van Overberghe [11, Sections 3.3–3.4] already cover vertex-transitive graphs through order $47$; their result does not itself exclude intransitive nonsolvable actions. Russell [10, Section 4] lists narrower prescribed-automorphism exclusions and does not supply this theorem. The present treatment of extensions and intransitive actions, together with its direct transitive argument and normalizer consequence, constitutes a coherent contribution beyond those results.

Scientific communication is sufficiently clear for acceptance. The main theorem, imported assumptions, proof strategy, decisive arguments and unresolved cases are all accessible in the main text. The abstract is informative, notation is consistent, and the limitations are unusually precise: solvability alone supplies neither a demonstrated search speedup nor an exclusion of free order-five symmetry. No new computational certificate is needed for the added natural-language proof.

Two modest improvements would strengthen the presentation:

- Add the relevant comparison with [12], which already proves that a hypothetical good conference graph $\mathrm{srg}(45,22,10,11)$ has an elementary abelian two-group of automorphisms fixing a vertex. That result is stronger within its restricted class; the present theorem applies without the conference assumption. This omission does not undermine the new general conclusion.
- At the start of Section 4, give a short roadmap distinguishing the odd-orbit reduction from the two transitive block cases. The argument is complete, but this would help readers track why the cases exhaust all nonsolvable actions.
