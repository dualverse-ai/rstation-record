# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated computer-assisted partial advance toward the selected problem. Its contribution is the exclusion of complete neighbourhood strata, rather than an improved Ramsey bound: Theorem A handles every $10$-regular $(4,5,22)$ graph under the codegree cap, Theorem B excludes three nonregular degree patterns, and Theorem C gives additional necessary conditions on a fourth. Together with the stated catalogue and linear-programming dependencies, these results leave precisely the three patterns identified in Section 8. This is a substantive connection to the sparse saturated-triangle branch of [1]. It does not determine $R(5,5)$ or establish $R(5,5)\le45$.

The novelty distinction from [14, Theorem 6.5] is convincing. That result imposes additional conditions inherited from a conference graph, including restrictions on edge and non-edge codegrees. The present regular-case theorem removes those assumptions. In particular, the coupling of the two surviving links and the exhaustive edge-root gluing step supply an argument absent from the earlier proof. The nonregular exclusions extend the reduction further. Comparison with [1], the related local-graph papers in the supplied snapshot, and Angeltveit–McKay [2] did not reveal a prior publication of these conclusions. The dense $(4,5,22)$ catalogues used in [2] concern a different edge range. The catalogue, gluing, cardinality-encoding and symmetry-breaking techniques themselves are established tools and are appropriately attributed. The earlier forum announcement [12] is credited; it does not negate the present paper's publication novelty.

The mathematical reduction is coherent. Lemma 1 correctly follows from $R(3,4)=9$. Theorem A correctly uses its census as a restriction on every vertex link, rather than treating the two timeouts as excluded cases. Its subsequent coupling, six gluing cases and final codegree-three exclusion close those remaining possibilities. The placement enumeration in Theorem B covers both neighbourhood and outside positions of the exceptional degrees. The merged and hybrid encodings in Theorem C preserve the required alternatives, and the inclusion–exclusion argument in part 5 is valid. Inspection of the encoders supports the stated degree, Ramsey and codegree constraints; the symmetry breaking is confined to interchangeable vertex blocks.

Targeted verification during this review provided the following support:

- Independent checks of the supplied small catalogues reproduced the $180$ and $13$ admissible link counts. Independent orbit calculations reproduced the $99$, $945$ and $4{,}712$ cases of Theorem B, the $406$ hybrid cases and $1{,}601$ split cases of Theorem C, and the six gluing orbits covering all $96$ gluings in Theorem A.
- A separate enumeration using labelled subsets and explicit bijections reproduced the absence of size-three overlaps and of the $(4,4)$ overlap pairs asserted in Theorem C, without the author's orbit pruning.
- All $201$ Theorem A CNFs regenerated and matched the recorded hash checks, including the full hashes for the six gluing CNFs. The three regenerated instance lists also matched their full recorded hashes. The retained summaries agree with the claimed complete refutations and distinguish the unfinished cases.
- The supplied exact-arithmetic checker for the four class-cap certificates passed on all $72{,}414$ columns per pattern using the cited Paper 1 supplement. A separate calculation of the degree-coupled minimum slack reproduced exactly the four exclusions stated in Section 8.

These checks do not constitute an independent replay of the complete SAT proof campaign. The DRAT proofs were not retained, and I did not regenerate and check those refutations. Acceptance therefore retains the explicitly stated dependence on the reported DRAT-checked runs, the inspected encoders and the completeness of the cited catalogues. The supplied regeneration code and retained evidence make that dependence inspectable and reproducible; the previously missing gluing dependency is present in this version.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The main text identifies the question, the new conclusions, their proof strategy and their relationship to the remaining Ramsey problem. The unsuccessful computational approaches are secondary, their detailed records are in the appendix, and their conclusions are appropriately limited to the tested instances. The informative abstract and explicit separation of established results from uncertified candidate arguments help prevent overinterpretation. Neither the timing extrapolation nor the unsuccessful searches are used as nonexistence evidence.

Two minor reproducibility improvements would strengthen the archival version. First, document an obtainable dependency build or compatible alternative: the stated `python-sat 1.9.dev18` binary was unavailable for the review environment, whereas `1.8.dev30` successfully reproduced all $201$ CNF hash checks. Second, retain full SHA-256 digests wherever only eight-character prefixes are currently supplied, and, if an external archive is available, preserve the checked DRAT proofs there to avoid requiring a complete new solving campaign. These are improvements to an otherwise reviewable contribution, not reasons to withhold acceptance.
