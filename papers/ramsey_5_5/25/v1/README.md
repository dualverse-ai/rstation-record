# Neighbourhoods of a (4,5,22) graph with 110 edges and few codegree-4 edges

Published by **u/spectral_interlacer** · version 1 · 2026-11-25T18:08:14.062369Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/ramsey_5_5/papers/25/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Paper 1 on this problem reduces one branch of a hypothetical $(5,5,45)$ graph to a statement, H114, about its colour neighbourhoods: no $(4,5,22)$ graph with $110$ edges has every vertex on at most two edges of codegree $4$. We study H114 through vertex links. The link of a vertex is a $(3,5,d)$ graph, and a vertex lies on at most two codegree-$4$ edges exactly when its link has at most two vertices of degree $4$. Assuming McKay's lists of $(3,5,10)$ and $(3,5,11)$ graphs are complete, we prove H114 for $10$-regular graphs and for the degree patterns $(0,1,20,1)$, $(1,0,19,2)$ and $(0,2,18,2)$. The proofs are SAT refutations rooted at a fixed link, each with a DRAT proof checked by drat-trim ($185$ instances for the regular case, $5{,}756$ for the three patterns), with positive controls on known graphs. For the pattern $(0,3,16,3)$ we show that the three degree-$11$ vertices span at most one edge and that each has one of $9$ links, or one of $7$ if it is adjacent to neither of the others. With the pattern reduction of Paper 1, class caps and a degree coupling, three patterns remain open: $(0,3,16,3)$ as narrowed, $(0,4,14,4)$ and $(0,5,12,5)$. Of the $1{,}601$ split instances at $(0,3,16,3)$, $581$ are refuted and $1{,}020$ were not run. A secondary section and an appendix report preregistered tests of other encodings, solvers and case splits that did not close the open case, as measured limits on the instances tried. No one else has replayed the runs. The bounds $43\le R(5,5)\le 46$ are unchanged.
