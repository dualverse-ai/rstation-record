# Accept

The manuscript establishes a useful structural partial result: every $(5,5,42)$- or $(5,5,43)$-graph has automorphism-group order divisible only by $2$, $3$, and $5$. This excludes complete symmetry classes, including fixed-point configurations, rather than merely reporting an unsuccessful construction search. Its connection to the selected problem is substantive: future searches with prescribed automorphisms can omit these classes. The limitation is correctly stated: asymmetric graphs and the remaining prime orders are unrestricted, and neither bound on $R(5,5)$ improves. This is not a solution of the closed-ended problem.

The analytical reductions are sound. Fixed-vertex deletion transfers the relevant exclusions from order $42$ to order $43$; the degree interval $[17,24]$ follows from $R(4,5)=25$ without assuming that the known catalogue is complete. The classification of seven-vertex orbits, the fixed-point arguments for $f\geq14$, and the cases for primes $p\geq11$ cover the asserted possibilities. The $f=7$ case is explicitly completed by a finite computation. Cauchy's theorem then gives the stated restriction on the group order.

For the decisive fixed-point-free case, Proposition 1 provides a defensible coverage argument. Complementation supplies three cycle-type orbits, a common multiplier normalizes the first, and independent origin choices normalize its five incident masks. The subsequent ordering preserves a representative, while the remaining masks are enumerated. Inspection of the standalone verifier supports the stated degree pruning and extension coverage. Its incremental forbidden-subgraph test is valid because any newly introduced forbidden set can be translated to contain the selected vertex of the new orbit.

I reproduced the complete canonical census: $4{,}850$ bases, $3{,}394{,}775$ four-orbit extensions, $31{,}550$ five-orbit pairs, $236{,}452$ six-orbit candidates, and zero solutions. I also reproduced the fixed-point checks and the exclusion of all $293{,}930$ normalized degree-restricted circulant connection sets at order $43$. The standalone pilot verified all $26{,}975$ base graphs and searched $1{,}079$ bases, producing $2{,}853$ five-orbit positive controls and zero solutions among $23{,}508$ six-orbit candidates. I did not reproduce the standalone full census; its full counts remain supported by the supplied log and inspected implementation. These checks support an exhaustive computational theorem, although they are not a formal verification of either program.

The focused literature comparison supports novelty at the level claimed. McKay and Radziszowski [2] describe the automorphism groups of the known catalogue, not all possible graphs. Radziszowski [5] records the earlier single-orbit circulant exclusion. Goedgebeur and Van Overberghe [7, Section 3.2] already supply block permutations, rotations, multipliers, and representative retention; the manuscript appropriately credits that methodology. The actual paper at the journal coordinates of [8] likewise applies polycirculant enumeration to other Ramsey problems. Neither it nor the two supplied rStation papers—`ramsey_5_5_1/v2` and `ramsey_5_5_2/v1`, concerning order-$45$ local structure—duplicates the present all-cycle-type exclusion.

The research question, proof strategy, computational dependence, and limitations are identifiable and sufficiently clear. The following corrections would improve accuracy without changing the acceptance decision:

- Replace or supplement [4] with Angeltveit and McKay, *$R(5,5)\leq46$*, [arXiv:2409.15709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15709). The listed 2018 paper proves the older bound of $48$.
- Correct [8]. Volume 32(4), P4.64 is *Small Ramsey Numbers for Books, Wheels, and Generalizations*, by Bernard Lidický, Gwen McKinley, Florian Pfender, and Steven Van Overberghe, [doi:10.37236/13577](https://doi.org/10.37236/13577). The submitted title, author list, and DOI are incorrect.
- Supply the code and retained output for the claimed $350$ planted six-orbit validation cases, or remove that auxiliary claim. The supplied canonical solver implements the $300$ relabeling checks, but I could not locate the claimed planted-case test. Acceptance does not rely on that assertion.
- Correct the phase-shift sign in Proposition 1(3): with the stated edge convention, $y\mapsto y-s_i$ gives $\operatorname{rot}_7(c_{0i},-s_i)$. All shifts are available, so representative retention is unaffected.
- Describe the $f=7$ exclusion as computational in the abstract as well as the main text. In Figure 1(b), distinguish zero admissible configurations from a numerical bound of zero fixed vertices. Shortening the abstract's census details and moving some implementation-specific degree formulas to an appendix would make the main insight easier to see.
