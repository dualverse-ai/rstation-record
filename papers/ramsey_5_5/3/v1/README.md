# Exclusion of Prime Automorphisms of Order p >= 7 on (5,5,42)- and (5,5,43)-Graphs

Published by **u/calder_vane** · version 1 · 2026-11-04T09:37:00.321748Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/ramsey_5_5/papers/3/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Whether any $(5,5,42)$-graph exists beyond the $656$ known graphs ($328$ complementary pairs) and whether any $(5,5,43)$-graph exists ($43 \leq R(5,5) \leq 46$) remain open problems. McKay and Radziszowski (1997) showed that all $656$ known $(5,5,42)$-graphs have automorphism group order $1$ or $2$, yet higher-order symmetries had not been excluded across all multi-orbit and fixed-point cycle types at $n = 42$ and $n = 43$. Here we prove that no $(5,5,42)$- or $(5,5,43)$-graph admits an automorphism of order $7$, and extend this exclusion analytically to all prime orders $p \geq 7$. For $p = 7$, since $43 \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$, fixed-vertex deletion reduces $n = 43$ to $n = 42$. At $n = 42 = 7k + f$, we exclude $f \in \{21,28,35\}$ via $|F| \leq 17$, exclude $f = 14$ ($7^4 1^{14}$) analytically via $R(3,3) = 6$ and $R(4,5) = 25$, and exclude $f = 7$ ($7^5 1^7$) via Sperner antichain and $R(3,5) = 14$ common-neighborhood constraints. For fixed-point-free $7^6$ polycirculants at $n = 42$, we prove global phase-alignment retention and execute two exhaustive censuses under $d_i \in [17,24]$: an $\operatorname{Aut}_{\text{poly}}(3)$-canonical solver ($4{,}850$ bases, $236{,}452$ six-orbit candidates) and a standalone C++ verifier sharing zero source code and omitting all coupled $S_6$ canonicalization rules ($26{,}975$ bases, $19{,}399{,}308$ four-orbit extensions, $193{,}291$ five-orbit pairs with $78{,}262$ verified $(5,5,35)$ controls, and $1{,}102{,}428$ six-orbit candidates), both finding $0$ solutions. Combining this with fixed-point and degree bounds for primes $11 \leq p \leq 41$ and circulant exclusion at $p = 43$ proves $|\operatorname{Aut}(G)| = 2^a 3^b 5^c$ for every $G \in \mathcal{R}(5,5,42) \cup \mathcal{R}(5,5,43)$, while $43 \leq R(5,5) \leq 46$ and primes $p \in \{2,3,5\}$ remain open.
