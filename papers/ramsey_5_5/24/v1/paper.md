# Exclusion of faithful $2^a\cdot3$ ($a\ge2$) automorphism actions at orders $42$–$45$ and extremal order-$40$ realizations for $R(5,5)$

## 1. Introduction and Scope

Call a simple undirected graph $G=(V,E)$ **Ramsey-admissible** if it contains neither a $5$-clique $K_5$ nor a $5$-vertex independent set $I_5$. The diagonal Ramsey number $R(5,5)$ is the smallest integer $N$ for which no $N$-vertex Ramsey-admissible graph exists; Exoo's $42$-vertex construction gives $R(5,5)\ge43$ [1], and Angeltveit and McKay give $R(5,5)\le46$ [2]. Because exhaustive search over unlabelled graphs is intractable at these orders, determining which finite groups $H$ can act faithfully by graph automorphisms on Ramsey-admissible graphs of orders $42\le N\le45$ constrains both constructive search and symmetry-based nonexistence proofs without assuming regularity or strong regularity.

An action of a finite group $H$ on an $N$-vertex set $V$ decomposes $V$ into transitive $H$-orbits $O_1,\dots,O_r$, where each orbit $O_i\cong H/S_i$ is determined up to $H$-equivariant bijection by the $H$-conjugacy class of a point stabilizer $S_i\le H$ of index $|O_i|=[H:S_i]$. Writing $\ker(H\curvearrowright H/S_i)=\bigcap_{h\in H}hS_i h^{-1}$ for the normal core of $S_i$, the combined action on $V$ is **faithful** if and only if $\bigcap_{i=1}^r\ker(H\curvearrowright H/S_i)=\{1\}$. Retaining any union of whole $H$-orbits gives an $H$-invariant induced subgraph; since Ramsey-admissibility is hereditary, refuting a faithful orbit-count tuple refutes every faithful tuple that dominates it coordinatewise.

Our first main result completes the classification of faithful actions of composite $\{2,3\}$-groups of order divisible by $12$ across orders $42$–$45$.

**Theorem 1 (computer-assisted; Exclusion of Faithful $2^a\cdot3$ Actions at Orders $42$–$45$).** Let $H$ be a finite group of order $2^a\cdot3$ with $a\ge2$ ($|H|\ge12$). No $(K_5,I_5)$-free graph on $42\le N\le45$ vertices admits a faithful action of $H$ by graph automorphisms.

Theorem 1 combines an elementary minimal-subgroup reduction (Lemma 1) to six minimal target groups—the five isomorphism classes of order $12$, namely $A_4$, $D_6\cong S_3\times C_2$, $C_{12}$, $C_6\times C_2$, and $\operatorname{Dic}_3\cong C_3\rtimes C_4$, together with the binary tetrahedral group $\operatorname{SL}(2,3)\cong Q_8\rtimes C_3$ of order $24$—with the published exclusion of $A_4$ [3] and $6{,}184$ independently checked reverse unit propagation (RUP) refutations ($305{,}773{,}252$ RUP clauses) covering all strict minimal faithful roots of $D_6$ ($5{,}173$), $C_{12}$ ($155$), $\operatorname{Dic}_3$ ($155$), $C_6\times C_2$ ($610$), and $\operatorname{SL}(2,3)$ ($91$) at orders $42$–$45$.

**Corollary 1 (Global $\{2,3\}$-Subgroup Restriction on $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$).** Let $G$ be a $(K_5,I_5)$-free graph of order $42\le N\le45$, and let $K\le\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ be any $\{2,3\}$-subgroup. Then $12\nmid|K|$; combined with $9\nmid|\operatorname{Aut}(G)|$ at orders $42$–$45$ [4], [5], either $K$ is a $2$-group ($|K|=2^a$) or $|K|\in\{3,6\}$ ($K\cong C_3$, $C_6$, or $S_3$). At orders $42\le N\le44$, where primes $p\ge5$ are also excluded [6], the full automorphism group $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ itself is either a $2$-group or isomorphic to $C_3$, $C_6$, or $S_3$ (or trivial).

*Proof.* By Cauchy's and Sylow's theorems, if $12\mid|K|$ for a $\{2,3\}$-group $K$, let $P\in\operatorname{Syl}_2(K)$ ($|P|=2^a\ge4$) and $Q\in\operatorname{Syl}_3(K)$. Since $9\nmid|\operatorname{Aut}(G)|$ at orders $42$–$45$ [4], [5], $|Q|=3$, so $|K|=2^a\cdot3$ with $a\ge2$, contradicting Theorem 1. At orders $42$–$44$, every prime $p\ge5$ fails to divide $|\operatorname{Aut}(G)|$ [6], making $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ itself a $\{2,3\}$-group. $\square$

**Explicit open scope.** Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 do not improve the numerical bounds $43\le R(5,5)\le46$ and leave four specific symmetry and order regimes open: (i) pure $2$-groups of order $2^a$ ($a\ge1$), which contain no element of order $3$; (ii) order-$3$ ($C_3$) and order-$6$ ($C_6$ with $n_6\ge4$, $S_3$ with $n_6\ge2$) actions [3]; (iii) fixed-point-free $5$-subgroups at order $45$ outside conference graphs [7], [8], [9]; and (iv) lower orders $N\le41$, specifically order $41$ for $D_6$, $C_{12}$, $\operatorname{Dic}_3$, and $C_6\times C_2$ (orders $N\ge46$ are already excluded by $R(5,5)\le46$ [2]).

In Section 5, kept strictly separate from the order $42$–$45$ exclusions, we establish constructive boundary realizations at orders $38$ and $40$: the sharp global maximum $N_{\max}(\operatorname{SL}(2,3))=40$ via `SL23_40` ($|\operatorname{Aut}|=192=2^6\cdot3$, restricting to faithful $C_{12}$ and $D_6$ actions at $N=40$) and a non-isomorphic realization `D6_40` ($|\operatorname{Aut}|=24\cong D_{12}$), together with analytical catalogue-overlap bounds, bilateral non-$(3,3,5)$-splittability, and RUP-certified $2$-flip asymmetric attachment rigidity.

## 2. Related Work

Angeltveit and McKay established $R(5,5)\le48$ [10] and subsequently $R(5,5)\le46$ [2] via linear programming over subgraph densities; the exact bound $R(4,5)=25$ [11] and small Ramsey numbers $R(3,5)=14$, $R(2,5)=5$ [12] govern our fixed-point attachment bounds. McKay and Radziszowski catalogued $656$ oriented ($328$ unoriented) $(5,5,42)$-graphs [13], all having $|\operatorname{Aut}(G)|\le2$. Prescribed cyclic and block-circulant symmetries in Ramsey constructions were studied by Goedgebeur and Van Overberghe [14], and prescribed-automorphism DRAT/flip-ball rigidity near $R(5,5)$ was studied by Russell [15]. Composite automorphism methods for strongly regular graphs were developed by Maksimović [16] and Crnković and Maksimović [17]; unlike [16], [17] and conference-graph analyses at $N=45$ [7], our results hold for arbitrary $(K_5,I_5)$-free graphs.

Within the rStation $R(5,5)$ programme, primes $p\ge5$ were excluded at orders $42$–$44$ in [6], while solvable and normal-$5$ restrictions at $N=45$ were studied in [8], [9]. Cyclic $C_6$ actions with $n_6\le1$ were excluded in [18]. Exclusions of $C_9$ [4] and $C_3\times C_3$ [5] imply $9\nmid|\operatorname{Aut}(G)|$ at orders $42$–$45$ (with $3$-subgroups at orders $43$–$45$ independently bounded in [19]). In [3], we excluded all faithful $A_4$ actions for $N\ge38$ ($1{,}849/1{,}849$ tuples at $42$–$45$, sharp cutoff $N_{\max}(A_4)=37$) and proved $n_6(C_6)\ge4$, $n_6(S_3)\ge2$ at $42$–$45$, $n_3(S_3)\le12$ universally, and $C_6\le K$ whenever $|K|=2^a\cdot3\ge12$ with $A_4\not\le K$. Finally, mixed $(3,3,5)$ extensions of McKay's $656$ order-$42$ graphs were analysed in [20]. None of [1]–[20] resolves faithful actions of $D_6$, $C_{12}$, $\operatorname{Dic}_3$, $C_6\times C_2$, or $\operatorname{SL}(2,3)$ at orders $42$–$45$, nor constructs order-$40$ $(K_5,I_5)$-free graphs with $|\operatorname{Aut}(G)|\in\{24,192\}$.

## 3. Minimal Subgroup Reduction and Cyclic Fixed-Point Bound

**Lemma 1 (Minimal Subgroup Reduction for Order $2^a\cdot3$, $a\ge2$).** Every finite group $H$ of order $2^a\cdot3$ with $a\ge2$ contains a subgroup isomorphic to $A_4$, $D_6$, $C_{12}$, $C_6\times C_2$, $\operatorname{Dic}_3$, or $\operatorname{SL}(2,3)$.

*Proof.* Suppose for contradiction that $H$ is a minimal counterexample of order $2^a\cdot3$ ($a\ge2$) containing no subgroup of order $12$ and no subgroup isomorphic to $\operatorname{SL}(2,3)$. Since every group of order $12$ belongs to $\{A_4,D_6,C_{12},C_6\times C_2,\operatorname{Dic}_3\}$, we have $a\ge3$ ($|H|\ge24$). Let $P\in\operatorname{Syl}_2(H)$ ($|P|=2^a$, $[H:P]=3$) and $Q\in\operatorname{Syl}_3(H)$ ($|Q|=3$). Left translation on $H/P$ defines a homomorphism $H\to S_3$ with kernel $K=\operatorname{core}_H(P)\trianglelefteq H$ of index dividing $6$, so $|K|\ge2^{a-1}\ge4$. Choose a minimal normal subgroup $M\trianglelefteq H$ with $M\le K$; because $K$ is a $2$-group, $M\cong\mathbb{F}_2^r$ for some $r\ge1$.

1. **Case $r\ge2$.** Since $\gcd(|Q|,2)=1$, Maschke's theorem decomposes $M$ into irreducible $\mathbb{F}_2[Q]$-submodules of dimensions $1$ and $2$ (from $t^3-1=(t-1)(t^2+t+1)$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$). If $M$ contains a $2$-dimensional irreducible submodule $W\cong V_4$, then $W\rtimes Q\cong A_4\le H$ has order $12$. Otherwise $Q$ acts trivially on $M$; any subgroup $W\le M$ of order $4$ yields $W\times Q\cong C_6\times C_2\le H$ of order $12$, impossible.
2. **Case $r=1$ ($|M|=2$, so $M=\langle z\rangle\le Z(H)$).** Since $a\ge3$, $|H/M|=2^{a-1}\cdot3\ge12$. By minimality of $H$, $H/M$ contains a subgroup $L/M$ of order $12$ or isomorphic to $\operatorname{SL}(2,3)$, and minimality forces $L=H$.
   - *Subcase 2a ($|H/M|=12$, $|H|=24$).* If $H/M\not\cong A_4$, then $H/M\in\{D_6,C_{12},C_6\times C_2,\operatorname{Dic}_3\}$ has a unique Sylow $3$-subgroup $QM/M\trianglelefteq H/M$, whence $QM\trianglelefteq H$ has order $6$. Choosing any subgroup $P_4\le P$ of order $4$ containing $M$ (possible since $|P|=8$), $P_4(QM)\le H$ has order $\frac{4\cdot6}{2}=12$, a contradiction. If instead $H/M\cong A_4$, then $P/M\cong V_4\trianglelefteq A_4$, so $P\trianglelefteq H$ has order $8$ and $Q\cong C_3$ acts nontrivially on $P/M$, requiring $3\mid|\operatorname{Aut}(P)|$. Among the five groups of order $8$, only $\mathbb{F}_2^3$ ($|\operatorname{Aut}|=168$) and $Q_8$ ($|\operatorname{Aut}|=24$) have $3\mid|\operatorname{Aut}(P)|$. If $P\cong\mathbb{F}_2^3$, Maschke's theorem splits $P=M\times W$ with $Q$-invariant $W\cong V_4$, giving $W\rtimes Q\cong A_4\le H$; otherwise $P\cong Q_8$ with faithful $C_3$-action on $P/Z(P)$, giving $H=P\rtimes Q\cong\operatorname{SL}(2,3)$, a contradiction.
   - *Subcase 2b ($H/M\cong\operatorname{SL}(2,3)$, $|H|=48$).* Here $P\trianglelefteq H$ has order $16$, $M=\langle z\rangle\le Z(P)$, and $P/M\cong Q_8$. Lift the quaternion generators $i,j\in Q_8$ to $x,y\in P$ and set $c=[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$. Since $x^2\in cM$ and $z\in Z(P)$, $x$ commutes with $x^2$ and $z$, hence with $c$; symmetrically $y$ commutes with $c$. Since $P$ is generated by $\{x,y,z\}$ with $z\in Z(P)$ and $c=[x,y]$ commuting with $x,y$, we have $\langle z,c\rangle\le Z(P)$ and $P/\langle c\rangle$ abelian, whence $[P,P]=\langle c\rangle$; conversely, $Z(P)M/M\le Z(P/M)=Z(Q_8)=\langle cM\rangle$ gives $Z(P)=\langle z,c\rangle$. From $x^2,c\in Z(P)$ we obtain $1=[x^2,y]=x[x,y]x^{-1}[x,y]=c^2$. Since $c$ projects to $-1\in Q_8$ whereas $z\in M$ projects to $1\in Q_8$, we have $c\ne z$ and $Z(P)=\langle c\rangle\times M\cong V_4$. Moreover, $(uz)^2=u^2z^2=u^2$, so squaring is constant on $M$-cosets and maps each non-central coset $xZ(P),yZ(P),xyZ(P)$ into $cM=\{c,cz\}$. Because $Q\cong C_3$ permutes these three cosets cyclically while fixing $z\in Z(H)$ and the unique generator $c$ of $[P,P]$, they share a common square $w=x^2=y^2=(xy)^2\in\{c,cz\}$ with $w^2=1$. Using $yx=xyc$, we compute $(xy)^2=x(yx)y=x^2y^2c$, so $w=w^2c=c$. Thus $P/\langle c\rangle$ has exponent $2$, i.e., $P/\langle c\rangle\cong\mathbb{F}_2^3$. By Maschke's theorem, the $1$-dimensional $Q$-fixed subspace $M\langle c\rangle/\langle c\rangle$ admits a $Q$-invariant $2$-dimensional complement in $P/\langle c\rangle$ whose preimage $R\le P$ has order $8$, is $Q$-invariant, and satisfies $R\cap M=\{1\}$. Hence $R\cong RM/M=P/M\cong Q_8$ and $R\rtimes Q\cong\operatorname{SL}(2,3)<H$, a final contradiction. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (Cyclic-$C_3$ Fixed-Point Bound).** Let $G$ be a $(K_5,I_5)$-free graph admitting an order-$3$ automorphism $\sigma\in H\le\operatorname{Aut}(G)$. If $G$ contains two vertex-disjoint $\sigma$-orbits $\Delta_1,\Delta_2$ of size $3$ of the same colour, then $|\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma)|\le20$. In particular, $|\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma)|\le20$ holds whenever either:
- *(a)* $\sigma=\tau^2$ for an element $\tau\in H$ of order $6$ having at least one regular $6$-orbit ($n_6(\langle\tau\rangle)\ge1$)—which holds for every faithful $\operatorname{SL}(2,3)$ action ($n_{24}+n_8\ge1\implies n_6(C_6)=4n_{24}+n_8\ge1$) and whenever $n_6(C_6)\ge1$ in $C_{12}$, $\operatorname{Dic}_3$, or $C_6\times C_2$; or
- *(b)* $H$ has at least one transitive $H$-orbit $O$ of size $3k\ge6$ on which $\langle\sigma\rangle$ acts freely (in particular, whenever $n_{12}+n_{6+}+n_{6-}+n_{6z}\ge1$ in $D_6$).

*Proof.* First verify that *(a)* and *(b)* both supply a same-coloured pair $(\Delta_1,\Delta_2)$ without requiring $\langle\sigma\rangle\trianglelefteq H$. Under *(a)*, inside a single regular $\langle\tau\rangle$-orbit $\{v,\tau v,\dots,\tau^5v\}$, the automorphism $\tau$ swaps the two $\sigma$-orbits $\{v,\sigma v,\sigma^2v\}\leftrightarrow\{\tau v,\tau^3v,\tau^5v\}$, forcing them to be monochromatic triangles of the same colour. In $\operatorname{SL}(2,3)$, although the four Sylow $3$-subgroups $\langle\sigma\rangle$ are nonnormal, the unique involution $z=-I\in Z(\operatorname{SL}(2,3))$ is central, so $\tau=(-I)\sigma$ has order $6$ with $\langle\tau^2\rangle=\langle\sigma\rangle$; every faithful action has $n_{24}+n_8\ge1$ (since $-I$ acts trivially on orbit sizes $12,6,4,3,1$), and restricting either size-$24$ ($4$ regular $C_6$-orbits) or size-$8=\operatorname{SL}(2,3)/C_3$ ($1$ regular $C_6$-orbit plus one $2$-orbit fixed by $\sigma$, as $-I$ acts freely on $\operatorname{SL}(2,3)/C_3$) gives $n_6(\langle\tau\rangle)=4n_{24}+n_8\ge1$. Under *(b)*, $O$ partitions into $k\ge2$ monochromatic $\sigma$-triangles $\Delta_1,\dots,\Delta_k$ (in $D_6$, $\langle\sigma\rangle=\langle r^2\rangle\trianglelefteq D_6$ is normal and $D_6$ permutes them transitively); even when $\langle\sigma\rangle$ is nonnormal in $H$, if $k\ge3$ then two $\Delta_j$ share a colour by pigeonhole, while if $k=2$ ($|O|=6$), $H$-transitivity on $O$ makes the Red subgraph $G[O]$ $d$-regular, so $2e(\Delta_1)+e(\Delta_1,\Delta_2)=\sum_{u\in\Delta_1}\deg_{G[O]}(u)=3d=2e(\Delta_2)+e(\Delta_1,\Delta_2)$, forcing $e(\Delta_1)=e(\Delta_2)\in\{0,3\}$.

Up to complementation, let $\Delta_1,\Delta_2$ both be Red $K_3$'s. Since $\Delta_1\cup\Delta_2$ has $6$ vertices, it cannot be a Red $K_6$, so at least one cross-edge $\{u_1,u_2\}$ ($u_1\in\Delta_1$, $u_2\in\Delta_2$) is Blue. Every fixed vertex $f\in\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma)$ attaches monochromatically to $\Delta_1$ and to $\Delta_2$. Partition $\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma)=F_{RR}\sqcup F_{RB}\sqcup F_{BR}\sqcup F_{BB}$ by attachment colours to $(\Delta_1,\Delta_2)$:
- $F_{RR}\cup F_{RB}$ is Red to $\Delta_1$ (a Red $K_3$), so it contains no Red edge (else a Red $K_5$ arises), giving $|F_{RR}\cup F_{RB}|\le4$ (else a Blue $I_5$ arises).
- $F_{BR}$ is Blue to $u_1\in\Delta_1$ and Red to $\Delta_2$ (a Red $K_3$), so $F_{BR}$ contains no Red edge (with $\Delta_2$) and no Blue $I_4$ (with $u_1$), giving $|F_{BR}|\le3$.
- $F_{BB}$ is Blue to $\{u_1,u_2\}$, which is itself a Blue edge, so $G[F_{BB}]$ is $(K_5,I_3)$-free; since $R(5,3)=14$ [12], $|F_{BB}|\le13$.
Summing gives $|\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma)|\le4+3+13=20$. $\square$

### 5.2. Catalogue-overlap separation, bilateral $(3,3,5)$ obstruction, and $2$-flip rigidity

**Proposition 2 (Catalogue-Overlap Separation and Bilateral Non-$(3,3,5)$-Splittability).** Let $\mathcal{C}_{656}$ denote McKay and Radziszowski's catalogue of $656$ oriented ($328$ unoriented, [`r55_42some.g6`](supplement/witnesses/r55_42some.g6)) $(5,5,42)$-graphs [13], every member $M\in\mathcal{C}_{656}$ satisfying $423\le|E(M)|\le438$ and $19\le\delta(M)\le\Delta(M)\le22$ [20].
1. **(Maximum common induced subgraph.)** Every graph $M\in\mathcal{C}_{656}$ shares an induced subgraph of order **at most $38$** with `SL23_40` and **at most $36$** with `D6_40`.
2. **(Bilateral $(3,3,5)$ obstruction.)** Call a Ramsey-admissible ($(K_5,I_5)$-free) graph $G$ **$(3,3,5)$-splittable** if $E(G)=X\sqcup Y$ decomposes into two triangle-free graphs $X,Y$ (so $(X,Y,\overline{G})$ is a $(K_3,K_3,K_5)$-free $3$-colouring). Across $\mathcal{C}_{656}$, RUP census ([`solve_subgroup_chirality_rup.cpp`](supplement/src/solve_subgroup_chirality_rup.cpp), [`auxiliary_verification_records.tsv`](supplement/auxiliary_verification_records.tsv)) confirms that **every** unoriented class ($328/328$) has exactly one orientation $(3,3,5)$-splittable ($326/328$ strictly denser on $431$–$438$ edges and $2/328$ strictly sparser). By contrast, **both orientations** of `SL23_40` ($215$ and $96$ RUP clauses) and **both orientations** of `D6_40` ($71$ and $86$ RUP clauses) are `UNSAT_RUP_VERIFIED` non-$(3,3,5)$-splittable. Because $(3,3,5)$-splittability is hereditary under induced subgraphs, neither `SL23_40` nor `D6_40` embeds as an induced subgraph into *any* $(5,5)$-graph (of any order) that admits a $(3,3,5)$-splitting in either colour.

*Proof of (1).* Suppose $H_k$ is a common induced subgraph of order $k$ between a $40$-vertex graph $G_0$ and an oriented graph $M\in\mathcal{C}_{656}$. Deleting $42-k=s$ vertices from $M$ ($\delta(M)\ge19$) removes at least $19s-\binom{s}{2}$ edges, so $|E(H_k)|\le|E(M)|-\bigl(19s-\binom{s}{2}\bigr)\le438-19s+\binom{s}{2}$. Conversely, deleting $40-k$ vertices from $G_0$ ($\Delta(G_0)=\Delta_0$) leaves $|E(H_k)|\ge|E(G_0)|-(40-k)\Delta_0$.
- For `SL23_40` ($|E|=408$, $\Delta_0=21$) at $k=39$ ($s=3$): $|E(H_{39})|\ge408-21=387>438-57+3=384$, contradiction ($k=40$ similarly gives $408>401$). Thus $k\le38$.
- For $\overline{\text{D6\_40}}$ ($|E|=420$, $\Delta_0=22$, matching any $M\in\mathcal{C}_{656}$ up to complementation) at $k=37$ ($s=5$): $|E(H_{37})|\ge420-3(22)=354>438-95+10=353$, contradiction (and similarly for $k\in\{38,39,40\}$). Exact subset minimization in [`verify_paper3_supplement.cpp`](supplement/verify_paper3_supplement.cpp) confirms both bounds across all $\binom{40}{k}$ subsets. $\square$

**Proposition 3 (computer-assisted; Asymmetric $2$-Flip Attachment Rigidity at $N=41$).** Neither `SL23_40` nor `D6_40` admits any $(K_5,I_5)$-free $41$-vertex extension $G'$ (without any symmetry assumption on $G'$) obtained by flipping at most $2$ interior edges of $G_0$ (covering all $1 + 780 + \binom{780}{2} = 304{,}591$ interior perturbations) and attaching a $41$st vertex $w$ (covering all $2^{40}$ attachment neighbourhoods).

*Proof.* Partitioning unordered interior pairs into $\operatorname{Aut}(G_0)$-orbits ($12$ orbits for `SL23_40`, $50$ orbits for `D6_40`) and testing exact flip counts $k\in\{0,1,2\}$ by fixing the minimum-index flipped orbit at $k\ge1$ while bounding a second free flip at $k=2$ via a Sinz cardinality counter ([`solve_coupled_sinz_surgery.cpp`](supplement/src/solve_coupled_sinz_surgery.cpp)) reduces the full $304{,}591\times2^{40}$ search space to $1+12+12=25$ (`SL23_40`) and $1+50+50=101$ (`D6_40`) exact CNF subproblems; $100\%$ ($25/25$ and $101/101$) are `UNSAT_RUP_VERIFIED`. $\square$
