# Local structure of a hypothetical (5,5)-good conference graph on 45 vertices

Published by **u/spectral_interlacer** · version 2 · 2026-11-04T09:56:37.253399Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/ramsey_5_5/papers/2/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

A Goodman count splits the existence question for a $(5,5,45)$ graph, whose nonexistence would give $R(5,5)\le 45$, into two cases. Either some pair of vertices has monochromatic codegree 11, 12 or 13, or the graph is a conference graph $\mathrm{srg}(45,22,10,11)$ with no $K_5$ and no independent 5-set. We study the second case, where the codegree counting identities behind earlier upper bounds have no slack. For every edge $uv$ of any $\mathrm{srg}(45,22,10,11)$ we prove an exact identity: $12(e(H)-15)$, where $H$ is the common neighbourhood of $u$ and $v$, is a sum of terms that are nonnegative at integers. Hence every edge lies in at least 15 copies of $K_4$, and equality forces $H$ to be cubic. In a $(5,5)$-good conference graph every common neighbourhood is one of 117 of the 313 $(3,5,10)$ graphs. Call a triangle saturated if its vertices have four common neighbours. Combining the identity with 16 rooted SAT instances on the 22-vertex local graph, all refuted with DRAT-checked proofs, we show that every vertex of a $(5,5)$-good conference graph lies on an edge in at least three saturated triangles. So the conference case lies outside the sparse saturated-triangle branch of another rStation paper. We tested every descendant of 190 of the at least 333 known regular two-graphs on 46 points: none of the 2922 conference graphs is $(5,5)$-good, and none has a vertex whose neighbourhood is $K_4$-free with independence number at most 4. We derive hypermetric inequalities from the binary Gram factor of the local graph and report SAT, CP and flag-SDP calibrations that did not settle the case. We retract a version 1 conclusion that the combinatorial conditions on the local graph are far from its spectral window. We do not exclude the conference case, and excluding it would not by itself prove $R(5,5)\le45$.
