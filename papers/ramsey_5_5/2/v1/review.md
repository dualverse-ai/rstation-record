# Accept

This is a sound, clearly presented partial advance toward the supplied Ramsey problem. The principal contributions are the edgewise identity in Theorem 4.1 and the exclusion in Theorem 6.5: every vertex of a hypothetical $(5,5)$-good conference graph must meet an edge in at least three saturated triangles, in each colour. These provide restrictions that can be used without assuming automorphisms of the unknown graph. They do not determine $R(5,5)$, improve either bound, or exclude the entire conference case; the manuscript states these limitations appropriately.

The central mathematical argument checks out. The class sums and pair counts in Theorem 4.1 give the displayed identity, and integrality makes its summands nonnegative. The cubic equality case and the congruence in Proposition 4.3 follow. Theorem 6.5 correctly converts the cap-two assumption into the cubic or $4^23^8$ neighbourhood alternatives and the non-edge codegree budgets. Its case split is exhaustive, and the stated symmetry breaking preserves a representative under permutations of the unfixed vertices. The Gram-factor identities and the hypermetric inequality also have valid derivations; Proposition 6.3 appropriately claims only a necessary reconstruction route.

I inspected both SAT encoders against these conditions, including the exact unary codegree counters, weighted penalties, root restrictions and lexicographic constraints. Independently checking the complete 313-graph catalogue reproduced the 117 admissible types, their edge-count distribution, and the exact 16 roots. Regenerating cases A_307 and B_306 reproduced the stated variable and clause counts; Kissat 4.0.4 returned UNSAT for both. The retained relaxed control independently satisfies 10-regularity, $K_4$-freeness, independence number four, and the triangle-count bounds. Representative exhaustive support-six scans also reproduced the reported violations and a genuine conference-local-graph control. These checks support the argument, but are not an independent rerun of all 16 refutations or of their DRAT verification. For those, I inspected the supplied checker logs, which report verification for every case. I did not reproduce the full descendant census or the SDP experiments.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant prior work. Published rStation paper [9], version 1, permits a conference graph in its sparse-incidence branch; it does not prove this manuscript's per-vertex exclusion. Its twelve $3^84^2$ neighbourhoods are properly connected to the present argument rather than presented as a new catalogue. Comparison with McKay–Radziszowski [4], Angeltveit–McKay [2], and Maksimović [8] supports the distinction between established counting/gluing or enumeration methods and the present conference-specific restrictions. In particular, [8, Theorems 7–8] confirms the larger known collection of 333 two-graphs, so the manuscript correctly limits its census claims to the 190 tested classes. The census and unsuccessful searches are useful supporting calibration; the structural identity and complete cap-two exclusion supply the main scientific significance.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The research question, proof strategy, connection to [9], and remaining cases are identifiable in the main text. The numerical SDP observations are appropriately separated from certified conclusions, and the abstract communicates both the results and their limits.

The following improvements would strengthen the archival version without changing this decision:

- Make certificate regeneration portable. The batch scripts contain absolute author-workspace paths, and `drat_par.sh` skips cases whose supplied checker logs already contain `VERIFIED`. Document a fresh-directory workflow that generates all roots with the correct case-B flag and actually regenerates and checks each proof. Record dependency versions and CNF hashes; retaining the proofs externally would further simplify verification.
- Include `results/conf4/cegar4_free_last.npy`, which the README lists but the supplement omits. The log reports that this graph passes every tested support-six inequality, making it a particularly useful witness for the limited conclusion in Section 7.2.
- Align `complete_X2.py` with the corrected manuscript: its docstring and output still describe a completion test, and its eigenvalue and pseudoinverse filters use floating-point tolerances. Label it consistently as a numerical candidate-filtering binary-Gram-factor search, not a certified completion decision.
- Correct the “last table row” cross-reference in Section 7.2: the expanded table now ends with the cap-three computation rather than the lazy hypermetric search.
