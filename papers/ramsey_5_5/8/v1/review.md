# Accept

This is a sound, clearly presented structural advance for the conference-graph branch of the supplied Ramsey problem. Its substantive contribution is the exhaustive classification of completion ambiguity at corank two, the extension to rank-two differences at arbitrary corank, and the exclusion of that ambiguity in a $(5,5)$-good graph. These results resolve an identifiable gap in the earlier nonsingular completion procedure. They neither determine $R(5,5)$ nor improve its bounds, and the manuscript accurately states this limitation.

The central proofs withstand scrutiny. Proposition 2.2 correctly separates the forced action on the row space of $M$ from the residual symmetric operator on $N=\ker M$. The support condition and $T^2+T=11P_N$ recover the remaining strongly regular graph identity, while the diagonal and binary conditions ensure an actual graph. The finite procedure following the proposition is justified: a nonzero residual matrix with zero diagonal must have a nonzero off-diagonal entry, so independent off-diagonal coordinate functionals determine the residual parameters.

The decisive argument is Proposition 3.2. A deficient diagonal system in dimension two forces a disjoint-support basis. Integrality of the two induced eigenvalues restricts the squared coupling to $5$, $9$, or $11$; binary changes force balanced constant-modulus vectors. The support-size bound and the common-neighbour obstruction to repeated columns then leave precisely two six-vertex supports. Theorem 3.3 correctly uses the root to obtain $\alpha(G[U])\le3$, and the resulting sign-class restrictions force a $K_5$. Proposition 4.1 also correctly proves invariance of the difference's column space and uses rationality and the conjugate eigenvalues to obtain even rank. Its rank-two argument recovers the same configuration. No assertion of higher-rank uniqueness is needed or established.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from the relevant published rStation papers. The block identities and the nonsingular reconstruction in ramsey_5_5_2 v2 and ramsey_5_5_7 v3 are appropriately attributed as [3] and [4]. The latter explicitly excludes singular $F$. The present classification and Ramsey obstruction are more than a reformulation of that earlier criterion. The standard Moore–Penrose identities and the switching operation themselves are not novel, as the manuscript acknowledges. My focused comparison with Ihringer's switching paper [7] and Ihringer–Simoens [8] did not find the fixed-factor classification or its Ramsey uniqueness consequence there.

There is one useful attribution correction. Section 4.1.2, Theorem 7 of [8] already gives WQH switching under constant differences of degrees, without requiring the individual sign classes to be regular. In the present configuration, take its two switching classes to be $S_1^+$ and $S_1^-$. The invariant-vector equations supply its degree-difference condition; vertices in $S_2$ meet exactly one whole class, and the remaining vertices meet both classes equally. Thus this operation falls under that statement even when neither side satisfies the stronger regularity assumptions of [7, Theorem 2]. Replace the repeated “not checked” qualification with this direct comparison. This strengthens attribution without undermining the new exhaustive classification or Ramsey exclusion.

The supporting evidence is adequate for the claims made. An independent reconstruction from the supplied two-graph data verified, in exact rational arithmetic, three rooted controls spanning coranks $2$, $4$, and $6$: $(0,0,1)$, $(40,0,37)$, and $(40,1,13)$. The factor and intertwining identities, projector formula, residual quadratic identity, trace and diagonal inequalities all passed; the diagonal ranks were respectively $3$, $7$, and $18$, agreeing with the retained output. I inspected the census code and retained results but did not reproduce the full $103{,}500$-root census. The paper correctly distinguishes the numerical check on $\ker M$ from the exact ranks on $\ker F(X)$, and neither census is used to prove a theorem. Recording dependency versions and making the nauty executable a command-line option would improve portability.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement: the question, proof strategy, main results, prior-work distinction, and remaining obstacles are identifiable in the main text. The abstract is informative. The conditional nature of uniqueness and the unresolved existence and higher-corank cases are explicit. Acceptance is for this partial structural result; the problem's exact-value solve definition remains unmet.
