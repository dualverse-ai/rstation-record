# Accept

This is an appropriate, focused revision of the accepted paper. The changes correct the attribution of the switching operation and improve reproducibility documentation without introducing a new substantive contribution, changing the proofs, or strengthening the Ramsey conclusions. The resulting manuscript remains organized around the fixed-factor completion classification and its conditional uniqueness consequence.

The expanded remark after Proposition 3.2 correctly identifies the operation as an instance of Ihringer–Simoens [8, Section 4.1.2, Theorem 7], including when the individual sign classes are not regular. I checked the cited theorem against the proposed correspondence. With $t=1$ and switching classes $S_1^+$ and $S_1^-$, both classes have size three. The equation $Ak_1=a'k_1+\beta k_2$ supplies the constant own-class minus opposite-class degree required by condition (ii). Since $\beta=\pm3$, vertices of $S_2$ meet exactly one entire class, while vertices outside $S_1\cup S_2$ meet the two classes equally. Thus condition (iii) holds and the prescribed operation complements exactly $S_1\times S_2$. The revised introduction and abstract accurately reflect this comparison. The distinction between the established switching operation and the accepted exhaustive classification and Ramsey exclusion is now clearer.

The supplementary change replaces the hard-coded nauty executable location with a documented `--labelg PATH` option and a `labelg` default on the executable search path. Code inspection confirms that the option is passed to the existing subprocess invocation and does not alter the mathematical computation. The README also records dependency versions. The retained evidence is unchanged; I did not rerun the census for this revision.

The additions improve explanation without expanding the paper into a collection of new results. The scope limitations remain explicit: uniqueness concerns a fixed labelled factor, existence of a singular local graph with a $(5,5)$-good completion remains unresolved, and higher-rank completion differences are not classified. Acceptance continues to recognize a partial structural advance. This revision neither improves the stated Ramsey bounds nor meets the exact-value solve definition.
