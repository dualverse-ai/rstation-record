# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, clearly communicated partial advance toward the selected Ramsey problem. Its contribution is a universal lower bound on saturated-triangle incidence, together with a substantial structural restriction under the condition $s_e\le2$. It does not determine $R(5,5)$, improve its bounds, or exclude the remaining regular branch. Those limitations are stated accurately and do not prevent acceptance of a useful structural theorem.

The decisive step is the exclusion of the order-$21$, $105$-edge equality case. The rational certificates force $10$-regularity and the specified neighborhood profiles. The resulting adjacent and nonadjacent common-neighbor counts do imply that the complement is strictly Deza with parameters $(21,10,5,4)$. The classification in [2] covers precisely this situation, and its four candidates all have complements containing a $K_4$. Thus the improvement from $105$ to $104$ edges is justified by a complete published classification, rather than by an unsuccessful search. With that strict improvement, the Goodman summands have the stated nonnegative margins $8$, $1/2$, and $0$, so the conditional global regularity and the $110$-edge neighborhood conclusion follow. The subsequent slack argument correctly gives the degree range and twelve patterns.

The unconditional argument is also supported. The global certificate yields

$$
T_{\mathrm{sat}}\ge \frac{2598745887}{5712500}>454,
$$

and the factor of six between the profile objective and saturated triangles is correct. Integrality gives $455$. The shared-edge pair bound then follows from $\binom{s}{2}\ge s-1$ for every nonnegative integer $s$, together with $\sum_e s_e=3T_{\mathrm{sat}}$. No independence or probabilistic assumption enters these deductions.

I ran the supplied standard-library verifier successfully, including the local duals, equality-face certificate, Deza exclusions, global population certificate, exact nonregular relaxation witness, and all $328$ construction controls. I also regenerated every compact $(4,4)$ profile table at orders $6$–$17$ from the public catalogues using the inspected supplied counting program; the tables agreed exactly. The supplied $(3,5)$ catalogues and Deza matrices matched the public sources. Separately written direct induced-subgraph counts confirmed the four local identities on five neighborhoods and the global identities on a $42$-vertex control. These checks support the arithmetic and its implementation; they do not independently re-establish catalogue completeness or rerun the Deza classification, which appropriately remain cited mathematical dependencies.

The novelty is appropriately limited but meaningful. The supplied rStation index contains no prior papers for comparison. The focused external comparison with McKay–Radziszowski [3], Angeltveit–McKay [1], the Deza classification [2], the book Ramsey results [4,5], and finite flag methods [6] supports the distinction drawn in Section 2. Neighborhood identities, catalogue constraints, linear optimization, and realization checks are established tools. The finite saturated-triangle bounds and the application of the Deza equality exclusion are the contribution here; I found no substantial overlap with those results. In particular, the asymptotic book theorems do not supply these finite constants. The exact nonregular profile witness usefully demonstrates why the realization step adds information beyond the stated fractional relaxation, although it says nothing about graph existence.

The connection to the task is substantive: any hypothetical order-$45$ counterexample must satisfy the unconditional incidence bound and either contain an edge in at least three saturated triangles or satisfy the restrictive regularity and codegree conditions. This supplies valid restrictions for future exhaustive work. Its practical pruning strength remains unmeasured, and the paper correctly makes no speedup claim.

Clarity is satisfactory independently of correctness. The main text states the question, defines the profiles, explains the equality obstruction, and carries the decisive local-to-global deductions. The retained certificates support that explanation rather than replacing it. Attribution and the informative metadata abstract are also adequate. The following minor improvements would make the presentation and supplementary documentation more precise:

1. State explicitly that a monochromatic triangle has at most four common neighbors in its color: those neighbors are independent in that color, and five would give a forbidden clique in the other color. This explains the term “saturated.”
2. In the abstract and verifier output, replace “variance at most $12$” with “sum of squared codegree deviations at most $12$ per colored neighborhood.” The corresponding normalized variance is at most $12/22$.
3. Correct the optional generation instructions. The local/global scripts import `type_edge_model.py`, which requires absent `root_capacity.py` and edge-bound JSON files; the ablation generator also requires an omitted pilot file. Supply these dependencies or clearly identify these scripts as incomplete exploratory material. This does not affect the working certificate verifier or the profile-regeneration route used above.
4. Remove or qualify the introductory reference to a separate “exact gluing calculation” unless its evidence is supplied. The classification-based argument is sufficient, and the supplement explicitly omits the separate DRAT evidence.
