# Accept

This revision appropriately clarifies and modestly extends the presentation of the accepted classification. The attribution of the two-defect control, restoration of the triangle-free hypotheses in the construction paragraph, corrected author list in [7], and explicit statement of the unchanged Ramsey bounds address the earlier review. Removing the duplicated abstract does not remove substantive content. The resulting manuscript remains focused on the exhaustive classification of mixed extensions with a familiar good deletion.

The added edge-count and degree tests are sound, limited consequences of the existing fourteen-class classification, rather than a new substantive contribution requiring a separate submission. Using an independent graph6 decoder, I obtained exactly two representatives with 455 red edges, eight with 456, and four with 457. The aggregate degree multiplicities are exactly 172, 138, 282, and 10 for degrees 20, 21, 22, and 23. If a mixed graph had any familiar good deletion, Theorem 1 would place the entire merged graph in one of these fourteen isomorphism classes. Its edge count and every vertex degree would therefore satisfy the reported restrictions. This proves the new contrapositive tests, including the statement that the degree witness need not be the deleted vertex. These tests remain conditional on a good deletion existing; they do not produce a new Ramsey graph or improve a general Ramsey bound.

The control identification is now concrete and reproducible. I downloaded the cited v1.0.1 control file and checked the displayed permutation against the first representative in `classes.g6`: all 903 unordered pairs agree after complementing the first control graph. The distinction from the Molnár et al. example also resolves the ambiguity identified in the previous review. My new checks concern the permutation and graph statistics; I did not repeat the unchanged SAT exhaustion or independently repeat the Table S16 comparison in this revision review. The supplementary files are byte-identical to those supporting the accepted version.

The additions preserve the paper's central explanation and its previously established distinction from the cited construction and local-search work. A small wording improvement would be to say “first data line” for both files in the control correspondence, since catalogue indices elsewhere are explicitly zero-based. No substantive revision is required. Acceptance remains for the finite classification and its consequences; the exact value of $R(5,5)$ remains unresolved.
