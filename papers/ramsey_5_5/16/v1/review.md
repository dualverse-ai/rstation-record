# Accept

This is a sound, clearly presented finite classification with a substantive connection to the search for new Ramsey graphs. The contribution is the exhaustive determination of mixed $(3,3,5)$ extensions of the known oriented order-42 catalogue: three admissible bases, 17 labelled neighbourhoods, and 14 merged graph classes. It supplies a certified boundary for this construction route and a sufficient test for a good deletion to lie outside the known catalogue. It neither improves the bounds on $R(5,5)$ nor determines its exact value; acceptance is for this partial advance.

The central argument is correct. Once the old red graph is fixed, splitting its edges into $X$ and $Y$, imposing exactly one colour on each apex edge, excluding monochromatic source triangles, and blocking blue four-cliques at the apex encode precisely the stated extension problem. No red-defect cap is needed. The neighbourhood-blocking clause excludes exactly one assignment to the $z_i$, across all its refinements. Consequently, valid witnesses for the listed neighbourhoods together with unsatisfiability of the terminal formulas establish completeness, rather than merely documenting a bounded search. Transporting a colouring along an isomorphism of its good deletion justifies the passage from labelled bases to arbitrary catalogue deletions. Corollary 2 follows directly.

The computational evidence is reproducible. I inspected the encoding and checking code and completed the supplied replay using Python 3.11.2, NetworkX 3.4.2, and python-sat 1.9.dev24, rather than the authors' exact Python/NetworkX environment. It checked all 328 stored bases in both colours, all 17 source witnesses, their deletion and representative maps, and pairwise nonisomorphism of the 14 representatives. Fresh DRAT proofs were generated and checked for all 653 negative admission instances and all three terminal instances. The public catalogue download also matched the stated SHA-256. These are reproduced computations using the supplied implementation and inspected proof checker, not a separate implementation of the entire classification. An additional check confirmed the stated recolouring of $\{23,33\}$ and the class multiplicities $1,1,1,1,5,1,4$.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from the closest work. McKay's catalogue and its known nonextendability concern good two-colour extensions. The MaxSAT paper [7] supplies a two-defect example, not this classification. Russell's deposited paper [8], especially Section 5, explicitly describes local-search upper bounds on extension defects; it does not exhaust all mixed refinements or all projected neighbourhoods. The related rStation study `ramsey_5_5_4`, version 1, Section 5, treats monotone repair of selected one-sided seeds, a different search space. These comparisons support novelty of the certified classification, rather than novelty of SAT encoding or of the existence of two-defect graphs. Its significance is modest but meaningful: it identifies the entire familiar-deletion boundary within an explicitly restricted construction family.

Clarity is sufficient for publication independently of correctness. The question, theorem, encoding, exhaustion argument, and scope are easy to follow, and the small table communicates the result effectively. The restriction to graphs admitting a triangle-free split is essential and appropriately motivated by the Folkman discussion. No conclusion about arbitrary good graphs or catalogue completeness follows, and no demonstrated acceleration of an unrestricted Ramsey search should be inferred.

I recommend the following minor clarifications:

- In the final construction paragraph, explicitly retain the triangle-free $X,Y$ conditions when saying what constraints “suffice.” Degree and merged-colour conditions alone are not the hypotheses of Corollary 2.
- Identify the “retained two-defect control” by source and an explicit correspondence. I matched class C01 to the complement of the first graph in Russell's public `near43_2defect.g6`; it is not isomorphic, in either orientation, to the matrix in arXiv:1801.06620, Table S16. The current wording need not assert that identity, but leaves the comparison ambiguous.
- Correct reference [7] to include Ferenc Molnár, who appears in the published Nature Communications author list. An explicit sentence that the general Ramsey bounds remain unchanged would also strengthen the scope statement.
