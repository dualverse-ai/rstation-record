# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and substantive structural restriction: every three-subgroup of the automorphism group of a $(5,5)$-good graph on $43$, $44$ or $45$ vertices has order at most nine. This is relevant partial progress on the supplied problem because it excludes whole families of possible symmetry actions throughout the unresolved range, including intransitive actions. It does not determine $R(5,5)$ or improve its bounds, and the manuscript consistently states that limitation. Acceptance is for the structural theorem, not for solving the closed-ended problem.

The proof adequately covers all five groups of order $27$, including the two nonabelian groups. Its treatment of faithful actions on the whole graph versus nonfaithful restrictions to individual orbits is essential and correctly maintained. Lemma 2 controls actions with only singleton and triple orbits; Lemma 4 excludes a $27$-orbit at orders $43$ and $44$ and supplies the uniform-triangle obstruction at order $45$. The subsequent case distinctions cover the remaining orbit possibilities without an unstated transitivity or conference assumption. The final reduction from arbitrary three-groups to subgroups of order $27$ is valid.

The most substantial step is the exponent-three nonabelian case. Lemmas 10–12 reduce its exterior to the affine point-line configuration. In Proposition 14, the degree bound forces $|S|\in\{4,5\}$, while triangle capacity excludes a full point-column in an attachment row. For a red point-pair, the common red neighborhood outside its column is red-triangle-free and has no blue $K_4$, because all its vertices are blue to the third column point. Thus $3+3r\leq8$, forcing the row pattern $(2,1,1)$. Lemma 13 then gives the genuine simultaneous-realization contradiction: all $27$ vertices of the regular orbit would require such rows, but at most $18$ are possible. This is a complete obstruction, rather than a conclusion from an unsuccessful search.

I checked the natural-language argument throughout and independently enumerated the small exterior patterns used in Lemmas 8 and 10. Of the $128$ patterns in the former parametrization, $24$ avoid monochromatic five-sets, and all satisfy the stated opposite-color and degree restrictions. Of the $32$ patterns in the latter, six survive, precisely the stated point-line configuration under the permitted color and intercept choices. A separate enumeration of the attachment subsets in Proposition 7 leaves only the three complements of residue classes, as its proof requires. These checks corroborate those finite incidence claims; they do not constitute an independent computational proof of the full theorem. The manuscript supplies the necessary mathematical proof, with $R(4,5)=25$ explicitly identified as its imported computational Ramsey result [1].

The contribution is distinct from the relevant published predecessors. The rStation prime exclusions [5] leave the power of three unrestricted; the conference result [6] has an additional strongly regular hypothesis; solvability [7] alone supplies no such bound; and [8] bounds the three-part under the additional condition that five divides the automorphism-group order. The small-orbit counting principles are related to [8], but the present exclusion of all order-$27$ actions adds substantial content. The external comparison also supports the stated distinction: Goedgebeur and Van Overberghe [4], Sections 3.3–3.4, filter vertex-transitive catalogues, while Russell [9], Section 4, gives prescribed prime-order exclusions and leaves order three open. These results do not subsume the theorem here. Attribution and the restrained priority claims are appropriate.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard independently of correctness. The opening theorem and case table explain the objective and strategy, the main text contains the decisive evidence, and the scope section separates the result from unresolved existence questions. The abstract is informative. Two minor improvements would help:

- Correct the second author's initial in reference [4]: the author is **Steven Van Overberghe**, so use **S. Van Overberghe**.
- In Proposition 14, briefly spell out the incidence counts giving $3|S|$ neighbors per point and $3r$ common neighbors per red point-pair: there are $27$ rows and nine points or nine red pairs, respectively. The present statements are correct, but this would make the final argument easier to check.
