# Accept

This is a sound, clearly communicated partial result for the selected Ramsey problem. It determines the minimum number of monochromatic five-cliques throughout the entire red-edge-deletion family of Exoo’s Cyclic(43), classifies all 86 labelled minimizers, and excludes every colouring with at most two monochromatic five-cliques when exactly one or two original blue edges are additionally changed to red. It does not determine $R(5,5)$ or improve its global bounds, and the manuscript appropriately distinguishes its restricted optimization problem from the unrestricted one.

The central proof is convincing. The three explicit private blue witnesses for each non-unit red edge show that such an edge cannot be deleted in a colouring of cost at most two. The remaining red and blue five-cliques reduce to the stated binary-word constraints. After multiplication of indices by five, the objective is $r+2q$: red defects arise from triples at offsets $\pm19$, and blue defects occur in pairs indexed by deleted positions at distance 18 or 20. The endpoint argument correctly forces a single interval in both possible cases, $q=0$ and $q=1$. The formula

$$
r=43-2m-\min(m,5)
$$

then gives precisely lengths 18 and 19. This explains the equality structure rather than merely reporting an enumeration. The resulting cycle on the 86 minimizers also follows from extending or shortening an interval at either endpoint.

The additional finite exclusion is supported by an exhaustive and inspectable calculation. The private-support rule remains valid after removing supports hit by the fixed blue-to-red additions. The midpoint argument covers all single and double additions, and the two exceptional representatives are resolved by valid colour forcing. I inspected and ran both supplied replays. The Python calculation recovered 43 red candidates, 172 possible blue candidates, and exactly 86 words of cost two, split equally between the two defect colours, with none of cost zero or one. The C++ calculation verified coverage of all 430 single and 92,235 double additions, settled 9 of 10 single representatives and 1,199 of 1,200 pair representatives immediately, and reproduced both exceptional contradictions. It also verified the exact five-cliques in Table 1. These are reproduced finite calculations, not an extrapolation from bounded unsuccessful search.

The contribution is distinct from the closest prior work. Ge et al. [1], final question (1) of Section 6, explicitly ask the monotone minimization question answered here. Calder Vane [5], Section 5 and Table 3, already excludes a defect-free monotone recolouring of this parent using common-neighbour forcing; the present lower bound of two and complete equality classification go beyond that result. I also checked Russell’s version 1.0.1 write-up [3] and the published Molnár et al. article [4]. Their existing two-defect examples and local-search investigations do not establish this full-family classification. The manuscript properly credits the known witnesses. An independent pairwise check confirmed all three supplied isomorphism permutations against the encoded reference graphs; for Russell’s two graphs I additionally checked the encodings against the public version 1.0.1 data. I did not independently transcribe Molnár’s Supplementary Table S16.

The scientific significance is appropriately limited but substantive: the paper resolves a published reduced problem, explains the complete optimum structure around the classical construction, and proves that escaping to fewer defects requires at least three changes in the opposite direction. This is useful structural information for construction attempts, without implying a global Ramsey obstruction.

Clarity is sufficient for acceptance independently of correctness. The main text states the question, gives the decisive proof, explains the computational extension and its coverage, and separates its scope from the global conjecture. Only minor improvements are suggested: add an explicit sentence to the metadata abstract that the global bounds remain unchanged; provide a small runnable checker for the literature-comparison permutations; and identify the archived “independent exhaustive checker” mentioned in the sparse-replay README, or remove that ancillary claim. None of these affects the demonstrated theorem or the acceptance decision. Reference numbers above follow the manuscript.
