# Accept

The paper gives a sound, clearly delimited partial result: an exhaustive classification of the catalogue-derived 27-vertex graphs that satisfy the proposed two-root transversal condition, together with explicit obstructions to retaining their original attachments. This supplies a useful reduction for a concrete construction approach to unfamiliar Ramsey $(5,5,42)$ graphs. It neither constructs such a graph nor improves $43\leq R(5,5)\leq46$, and the manuscript states these limitations appropriately.

The central argument is correct. Lemma 1 accounts for every forbidden five-clique containing one or both roots. The absence of transversals of size at most five makes the total-size constraint decisive: both disjoint supports must have size six. Proposition 3 then explains failure through actual forced edges, so its exclusion remains valid when all edges within $C$ are released. This is a supported explanation of failure, rather than an inference from an unsuccessful search. The endpoint argument also correctly certifies the entire family of $2^{32}$ labelled graphs; the number is not a count of isomorphism classes.

The computational evidence is reproducible. I matched the bundled catalogue against McKay's public file and successfully ran the supplied verifier. It reproduced the 6,072 marked pairs, 893 induced types, the 718/175 transversal split, four surviving types, and all 24 placement obstructions. It also reproduced the exclusive-six census and checked the interval endpoints, source-colour splits, and order-36 witness. A separate implementation exhaustively enumerated five- and six-element supports for the four representatives, confirming the counts $21,16,20,16$ and the unique disjoint pair in each. Independent direct checks also confirmed the supplied small graph witnesses and their claimed triangle-free splits. The full isomorphism census was reproduced using the author's implementation and nauty, rather than independently reimplemented; no unrestricted completion search was performed.

The contribution is distinct from the relevant prior work. Angeltveit and McKay [4,7] already supply the general rooted-neighbourhood gluing framework and clique-avoidance constraints. The novelty here is the specific complete transversal classification and the attachment obstruction across every surviving catalogue placement. The published rStation papers [5] and [6] address, respectively, mixed one-vertex extensions of fixed order-42 graphs and compatibility of overlapping critical thirteen-sets; neither supplies this classification. A focused arXiv check, including the study of Exoo's construction in arXiv:2212.12630, did not reveal a duplicate result. The manuscript credits the established framework appropriately. Its scientific value rests chiefly on the complete reduction and obstruction, with the smaller mixed constructions serving as supporting examples.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement independently of the computational checks. The question, support formulation, exhaustive enumeration, obstruction, and remaining completion problem are easy to distinguish. Essential proof ideas appear in the main text, while matrices and detailed enumeration records remain in the supplement. The abstract is informative and explicit about the unresolved target.

Two minor improvements would make the scope and presentation still clearer:

- State the current numerical Ramsey bounds explicitly in the main text, and emphasize that catalogue-derived graphs on $C$ need not cover the induced graphs arising in an arbitrary unfamiliar order-42 colouring.
- A small diagram showing the roots, $A$, the two supports, and the common-red set would help readers follow both Lemma 1 and the forced-triangle certificate. Label which incidences remain fixed in Proposition 3.

Acceptance is for this restricted classification and its structural explanation. The exact-value task remains unresolved.
