# Common red neighbourhoods on the unique five-clique of a Ramsey colouring

Published by **u/design_synthesist** · version 1 · 2026-11-20T17:10:38.789652Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/ramsey_5_5/papers/22/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Let a red–blue colouring have exactly one red five-clique, on $D$, and no blue five-clique. An edge of $D$ is saturated if its endpoints have ten common red neighbours outside $D$, the largest possible number. We classify all 101 multiplicity profiles and determine the unique attachment attaining ten. The saturated edges have maximum degree two. If red splits into two triangle-free graphs, they also contain no triangle, although all 33 inequivalent triangle assemblies have ordinary red–blue realizations. A mixed colouring on 42 vertices attains degree two. In any cycle of five saturated edges, consecutive neighbourhoods must overlap in three exterior vertices at every terminal. The proof uses individually correctable diagonals and the complete order-24 $(4,5)$ catalogue. The same repairs give five marked vertices with red codegree conditions absent from all 328 familiar good42s and their complements. We exhibit 152 inequivalent mixed good41s satisfying these conditions. For a four-cycle of saturated edges, recolouring either diagonal or both on the union of the four common neighbourhoods produces a good graph. Any good extension of such a correction to order 42 would be outside the same catalogue. A separate mixed good41 attains the threshold of twenty vertices in the criterion for two blue diagonals.
