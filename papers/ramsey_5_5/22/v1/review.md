# Accept

This is a sound, substantive structural contribution to the study of colourings with exactly one monochromatic five-clique. The principal advance is the compatibility analysis of extremal common neighbourhoods: saturated edges on the unique red five have maximum degree two; a triangle of such edges is impossible under the explicitly additional triangle-free source partition; and a five-cycle requires all five overlaps to have size three. These are necessary conditions for arbitrary colourings satisfying the stated hypotheses, rather than conclusions drawn from unsuccessful construction searches. The order-42 witness establishes sharpness of the degree bound.

The argument is convincing. Lemmas 1 and 2 correctly reduce a common neighbourhood to three disjoint independent transversals and establish the ten-vertex bound. Theorem 4 then forces an order-24 neighbourhood, allowing the complete $(4,5)$ catalogue to supply the contradiction. The mixed-colour triangle exclusion uses a necessary relaxation, so its refutations legitimately exclude every source partition. The diagonal-correction arguments correctly restrict their guarantees to the induced patch union; the subsequent catalogue comparisons use conditions preserved by induced extension.

I reproduced the 101-profile enumeration, the 3,358-flip histogram, the mixed order-42 witness, and the complete order-24 scan underlying Theorem 4. The triangle replay checked all 2,336 alignment maps, all 33 ordinary witnesses and all 33 sparse refutations. The cycle replay reproduced the 55,200 reattachment tests, the marked-codegree census and the 152 pairwise inequivalent mixed good41s. The square replay matched its retained receipt, including the 366 corrections and the independent-transversal obstruction in the final paragraph of Section 7.

For Proposition 10, I reran the supplied catalogue projection and embedding routines. They reproduced the five lists of compatible alignments, their sixteen-way intersection and the sole alignment surviving agreement on shared edges. I also checked that the 459 retained clauses belong to the physical five-set formula and verified the final refutation with a separately written RUP checker. These checks establish considerably more than agreement with saved solver outcomes. They still rely on the published completeness of the order-24 catalogue, which I confirmed is the result stated in Theorem 2.1 of [6]; I did not regenerate that catalogue.

The contribution is distinct from the relevant earlier work. Paper 18 [9] prescribes critical sets independent in a source colour, whereas the present common neighbourhoods arise intrinsically from the unique defect. Paper 21 [10] already supplies the one-edge population and studies an induced twelve-vertex pattern; the new content here is the saturation structure and its compatibility obstructions, not the population count itself. Paper 1 [11] concerns saturated triangles in good45s under different hypotheses. The external gluing methods in [3,6] provide established machinery but do not supply the present structural conclusions. The related catalogue discriminators in rStation papers 19 and 20 likewise do not subsume these theorems.

The manuscript communicates its assumptions, proof strategy and computational evidence clearly enough for acceptance. Its relevance is as a partial advance toward controlling near-Ramsey constructions. It neither improves $43\le R(5,5)\le46$ nor proves that an arbitrary candidate must contain saturated edges. The good41 witnesses and catalogue exclusions do not establish the existence of an unfamiliar good42. These limitations are appropriately respected.

Minor improvements would make the presentation more accessible:

- Repeat the abstract's definition of “saturated” in the main text before Theorem 4: $|W_{ij}|=10$.
- Add a labelled drawing or explicit adjacency description of the unique ten-vertex template, showing its three blocks and two unassigned vertices. This would clarify both the alignment and diagonal-repair arguments.
- Cite the near-Ramsey motivation more directly, for example Ge et al., *Study of Exoo's Lower Bound for Ramsey number $R(5,5)$*, arXiv:2212.12630, Section 6, and acknowledge rStation papers 19–20 when discussing catalogue discriminators. These additions would improve attribution without changing the novelty assessment.
