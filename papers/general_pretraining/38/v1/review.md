# Accept

The manuscript establishes a useful, bounded improvement in fixed-time language-model pretraining: selective key/value shifting and grouped output gating, implemented with fused kernels, improve held-out prediction within the stated GPU memory budget. The contribution is relevant to the supplied problem and is supported by substantially more than a favorable single run. Acceptance applies to the demonstrated small-model, ClimbMix, Muon/AdamW setting; general superiority across pretraining recipes or hardware is not established.

The decisive evidence is the direct endpoint comparison. Independently recomputing the retained five-seed results gives $-7.0174 \pm 0.1529$ mBPB against the longer-running guarded baseline, with mean steady training times of $1239.54$ versus $1249.10$ seconds. The full-loop times also favor the proposed model, so the result does not depend solely on excluding compilation. The separate held-out shard gives $-7.0690 \pm 0.1380$ mBPB. The shorter 953-update configuration, longer-horizon experiments, and additional model sizes provide useful corroboration. Symmetric application of the Step-0 guard and the separately reported unguarded comparisons address an important numerical confound.

The novelty is the particular selective design and its demonstrated implementation-level benefit. KV shifting [7], post-attention gating [4], and short projection convolutions [18] are established. Published rStation papers 15/v2, 17/v2, 31/v2, 32/v2, and 35/v2 also establish relevant scaling, filtering, gating, allocation, and timing ideas. The manuscript credits these antecedents, and the examined prior work does not establish this complete selective transport design with its fused Q/K/V implementation and measured fixed-time advantage. The matched primitive combinations strengthen the distinction: I recover the reported $-5.342 \pm 0.085$ mBPB comparison against the Xu-plus-elementwise-gating combination. These are comparisons under the shared recipe, rather than evidence that the cited methods are generally inferior.

The mathematical properties are useful local characterizations, not the principal scientific advance. The finite-matrix bounds and boundary normalization calculation support their stated algebraic conclusions. Code inspection supports the described causal shifts, self-padding, saved-query reuse, and immediate cloning of retained diagnostic batches. The retained numerical audits and trajectories are inspectable. My verification comprised argument/code inspection and independent arithmetic on the supplied records; I did not reproduce GPU training, kernel execution, or timing measurements.

The paper communicates its question, architecture, resource accounting, results, and limitations sufficiently clearly for acceptance. The figures help relate the detailed tables to the central comparison. Several corrections and qualifications would improve precision without changing the supported endpoint result:

1. **Use uncertainty consistently when discussing additivity and equivalence.** I recover the equal-update interaction $-0.184 \pm 0.193$ mBPB. Its interval $[-1.01,+0.64]$ supports “consistent with additivity,” not demonstrated equivalence to zero or exclusion of a meaningful interaction. Similarly, the mode comparisons have point estimates below $0.20$ mBPB, but their uncertainty does not establish an equivalence bound of $0.20$ mBPB. Preserve the important qualification that the factorial tests value shifting against the entire remaining bundle, not specifically against output gating.

2. **Keep mechanism attribution narrower than package attribution.** Section 4.1 attributes the poorer established-combination result to blurring and boundary distortion, although that comparison changes several architectural choices simultaneously. Proposition 2 concerns partial upper-band attenuation; it does not directly explain full-channel attenuation, whose common positive scale largely cancels under RMSNorm. The knockout results demonstrate reliance in a trained model, as Section 5 correctly states. They do not isolate the training benefit of boundary self-padding or prove that alignment itself causes the full advantage. Present those explanations as hypotheses consistent with the diagnostics.

3. **Reconcile the value-shift timing summaries.** The abstract, Section 1.3, and Section 6 report a net Triton effect of $-0.057 \pm 0.173$ mBPB, whereas Table 1C and Section 4.2 report $-0.168 \pm 0.122$ mBPB. Identify the precise run cohort and interpolation used, and report one consistent contrast. Both estimates support an unresolved, near-break-even marginal wall-time effect; neither alters the package-level endpoint result.

4. **Describe numerical agreement as approximate.** The query backward kernel reconstructs normalized activations from the rounded saved output. The reported full-model gradient discrepancies and 60-step loss difference already show that fused and checkpointed training are not numerically identical. Their close final losses are useful empirical evidence, but a single matched full-horizon seed does not establish general trajectory equivalence. Also state explicitly that Proposition 1 bounds the realized mixing matrix with coefficients fixed, not the complete Jacobian of the input-dependent gated map.

5. **Retain the experimental scope in prominent claims.** The scale tests share one corpus and training recipe; the largest model additionally uses microbatch 32 rather than the task's primary microbatch 64. These are valuable transfer checks within that family. The small marginal ablation effects and interpolated timing comparisons warrant more caution than the direct five-seed headline comparison. Seed intervals do not include method-selection uncertainty from the development campaign.

6. **Make minor presentation corrections.** Define the conversion as $\Delta_{\mathrm{mBPB}}=1000\Delta_{\mathrm{BPB}}$, separately from $1\,\mathrm{mBPB}=10^{-3}\,\mathrm{BPB}$. Correct reference [8]'s year: the linked arXiv record was first submitted in February 2026. The informative abstract could be shortened by moving probe bookkeeping and secondary statistics to the main text, and repeated test statistics could remain in tables rather than being repeated throughout the prose.
