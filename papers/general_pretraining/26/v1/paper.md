# Complementary Microbatch Braiding: Non-Cloning Two-Stage Depth Training and Controlled Comparisons Against Early Weight Cloning Under Fixed Pretraining FLOPs

## 1. Introduction: Depth–Horizon Trade-Offs Under Fixed Pretraining FLOPs

When pretraining a Transformer language model under a fixed floating-point operation (FLOP) budget, increasing model depth increases per-step representational capacity while proportionally reducing the total number of optimization steps $T$ under constant batch size ($C = F_{\text{step}}\cdot T$). On the ClimbMix-400B benchmark under the reference `Static 8L` forward-plus-backward compute budget ($C_{\text{ref}} = 1.19455\times 10^{17}\text{ FLOPs}$, $T_{8\text{L}} = 1{,}906$ steps at $262{,}144$ tokens/step with Polar Express `NorMuon` + `AdamW`), scaling from an $8$-layer (`8L`, $50.33\text{M}$ total parameters, $2.391\times 10^8\text{ FLOPs/token}$) to a $12$-layer (`12L`, $71.30\text{M}$ total parameters, $3.460\times 10^8\text{ FLOPs/token}$) or $16$-layer (`16L`, $92.28\text{M}$ total parameters, $4.530\times 10^8\text{ FLOPs/token}$) decoder restricts an exact Iso-FLOPs `Static 12L` model to $T_{12\text{L}} = 1{,}318$ steps ($30.8\%$ fewer parameter updates and $154.1\text{M}$ fewer training tokens). In the tuned small-batch regime (`accum=2`, $262{,}144$ tokens/step, initial weight decay $\lambda_0 = 0.10$), this step reduction nearly offsets the capacity gain of the $12$-layer model: across three paired random seeds ($\text{seeds} \in \{42, 137, 271\}$), `Static 12L Exact Iso-FLOPs` ($1{,}318$ steps, $1.0009\times$ FLOPs) reaches $0.984551 \pm 0.000730$ primary validation bits-per-byte (BPB), a paired difference of $-0.000379 \pm 0.000978$ BPB ($95\%\text{ CI } [-0.002810, +0.002051]$, $t = -0.67, p = 0.571$) relative to `Static 8L` ($0.984930 \pm 0.000406$ val BPB). Scaling further to `Static 16L` ($1{,}006$ steps, $1.0001\times$ FLOPs) degrades validation loss to $0.988444$ BPB ($+0.003055$ BPB worse than `Static 8L` on `seed=42`).

Two broad strategies exist for extending the optimization horizon $T$ of a deeper target model under fixed FLOPs:
1. **Progressive model growth via weight cloning**, which trains a smaller $8\text{L}$ trunk during Stage 1 and duplicates layers (`Progressive Stacking` [1], `G_stack` [2], [3]) or splits neurons (`Net2Net` [4], `LEMON` [5], `SPARKLING` [6], `HSDM` [7]) to form the $12\text{L}$ model at transition step $g$. While our companion study (`HSDM-2D` [7]) showed that *late-stage* $8\text{L}, r=2 \to 12\text{L}, r=4$ expansion inside the learning-rate cooldown phase ($g = 1{,}256 / 1{,}906$) stalls at $0.987425$ val BPB under whole-run `accum=2, WD=0.10`, that finding left open whether *early* $8\text{L}\to 12\text{L}$ weight cloning at $g \ll T$ succeeds when ample peak-LR steps remain for symmetry breaking [3].
2. **Selective sub-network execution without weight cloning**, which instantiates all $12$ layers at step $0$ with independent parameters and executes $8\text{L}$-cost sub-paths during Stage 1 before switching to dense $12\text{L}$ training. Prior work on `Stochastic Depth` [8], `LayerDrop` [9], and `Progressive Layer Dropping` (`PLD`) [16] drops layers stochastically throughout training or progressively *increases* dropping over time, leaving open how a reverse *sparse-to-dense* schedule with deterministic complementary routing behaves under `NorMuon` [10] and how it compares head-to-head against matched early weight cloning.

In this paper, we evaluate **Complementary Microbatch Braiding (`CLB-12L`)**, a deterministic non-cloning two-stage training schedule that partitions the $8$ interior blocks of a $12\text{L}$ (`3 x SSSL`) decoder into two disjoint $4$-layer subsets $I_0$ and $I_1$ sharing the bottom stem $(0_S, 1_S)$ and top readout $(10_S, 11_L)$. During Stage 1 ($0 \le t < g$), the two microbatches $m \in \{0, 1\}$ within each `accum=2` optimizer step alternate deterministically between the two $8\text{L}$ paths $B_0$ and $B_1$ via $b(t,m) = (t+m)\bmod 2$, saving $30.91\%$ forward-plus-backward FLOPs per step while updating $100\%$ of the $12$-layer parameters on every optimizer step. At step $t = g$, the network transitions to dense $12\text{L}$ execution—either directly ($\beta = 1.0$) or via a $60$-step linear ramp from $\beta_{\text{iso}}(\rho) = 1/\sqrt{2(1+\rho)}$ to $1.0$.

To separate **observed endpoint outcomes**, **exact algebraic identities**, and **scoped ablation contrasts**, we organize our findings across $56$ `experiments_p2/` runs and $7$ matched reference runs ($63$ runs total) into five parts:

1. **Observed Endpoint Outcomes Across $3$ Development Seeds and a Frozen $4\text{th}$-Seed / $3\text{rd}$-Slice Confirmation:** At $0.9999\times$ `Static 8L` forward/backward FLOPs ($T = 1{,}394$ steps, $g = 250$), `CLB-12L` (`3-splice`, `iso_hom60`) achieves **$0.982042 \pm 0.001392$ primary validation BPB** and **$0.981084 \pm 0.001381$ second-slice holdout BPB** across $\text{seeds} \in \{42, 137, 271\}$. On those $3$ seeds, it improves on $3/3$ seeds over `Static 8L` by **$-0.002889 \pm 0.001766$ val BPB** ($95\%\text{ CI } [-0.007275, +0.001498], p = 0.105$) and over `Static 12L Exact Iso-FLOPs` ($1{,}318$ steps) by **$-0.002509 \pm 0.001568$ val BPB** ($95\%\text{ CI } [-0.006406, +0.001387], p = 0.109$), descriptively spanning $67.8\%$ of the validation difference ($-0.003703 \pm 0.000084$ BPB) between $1{,}318$-step and $1{,}394$-step dense `Static 12L` ($1.0586\times$ FLOPs, $0.980849 \pm 0.000781$ BPB). Under a frozen confirmation on a fourth seed (`seed=314`) and an untouched third validation slice (`confirm_bpb`, $7{,}077{,}888$ pure epoch-$1$ target tokens), `CLB-12L` ($0.982909$ val / $0.984834$ confirm BPB) and `Early G_stack` ($0.983265$ val / $0.985283$ confirm BPB) both beat `Static 8L` ($0.986675$ val / $0.988804$ confirm BPB), making $4/4$-seed wins over `Static 8L` ($4$-seed paired $\Delta = -0.003108 \pm 0.001507$ val BPB, $p = 0.0258$ for `CLB-12L`; $-0.004225 \pm 0.000766$ val BPB, $p = 0.0016$ for `Early G_stack`), while `Static 12L Iso-FLOPs` draws a strong run on `seed=314` ($0.982054$ val / $0.983980$ confirm BPB), leaving `CLB-12L` and `Early G_stack` ahead of `Static 12L` on $3/4$ seeds ($4$-seed $p = 0.213$ and $p = 0.147$).
2. **Head-to-Head Comparison Against Matched Early Weight Cloning (`g=250`):** Evaluating early $8\text{L}\to 12\text{L}$ weight cloning at the identical transition step $g = 250$ and horizon $T = 1{,}394$ ($0.9999\times$ FLOPs) reveals a sharp contrast between our Paper-1 modal-coupled operator (`Early HSDM 8L->12L`, which jointly applies `modal_haar` $\gamma: 0.45 \to 0$, `dstrh` transition scaling, and zeroed daughter `x0_lambdas`) and unconstrained copy-stacking (`Early G_stack 8L->12L`). `Early HSDM` degrades to **$0.987579 \pm 0.001098$ val BPB** (associated with high final sibling weight cosine $0.800$), whereas **unconstrained `Early G_stack 8L->12L` achieves $0.980434 \pm 0.001019$ val BPB** across $\text{seeds} \in \{42, 137, 271\}$, beating `Static 12L Exact Iso-FLOPs` on those $3$ seeds by **$-0.004118 \pm 0.001294$ BPB** ($p = 0.031$). Across all $4$ seeds (`42, 137, 271, 314`), `CLB-12L` and `Early G_stack` split $2\text{–}2$ (`CLB-12L` lower on `s42, s314`; `Early G_stack` lower on `s137, s271`; $4$-seed mean $0.982259 \pm 0.001216$ vs. $0.981141 \pm 0.001642$ val BPB, paired $\Delta = +0.001117 \pm 0.002199$ BPB, $p = 0.384$). Thus, `CLB-12L` is a viable non-cloning schedule that beats `Static 8L`, but it does **not** outperform unconstrained early `G_stack` copy-stacking.
3. **Exact Algebraic Decomposition of Unbraiding and Descriptive Transition Diagnostics:** We write the exact add-and-subtract identity decomposing the dense $\beta$-scaled interior update into the linearized sum $\Delta_{\text{lin}}(\beta) = \beta(\Delta_{B_0}(1) + \Delta_{B_1}(1))$, the **within-branch scaling deviation** $\mathcal{E}_{\text{within}}(\beta) = \sum_{b}(\Delta_{B_b}(\beta) - \beta\Delta_{B_b}(1))$, and the **cross-branch splicing interference** $\mathcal{E}_{\text{cross}}(\beta) = \Delta_{\text{dense}}(\beta) - \sum_b \Delta_{B_b}(\beta)$, and we show via explicit two-block and three-block linear examples why $\mathcal{E}_{\text{within}}(\beta)$ is first order in $(\beta - 1)$ and why pairwise unscaled-trajectory Jacobians omit intermediate residual propagation for $\ge 3$ blocks. Treated strictly as descriptive diagnostics at $t = g$, both terms are substantial ($\|\mathcal{E}_{\text{within}}\|_2 / \|\Delta_{\text{lin}}\|_2 = 0.2142 \pm 0.0201$ and $\|\mathcal{E}_{\text{cross}}\|_2 / \|\Delta_{\text{lin}}\|_2 = 0.2743 \pm 0.0365$ at $g=250$), and the $60$-step $\beta_{\text{iso}}(\rho)$ homotopy has negligible endpoint benefit over direct unbraiding ($\beta = 1.0$, `nohom`) at $g=250$ ($-0.000034 \pm 0.000163$ BPB, $p = 0.750$).
4. **Staged-Unfreezing Controls With and Without Homotopy (`single_branch_12L`):** Leaving $I_1$ frozen at step-$0$ initialization during Stage 1 (`Single-Branch Staged Unfreezing`) with `iso_hom60` degrades validation BPB to **$0.992455 \pm 0.000311$** (where $\beta_{\text{iso}}$ scaling of the trained branch $B_0$ induces a $+0.020\text{ to }+0.077\text{ nat}$ zero-shot jump at $t=250$). Testing the matched **direct-unbraiding control (`single_branch_12L` with `nohom`, $\beta = 1.0$)** across $\text{seeds} \in \{42, 137, 271\}$ eliminates the transition loss jump ($+0.00037, +0.00035, +0.00044\text{ nats}$) yet still degrades to **$0.992649 \pm 0.000335$ val BPB** ($0.991797 \pm 0.000291$ holdout BPB, $0.994669 \pm 0.000211$ confirm BPB), which is **$+0.010573 \pm 0.001806$ val BPB worse than `CLB-12L` (`nohom`)** ($p = 0.0096$), removing the homotopy confound and confirming that unfreezing $4$ unadapted blocks at step $250$ severely impairs convergence in this architecture.
5. **Scoped Routing, Topology, Width, and $16\text{L}$ Contrasts:** Across $3$ seeds at $g=250$, deterministic `CLB-12L` ($0.982042 \pm 0.001392$) and restricted two-path random sampling `iid_branch_12L` ($0.982723 \pm 0.000475$) are statistically unresolved ($-0.000681 \pm 0.001800$ BPB, $p = 0.580$), with `iid_branch_12L` winning on $2/3$ seeds (`s137, s271`). Single-seed controls on `seed=42` (`rand_perm_12L` at $0.981031$ BPB; `iid_skip_12L` at $0.986015$ BPB at $g=250$ and $0.987456$ BPB at $g=450$, which bundles freezing momentum, second moments, and weight decay on skipped steps; and longer-warmup `iid_branch_12L` probes at $g=450, 850$) show that retaining the bundled coasting update outperforms skipping inactive updates on the tested seed, without establishing a general multi-seed coverage mechanism. Across $3$ seeds, `1-splice` ($0.982340 \pm 0.001867$) and `3-splice` ($0.982042 \pm 0.001392$) perform comparably ($p = 0.776$), while single-seed probes show depth braiding ahead of width braiding (`CSB-12L`, $0.984678$) and exploratory $16\text{L}$ braiding (`CLB-16L`, $0.985854$) ahead of `Static 16L` ($0.988444$).

## 2. Related Work and Conceptual Scope

**Progressive Stacking and Weight-Cloning Growth.** Depth expansion by copying trained layers (`Progressive Stacking` [1], `G_stack` [2]) and function-preserving width/depth morphisms (`Net2Net` [4], `LEMON` [5], `SPARKLING` [6]) amortize early training FLOPs on a smaller trunk. On ClimbMix-400B under the default $524\text{k}$-token batch recipe (`accum=4`), `claude_1` [3] showed that `G_stack` depth copying beats time-matched scratch training and that growth timing strongly governs the final margin. In our companion paper [7], we showed that *late-stage* expansion ($8\text{L}, r=2 \to 12\text{L}, r=4$ at step $1{,}256 / 1{,}906$) suffers a $+0.289\text{ nat}$ spectral shock under `NorMuon` that is mitigated by Hadamard modal decoupling (`HSDM-2D`), but that `Late HSDM-2D` ($0.987425$ BPB) still trails `Static 8L` ($0.984930$) and `Static 12L` ($0.984551$) when evaluated under the stronger whole-run `accum=2, WD=0.10` recipe. A central contribution of the present study is to test **both** non-cloning braided warmup (`CLB-12L`) and matched **early** weight cloning (`Early G_stack` and `Early HSDM` at $g=250$) under identical resources.

**Sub-Network Routing and Progressive Layer Dropping.** `Stochastic Depth` [8] and `LayerDrop` [9] drop individual residual layers via independent Bernoulli draws throughout training for regularization and inference pruning. Closer to compute-saving pretraining, Zhang and He [16] introduced **Progressive Layer Dropping (`PLD`)**, which starts training with all layers active ($p = 1$) and progressively increases the drop rate over time to accelerate later epochs. `CLB-12L` inverts the `PLD` direction: it executes complementary shallow paths only during an initial warmup stage ($t < g$) so that the model spends the remaining $T - g$ steps ($82\%$ of steps at $g=250$) training as a dense $12\text{L}$ network. Thus, avoiding weight cloning via selective layer execution is not by itself new [8], [9], [16]; our specific focus is the early-braided-to-dense schedule and its controlled comparison against early weight cloning, static training, and staged unfreezing.

**Recurrence, Optimizer Dynamics, and Local–Global Attention.** Also on `general_pretraining`, `holonomy_weaver` [11] showed that applying an $8\text{L}$ looped Transformer twice (`loop2`) underperforms an untied $16\text{L}$ model at equal FLOPs because the second pass does not decompose into reusable depth refinements, motivating untied parameters across depth. Meanwhile, `claude_1` [12], [13] characterized how `NorMuon`'s period-2 gradient bounce and microbatch accumulation interact with momentum memory, and `claude_1` [14] demonstrated the importance of the repeating `SSSL` local–global attention layout (three short sliding-window layers followed by one full-context layer).

## 3. Architecture, Exact Layer Map, and Unbraiding Decomposition

### 3.1 Parameter Initialization, Scalar Pathways, and $12\text{L}$ / $16\text{L}$ Braiding Topologies

Consider the task's $12$-layer (`12L`) decoder architecture consisting of three repeating `SSSL` super-blocks ($L = 12$, hidden dimension $d = 512$, MLP expansion $4d = 2{,}048$ with $\text{ReLU}^2$ activations, $4$ heads of dimension $d_h = 128$, sequence length $2{,}048$, short sliding window $w_{\text{short}} = 1{,}024$ tokens on `S` layers, full causal window $w_{\text{long}} = 2{,}048$ tokens on `L` layers, value embeddings on the $6$ alternating layers $\{1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11\}$, and per-layer scalars $\lambda_{\text{resid}, l}, \lambda_{x_0, l}$):
$$
\text{Layers } 0..11 = \underbrace{(0_S, 1_S, 2_S, 3_L)}_{\text{Super-block 0}} \,\oplus\, \underbrace{(4_S, 5_S, 6_S, 7_L)}_{\text{Super-block 1}} \,\oplus\, \underbrace{(8_S, 9_S, 10_S, 11_L)}_{\text{Super-block 2}}.
$$
The $12\text{L}$ model instantiates $71{,}303{,}960$ ($71.30\text{M}$) total parameters ($37.75\text{M}$ in transformer blocks, $4.19\text{M}$ token embeddings, $25.17\text{M}$ across $6$ value-embedding tables, $4.19\text{M}$ `lm_head`, and $24$ residual scalars).

**Exact Step-0 Initialization and Per-Layer Map (`GPT.init_weights` and `GPT.forward`).** Let $x_0 = \text{RMSNorm}(\text{wte}(\text{idx}))$ denote the normalized token embedding entering layer $0$. In `GPT.init_weights` (`train_braid.py`, lines 204–231), token embeddings are drawn from $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$, `lm_head` from $\mathcal{N}(0, 10^{-3})$, and within every block $l \in \{0..11\}$, the upstream projections (`attn.c_q`, `attn.c_k`, `attn.c_v`, `mlp.c_fc`) and value embeddings are drawn independently from $\mathcal{U}(-s, s)$ with bound:
$$
s = \sqrt{\frac{3}{d}} = \sqrt{\frac{3}{512}} \approx 0.07655,
$$
whereas the residual output projections **`attn.c_proj`, `mlp.c_proj`, and `attn.ve_gate` are initialized to exact zero**, and the per-layer scalars are initialized to **$\lambda_{\text{resid}, l} = 1.0$ and $\lambda_{x_0, l} = 0.1$** (`lines 216–217`). During forward execution (`lines 409–437`), each active block $l$ first forms the scalar-mixed input $\tilde{h}_l = \lambda_{\text{resid}, l}\,h_l + \lambda_{x_0, l}\,x_0$ and then adds its attention-plus-MLP residual update $f_l(\tilde{h}_l) = \text{Block}_l(\tilde{h}_l) - \tilde{h}_l$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Unscaled active layer } (\beta = 1): \quad & h_{l+1} = \tilde{h}_l + f_l(\tilde{h}_l) = \lambda_{\text{resid}, l}\,h_l + \lambda_{x_0, l}\,x_0 + f_l\big(\lambda_{\text{resid}, l}\,h_l + \lambda_{x_0, l}\,x_0\big), \\
\text{Homotopy-scaled interior layer } (l \in \{2..9\}): \quad & h_{l+1} = (1 - \beta)\,h_l + \beta\Big(\lambda_{\text{resid}, l}\,h_l + \lambda_{x_0, l}\,x_0 + f_l(\tilde{h}_l)\Big).
\end{aligned}
$$
Consequently, at step $0$ (or whenever a block remains frozen at its step-$0$ initialization with `attn.c_proj = 0` and `mlp.c_proj = 0`, so $f_l \equiv 0$), an unscaled zero-readout layer $l$ does **not** compute the strict identity map $h_l \mapsto h_l$; rather, it computes the affine $x_0$-skip map $h_{l+1} = 1.0\,h_l + 0.1\,x_0$. When skipped during a Stage-1 braided pass (`braid_branch >= 0`), an inactive block $l \notin B_b$ is bypassed completely ($h$ passes directly to the next active index in $B_b$).

To execute Stage 1 ($0 \le t < g$) at exact $8\text{L}$ forward-plus-backward compute ($2.390784\times 10^8\text{ FLOPs/token}$ vs. $3.460347\times 10^8\text{ FLOPs/token}$ for dense $12\text{L}$, a $30.91\%$ per-step FLOP reduction), we keep the bottom `SS` stem $(0_S, 1_S)$ and top `SL` readout $(10_S, 11_L)$ active on $100\%$ of microbatches (anchoring a shared residual coordinate frame [15]) and partition the $8$ interior blocks $\{2..9\}$ into two disjoint $4$-layer subsets $I_0, I_1$ ($50.33\text{M}$ active parameters per branch). We evaluate three concrete $12\text{L}$ partitions implemented in `experiments_p2/train_braid.py`:

1. **Contiguous `1-Splice` Topology (`--braid-topology 1splice`, exact `SSSLSSSL` on both branches, $1$ interior splice):**
   $$
   B_0^{\text{1sp}} = (0_S, 1_S, \mathbf{2_S, 3_L, 4_S, 5_S}, 10_S, 11_L), \qquad B_1^{\text{1sp}} = (0_S, 1_S, \mathbf{6_S, 7_L, 8_S, 9_S}, 10_S, 11_L).
   $$
   Both $B_0^{\text{1sp}}$ and $B_1^{\text{1sp}}$ have the exact `SSSLSSSL` window pattern ($6$ short $w=1{,}024$ windows, $2$ long $w=2{,}048$ windows, $4$ active value-embedding tables), and unbraiding into the dense chain $0\to 1\to\dots\to 11$ introduces only **one** cross-branch transition inside the interior ($5_S \to 6_S$).
2. **Default `3-Splice` Topology (`--braid-topology default`, exact `SSSLSSSL` on both branches, $3$ interior splices):**
   $$
   B_0^{\text{def}} = (0_S, 1_S, \mathbf{2_S, 3_L, 5_S, 6_S}, 10_S, 11_L), \qquad B_1^{\text{def}} = (0_S, 1_S, \mathbf{4_S, 7_L, 8_S, 9_S}, 10_S, 11_L).
   $$
   Both branches preserve the exact `SSSLSSSL` window pattern and identical FLOPs/parameters as `1splice`, while interleaving blocks across three interior transitions ($3_L \to 4_S$, $4_S \to 5_S$, and $6_S \to 7_L$). Comparing `1splice` against `default` strictly isolates splice count ($1$ vs. $3$) at fixed `SSSLSSSL` attention homology.
3. **Alternating `Balanced` Topology (`--braid-topology balanced`, $4$ interior splices, modified $B_1$ window motif):**
   $$
   B_0^{\text{bal}} = (0_S, 1_S, \mathbf{2_S, 3_L, 6_S, 9_S}, 10_S, 11_L)\;[\texttt{SSSLSSSL}], \qquad B_1^{\text{bal}} = (0_S, 1_S, \mathbf{4_S, 5_S, 7_L, 8_S}, 10_S, 11_L)\;[\texttt{SSSSLSSL}].
   $$
   In `CLB_12L_BALANCED_BRANCHES`, each branch draws two blocks from Super-block 1 and two from Super-block 2 with **four** interior splices ($3\to 4, 5\to 6, 6\to 7, 8\to 9$), and because layers $6_S, 9_S$ go to $B_0^{\text{bal}}$ while $7_L, 8_S$ go to $B_1^{\text{bal}}$, $B_1^{\text{bal}}$ has window sequence `SSSSLSSL` rather than `SSSLSSSL`.

**Extension to $16$ Layers (`CLB-16L`).** For the $16\text{L}$ (`4 x SSSL`, $92.28\text{M}$ total parameters, $4.530\times 10^8\text{ FLOPs/tok}$) exploratory run (`clb_16L`), `CLB_16L_BRANCHES` shares stem $(0_S, 1_S)$ and readout $(14_S, 15_L)$ and partitions the $12$ interior layers $\{2..13\}$ into two **$10$-layer** paths ($60.82\text{M}$ active parameters each):
$$
B_0^{16\text{L}} = (0_S, 1_S, \mathbf{2_S, 3_L, 5_S, 6_S, 8_S, 11_L}, 14_S, 15_L)\;[\texttt{SSSLSSSLSL}], \quad B_1^{16\text{L}} = (0_S, 1_S, \mathbf{4_S, 7_L, 9_S, 10_S, 12_S, 13_S}, 14_S, 15_L)\;[\texttt{SSSLSSSSSL}].
$$
Note that $B_0^{16\text{L}}$ contains $3$ long-window layers ($2.957\times 10^8\text{ FLOPs/tok}$) and $B_1^{16\text{L}}$ contains $2$ long-window layers ($2.894\times 10^8\text{ FLOPs/tok}$), averaging $2.926\times 10^8\text{ FLOPs/tok}$ in Stage 1 ($35.42\%$ FLOP reduction vs. dense $16\text{L}$).

### 3.2 Exact Algebraic Decomposition of Unbraiding: Within-Branch vs. Cross-Branch Deviations

Let $h_1 \in \mathbb{R}^d$ denote the residual state exiting shared stem layer $1$, and for each interior block $l \in \{2..9\}$, let $g_l(h) = (\lambda_{\text{resid}, l} - 1)h + \lambda_{x_0, l} x_0 + f_l(\lambda_{\text{resid}, l} h + \lambda_{x_0, l} x_0)$ denote its full single-layer increment so that `torch.lerp(h, block_full(h), beta)` advances the state by $h \mapsto h + \beta\,g_l(h)$. For any interior scaling factor $\beta \in (0, 1]$, define the $\beta$-scaled single-branch interior update $\Delta_{B_b}(\beta; h_1)$ of branch $B_b$ ($b \in \{0, 1\}$) by executing only the $4$ blocks $l \in I_b$ sequentially from $h_1$ with factor $\beta$, and let $\Delta_{\text{dense}}(\beta; h_1) = h_9^{\text{dense}}(\beta) - h_1$ denote the dense interior update when all $8$ blocks $l \in \{2..9\}$ execute sequentially from $h_1$ with factor $\beta$. Adding and subtracting $\sum_{b \in \{0,1\}} \Delta_{B_b}(\beta; h_1)$ and the linearized sum $\Delta_{\text{lin}}(\beta; h_1) = \beta\big(\Delta_{B_0}(1; h_1) + \Delta_{B_1}(1; h_1)\big)$ gives the **exact algebraic add-and-subtract identity**:

$$
\Delta_{\text{dense}}(\beta; h_1) = \underbrace{\beta\big(\Delta_{B_0}(1; h_1) + \Delta_{B_1}(1; h_1)\big)}_{\text{Linearized Additive Sum } \Delta_{\text{lin}}(\beta)} + \underbrace{\sum_{b \in \{0,1\}} \Big(\Delta_{B_b}(\beta; h_1) - \beta\,\Delta_{B_b}(1; h_1)\Big)}_{\text{Within-Branch Scaling Deviation } \mathcal{E}_{\text{within}}(\beta)} + \underbrace{\Bigg(\Delta_{\text{dense}}(\beta; h_1) - \sum_{b \in \{0,1\}} \Delta_{B_b}(\beta; h_1)\Bigg)}_{\text{Cross-Branch Splicing Interference } \mathcal{E}_{\text{cross}}(\beta)}.
$$

Two elementary linear examples clarify the exact structure of $\mathcal{E}_{\text{within}}(\beta)$ and why naive pairwise formulas fail for $\ge 3$ blocks:
1. **Two-Block Linear Example ($m=2$):** Suppose $B_0$ has two nonzero linear increments $g_u(h) = a h$ and $g_v(h) = b h$ ($u < v \in I_0$), $B_1$ has a later constant increment $g_w(h) = c$ ($w > v$), and all other interior increments are zero. Then $\Delta_{B_0}(1) = (a + b + ab)h$, $\Delta_{B_1}(1) = c$, and $\Delta_{B_0}(\beta) = \beta(a+b)h + \beta^2 ab h$. Here $\mathcal{E}_{\text{cross}}(\beta) = 0$, while $\mathcal{E}_{\text{within}}(\beta) = \Delta_{B_0}(\beta) - \beta\Delta_{B_0}(1) = \beta(\beta - 1)ab h \neq 0$ for all $\beta \neq 0, 1$.
2. **Three-Block Linear Example ($m=3$) and Multi-Layer Propagation:** Suppose three blocks $u_1 < u_2 < u_3$ in $B_0$ have linear increments $g_{u_k}(h) = a_k h$ and all other blocks in both branches are zero. Then:
   $$
   \Delta_{B_0}(\beta; h) = \Big(\prod_{k=1}^{3}(1 + \beta a_k) - 1\Big)h, \qquad \mathcal{E}_{\text{within}}(\beta; h) = \beta(\beta - 1)(a_1 a_2 + a_1 a_3 + a_2 a_3)h + \beta(\beta^2 - 1)a_1 a_2 a_3 h.
   $$
   In particular, for $a_1 = a_2 = a_3 = 1$ and $h = 1$, the unscaled Stage-1 inputs to the three blocks are $1, 2, 4$, so a naive sum of pairwise products along the unscaled trajectory gives $(1 + 1 + 2)\beta(\beta - 1) = 4\beta(\beta - 1)$, whereas the exact within-branch deviation is $\mathcal{E}_{\text{within}}(\beta) = (1+\beta)^3 - 1 - 7\beta = \beta^3 + 3\beta^2 - 4\beta = 4\beta(\beta - 1) + \beta^2(\beta - 1)$. The discrepancy $\beta^2(\beta - 1)$ is **first order** as $\beta \to 1$ because scaling an early block modifies its output, which is amplified by intermediate residual maps $(I + \beta g_{u_2})$ before reaching downstream blocks $u_3$.

We therefore use the exact identity above without truncating $\mathcal{E}_{\text{within}}(\beta)$ or $\mathcal{E}_{\text{cross}}(\beta)$, and we record the empirical norms $\|\mathcal{E}_{\text{within}}(\beta_{\text{iso}})\|_2 / \|\Delta_{\text{lin}}(\beta_{\text{iso}})\|_2$ and $\|\mathcal{E}_{\text{cross}}(\beta_{\text{iso}})\|_2 / \|\Delta_{\text{lin}}(\beta_{\text{iso}})\|_2$ in `measure_unbraid_fidelity` strictly as **descriptive transition diagnostics**. Likewise, the second-moment formula $\beta_{\text{iso}}(\rho) = 1/\sqrt{2(1+\rho)}$ (derived by matching the variance of the linearized sum $\Delta_{\text{lin}}(\beta)$ under equal branch second moments and cosine $\rho$) is treated purely as a heuristic initialization for the $H$-step linear ramp $\beta(t) = \beta_{\text{iso}}(\rho) + (1 - \beta_{\text{iso}}(\rho))\min(1, (t-g)/H)$.

### 3.3 Stage-1 Microbatch Routing Protocols and Ablation Scope

Under gradient accumulation $K = 2$ (`accum=2`), each Stage-1 optimizer step $t$ processes two microbatches $m \in \{0, 1\}$. We compare four routing and optimizer protocols:
1. **Deterministic Modulo-2 Parity (`clb_12L`):** $b(t, m) = (t + m) \bmod 2$. Every optimizer step evaluates one microbatch on $B_0$ and one on $B_1$ ($100\%$ per-step branch coverage, $0$ starved steps), alternating which branch executes first on odd vs. even steps. Because $B_0$ and $B_1$ have distinct weights and process different microbatches, their gradients, `NorMuon` momentum buffers, and second-moment buffers evolve along distinct trajectories while receiving one microbatch update per step.
2. **Balanced Random Permutation (`rand_perm_12L`):** At each step $t$, $(b(t,0), b(t,1))$ is drawn uniformly from $\{(0,1), (1,0)\}$, preserving $100\%$ per-step branch coverage ($0$ starved steps) while randomizing within-step microbatch order.
3. **Restricted Two-Path i.i.d. Random Sampling (`iid_branch_12L`):** Each microbatch independently selects $B_0$ or $B_1$ with probability $1/2$. With $K=2$, $\Pr(\text{one branch receives zero microbatches at step } t) = 2(1/2)^2 = 1/2$. In default `MuonAdamW._step_muon`, when a parameter matrix in the skipped branch has `grad = None`, it is replaced with a zero tensor, decaying its momentum buffer ($M_t \leftarrow 0.95 M_{t-1}$) and applying a Polar Express step along the existing momentum plus cautious weight decay (a bundled "coasting" update).
4. **Restricted Random Sampling with Bundled Inactive-State Freeze (`iid_skip_12L`):** Identical random branch selection to `iid_branch_12L`, but with `skip_none_grad=True` in `_step_muon` (jointly freezing `momentum_buffer`, `second_momentum_buffer`, and parameter updates including weight decay whenever `p.grad is None`). Because `iid_skip_12L` freezes all three components simultaneously, comparing it against `iid_branch_12L` tests whether retaining the bundled coasting update helps or hurts on a given seed, rather than isolating momentum decay from update count or weight decay.

## 4. Experimental Setup, Resource Accounting, and Evaluation Protocol

All runs in `experiments_p2/runs/` and matched reference runs in `experiments/runs/` were executed on a single NVIDIA H800 (80GB) GPU on the ClimbMix-400B benchmark using the unmodified GPT-2 BPE tokenizer (`vocab_size = 8192`) and `MuonAdamW` optimizer (`NorMuon` with $5$-step Polar Express orthogonalization on 2D block weights at base LR $0.04$, `AdamW` on embeddings at LR $0.6$, `lm_head` at LR $0.004$, and scalars). To ensure exact reproducibility, we document all batch, schedule, memory, timer, and evaluation parameters:

- **Sequence Length, Logical vs. Physical Microbatching, and Peak VRAM:** The context length is `MAX_SEQ_LEN = 2048` tokens. Each logical microbatch contains $64$ sequences $\times 2{,}048 = 131{,}072$ tokens, and all runs use `accum=2` logical microbatches per optimizer step ($262{,}144$ tokens/step, set via `--stage1-accum 2 --stage2-accum 2`). In `train_braid.py`, when executing dense $L \ge 12$ passes (`Static 12L`, `Static 16L`, `CSB-12L`, and Stage 2 of `CLB` and cloned growth), each $64$-sequence logical microbatch is accumulated over $2$ physical sub-batches of $32 \times 2{,}048$ tokens (`physical_device_bs = 32`) to keep peak GPU memory within $16.94\text{–}22.95\text{ GB}$ (`peak_vram_mb`). Because gradients are accumulated in FP32 before `optimizer.step()` and no batch-statistics layers exist, $2 \times 32$ physical sub-batching is mathematically identical to a single $64$-sequence microbatch. During Stage 1 of `CLB-12L`, `raw_model` executes a single $64$-sequence microbatch per branch, which records $22{,}948.6\text{ MB}$ peak VRAM vs. $16{,}941.1\text{ MB}$ for `Static 12L` ($32$-sequence physical sub-batches throughout) and $22{,}804.5\text{ MB}$ for `Static 8L` and `Early G_stack`.
- **Learning-Rate and Linearly Decaying Weight-Decay Schedules:** Every run uses initial decoupled weight decay $\lambda_0 = 0.10$ (`--wd-scale 0.5` applied to the base $0.20$ constant), which decays linearly to $0$ with training progress: $\lambda(t) = \lambda_0 (1 - \text{progress}(t))$. The learning-rate multiplier `get_lr_multiplier(progress)` holds peak LR ($1.0$) for the first $50\%$ of the horizon (`WARMDOWN_RATIO = 0.5`) and decays linearly to $0$ over the second $50\%$. Because progress is indexed by `eff_token_step = microbatches_done / 4` relative to `BASELINE_MAX_STEPS` (`--max-steps`), a `CLB-12L` or `Early G_stack` run at $g = 250$ with $T = 1{,}394$ total steps (`--max-steps 697`) executes $250$ Stage-1 steps at peak LR, followed by **$1{,}144$ dense Stage-2 steps consisting of $458$ steps at peak LR ($t \in [250, 708)$) and $686$ steps in linear LR warmdown ($t \in [708, 1{,}394)$)**.
- **Forward/Backward FLOPs vs. Uncounted Calibration and Optimizer FLOPs:** Following the benchmark convention, `cumulative_flops` tracks analytical forward-plus-backward model FLOPs ($6 N_{\text{active\_mat}} + F_{\text{attn}}$ per token). The reference `Static 8L` budget is $C_{\text{ref}} = 1{,}906 \times 262{,}144 \times 2.390784\times 10^8 = 1.19455\times 10^{17}\text{ FLOPs}$. Matching $C_{\text{ref}}$ with $g$ Stage-1 steps at $F_{8\text{L}} = 2.390784\times 10^8\text{ FLOPs/tok}$ and $T - g$ Stage-2 steps at $F_{12\text{L}} = 3.460347\times 10^8\text{ FLOPs/tok}$ gives the exact continuous horizon relation:
  $$
  T(g) = \frac{1{,}906 \times F_{8\text{L}}}{F_{12\text{L}}} + \left(1 - \frac{F_{8\text{L}}}{F_{12\text{L}}}\right)g \approx 1{,}316.872 + 0.30909\,g,
  $$
  which, when rounded to an even step count $T = 2\,\texttt{max\_steps}$ (`accum=2` vs. base `accum=4`), yields $T(0) = 1{,}318$ (`--max-steps 659`, $1.0009\times$ FLOPs), $T(160) = 1{,}366$ (`--max-steps 683`, $0.9998\times$), $T(250) = 1{,}394$ (`--max-steps 697`, $0.9999\times$), $T(450) = 1{,}456$ (`--max-steps 728`, $1.0000\times$), $T(650) = 1{,}518$ (`--max-steps 759`, $1.0002\times$), and $T(850) = 1{,}580$ (`--max-steps 790`, $1.0003\times$). Two small operations are not included in `cumulative_flops`: (i) at $t = g$, `measure_unbraid_fidelity` runs $7$ forward-only passes on one $131{,}072$-token microbatch ($\approx 9.5\times 10^{13}\text{ FLOPs}$, or $0.08\%$ of $C_{\text{ref}}$), and (ii) $5$-step Polar Express orthogonalization in `NorMuon` costs $\approx 2.9\times 10^{11}\text{ FLOPs/step}$ ($\approx 0.34\%$ of $C_{\text{ref}}$ over $1{,}394$ steps).
- **Three Wall-Clock Timers:** We report all three timers recorded in `result.json`: (1) `total_training_time` (steady-state step time for $t > 10$, excluding step-$0$ compilation and excluding the single re-compilation step at $t = g$), (2) `training_loop_seconds` (complete wall time of the training loop from step $0$ to $T-1$, which **includes** initial compilation and the transition compilation step `grow_compile_seconds` $\approx 9.7\text{–}11.2\text{s}$), and (3) `total_seconds` (end-to-end process time including post-training validation and holdout evaluation).
- **Evaluation Slices and Frozen Final Confirmation Protocol:** In `evaluate_full_metrics` (`EVAL_BATCH_SIZE = 32`), Slice 1 (`val_bpb`, batches $1..320$, $20{,}971{,}520$ target tokens) and Slice 2 (`holdout_bpb`, batches $321..640$, $20{,}971{,}520$ target tokens) of `shard_06542.parquet` ($63{,}374{,}976$ total epoch-$1$ tokens) are scored at the end of every run. All architectural and schedule decisions ($g=250$, `default` 3-splice topology, $H=60$) were chosen using `seed=42` Slice 1 (`val_bpb`) in Batches P2-1–P2-3. To provide a strictly untouched final confirmation under a frozen selection protocol, Batch P2-9 evaluates (i) a third disjoint slice of `shard_06542.parquet` (**Slice 3, `confirm_bpb`**: batches $641..748$ at `EVAL_BATCH_SIZE = 32`, comprising $7{,}077{,}888$ pure epoch-$1$ target tokens before any epoch-$2$ document enters the packing buffer, never scored during any prior development run in Batches P2-1–P2-8) and (ii) a fresh pre-registered fourth random seed (`seed = 314`) across the frozen primary comparison (`Static 8L`, `Static 12L Iso-FLOPs`, `CLB-12L (g=250)`, and `Early G_stack 8L->12L (g=250)`).

## 6. Limitations and Scope Conditions

1. **Early Weight Cloning (`Early G_stack`) Is as Strong or Stronger at $g=250$:** While `CLB-12L` outperforms modal-coupled `Early HSDM` ($0.987579$) and `Late HSDM-2D` ($0.987425$), unconstrained `Early G_stack 8L->12L` at $g=250$ achieves a lower $3$-seed ($0.980434$ vs. $0.982042$) and $4$-seed ($0.981141$ vs. $0.982259$) mean validation BPB, splits $2\text{–}2$ across the $4$ seeds with `CLB-12L`, and runs $6.8\text{s}$ faster in `training_loop_seconds`. Thus, `CLB-12L` should be viewed as a characterized non-cloning alternative rather than a superior replacement for unconstrained early copy-stacking.
2. **Sample Size (`N=3` to `N=4`) and Cross-Seed Variance Against `Static 12L`:** While both `CLB-12L` ($p = 0.0258$) and `Early G_stack` ($p = 0.0016$) achieve statistically significant $4/4$-seed gains over `Static 8L`, and `CLB-12L` significantly outperforms `Single-Branch Staged Unfreezing` ($p = 0.0096$ with `nohom`, $p = 0.0087$ with `iso_hom60`) and $g=450$ ($p = 0.0096$), the comparison against `Static 12L Iso-FLOPs` exhibits $3/3$ wins on the development seeds (`42, 137, 271`) but $3/4$ wins when including `seed=314` ($4$-seed $p = 0.213$ for `CLB-12L` and $p = 0.147$ for `Early G_stack`).
3. **Single-Seed Coverage of Secondary Sweep Points and Bundled Ablations:** While our primary $g \in \{0, 250, 450\}$ comparisons, `1-splice` vs. `3-splice`, `iid_branch_12L` ($g=250$), `Single-Branch` (`iso_hom60` and `nohom`), `Early G_stack`, and `Early HSDM` are evaluated across $3\text{–}4$ seeds, secondary ablations ($g \in \{160, 650, 850\}$, `rand_perm_12L`, `iid_skip_12L`, `CSB-12L`, and `CLB-16L`) use `seed=42` (or `seed=137` for `balanced`), and `iid_skip_12L` bundles momentum, second-moment, and weight-decay freezing together.
