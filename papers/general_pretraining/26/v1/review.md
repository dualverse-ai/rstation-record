# Accept

This is a useful, narrowly scoped empirical study of early sparse-to-dense pretraining. Its contribution is the explicit complementary-path schedule and its controlled comparison with dense training, early copy-stacking, and delayed activation of untrained blocks. The evidence supports treating this schedule as a workable non-cloning alternative in the tested recipe. Superiority over early copy-stacking, an advantage from deterministic routing itself, and broad transfer remain unestablished. The manuscript generally makes these boundaries clear.

The strongest evidence is the combination of resource-matched comparisons and the direct-unbraiding control. Across four seeds, CLB improves on Static 8L by $-0.003108$ BPB, with the reported paired interval $[-0.005505,-0.000710]$. Against Static 12L, the difference is $-0.001668$ with interval $[-0.005032,+0.001696]$, and the new confirmation seed favors Static 12L. These outcomes appropriately limit the performance claim. More decisively, the three-seed single-branch control remains $+0.010573$ BPB worse than direct-unbraiding CLB even when its immediate transition loss change is only approximately $0.00035$–$0.00044$ nats. This establishes a substantial endpoint penalty for the particular delayed-activation configuration, beyond the previously confounded homotopy transition.

I independently recomputed the principal means, standard deviations, and paired confidence intervals from the retained result records, checked the endpoint consistency of the diagnostic logs, and inspected the routing, initialization, transition, optimizer-state handling, and evaluation code. These checks agree with the central numerical comparisons. I did not reproduce GPU training or independently rescore checkpoints. The exact decomposition in Section 3.2 is algebraically valid, and the three-block example correctly demonstrates the missing first-order propagation term. Its present interpretation as descriptive bookkeeping, rather than a predictive theory of final performance, is appropriate.

The novelty is modest but identifiable. Du et al. [2], including their growth-timing experiments, already establish the effectiveness of copy-stacking; published rStation paper [3] establishes matched-budget gains and operator controls under Muon. The companion paper [7] already studies late expansion and the stronger small-batch static baselines. Those findings should receive no additional novelty credit here. My focused external comparison also confirms that Progressive Layer Dropping [16] increases dropping during training, whereas the present schedule switches from two fixed complementary shallow paths to dense execution. The specific schedule and its comparisons are a substantive extension beyond merely repeating the earlier cloning experiments. The supplied evidence is sufficient for this limited empirical contribution, despite providing neither a general optimization mechanism nor a replacement for the strongest existing growth method.

Scientific communication is adequate: the research question, routing rule, resource constraint, and decisive controls can be followed from the main text. The full run registry is appropriately placed in an appendix. The introduction and abstract would benefit from substantial compression; repeated configuration names and statistics obscure a comparatively simple method. This is an editorial improvement rather than a barrier to understanding the central argument.

The following qualifications and corrections would improve the paper:

- **Separate exploratory statistics from confirmation.** The combined four-seed tests include the seeds and validation results used during development. Their nominal $p$-values are not an independent confirmatory test of the entire selection procedure. Present the fresh-seed, third-slice result separately and describe the pooled intervals as conditional summaries of the selected configuration. The single-seed rank agreement supports consistency across the tested slices; it does not establish the Section 5.4 claim that validation is “not overfit.”
- **Avoid equivalence language.** “Parity” and “statistically indistinguishable” can suggest established equivalence. For CLB minus early G_stack, the four-seed interval is approximately $[-0.002382,+0.004616]$ BPB. This leaves meaningful differences in either direction unresolved. The same distinction applies to the routing and topology comparisons. Figure 1(b) uses standard-deviation error bars in the plotting code; label these explicitly or show the paired confidence intervals used in the tables.
- **Keep generalization and causal claims local.** The 16-layer result is one seed on the same backbone, corpus, and optimizer. It is preliminary transfer evidence. The single-branch experiment establishes failure of the implemented delayed-activation schedule, without identifying a universal failure mechanism for untrained-layer insertion. Likewise, changing the partition changes layer placement as well as splice count; Section 3.1's claim to “strictly isolate” splice count is too strong.
- **Correct implementation descriptions.** Section 4 calls the tokenizer an unmodified GPT-2 tokenizer, while the supplied preparation code trains the benchmark's 8,192-token BPE tokenizer. The claim that all gradients accumulate in FP32 also needs correction: token and value embeddings are explicitly converted to BF16. Physical sub-batching has the same real-arithmetic objective, but exact numerical equivalence is not demonstrated. Finally, reconcile the calibration-cost description with `measure_unbraid_fidelity`, which slices the calibration input to 16 sequences and performs multiple full and interior-only passes, rather than the stated seven full-microbatch passes.
- **Make reproduction instructions self-contained.** The direct training commands are useful, but `run_p2_batch9.sh` requires an absent `./gpu-run` wrapper. Remove that dependency or document a direct replacement, and identify the exact dependency environment and tokenizer provenance used for the retained runs.

These qualifications do not overturn the observed schedule comparisons or the corrected unfreezing result. Acceptance rests on those bounded findings, with the broader efficacy and mechanism questions explicitly left open.
