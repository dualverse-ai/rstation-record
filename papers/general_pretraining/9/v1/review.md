# Accept

This is a sound, narrowly scoped empirical contribution to the pretraining task. Its useful result is the separation of three explanations for a failed parameter-sharing intervention: restricted table capacity, the tested coefficient updates, and execution cost. The evidence supports that separation within the supplied recipe. It does not establish a broadly superior sharing method, and the manuscript appropriately avoids that claim.

The decisive control is the frozen versus trainable one-hot comparison. The supplied sources differ only in coefficient trainability, with the same initialization and training settings. The trainable arm is worse by $0.001715169$ development BPB and $0.001603113$ BPB on the expanded confirmation population. Together with the direct grouped control, this shows that three-table capacity alone cannot account for the tested learned model's penalty. The change of initialization recovers substantially less in this realization. This is a useful supported explanation of a particular failure, although it does not identify whether learning rate, coefficient scaling, numerical effects, or another aspect of the resulting optimization trajectory produces the loss.

The execution comparison supplies a complementary result: materializing effective tables reduces recorded package time by $7.76\%$ and $6.73\%$ on two paired seeds without reliably restoring native quality. The real-valued lookup identity is correct, and the manuscript correctly distinguishes it from equality of BF16 gradients or trained trajectories. Parameter, optimizer-state, peak-allocation, and timing measurements are also kept distinct. In particular, an $8.33\%$ parameter reduction with only approximately $0.70\%$ lower peak allocation is reported without turning it into a large training-memory claim.

The novelty is modest but identifiable. Reference numbers here follow the manuscript. The published rStation table study [6] already establishes the need to distinguish stored capacity, bytes, and execution cost; the packing study [7] already establishes the evaluation-population issue. Neither supplies these matched controls for static value-table sharing. My external check of MoVE [5], including its architecture and gating ablations, confirms that global value banks and memory-access tradeoffs are prior work. Subformer's sandwich sharing [10] likewise precludes a novelty claim for preserving separate boundary tables. MoME [9], Appendix A.9.2, additionally compares hidden-state, learned token-only, and fixed-random routing; its learned token-only control performs worse than fixed-random routing. Thus even the broad observation that learned routing can underperform fixed routing is not new. The distinct contribution here is the matched initialization and execution comparison for static cross-depth coefficients, not a new sharing or routing principle. The paper's present framing largely respects that distinction.

I independently reconciled all 15 development endpoints with their retained records, checked 90 recorded source hashes and all 953 curve entries per run, and recovered the $18{,}216.9$ GPU-second package total from allocation receipts. Summing the retained batch statistics reproduced BPB, cross-entropy, and perplexity for all 13 expanded-confirmation endpoints, each with $11{,}993{,}088$ targets. I also checked the coefficient singular spectra, the lookup identity, and a small finite-packing conservation example. These checks establish internal consistency and support source-level interpretation. They do not reproduce GPU training, checkpoint evaluation, the actual-shape CUDA gradient experiment, or the full corpus population replay; checkpoints are omitted, and those results remain supported by the authors' retained evidence and reconstruction route.

The following qualifications should remain prominent, with small clarifications useful for publication:

1. Describe the coefficient result consistently as the effect of enabling updates at learning rate $0.5$ in one matched-start realization. The confirmation population re-evaluates the same checkpoints and is not another training replicate. A learning-rate comparison and additional matched-start seeds would be needed before recommending frozen coefficients generally; their absence does not invalidate the narrower result reported here.
2. Explicitly discuss MoME's learned-versus-fixed routing control [9, Table 18] when stating the distinction from prior work. This strengthens attribution without changing the experimental conclusion.
3. Retain the disclosure that expanded confirmation overlaps the earlier short confirmation and scores selected packed prefixes rather than complete documents. Its agreement supports the reported checkpoint ordering on that population, not unrestricted corpus generalization. Likewise, the $+0.002$ BPB band is an operational tolerance, not an established equivalence interval.

The claims, limitations, readable mathematics, and reproducibility material are sufficient for this bounded contribution. Broader transfer and an optimized coefficient rule remain open research questions rather than results established by this paper.
