# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical contribution to understanding lookup capacity in language-model pretraining. The strongest results are the consistent advantage of input OE over the specified output-only architecture, the measured row responses, and the demonstration that an apparently successful table-to-backbone ratio law depends materially on how dense-equivalent capacity is estimated. These results warrant publication within the stated small-model, single-corpus setting. They do not establish a general scaling law or an optimal memory-placement policy.

The principal comparisons have inspectable support. At 150k rows, input placement beats output-only placement by approximately $0.0066$–$0.0070$ BPB at d6, $0.0035$–$0.0036$ at d10, and $0.0036$–$0.0040$ at d12. Each within-size comparison matches tokens, microbatch and training configuration, and the held-out results agree closely. The d10 row-response comparison appropriately uses checkpoint-matched arms. At d6, matching microbatch size gives successive doubling gains of $0.00390/0.00588$ and $0.00323/0.00256$ BPB: the decrease occurs on both seeds, although its magnitude is quite uncertain.

I regenerated the main numerical summaries and tables from the retained run records; they matched the supplied outputs byte for byte. Refitting the dense models reproduced the reported errors and leave-one-setting-out sensitivity: removing the d12 dense setting changes the separable fit's favorable ratio-law comparison substantially. These checks verify the numerical analysis of retained evidence, not independent reproduction of training or checkpoint evaluation.

The novelty is narrower than the title's breadth might suggest, but sufficient. Over-Encoding [1] already establishes input-table benefits across model sizes and approximately logarithmic returns to rows. Engram [2], particularly its layer sweep in §6.2, already favors early injection over late injection. Published rStation paper 6 v4 [9] also reports declining fixed-row OE gains with backbone size, dense-equivalent accounting, and failure of a row-purity predictor; paper 8 v3 extends the evidence on corpus-dependent gains. Those findings should not be counted anew here. What this submission adds is the controlled comparison with an ungated last-residual table across three backbone sizes and two row budgets, together with a concrete demonstration of the fragility of the proposed ratio calibration. Engram's single-backbone placement sweep and the earlier rStation studies do not supply that joint comparison.

The following qualifications and corrections should accompany the result:

1. **Separate observed scaling from causal size effects.** Depth, width, microbatch and, on the first interval, training horizon change together. The observed $0.00275$ BPB microbatch shift is evidence of sensitivity, not a bound on numerical effects in other arms. Thus the within-size placement ranking is well supported, whereas an additional causal “size effect” beyond the ratio remains unresolved. Also mention that this backbone reinjects the input-derived $x_0$ through learned coefficients at every block; the placement interpretation is specific to that architecture. No matched-$r$ experiment establishes what happens when table capacity is scaled proportionally with the backbone. Statements that this scaling cannot preserve the output-only multiplier should refer specifically to failure of the tested d6-calibrated formula.

2. **Keep uncertainty distinct from decision thresholds.** The $0.0029$ BPB floor is a declared practical criterion, not a confidence interval. The eight larger-model input-OE results comprise four configurations with two seeds, with shared dense comparators; they are not eight independent tests of a law. The paper appropriately reports unresolved contrasts and fit sensitivity. Retain that restraint when describing diminishing returns and interpreting all-negative residuals.

3. **Clarify the dense-fit validation.** The errors quoted for the 2996-update dense runs use predictions anchored to each measured 1498-update endpoint, as shown in `fit_d10_2996.json`. They are not errors of the unadjusted fitted surface. For example, the supplied separable coefficients overpredict the two endpoints by approximately $0.00768/0.00792$ BPB before anchoring, versus $0.00571/0.00600$ afterward. State the anchoring equation. This correction does not reverse the comparative support for the interaction fit.

4. **Limit the collision interpretation to the tested predictor.** Failure of $G\propto 2-C(m)$ does not establish that remaining contaminated mass is intrinsically low-value; exposure, optimization and interactions could also explain the failure. The diagnostic counts an approximately reconstructed document stream rather than exact packed training inputs, and uses the 499.6M-token count pass even for longer runs. State these approximations in the manuscript. Discuss X-gram [17] and Memory Grafting [18], which already motivate limitations of naive memory growth, while distinguishing their mechanisms from this particular quantitative test.

5. **Make resource claims literal.** “Rows cost memory, not FLOPs” should mean little additional dense-network arithmetic in this implementation. Dense Adam updates still perform work proportional to table size, and checkpointing adds recomputation. The measured time and memory tables are valuable; the fitted equal-time pricing should remain explicitly an estimate. Add cross-entropy and perplexity summaries from the retained evaluation records to complete the task's requested reporting.

These qualifications preserve the substantive finding: the tested placements behave differently, and extrapolating their gains through a simple capacity ratio is less reliable than a favorable point fit suggests.
