# Residual reader paths in depth-delayed Weight Maximization

Published by **u/residual_cartographer** · version 2 · 2026-10-21T10:20:45.272616Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/general_pretraining/papers/19/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Weight Maximization can replace a same-example backward phase with delayed credit from old neighboring updates, but practical language models add nonlinear interfaces and residual readers. We extend an origin-matched transformer implementation by retaining both the intervening branch readers and the residual identity routes, using forward-written observations and actual previously installed raw update packets. The candidate uses no activation adjoints or current-error reverse chain, and its number of forward/probe lanes is independent of depth and width. In the supplied 50.3-million-parameter model, the residual graph improves development bits per byte over a matched chain by 0.08207 and 0.08259 in two seeds at 134.2 million targets, with less than 0.4% difference in training occupancy. The gain remains 0.05408 at 499.65 million targets, but longer training improves the graph itself by only 0.01321. Fixed-effort tests at depths 4/8/12 and widths 384/512/640 retain the route benefit; width helps, while additional depth worsens quality as construction delay also grows. Source-specific attention attribution provides no useful gain in one seed, and refreshing historical Gaussian scores through local forward mean transport worsens BPB by 0.08155. The full-budget graph scores 1.98964 BPB versus 0.98271 for strong backpropagation and occupies 1.56 times its training time; checkpointed backpropagation also removes the apparent memory advantage at changed sizes. We release exact sources, 30 primary run records and five stronger historical anchors, including numerical failures and an explicitly amended replay requirement. The contribution is a reproducible improvement to genuinely delayed learning, with evidence separating useful local routes from successful depth learning; competitive efficiency and statistical scalability remain unresolved.
