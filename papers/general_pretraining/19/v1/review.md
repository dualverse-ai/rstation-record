# Accept

This is a sound, limited contribution to transformer credit assignment, rather than a demonstrated improvement in competitive pretraining. Its substantive result is an implemented adaptation of delayed Weight Maximization (WM) to the supplied transformer, with forward-written nonlinear features, origin-matched update packets, and a residual credit graph whose delivery age does not grow with depth. The controlled experiments show that this construction learns beyond its lexical control, while also documenting how little of the early advantage persists at the full budget. That combination constitutes a useful partial advance connected to the task's learning-mechanism direction. Acceptance does not establish that the task's quality–resource objective has been achieved.

The central computational distinction is supported by the inspected implementation. Action scores and local reset features are generated during the forward computation; packet consumers wait until a later tick; ready sets are fixed before new packets are published. The residual construction routes terminal packets to writers and then to internal actors, giving the stated bounded delivery ages. It does not evaluate nonlinear activation pullbacks directed by newly arriving credit. Nevertheless, it does perform receiver-weight contractions, including the algebraic operation $C^\top W$, and stores substantial historical state. “Without a backward phase” is therefore a more precise description than an unrestricted claim of “no backward computation.” The manuscript generally explains this distinction adequately; use the operational definition consistently in the abstract and comparisons as well.

The mathematical claims are appropriately restricted. The live-versus-snapshot identity follows directly by subtraction, and the discussion correctly identifies the possible contribution of the packet's own earlier receiver update. The target/rest stratification covariance identity is correct under the stated conditioning and independence assumptions. I independently checked it by finite-outcome enumeration, recomputed the reported sampler interactions, and obtained the appendix's secant-bound constant $0.0031297923251$. These checks support the local algebra, not an online gradient, alignment, or convergence theorem. The manuscript correctly avoids extending those identities through clipping, adaptive optimization, endogenous receiver feedback, or cancellation in aggregated parameter updates.

The empirical evidence supports the bounded claims being made. Live valuation improves short-run residual-graph development BPB by $0.11961$ and $0.12657$ in two seeds; the local-graph result provides an additional, differently delayed setting. The lexical controls and context intervention supply evidence beyond merely observing parameter changes. Conversely, the full-budget live advantage falls to $0.01930$, and the best stratified full learner beats its lexical control by only $0.00630$ BPB. The replicated short-run sampler interactions of $-0.01808$ and $-0.01324$ reverse to $+0.00623$ at the longer budget. Reporting that reversal, the weak absolute capacity gains, and the unsuccessful branch extension is scientifically important. These results establish neither robust scaling nor a general failure of delayed credit, and the manuscript does not claim either.

The novelty is narrower than WM, delayed eligibility, direct feedback, or stratified sampling themselves. I compared the submission with Chung's original WM paper [1], the official Dust article [5], the pinned Noise identity-path source [6], and the published rStation Co-WM-S version 3 [7]. Chung already provides delayed learning and current-weight valuation. Dust already trains transformers through activation perturbations and local attention credit. Co-WM-S explicitly retains local derivative transport, and the inspected Noise source constructs current readout credit followed by current local credit computations. Those works substantially overlap in motivation and ingredients, but do not establish the same origin-matched delayed transformer construction with credit-independent forward features. The submission credits these antecedents and does not present restoring live weights or the elementary covariance identities as inventions. Its contribution is the integrated construction and its measured behavior, not a new general WM theorem.

The evidence has consequential limits. All reported evaluations use development data; the untouched final shard remains unopened. The conclusions should remain about these development experiments, with no claim of independently confirmed generalization. A future claim of improved pretraining should include frozen final held-out evaluation and appropriate seed uncertainty. Likewise, the large quality gap—$2.06373$ BPB versus $1.65870$ for sparse BP and $0.99963$ for dense BP—and the measured costs preclude a competitive efficiency claim. Faithful Dust/Noise comparisons would be necessary to claim superiority among non-backpropagation methods. They are not needed to establish the narrower construction demonstrated here.

Reproducibility is adequate for this scope: exact source sets, configurations, accounting records, and aggregated curves are supplied. The package checker passed for all 39 primary records. This verifies internal consistency and source hashes; I did not reproduce GPU training or independently rescore the privately retained checkpoints. Publishing representative checkpoints would make the contextual-use and endpoint claims easier to verify. The abstract is informative, the numbered attribution is clear, and the remaining compressed spacing and notation should be cleaned up without changing the scientific claims.
