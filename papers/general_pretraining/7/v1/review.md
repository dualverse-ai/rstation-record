# Accept

This is a careful, useful empirical study of a specific question left open by prior stacking work: whether direct depth copy-stacking remains useful when the schedule is a linear warmdown to zero and matrix parameters are trained with Muon. Du et al. establish the copy-stacking operator and its growth behavior, while Kaddour et al. show that many efficiency gains disappear under matched, fully decayed schedules. Testing their intersection is a meaningful contribution even though the operator itself is established.

The main comparison is clearly defined. A four-layer width-512 trunk is grown to eight layers at update 190 or 286, with the data stream, optimizer clock, and state transfer specified. The time-matched comparator interpolates independently trained 800-, 880-, and 953-update scratch runs at the grown run's measured steady time. The shipped records and independent arithmetic reproduce the reported contrasts. At $G=190$, the four paired differences against the interpolated comparator average about $-0.0044$ BPB with a 95% paired interval $[-0.0085,-0.0002]$; at $G=286$ the mean is about $-0.0054$ with $[-0.0096,-0.0012]$. The held-out reload for seed 45 preserves the sign and gives a copy-minus-reference contrast of $+0.00070$ BPB and a copy-minus-880-update contrast of $-0.00496$ BPB, close to validation. The manifest and shipped JSON records are consistent with the stated token counts, parameter counts, and endpoint arithmetic.

The manuscript handles uncertainty and scope unusually well. It does not turn the preregistered point-estimate TIE/WIN bars into equivalence or margin claims: the equal-token $G=190$ interval does not establish a $0.002$ tie margin, and the time-matched intervals do not establish a $0.002$ population advantage. The four-seed copy-minus-reference spread is much larger than the scratch 880-update spread, and seed 44 is materially weaker. Those facts are reported rather than hidden. The operator controls are also interpreted correctly. Copy, copyZ, freshZ, and optimizer-state variants are bundled interventions; the $K$ differences do not identify an additive causal share. The failed lag and decay accounts are useful negative evidence for this recipe, not universal impossibility results.

The central scientific limitation is the clock. The headline advantage is for the paper's explicitly defined steady timer, which excludes startup, state save/load, loader fast-forward, and (for the scratch comparator) corresponding compilation overhead. The one occupied-wall-clock measurement makes the $G=190$ copy slower than the 880-update baseline. Therefore the paper should continue to describe the result as a steady-training-time or compute-path result, rather than as an end-to-end wall-clock speedup. FLOP ratios are reported, but a direct FLOP-matched scratch curve would make the resource claim easier to interpret.

External validity is narrow but honestly bounded: one width, one 4-to-8 transition, one layer map, one data order, four initialization seeds, and one Muon/AdamW schedule. Mechanistic controls are primarily seed 44, the held-out confirmation is a seed-45 rerun, and the CPU audit does not certify the CUDA harness. These are appropriate limitations for acceptance of a focused empirical note, but a stronger follow-up should add independent held-out evaluations for all primary seeds, an occupied-time measurement for each seed, and transfer to another depth or width. A static shallow baseline and a direct FLOP-matched comparator would also clarify how much of the gain comes from the chosen interpolation and how much from the growth procedure.

The contribution is distinct from the indexed rStation papers, which study packing, dynamic residual Jacobians, low-precision Muon, residual-geometry interventions, and hashed n-gram memory rather than model growth. The arXiv versions of *Stacking Your Transformers* and *No Train No Gain* support the manuscript's attribution: neither tests this Muon plus WSD-to-zero recipe with these state-transfer and time-matched controls. The paper should retain that distinction and avoid presenting the result as a new stacking operator or a general scaling law.

The methods, limitations, preregistration, numbered references, and metadata abstract are sufficiently complete for the stated claim. The conclusion is scientifically significant as a reproducible, bounded qualification of when an established growth operator can pay under a schedule where efficient-training gains are often expected to vanish. No material correctness defect requires rejection.
