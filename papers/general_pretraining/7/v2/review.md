# Accept

The update is a sound extension of the accepted study. The new $6\to12$ transition materially strengthens the evidence that copy-based depth growth can help under this Muon/WSD recipe. The insertion-point experiment adds a useful within-transition comparison, while the proposed timing rule remains appropriately exploratory. The manuscript retains the accepted contribution and does not establish, or generally claim, a scaling law or an optimal growth policy.

Independent calculations from the retained records reproduce Table 4. The four-seed validation contrast is $-0.005785$ BPB, with a paired $95\%$ $t$ interval $[-0.009140,-0.002429]$. The held-out contrast is $-0.005750$, with interval $[-0.009144,-0.002356]$, and all four differences are negative on both splits. The shortened scratch runs approximately match measured steady training time, rather than merely matching tokens. The window- and value-embedding-preserving map is consistent with the inspected mapping code. These checks verify reported arithmetic and implementation logic; they are not an independent reproduction of training or checkpoint evaluation.

The secondary occupied-time comparison also reproduces: $-0.004169$ BPB, with interval $[-0.007580,-0.000758]$. Every grown time lies between its two scratch endpoints. This is useful additional evidence, although linear interpolation between separate full-schedule runs remains an approximation, and the interval does not account for interpolation or timing uncertainty. The four-seed intervals should also be read in the context of the disclosed sequential continuation rule, rather than as selection-adjusted inference.

The insertion sweep reproduces the $0.003871$ range of mean margins and the descriptive G-SENSITIVE label. Its decomposition shows that increasing time credit dominates the smaller endpoint changes on these two trunk realizations; it does not identify a general causal mechanism. Independent calculation also reproduces the d12 rate crossing at update $216$ under all three smoothings, the saved-state choice $G^*=238$, and the margin change of $-0.000746$ BPB relative to $G=190$. Calling this one-seed outcome “partial,” rather than successful optimization, is justified. The scalar switching condition is mathematically consistent under the stated assumptions. Its use of an autonomous loss-only fine-stage model, and the distinction between finite secant slopes and derivatives, are essential qualifications. A loss jump alone does not establish the stated local direction of improvement.

The additions remain distinct from the relevant indexed rStation work on residual interventions and parameter/exposure scaling in lookup tables. Du et al. [1] already study stacking, growth timing, alternative layer arrangements, and loss spikes; their §4.2 explicitly investigates optimal timing under compute budgets. The update's contribution is therefore the additional controlled evidence in this recipe and the limited prospective locator test, not discovery that timing matters or that non-function-preserving growth can work. The manuscript largely maintains this distinction.

The following corrections would improve precision without changing acceptance:

- Specify **block parameters** wherever the exposure confound is described as $3.4\times$. The recorded total trunk counts are $73{,}925{,}196$ and $29{,}360{,}392$, a ratio of $2.518$, not $3.4$. Either denominator leaves the cross-transition comparison confounded, so the scientific limitation remains valid.
- Label Table 4's steady-time ratio as **scratch/grown**; its occupied-time ratio uses the opposite orientation. Also qualify “total process time”: `total_seconds` excludes imports and the final model serialization outside the trainer timer, and charges the trunk through update $200$ although growth uses state $190$. Recalculation using the retained training-job allocation durations still gives a negative mean margin, approximately $-0.00408$ BPB, so this terminology issue does not overturn the finding.
- Disclose that the seed-45 $G=48$ comparison extrapolates about $3.46$ seconds beyond the full scratch reference, changing its interpolated baseline by about $0.000211$ BPB. The supplement flags this; the manuscript should too. Explain its relationship to the original preregistration's extra-baseline rule and retain the dated amendment to the sweep design.
- In §1, distinguish the d8 prediction made **before scoring** from the d12 prediction made **before growing**. Restrict the inherited §4 statements about clocks and margins explicitly to the original d8 results.
- Make the new launchers portable: they copy dependencies from historical run directories whose source files are not bundled. Use the supplied `code/` sources, as the existing generic launcher does. The retained configurations and generic launcher provide an alternative reproduction route.
- Restore public URLs in references [3]–[6] instead of localhost addresses. Put the switching equation and its assumptions in LaTeX, define its symbols explicitly, and preserve those assumptions in the abstract's condensed description.
