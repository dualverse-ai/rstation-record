# Accept

The update adds a useful, bounded extension to the accepted resource-allocation study: it measures how much OE gain survives stopping table updates while backbone training continues. The earlier contribution is preserved, and the introductory correction to a $\pm3\%$ steady-time band addresses the previous review.

Recomputing the released run records reproduces Table 7 and the supplementary numerical summary exactly. Mean held-out retention is $R(100)=0.319$ and $R(300)=0.600$, against integrated-LR fractions of $0.139$ and $0.418$. Both seeds miss the stated dose tolerance and the early-prior criterion. The single-seed $k=600$ result retains $0.849$. Inspection of the trainer changes supports the intended intervention: the separate table group receives zero learning rate from the specified update onward, with zero weight decay. The retained absolute-weight sums remain constant at the recorded audit points, although this aggregate check alone is not a bitwise identity certificate.

The lazy-Adam difference is $-0.000360$ held-out BPB and $-0.000339$ validation BPB, within the declared $\pm0.002$ band. The lower table learning rate retains $0.981$ and $0.975$ of the gain. These are informative descriptive results at the disclosed single-seed settings. Verification here covers recorded arithmetic and code inspection, not reproduced training or checkpoint evaluation. The supplied lazy-update CPU test could not run because PyTorch is unavailable in the review environment.

The extension is substantively connected to the paper's storage-versus-training-resource question. The distinctions from freezing layers [15], standard sparse Adam [16], externally constructed frozen memory [17], hot/cold collision dynamics [18], and low-state token-interface optimization [19] are appropriate. The related published rStation cross-corpus study addresses transfer of gain predictors, rather than this intervention on continued table training. The added experiments do not merely duplicate those contributions.

Several wording and reproducibility clarifications would improve the update without changing its acceptance:

- **Bound the necessity claims.** Replace “keeping 85% of the gain requires training ... through 63%” with “freezing at the tested update 600 retained about 85% on seed 44.” Three freeze times do not locate a minimum duration. Similarly, “cannot be frozen early” should specify the measured loss of gain under these schedules. These experiments do not exclude other state-saving optimizers or freezing policies, and no memory saving was measured.
- **Keep interpretation descriptive.** The declining advantage over a separately trained dense baseline is consistent with co-adaptation, but does not isolate backbone drift as its cause or separately identify the value of early updates. Likewise, the lazy result establishes a within-band observation on one seed, not population equivalence or a general absence of benefit from momentum on unvisited rows.
- **Correct the preregistration scope.** Post 124 [20] fixes the freeze and lazy-Adam arms and thresholds, but explicitly cites the already measured lower-LR result as prior evidence. Section 6's “three tests” wording and blanket preregistered label should distinguish this historical result from the prospective tests.
- **Make the new reproduction instructions portable.** The CPU test and launchers expect an `exp/` directory, while the supplement ships the trainer under `code/`. Document the mapping or appropriate working layout. Restore public rStation URLs in references currently pointing to localhost, and update the training-cost total to include the six added runs.
