# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution and adds a meaningful, mathematically checked extension. Proposition 6 gives the stated Wiener–MMSE shrinkage rule under an explicit isotropic sensitivity/noise model; the pure-Python verifier exhaustively checks its minimizer, the three-token expectation identity, Hadamard orthogonality, the leverage-inversion example, and the optimizer correspondence. Running that verifier passed all propositions and all 208 ledger arms (623 retained seed records). The new d6 Rake-versus-Wiener comparisons at the same key learning rate, the sparse gather check, and the Native 50.3M endpoints are internally consistent with the supplied JSON records. The paper also continues to distinguish fixed-token results from equal-time comparisons, and it states the short horizon and repeatedly inspected validation limitation.

The verifier establishes algebraic and record consistency; it does not independently reproduce the GPU training or timing measurements.

The attribution of the new training gains needs tightening. The older $\gamma_K=0.85$ controls in `exp49`/`exp53`/`exp55` use $\eta_K=0.012$, whereas the exact-optimizer Rake/MMSE arms in `exp59`/`exp60` and both Native `exp61` arms use $\eta_K=0.024$. Thus the reported improvements over $\gamma_K=0.85$ do not isolate the Wiener–MMSE gather from a twofold key learning-rate change. The matched-$\eta_K=0.024$ Rake-versus-MMSE d6 comparisons do support a filter effect, but the Native comparison and the direct $\gamma_K$ contrasts should be described as joint recipe comparisons unless same-rate $\gamma_K$ controls are added.

Two numerical statements should be corrected. From the displayed 720-step per-seed values, the Wiener/Hadamard-plus-leverage arm differs from the earlier $\gamma_K=0.85$ arm by approximately $-0.00383 \pm 0.00083$ BPB (sample SD), rather than $-0.0040 \pm 0.0002$. At 360 steps, the displayed $-0.0163/-0.0216$ contrast is $75.5\%$, not $66.2\%$; the latter percentage is close to an upper-half value and should be labeled that way if intended. These are reporting errors, while the underlying endpoints are retained and reproducible.

The current supplement necessarily removes many earlier intermediate trajectories because of the size limit. The README’s versioned archive pointer is useful, but the published location should remain resolvable to readers; otherwise retain at least a compact trajectory summary. The metadata abstract is informative but substantially longer than the suggested 200–300-word range and could be shortened.
