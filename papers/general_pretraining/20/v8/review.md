This update preserves the accepted contribution and adds a useful, bounded result: concentrating a fixed query/key probe budget on upper-half blocks improves the quality–cost trade-off within the general delayed-WM trainer. The new pure-$M=4$ control separates the exploratory `ve_gate` effect, while the independent compositional and terminal-bus controls show that those additions do not explain the gain. The update also retains the substantive distinction from the cited upper-block writer intervention in [5] and the coordination study in [6].

The retained records support the new numerical claims. The isolated-RNG full-depth $M=2$ arm gives $1.9756\pm0.0017$ DEV BPB in $21.77$ s; pure full-depth $M=4$ gives $1.9672\pm0.0005$ in $27.71$ s; fixed-probe upper-half $M=4$ gives $1.9699\pm0.0001$ in $21.05$ s; and upper-half $M=2$ at 840 steps gives $1.9609\pm0.0006$ in $21.71$ s, versus $1.9720\pm0.0006$ in $21.55$ s for the same-trainer four-matrix control. I recomputed the new means and paired differences from the three seed records. The supplied pure-Python verifier passes the propositions and all 155 ledger arms (464 runs); this verifies the retained identities and records, rather than independently reproducing GPU training or timing.

The earlier review corrections are addressed: the lower-half and long-window rows now match their JSON records, the 438-step validation value is corrected, parameter accounting and timing boundaries are explicit, and the lack of an untouched confirmation split is stated. The abstract remains self-contained and informative at approximately 255 words. The claims are appropriately limited to short developmental horizons and repeatedly inspected DEV/VAL-B streams, and the Native-scale results are described as fixed-token and memory-footprint comparisons rather than equal-compute wins.

One wording refinement would improve precision. “100% RNG-matched” should be labeled “main model/data RNG-stream matched”: the isolated per-probe generators are independent of that stream, but their states advance differently when different layer sets are active, so the query/key perturbation tensors are not bit-for-bit matched across every depth arm. The result remains a valid three-seed depth/probe-budget comparison; the narrower wording would prevent an over-strong mechanistic interpretation. The upper-half advantage should likewise remain specific to this trainer, probe allocation, and short horizon.
