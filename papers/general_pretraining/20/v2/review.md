# Accept

The update preserves the accepted methodological contribution and adds a useful implementation extension: fused evaluation of the existing WM contraction, supported by short-horizon optimizer and comparator experiments. The revised alignment reference, expectation-bound wording, and identification of the kurtosis audit trajectory address the previous review appropriately. The new FP32-cache clock controls also provide substantially better evidence that the reported installation contrast is not explained by the earlier asymmetric cache rounding. Acceptance remains for a bounded local-learning result, not competitive full-budget pretraining or a general scaling law.

The fused kernels implement the stated column contraction without materializing the large intermediate tensor. The retained results support a substantial improvement over the previous implementation, and the optimizer ablations support the reported developmental improvement from approximately $2.1273$ to $2.0682$ BPB. Reporting the unsuccessful Native optimizer transfer is valuable. I inspected the relevant trainers and kernels, ran the supplied standard-library verifier successfully, and independently checked new endpoint aggregation and selected numerical comparisons. I did not reproduce GPU training or timing. Several verifier checks merely compare stored summaries against hard-coded constants; passing them does not independently validate experiments, operational counters, or every manuscript table.

The following qualifications and corrections should accompany this version:

1. **Restrict the causal interpretation of the perturbation comparisons.** Sections 2 and 3.5 describe scalar averaging versus column contraction as the “sole difference” between SR-LNP and delayed WM. The implementations also differ in credit timing, perturbation location and attention-credit treatment. `train_dust_d6_fast` constructs `g_bus_hat` and immediately uses it for embedding and hidden-layer estimates, whereas WM uses delayed packets. Its value perturbations are scored locally with $\gamma=0$, while WM uses the attention-filtered trace. These are useful comparisons between specified recipes, but they do not isolate sibling variance alone. Likewise, the zero-same-step-relay characterization should apply to the delayed WM trainer, not indiscriminately to the comparator arms.

2. **Keep SR-NP explicitly an author-defined comparator.** The withdrawal of superiority claims over published Dust [9] is appropriate and consistent with the official article's different population allocation and training procedure. In the new code, both projection writers use `g_hat_wr` obtained by perturbing the residual after the MLP. For `attn.c_proj`, this omits its effect through the same block's MLP; suffix recomputation does not restore that omitted path. The head also receives an analytic local loss gradient rather than vocabulary-slab perturbations. Describe these choices alongside the implemented Gaussian centering and future-token accumulation. The supplied $1.47$ unit check tests discounted accumulation, not equivalence of the complete estimator to Dust or to the full network gradient.

3. **Reconcile the new timing and fidelity tables with their cited artifacts.** Table 4 gives the grouped $G=64$ kernel a $37.3\times$ speedup and relative error $2.4\times10^{-8}$. `benchmark_uncontended_timings.json` instead records $4.51/1.03\approx4.4\times$ and relative errors around $2.3\times10^{-3}$. The near-machine-precision agreement belongs to the $G=2048$ case. Some Table 6 estimates also disagree with the retained benchmark: width-768 WM is $17.71$ seconds there, rather than $16.13$, and width-256 SR-LNP $K=16$ is $6.53$, rather than $7.31$. These corrections do not reverse the principal measured comparisons. Distinguish measured training-region time from complete elapsed runtime and retain the stated exclusions. The Native $2.75\times$ comparison also changes optimizer settings and accuracy; it is not evidence of equal-quality acceleration by a kernel-only intervention.

4. **Treat optimizer mechanisms as hypotheses beyond the actual ablations.** The statement that the co-design gain “requires” column-isolated WM is stronger than the evidence: Table 5 also shows matched-subset backpropagation improving from $2.1080$ to $2.0532$ with the optimizer package. Failure of one SR-LNP configuration to benefit establishes that particular interaction. Similarly, the failed $1/\sqrt{BT}$ adjustment does not isolate damping or annealing as the cause of Native non-transfer. The retained geometry record supports the quoted mean attention shifts, but its per-layer vectors differ from those printed in Section 4.6, and its `paper20_prepolar` stable rank is $127.24$, not Table 5's $143.16$. Reconcile the records and provide the geometry measurement code, state selection and seed provenance before using these diagnostics as mechanistic evidence.

5. **Clarify attribution and experimental bookkeeping.** The snapshot's paper [5], version 5, already describes SRAM-fused outgoing-column contractions; [8] supplies the pre-polar optimizer foundation. The present specialization and integration are useful extensions, rather than first introductions of those general ideas. Update the distinction accordingly. Recompute the arm classifications from an explicit ledger: `exp37_39_master_results.json` alone contains five backpropagation arms, so the headline accounting with only two backpropagation references is misleading. Simplify the abstract around the supported implementation and short-horizon findings, retaining frozen query/key scope, repeatedly inspected validation data, and the lack of successful Native optimizer transfer.
