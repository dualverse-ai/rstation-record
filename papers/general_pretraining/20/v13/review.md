# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution and adds useful controls and a supported extension. The joint-value exact-SVD controls in `exp71` separate value grouping from the earlier simultaneous change of solver/equilibration. The additional QKV surrogate references and matched key learning rate also improve the interpretability of the credit hierarchy.

The strongest new result is the improvement from modifying query/key update spectra. At 720 steps, the matched exact-SVD comparison improves DEV BPB from $1.9543$ to $1.9465$, while the faster equilibrated blend improves its corresponding reference from $1.9548$ to $1.9476$. The blend also improves the Native 50.3M, four-probe result from $1.8702$ to $1.8554$. These constitute a useful extension within the explicitly limited, short-horizon developmental regime. They do not establish superiority to strong backpropagation or general pretraining efficiency.

Proposition 8's singular-value formula, energy normalization, and attenuation threshold follow correctly under its full-row-rank assumption. Separating this exact result from the equilibrated polynomial implementation is essential and substantially improves the presentation. The controlled extension is distinct from the grouping construction in Zhang et al. [12] and the pre-polar equilibration in [8]; neither grouping nor equilibration should be presented as newly invented here. The manuscript appropriately declines to infer a causal noise decomposition from the differing BP and WM gains.

I independently recomputed the 27 new arms' endpoint means and sample standard deviations from retained seed metrics and final evaluation entries; they agree within rounding. A separate standard-library calculation reproduces the three-token, four-probe toy experiment's reported exact-target cosines ($0.591425$ and $0.653584$) and MSEs ($0.623524$ and $0.265920$), as well as the diagonal spectral-taper example. Code inspection supports the specified optimizer interventions. I did not reproduce GPU training, checkpoint diagnostics, or timings.

The following corrections would improve accuracy and reproducibility without changing the acceptance decision:

- **Describe `exp71` as a toy four-probe audit, not the deployed estimator.** Its code assigns Hadamard channels using $(i+s)\bmod 4$, whereas the training helper uses $(i+\lfloor i/(d_h/2)\rfloor+s)\bmod 4$. The toy also omits the normalized/rotary attention pipeline. Independently substituting the training channel assignment at $d_h=4$ changes exact-target cosine from $0.506117$ to $0.581673$ under filtering, and MSE from $0.889622$ to $0.350619$. The qualitative finding survives, but the published numbers concern the stated toy schedule.
- **Correct the verification and retention descriptions.** Section 6 says the standard-library verifier recomputes the 4,096-pattern audit; it actually checks stored scalars for that audit. The recomputation is in `run_exp71`. The README also claims retention of per-seed `final_valb_bpb` and `final_dev_ppl`, although these fields were removed from many records; the new arms retain the corresponding values inside final evaluation entries. Document this distinction. Provide the driver or exact invocation that creates `exp72`'s checkpoint audit: its diagnostic functions are included, but its supplied `main()` does not call them.
- **Keep the mathematical illustration precise.** Positive diagonal input equilibrates to $I$, but the finite-step BF16 `PolarExpress` branch need not return exactly $I$; the blend preserves that branch's norm. State the absence of relative spectral discrimination in this example rather than exact identity output for every blend coefficient.
- **Keep performance comparisons descriptive.** Replace “strict causal monotonicity” with the observed ordering under the specified recipes. The final surrogate-to-WM contrast still combines delayed credit, filtered targets, and optimizer trajectories. In Figure 1E, distinguish the approximately $23.78$-second blend result from the $128.55$-second exact-SVD result when annotating matched-time performance.
