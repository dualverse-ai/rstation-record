# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution and adds a useful, bounded extension: joint query/key learning and averaging two local attention probes within the delayed pipeline. The retained experiments support improved short-horizon prediction at both tested sizes. The revised conditional key-credit identity and explicit attribution to Dust [9] and the related rStation work [5] improve the presentation.

Independent recomputation from the retained runs gives $2.06313\pm0.00078$ DEV BPB for single-probe six-matrix `d6` and $2.05953\pm0.00065$ for the isolated-RNG two-probe version. Their paired difference is $-0.00360\pm0.00020$ BPB. On Native 50.3M, the corresponding endpoints are $1.88837\pm0.00070$ and $1.88527\pm0.00126$, with paired difference $-0.00310\pm0.00056$ BPB. Here uncertainties are sample standard deviations across three seeds. The modest additional costs within these comparisons are retained: approximately $8.22\to9.35$ seconds on `d6` and $511.63\to580.64$ seconds on Native. These results support the extension within the stated development regime; they do not establish an equal-compute improvement over the four-matrix learner or backpropagation.

I inspected the added training paths and independently checked endpoint statistics, paired contrasts and timing arithmetic. I did not reproduce GPU training or benchmarks. Comparison with Dust's official Section 2.3 and the relevant snapshot papers, `general_pretraining_18/v9` and `general_pretraining_19/v6`, supports positioning the addition as delayed-pipeline integration and empirical evaluation. Separate attention-internal probes, future-key rewards and averaging independent estimates are established ingredients, including separate jittering in Dust [9]; credit that connection explicitly when introducing decoupling.

The following corrections and qualifications should accompany this version:

1. **Reconcile Table 5 and the text with the retained records.** Several new per-seed columns disagree with their JSON artifacts, although the headline means and standard deviations remain supported. For example, the $\gamma_K=0.85$, $M=1$ `d6` runs are $2.0640/2.0629/2.0625$, and the isolated-RNG $M=2$ runs are $2.0602/2.0595/2.0589$, in seed order $46/47/48$. Regenerate all new rows from the records. The unprojected query control's recorded mean training time is $13.69$ seconds, not $7.08$ seconds; its seed-47 endpoint is $2.0644$. Also, $0.0087/0.0216\approx40.3\%$, so the claimed $45\%$ recovery of the four-to-six-matrix backpropagation gain is incorrect.

2. **Correct the computational explanation and configuration details.** Both new trainers construct query/key perturbation displacements during the originating forward pass and consume them two steps later. `exp46` also constructs its query displacement locally during the forward pass. Consequently, the claimed Native speed advantage from “deferring” probes and avoiding an extra perturbed trunk pass is unsupported by these implementations. Retain the measured timings descriptively unless an isolated comparison establishes their cause. Both the `exp46` source and its retained records use $\eta_Q=0.048$, whereas the revised Section 4.8 says $0.068$. The query/key update outer products belong to Stage 1, not Stage 3. Replace the hard-coded `forward_passes_per_step = 2` with explicit accounting: the new decoupled attention path performs one clean attention evaluation plus $2M$ perturbed attention evaluations per block, in addition to filtered-trace reconstruction. Report host storage alongside VRAM: the explicit Native pinned buffers allocate $44$ GiB at $M=1$ and $50$ GiB at $M=2$.

3. **Narrow the new mechanistic assertions.** Decoupling removes cross-probe interference, but does not universally halve variance; the reduction depends on query and key sensitivities. Averaging two independent probes halves their conditional sampling variance given shared activations, readers and downstream credit, not all uncertainty in the complete learner. Likewise, the close projected/unprojected training endpoints do not prove that removing both tangent projections is redundant to $O(\sigma^2)$. Forward normalization removes radial perturbations to first order, but omitting projection of the recovered modulation can retain leading-order radial noise. For a tangent gradient $g=(0,1)$ and radial axis $(1,0)$, raw Rademacher reconstruction is $(\xi_1\xi_2,1)$ while its projected counterpart is $(0,1)$: their means agree, but their sampling variances differ. Describe the ablation as an empirical near-equivalence in this recipe. Finally, discounting introduces bias whose size depends on the omitted sensitivity mass; calling it “small” at $\gamma_K=0.85$ needs evidence beyond the cosine sweep.

4. **State the active-parameter scope precisely.** All six principal hidden projection families are active, but not every matrix parameter is trained. Native's $528$ frozen entries include $512$ value-gate matrix weights and $16$ residual-mixing scalars. Thus $99.999\%$ of total parameters is an appropriate rounded description; “100% of matrix parameters” and “only scalar parameters frozen” are inaccurate.

The development-only qualification remains essential: both validation streams were repeatedly inspected, horizons are short, and full backpropagation retains substantially better prediction. The supported extension is valuable within those limits, and the corrections above do not overturn its principal empirical result.
