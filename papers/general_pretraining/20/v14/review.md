# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution and adds a supported empirical extension. Most convincingly, at fixed asymmetric mixing coefficients $(\gamma_Q,\gamma_K)=(0.20,0.35)$, replacing the linear blend with the cubic Gram blend improves DEV BPB from $2.0375$ to $2.0337$ at 360 steps and from $1.9467$ to $1.9407$ at 720 steps. The width experiments, exact-SVD comparisons, and BP controls make this more informative than an isolated optimizer variant. The Native 50.3M exact-SVD comparison also supports transferring linear tapering beyond the smaller model.

Proposition 9's exact cubic-transform identity and normalized singular-value formula follow correctly under the stated full-row-rank assumption. The scientific contribution is the controlled application to delayed WM and its observed interaction with the credit estimator. Spectral cubing itself is elementary, and optimal singular-value shrinkage is established prior work. The additions remain distinct from the equilibration in [8] and the weight-based probe shaping in the published version of [10].

I independently recomputed the 30 new arms' endpoint means and sample standard deviations from the retained seed records and checked their agreement with final evaluation entries. They agree within rounding. Independent scalar calculations also confirm Proposition 9's diagonal specialization, but reveal numerical errors in its manuscript illustration. I inspected the new update implementations; I did not reproduce GPU training, checkpoint diagnostics, or timings.

The following qualifications and corrections are important:

1. **Keep the noise interpretation conditional.** The reported fraction below a median-calibrated Marchenko–Pastur threshold does not establish that approximately $75\%$ of modes are perturbation noise. The additive white-noise, low-rank-signal assumptions underlying [13] are not established for these correlated, momentum-filtered WM tensors. Describe this as an MP-inspired threshold diagnostic and shrinkage heuristic. The BP/WM contrast is useful empirical evidence, consistent with the manuscript's final caveat, but does not identify noise as the unique cause. Likewise, BP here is free of the added perturbation estimator, not free of minibatch noise. Frobenius renormalization and the implementation's top-mode fallback when all shrinkage coefficients vanish further distinguish the deployed update from the optimal matrix denoiser.

2. **Separate the cubic branch from the complete update.** The $1/27$ amplitude and $1/729$ energy ratios apply to the cubic branch relative to its leading mode when $\sigma_r/\sigma_1=1/3$. They do not describe the full blend, which retains the polar contribution $(1-\gamma)$. Exact singular-vector preservation and $\|U\|_F^2=d_h$ apply to the exact-SVD construction; the equilibrated finite-step implementation generally has different singular vectors and preserves its own polar-branch norm. On positive diagonal inputs, the check demonstrates equality of normalized directions/relative spectral contrast, not equality of raw update matrices or machine-precision agreement of the BF16 implementation.

3. **Correct the new formulas, numbers, and attribution.** For $\hat G=\operatorname{diag}(4,2,1,1)$, the cubic blend's tail-energy fractions are $37.6339\%$ and $27.0936\%$ at $\gamma=0.20$ and $0.35$. Its cosines with $\bar G$ are $0.780414$ and $0.853246$, and with the specified rank-two polar reference are $0.778594$ and $0.819635$. These agree with the supplied checker, not the manuscript's numbers. Write the shrinker explicitly as zero for $y\leq1+\sqrt\beta$: the positive-part radicand alone does not enforce that branch. Correct [13]: arXiv:1405.7511 is Gavish and Donoho's *Optimal Shrinkage of Singular Values*; the cited hard-threshold title belongs to arXiv:1305.5870.

4. **Describe small performance differences as observed means.** Against 720-step four-matrix BP, the cubic blend's paired seed differences are $+0.0013,-0.0024,-0.0037$ BPB. Their mean is $-0.0016$, with a descriptive three-seed $95\%$ paired-$t$ interval of approximately $[-0.0080,0.0048]$. Thus “strictly beats” overstates the evidence. Strong six-matrix BP remains substantially better. Native 50.3M cubic tapering also has no established advantage over the linear blend: its two-probe mean advantage is small relative to seed variation, and its four-probe result is worse on every retained seed. Correct the accompanying aspect-ratio statement: $1/4$ is larger than $1/6$, and the asserted higher spike variance needs evidence.

5. **Align reproducibility descriptions with the retained artifacts.** The updated numerical checker and checkpoint-audit driver address useful points from the previous review. However, the README still overstates retained per-seed VAL-B/PPL fields in older records. Document the actual retention and archive locations. In Figure 1E, give the $98.28$-second Gavish–Donoho timing beside its bracketed result so it cannot be mistaken for the $29.07$-second cubic-blend result.

These corrections preserve the supported short-horizon empirical advance; the results do not establish general pretraining efficiency or a validated decomposition into signal and perturbation-noise modes.
