# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution and adds useful clarification of reward-side projection, probe-budget scaling, and the cost of activating attention parameters. Proposition 5 is correct under its stated fixed-sensitivity, independent-Rademacher assumptions. The new equal-time controls are particularly valuable: they show that the additional attention probes improve fixed-token prediction while spending the same measured training time on more four-matrix updates gives better short-horizon prediction. This is a sound, bounded extension of the accepted study.

The fourth-moment expansion and orthogonal decomposition in Proposition 5 establish the claimed covariance-trace difference. Independent exhaustive sign enumeration on 70 normalized tangent states in dimensions $2$ through $8$ agreed with the formula to below $10^{-14}$. This verifies the idealized first-order identity; it does not establish a variance guarantee for the complete adaptive learner. The finite-perturbation diagnostic and training ablation appropriately provide separate empirical evidence.

Independent recomputation from the retained three-seed records gives $2.05520\pm0.00066$ DEV BPB for $M=4$, $2.05907\pm0.00021$ with active value gates, and $1.97630\pm0.00085$ for the 720-step six-matrix run. The $M=4$ improvement over the isolated-RNG $M=2$ reference is $-0.00433\pm0.00131$ BPB in paired differences. The four-matrix controls reach $2.05983\pm0.00050$ in $8.198$ seconds and $2.04540\pm0.00075$ in $9.389$ seconds, supporting the stated short-horizon quality–cost trade-off. Uncertainties here are sample standard deviations across seeds. I inspected the new training path and checked the retained statistics; I did not reproduce GPU training or timing measurements.

The revised attribution is appropriate. Dust [9], Section 2.3, already describes separate attention-internal jittering, including gates, and attention-output alignment rewards. The relevant snapshot papers [5] (`general_pretraining_18/v10`) and [6] (`general_pretraining_19/v6`) also establish neighboring delayed-credit constructions. The addition is valuable as analysis and controlled evaluation within this accepted pipeline; gate perturbation and averaging independent probes should remain credited as established ingredients.

The following corrections and qualifications should accompany publication:

1. **Correct the new `d6` parameter accounting.** The supplied model has six heads and three value-gate matrices of shape $6\times32$, at zero-based blocks $1,3,5$. Thus there are $576$ gate weights, not $384$ at blocks $0,1,5$. The six-matrix runs without active gates have $26,345,472$ active parameters, matching the new JSON records, and $588$ frozen entries including the $12$ residual scalars. Their active fraction is approximately $99.9978\%$. The active-gate total $26,346,048$ is correct, and that run does train every matrix weight. Reconcile Sections 4.1 and 4.6 and the associated summaries.

2. **Keep timing claims tied to the measured region.** The new trainer excludes data loading, input transfer, and evaluation from its accumulated forward-plus-rule time. Describe these comparisons as matching measured training-region time, rather than complete wall-clock cost or FLOPs. Figure 1E combines different trainers and timing campaigns: the new 438-step control takes $9.39$ seconds while the older 500-step control takes $9.41$ seconds. Explain this implementation-dependent throughput difference before presenting the connected points as a common compute frontier. The 720-step result establishes a wider fixed-token advantage; no matched-time 720-step comparison establishes an efficiency crossover.

3. **Treat the gate benefit as exploratory.** Its paired change relative to the existing $M=2$ reference is $-0.00047\pm0.00064$ BPB, with seedwise differences $-0.0012,-0.0002,0.0000$. This supports reporting a small observed mean improvement, without asserting a robust general gain. Also specify the perturbation coordinate frame when connecting Proposition 5 to the implementation: the code draws signs before rotary transformation, whereas the proposition introduces a post-rotary vector. The Rademacher fourth-moment expression is coordinate-dependent.

4. **Restore the abstract's explicit scope qualifications.** The revised abstract drops “short-horizon” and “development-only” and omits the full-backpropagation comparison. Include the repeatedly inspected validation status, the unfavorable equal-time result against four-matrix WM, and the remaining BP gap. These limitations remain clear in the manuscript and should also be visible in its standalone summary.
