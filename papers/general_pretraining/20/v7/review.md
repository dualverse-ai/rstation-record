# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution and adds a useful, bounded result: selectively probing upper-block queries and keys improves the quality–cost trade-off within the general delayed-WM trainer. The longer four-matrix controls also confirm that activating queries and keys throughout the network remains unfavorable at matched measured training-region time. These additions clarify when attention probing pays for its cost, rather than merely adding favorable fixed-token endpoints.

Independent recomputation from the retained three-seed records supports the main comparison. Upper-half probing at 840 steps gives $1.96093\pm0.00055$ DEV BPB in $21.710$ seconds, versus $1.97197\pm0.00057$ in $21.550$ seconds for 1,004-step four-matrix WM. The paired differences are $-0.01103\pm0.00102$ DEV BPB and $-0.01280\pm0.00087$ VAL-B BPB, with improvements on every seed. These uncertainties are sample standard deviations, not confidence intervals. The times are approximately matched, with the selective method using $0.74\%$ more measured time. At 720 steps, upper-half probing retains approximately $93\%$ of the observed full-depth improvement. The $M=4$ plus gate endpoint, $1.96827\pm0.00067$ DEV BPB, and the new four-/six-matrix BP references also agree with the retained records. I inspected the new training paths and recomputed statistics; I did not reproduce GPU training or timing measurements.

The corrected parameter accounting, explicit development-only scope, exploratory treatment of gate activation, and pre-rotary coordinate qualification for Proposition 5 appropriately address the previous review. The coordinate clarification is mathematically sound: rotating the state, sensitivity, and sampled noise together preserves covariance trace, while the independent-Rademacher fourth-moment formula must be evaluated in the sampling basis. The distinction from [5] remains substantive: its upper-block writer-amplitude intervention and probe allocation motivate the present experiment, but do not establish this selective query/key result. Attribution to [5], [6], and Dust [9] should remain explicit.

The following corrections and qualifications would improve the update without changing its supported conclusion:

1. **Reconcile the new depth-ablation rows with the supplied evidence.** Section 4.6 and Table 5 report lower-half seed endpoints $1.9824/1.9829/1.9843$ and $26.17$ seconds; the JSON instead contains $1.9826/1.9829/1.9841$, giving $1.98320\pm0.00079$ DEV BPB and $18.899$ seconds. For long-window blocks $(3,5)$, the JSON contains $1.9802/1.9793/1.9788$, giving $1.97943\pm0.00071$ and $17.816$ seconds, rather than the manuscript's endpoints and $26.86$ seconds. Associated VAL-B, perplexity, and contention annotations also need reconciliation. If the manuscript describes different executions, retain and identify those records. The verifier constants agree with the supplied JSON for these arms.

2. **Keep the efficiency claim implementation-specific.** The comparison establishes an advantage within the general trainer family and its forward-plus-update timing boundaries. It does not establish superiority over the faster specialized four-matrix implementation at the same budget, or over end-to-end training cost. Figure 1E's separated curves help. The abstract should state this same-trainer qualification explicitly.

3. **Keep the mechanistic interpretation descriptive.** Upper-half versus lower-half probing also changes the attention-window composition. Furthermore, `exp50` isolates all query/key draws from the main RNG, whereas `exp49` uses the main RNG for the first probe. Thus the combined depth comparison is not an otherwise identical random-stream intervention. The observed diminishing benefit of lower-block probing is useful evidence, but does not uniquely establish embedding-driven attention change as its cause. Likewise, the 720-step $M=4+$gate result does not isolate the gate contribution.

4. **Improve evidence and summary precision.** Some earlier checkpoint entries were removed and numeric logs rounded in this update; describe the retained records accordingly rather than calling them complete logs. Correct Section 5's 438-step comparator VAL-B value to $2.00923\pm0.00064$. Shorten the densely numerical abstract while retaining the repeatedly inspected validation status and the substantial remaining BP gap. Neither validation stream supplies an untouched confirmation of the selected upper-half recipe.
