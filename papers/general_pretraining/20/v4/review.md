# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution and adds a useful, bounded extension to active query learning. The new normalization control strengthens the evidence that the Native-scale group-size improvement survives matching the estimator prefactor. The revised isolated benchmarks also address the previous review: startup is separated from steady-state measurements, per-step observations and dispersion are retained, and the implementation package's precision changes are disclosed.

The new query-learning results are supported by the retained records. Recomputing the endpoints gives $2.06413\pm0.00055$ DEV BPB for spherical active queries on `d6`, versus $2.06487\pm0.00060$ for the matched Euclidean control. On Native 50.3M, the result is $1.88970\pm0.00080$; paired differences from the accepted four-matrix baseline are $-0.0089$, $-0.0074$, and $-0.0079$ BPB, averaging $-0.00807\pm0.00076$. These are useful short-horizon development results. They do not establish an equal-compute advantage: Native forward-plus-rule time increases from approximately $3.26$ to $6.01$ seconds per step, alongside the disclosed reduction in GPU memory through host offloading. The gap to full backpropagation remains substantial.

Code inspection supports the delayed query-credit construction and the distinction between its training path and the oracle-assisted attention diagnostic. I ran the inspected standard-library verifier and independently recomputed the new endpoint statistics and retained timing summaries. The revised isolated forward-plus-rule ratios are approximately $1.44\times$, $1.67\times$, and $6.33\times$. These checks establish arithmetic consistency and support the local algebra; I did not reproduce GPU training or timing. Three consecutive timing samples measure within-run variation, not uncertainty across independent benchmark runs.

The following corrections and qualifications should accompany the accepted extension:

1. **Describe the query ablation as implemented.** Section 4.6 says the Euclidean control removes tangent projection, but `run_exp45_d6_attn_routing_probe.py` applies `project_out_forward_radial_nullspace` to both exploration and recovered query modulation in both arms. The switches change Frobenius retraction and whether perturbations are added to unit-RMS or raw queries. Thus the approximately $0.00073$ BPB contrast supports that combined intervention, not a separately identified tangent-projection benefit. Likewise, the larger comparison with frozen queries includes activating query learning as well as its optimizer settings.

2. **Keep the key-credit identity explicitly conditional and first-order.** With fixed downstream credit, future accumulation has expectation
   $$
   \mathbb{E}[\widehat g_{k_s}^{(\gamma)}]
   =\sum_{\tau\ge s}\gamma^{\tau-s}J_{\tau s}^{\top}g_\tau
   $$
   over the applicable attention window in the linearized model. Undiscounted accumulation, $\gamma=1$, restores the omitted future terms. At $\gamma=0.98$, future terms are included but discounted; this does not eliminate bias relative to the undiscounted derivative, as Section 5 currently suggests. The two-token verifier exhaustively averages finite-difference probes with a $10^{-9}$ tolerance; it is a numerical illustration of the first-order identity, not exact finite-amplitude unbiasedness. The reported cosine hierarchy should also remain specific to these estimators and the initialized diagnostic state; $1/\sqrt{d_h}$ is not a universal parameter-gradient cosine law.

3. **Narrow the epsilon mechanism claim.** The new experiment establishes that increasing head AdamW epsilon worsens this Native recipe by approximately $0.0288$ BPB. It does not directly confirm a $64\times$ reduction in realized self-update drift or establish rare-token over-damping. Proposition 4(2) contains a sum over other tokens as well as the $1/(BT)$ prefactor, and adaptive moments change with the batch. Architecture, context, optimizer settings and training horizon also differ between `d6` and Native. Present this mechanism as an interpretation consistent with the observations. Similarly, retain the $96.9\%$ normalization decomposition as a descriptive contrast along the tested intervention path.

4. **Make the attention extension's attribution explicit.** The official Dust article [9], Section 2.3, already describes per-head attention-output rewards and future credit for keys versus token-local query credit. The snapshot's `general_pretraining_18/v8` also analyzes token-local truncation and query/key probing. These overlap with the explanatory ingredients, while the delayed-pipeline integration, optimizer controls and Native transfer remain substantive additions here. Credit those connections directly in Section 4.6 rather than presenting the hierarchy itself as wholly new.

5. **Reconcile the remaining presentation details.** Table 7 uses seed 46's Native active-query timings, whereas the text uses the three-seed mean; label this distinction or report consistent means. The new `exp44` claims of $3599.5$ and $3678.7$ ms uncontended timings lack identifiable supporting records in the supplied benchmarks; retain those measurements or use the recorded run means. Inverse rotation of BF16-cached queries is approximate recovery, not exact recovery of the original FP32 queries. Finally, fix the metadata abstract's doubled LaTeX backslashes and shorten its dense experimental inventory while preserving the development-only scope and comparator limits.
