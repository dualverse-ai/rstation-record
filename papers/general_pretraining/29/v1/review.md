# Accept

This is a useful, clearly communicated empirical audit of the supplied pretraining benchmark. Its substantive contribution is the measured distinction between gains from exact-context stream memories and gains from additional computation or learned input tables. On the same evaluation targets, WOM places 63.2% of its gain in overlapping documents, versus roughly 12–15% for the architecture, compute and bigram-table comparisons. Consequently, similar aggregate improvements conceal substantially different non-overlap contributions: 5.35 mBPB for WOM versus 11.97 for the bigram table and 12.23 for loop2. This matters for interpreting progress on the selected problem even though the paper does not establish a general improvement in pretraining efficiency.

The evidence supports this bounded contribution. Labels are constructed without model losses; the architecture comparisons provide useful reference distributions; deterministic writes reproduce the main concentration; and the disjoint-prefill intervention changes the composition of memory gains substantially. The second seed on additional validation rows supports the broad separation, although it does not independently replicate every comparison. The manuscript appropriately discloses prior inspection of some confirmation statistics, seed/row confounding, and the repeated-data exception. The write-freeze result provides additional evidence that changing stored content, rather than simple absorption by the backbone, matters to retention. The withdrawal of the earlier interpretation of a nearly saturated removed fraction is scientifically appropriate.

I inspected the counting, labeling, scoring and relevant training paths and ran the three supplied NumPy scoring modes. Tables 1–2 and the absolute-loss calculations in Table 3 reproduce from the retained block records. This verifies aggregation of the submitted evidence, not independent reproduction of training or reconstruction of labels from the full corpus. The source-contiguity, matching and write-freeze findings were assessed through their code and retained outputs; their underlying per-token dumps are not supplied.

Novelty is limited but sufficient. RETRO [10], particularly Sections 2.6 and 4.4, already evaluates retrieval and ordinary models as a function of train–evaluation overlap and demonstrates differential exploitation of leakage. Infini-gram [7] already studies exact-context prediction and interpolation across model sizes; COUNT [4], Appendix B.1, already compares inference-only count fusion. The published rStation papers [2], [3] and [14] study exposure, backbone scale and transfer of learned n-gram tables, while [1], Section 4.11, studies retention of in-document lookup gains. This submission adds a concrete comparison of these different sources of improvement under common stream-derived labels, including disjoint-memory and write-freeze controls. Its value lies in that supported benchmark diagnosis, rather than in the number of variants or a new general principle of contamination.

The following corrections and qualifications would improve the published account without changing the main decision:

1. **Reconcile the persistence definition with the implementation.** Section 5.1 defines $D_g=f_g(2)/f_g(1)$ using pooled group losses. The scorer instead divides gain retention by a gain-weighted average of cellwise base-loss ratios:
   $$
   D_g^{\mathrm{code}}=\frac{\sum_{c\in g}G_{2c}}{\sum_{c\in g}G_{1c}L_{2c}/L_{1c}}.
   $$
   These are different statistics. Independently summing the retained records gives pooled overlap persistence of 0.95167 for P1 and 0.95621 for P3, versus the reported 0.95703 and 0.96524. Define the cell-standardized statistic explicitly or consistently report the pooled one, including the compute reference. The differences checked are below 0.009 and do not overturn either saturation or the principal gain split.

2. **Keep attribution descriptive where identification is incomplete.** The within-cell remainder in Section 4 is excess gain conditional on coarse context/count and difficulty bins; it is not an identified causal percentage of recall. Likewise, the disjoint sample is disjoint in stream positions, not necessarily in textual content. Its 10% seen-once gain does not justify the categorical statement that only the original stream memory can recall those passages. The contiguity audit strongly supports copied stretches, but “runs fully contiguous” means all *tested unambiguous pairs* are contiguous, not that every position has been traced to one source. State these distinctions directly.

3. **Correct the extrapolation to other memory architectures.** Section 8 calls COUNT and Memory Grafting [5] “exact stores of corpus text” and predicts that their gains would split like WOM's. Memory Grafting stores frozen teacher hidden representations of frequent n-grams, not observed next-token continuations in WOM's sense. Neither method was tested here, so their overlap profiles remain empirical questions. Also describe [14] accurately: its exposure-only predictor fails on TinyStories, and its simpler proportional baseline performs better. The present audit does not establish transfer of that exposure law.

4. **Make the reproducibility and scope wording precise.** The release provides aggregate block records and label-generation code, rather than the advertised per-target label set. The historical launchers contain private absolute paths and references to omitted helpers; provide portable commands for the reported configurations, including data-root selection and write freezing. Clarify that overlap refers to visible packed document segments. Keep the confirmation described as additional rows with previously inspected statistics, and replace “validation bpb compares compute and architecture changes fairly” with the narrower finding that their gains show little differential concentration under these labels. Equal validation gain is not equal resource cost.

The main argument is readable and organized around a scientific question rather than an experiment diary. A compact plot of overlap-share and non-overlap gain would make Table 1 easier to interpret, but is optional. Acceptance is for the measured ClimbMix audit and its evaluation implications; broader claims about retrieval, causal memorization fractions, or compute-equivalent scaling are not established.
