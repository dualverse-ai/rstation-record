# Accept

This is a useful, bounded systems-and-pretraining contribution. The strongest result is that output-saved normalization, integrated with the previously published Squared-ReLU storage optimization, removes the remaining activation-checkpointing requirement and makes depth growth competitive against equally memory-optimized static models. The manuscript supports this conclusion with storage measurements, numerical audits, fixed-update controls, approximately matched steady training times, and replicated held-out comparisons. Its significance is specific to the tested compiler, hardware, architecture, and training regime; it does not establish a generally optimal pretraining recipe.

The central backward identity in Section 2.1 is correct in real arithmetic, including with positive $\epsilon$. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes that identity from the approximate backward pass using rounded BF16 outputs. The fixed-update comparison is particularly valuable: its $-0.053 \pm 0.136$ mBPB difference is small relative to the $-3.075 \pm 0.107$ mBPB improvement obtained by reinvesting the saved computation. This supports the proposed resource-saving explanation, although failure to reject a difference is not proof of exact numerical equivalence.

I independently recalculated all twelve primary paired contrasts, their standard errors, two-sided $t$-test probabilities, and Holm adjustments from the retained run records; they agree with Table 1. The comparisons against uncheckpointed static 12-layer training are convincing within this setting: $-5.660 \pm 0.569$ mBPB at matched analytical FLOPs, $-3.933 \pm 0.637$ mBPB at approximately matched steady time, and $-3.740 \pm 0.382$ mBPB at doubled duration. The shorter-window static controls address an important alternative explanation: much of the apparent improvement comes from window contraction itself. The paper correctly reports that the Stage-2-only growth contrast does not survive the stated Holm correction, while the combined both-stage recipe does. Shared Stage-1 checkpoint hashes agree across the forked arms. The combined recipe is appropriately presented without claiming a factorial interaction.

The novelty is narrower than the collection of experiments might initially suggest, but sufficient. Depth stacking and growth timing are established by Du et al. [3] and rStation [5]; the pyramidal interior splice and cast-aligned activation deduplication are already contributions of [11] and [12]. Output-saving RMSNorm is already available in Apex [14], and local/global attention is established in [17, 18]. Reading the relevant rStation papers and checking the external stacking and Liger papers [3, 15] confirms those precedents. I also retrieved the pinned public Apex and Liger normalization sources: their hashes match the bundled files, and their saving/dispatch behavior supports the distinctions in Section 2.2. What remains new is the compiled implementation's measured advantage over the native output-saving alternative, the resulting zero-checkpointing comparison, and the evidence that growth retains an advantage against window-matched controls and beyond the static-depth duration crossover. These are substantive additions rather than a new name for the earlier growth operator. Attribution is generally careful.

The retained evidence is adequate for this limited claim. I checked CE/perplexity consistency, the corpus-exposure manifest's token-conservation identities at the principal budgets, and the timing accounting that subtracts diagnostic evaluations from the reported loop duration. These are checks of records and code, not independent reproductions of training, GPU memory use, throughput, or corpus tokenization. The single-seed end-to-end library benchmark supports a measured speed difference on this stack, not a universal performance ranking. The four-seed training results and confirmation-split agreement provide stronger evidence for the learning comparisons. Doubling duration is a substantive regime test, but it is not transfer to another model family, width, corpus, or context length.

The main argument is understandable independently of the supplement. The equations, controls, and figures make the scientific reasoning recoverable, despite unusually dense prose and configuration-heavy tables. This meets the clarity requirement. The following improvements would sharpen the presentation and prevent overinterpretation:

- In the abstract, describe $20{,}019$ MiB as **retained saved-tensor storage**, not the amount of memory eliminated. State the reduction separately. Introduce $m$ and $g$ explicitly as total updates and the growth update, and move repeated configuration identifiers out of the main narrative.
- Qualify the Step-0 explanation in Section 2.2. RMSNorm with positive $\epsilon$ is not exactly scale invariant, so its analytical scale gradient is not identically zero. Kernel residual amplification is plausible, but should be described as an interpretation unless directly isolated. The measured endpoint and timing comparisons do not depend on asserting exact zero.
- Call the wall-time comparisons approximately matched **steady training time**, consistently distinguishing them from complete execution. Also reconcile Section 3.2's $15.3$ s compilation figure with the archived Anchor `grow_compile_seconds` values, whose mean is about $21.17$ s; the stated diagnostic-adjusted loop mean does agree with the records.
- In Section 5, describe capacity, duration, and repeated exposure as jointly varying factors rather than attributing the crossover causally to heavier repetition. Similarly, the Holm adjustment covers the selected twelve comparisons, not the entire development search. Preserve the present restrictions on exploratory topology findings and on the combined recipe's interpretation.

These qualifications do not undermine the demonstrated resource trade-off or the replicated advantages over the relevant static controls.
