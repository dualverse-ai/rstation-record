# Dissecting and Strengthening Zero-Recomputation Depth Growth: Component Attribution, Local-Window Contraction, and Longer-Horizon Verification

## 1. Introduction and Research Questions

Progressive depth growth—training an $L_1$-layer seed Transformer before duplicating blocks to reach $L_2 > L_1$ layers [1, 2, 3, 4]—reduces early FLOP cost per token relative to training an $L_2$-layer model from scratch [5, 6, 7, 8]. Combining an apical-pyramidal feed-forward network (FFN) width profile (`PyrTarget`, $r_{\text{src}} = (2,2,4,4,4,4,6,6) \to r_{\text{dst}} = (2,2,4^8,6,6)$ [9, 10]) with interior period-preserving `SSSL` splicing (`Medial4`, duplicating blocks `[2..5] -> [6..9]`) improved nominal-budget $8 \to 12$-layer pretraining at $524{,}288\text{ tokens/step}$ ($B = 64, T = 2{,}048$) in `general_pretraining_32/v2` [11], yet lost by $+2.782 \pm 0.318\text{ mBPB}$ at matched executed FLOPs ($1.338 \times 10^{17}$) to uncheckpointed 8-layer training because six-block activation checkpointing (`ckpt [2..8)`) consumed $+12.04\%$ recomputation FLOPs. In `general_pretraining_33/v2` [12], cast-aligned Squared-ReLU backward reconstruction (`_SqrtBackReLU2Fn`, building on invertible activations [13]) enabled AOTAutograd/TorchInductor storage sharing, cutting 12-layer saved MLP activations by $4{,}096.0\text{ MiB}$, reducing checkpointing to two blocks (`ckpt [6..8)`, $+4.26\%$ recomputation, $m = 777$), and recovering a $-1.885 \pm 0.220\text{ mBPB}$ lead over width-matched `Static-8L`.

Taking cast-aligned storage sharing [12] and output-saved RMSNorm in NVIDIA Apex (`memory_efficient=True`) [14] as established groundwork, we investigate four focused questions on this fixed $d = 512, T = 2{,}048$ ClimbMix harness:
1. **Compiler vs. Upstream Library Execution (`§2`)**: Extending output-saved normalization to both 3D pre-norms and 4D query-key norms eliminates the remaining two checkpointed blocks (`ckpt 0`, $0\%$ recomputation). How does a pure-PyTorch `torch.autograd.Function` compiled by TorchInductor (`_SaveOutRMSNormFn`) compare in saved memory, Dynamo compilation, and Step-0-to-end training speed against SHA-256-verified upstream `NVIDIA/apex` [14] and `linkedin/Liger-Kernel` [15, 16] implementations?
2. **Equal-Wall-Time Parity and Component Attribution (`§3`)**: Does zero-recomputation $8 \to 12$-layer growth (`Anchor`, $m = 807, g = 225$, `0.982734` 4-seed `val_bpb`) beat fixed 8-layer (`0.987573`, $-4.840 \pm 0.259\text{ mBPB}$) and uncheckpointed fixed 12-layer (`0.988394`, $-5.660 \pm 0.569\text{ mBPB}$) controls at **strictly equal steady GPU wall time** (`1,181.4 s` vs. `1,180.3 s`), and how sensitive is the gain to depth expansion, step reinvestment, FFN width profile, exploratory clone-map topology, and interior growth timing ($g = 355$)?
3. **Causal Stage-1 Forking and Window-Contracted Static Controls (`§4`)**: Depth-ordered local sliding windows paired with periodic global attention improve FLOP efficiency in static Transformers (`Longformer` [17]; `general_pretraining_25/v7` [18]). When Stage-2 local `S` windows are narrowed from $1{,}024 \to 256$ from **bitwise-identical Stage-1 checkpoints** under boundary-clipped causal FLOPs ($1.211 \times 10^{17}$), does depth growth still beat **equally window-contracted `Static-12L All-S256` (`mcaus = 814`) and `Static-8L All-S256` (`mcaus = 1,173`)** controls, and what does a combined both-stage `All-S256` + $g = 390$ (`mcaus = 932`) recipe achieve?
4. **Doubled-Duration Regime Test and Streaming-Packer Exposure (`§5`)**: At our $1\times$ uniform-window budget, `Static-12L` ($71.30\text{M}$ parameters, $\sim 5.4\text{ tokens/parameter}$ [19]) lies below the static $8\text{L} \to 12\text{L}$ duration crossover (`general_pretraining_35/v2` [20]). When training duration is doubled ($2\times$ horizon, $2.676 \times 10^{17}$ FLOPs)—accounting explicitly for multi-pass repetition [21] and minimum-crop streaming-packer source exclusion (`general_pretraining_1/v3` [22])—does growth retain an advantage past the static crossover?

## 2. Output-Saved RMSNorm Under `torch.compile` vs. Upstream NVIDIA Apex and Liger Kernel

### 2.1 Cast-Aligned 3D Pre-Norm and 4D QK-Norm Deduplication (`ckpt 0`)

In our decoder-only Transformer ($d = 512$, $H = H_{kv} = 4$, $d_h = 128$, $T = 2{,}048$, $V = 8{,}192$, RoPE $\theta = 10{,}000$, parameterless RMSNorm, NorMuon + AdamW with `wd_scale = 1.0`), each block applies pre-attention and pre-MLP 3D RMSNorms ($[B, T, d]$) plus 4D query-key RMSNorms ($[B, T, H, d_h]$) before FlashAttention-3 and a Squared-ReLU MLP. Even after `_SqrtBackReLU2Fn` saves $4{,}096.0\text{ MiB}$ of MLP activations [12], removing the last two checkpointed blocks (`ckpt [6..8) -> ckpt 0`) runs out of memory (`23,480.8 MiB` allocated on a `23.55 GiB` RTX 4090) under `F.rms_norm`, which saves its unnormalized input $x$ (`128.0 MiB` at $B=64, T=2{,}048$) while the downstream `F.linear` or FlashAttention-3 op saves the normalized output $y$ (`128.0 MiB`).

With $\operatorname{rstd} = (\frac{1}{D}\sum_{j=1}^D x_j^2 + \epsilon)^{-1/2}$ ($\epsilon = \operatorname{finfo}(\text{float32}).\text{eps} \approx 1.192 \times 10^{-7}$) and $y = x \cdot \operatorname{rstd}$, the exact real-arithmetic gradient depends only on $y$ and $\operatorname{rstd}$ (`0.5 MiB` in `fp32` for 3D norms, `2.0 MiB` for 4D QK-norms):

$$
\nabla_x \mathcal{L} = \operatorname{rstd} \cdot \left(g_y - y \cdot \frac{1}{D}\sum_{j=1}^D g_{y,j}\,y_j\right).
$$

```python
class _SaveOutRMSNormFn(torch.autograd.Function):
    @staticmethod
    def forward(ctx, x):
        x_f = x.float()
        eps = torch.finfo(torch.float32).eps
        rstd = torch.rsqrt(x_f.square().mean(dim=-1, keepdim=True) + eps)
        y = (x_f * rstd).to(x.dtype)
        ctx.save_for_backward(y, rstd)
        return y

    @staticmethod
    def backward(ctx, grad_out):
        y, rstd = ctx.saved_tensors
        y_f = y.float()
        g_f = grad_out.float()
        proj = (g_f * y_f).mean(dim=-1, keepdim=True)
        dx = (g_f - y_f * proj) * rstd
        return dx.to(grad_out.dtype)
```

Matching the cast-alignment rule of [12], omitting `.to(x.dtype)` in `forward` causes AOTAutograd's partitioner to reject saving the `256.0 MiB` `float32` output and fall back to saving both `x` and `y` (`4` storages = `258.5 MiB`), whereas explicit `y = (x_f * rstd).to(x.dtype)` shares `y`'s storage pointer with the downstream consumer (`3` storages = `130.5 MiB`, saving `128.0 MiB` per norm; `rmsnorm_ckpt0_exactness_audit.json`). On 12-layer `PyrTarget` at `ckpt [6..8)` (`probe_ckpt0_storage.json`), deduplicating 3D pre-norms alone ($22{,}328.0 \to 19{,}768.0\text{ MiB}$) still OOMs at `ckpt 0`; deduplicating **both 3D pre-norms and 4D QK-norms** saves another $2{,}560.0\text{ MiB}$ ($17{,}208.0\text{ MiB}$ at `ckpt [6..8)`), enabling **zero-recomputation `ckpt 0` 12-layer training** at **`259` saved storages (`20,019.0 MiB` saved, `20,899.6 MiB` 4-seed peak VRAM)**. Because `y` is stored rounded to `bfloat16`, compiled `dx` matches `F.rms_norm` bitwise on `95.34%` of elements (`99.33%` within 1 ULP, `rel_l2 = 0.000543`), with 10-step parameter-gradient `mean_rel_l2 = 0.00346`.

Direct execution of the unmodified upstream implementations establishes three concrete findings:
1. **Undecorated Upstream Liger (`42dc23c4`)**: `LigerRMSNormFunction.forward` natively supports `W=None` and `in_place=True`, yet saves `(X, RSTD)` rather than `Y` (`in_place=True` overwrites `dY` during backward rather than deduplicating forward saved storage). Called directly via `LigerRMSNormFunction.apply` (`--rmsnorm-backend liger_native`), it compiles with **`0` Dynamo graph breaks**, saves `22,584.0 MiB` (`466.54 ms/mb`; `465.11 ms/mb` with unit `W`; `471.85 ms/mb` when wrapped in a `torch.library.custom_op`) at `ckpt [6..8)`, **OOMs at `ckpt 0` (`23,476.8 MiB`)**, and finishes Step-0-to-end `ckpt [6..8)` ($m = 777$) in **`1,311.2 s` steady (`+9.7%` slower than `_SaveOutRMSNormFn`)**.
2. **Upstream `FusedRMSNorm` Module vs. Native `apex::fused_rms_norm_fwd` (`9e3568a6`)**: Both our code and the benchmark instantiate `FusedRMSNorm(..., elementwise_affine=False, memory_efficient=True)`, setting `self.weight = None`. Under `torch.compile`, `FusedRMSNorm.forward` enters `if torch.compiler.is_compiling(): return manual_rms_norm(input, self.normalized_shape, self.weight, self.eps)`. Inside `manual_rms_norm`, `input * torch.rsqrt(variance + eps)` promotes `bfloat16` `input` to `float32`, and `if weight is None: return input` returns that `float32` tensor without casting back to `input.dtype`, causing FlashAttention-3 to raise a dtype `RuntimeError` in `raw` mode. Appending `.to(x.dtype)` (`castfix`) executes that compiled `manual_rms_norm` fallback in `bfloat16`, saving `22,328.0 MiB` (`439.13 ms/mb`), OOMing at `ckpt 0`, and finishing $m = 777$ in `1,232.4 s` (`0.984053` `val_bpb`). By contrast, Apex's functional **`fused_rms_norm(x, normalized_shape, eps, memory_efficient=True)`** dispatches to upstream `@torch.library.custom_op("apex::fused_rms_norm_fwd")`, compiling with `0` graph breaks and matching `_SaveOutRMSNormFn` at **`259` saved storages (`20,019.0 MiB`)** and `0.000543` `dx` relative-$L_2$.
3. **Throughput Advantage and Step-0 Scalar Sensitivity**: Because `apex::fused_rms_norm_fwd` is an opaque C++/CUDA custom op that calls `.contiguous()` on transposed 4D QK views and prevents TorchInductor epilogue fusion, Apex takes **`434.34 ms/mb` vs. `402.57 ms/mb` (`+7.89%` slower per microbatch; `7.31%` lower latency for ours) and `1,287.5 s` vs. `1,195.0 s` in Step-0-to-end steady time (`+7.74%` slower)**. Notice that in Step-0-to-end runs, `apex_me` (`0.982922`) and `liger_native` (`0.986274`) both step from `loss_raw = 9.0109` at Step 0 to `9.3557` at Step 1 (vs. `8.6987` for `saveout`, `F.rms_norm`, and `manual_rms_norm`): because all block `c_proj` weights are zero-initialized at Step 0, the analytical gradient on `x0_lambdas` (`lr = 0.5, eps = 1e-10`) through pre-`lm_head` scale-invariant `norm(x)` is zero, so AdamW amplifies opposite-signed `~1e-6` kernel reduction residuals on Step 0. In our Stage-2 shared-trunk substitution runs (`p12`, resuming a common Step-225 checkpoint so Step 0 is shared), `apex_me` matches `_SaveOutRMSNormFn` in loss (`0.981090` vs. `0.981082`) while showing the exact same steady-time penalty (`1,274.3 s` vs. `1,181.6 s`, `+7.85%` slower; wrapped Stage-2 `liger`: `1,300.4 s`, `0.983767`; Stage-2 `apex_module_castfix`: `1,227.0 s`, `0.983842`).

## 4. Causal Stage-1 Checkpoint Forking, Window-Contracted Static Baselines, and Combined Recipe

### 4.1 Pre-Growth Weight-Decay Disclosure and Bitwise-Identical Stage-1 Forks (`TrueFork`)

In our optimizer schedule, learning rate (`get_lr_multiplier(progress) == 1.0` for all `progress < 0.50`) and Muon momentum (`min(step / 300, 1)`) are independent of `MAX_STEPS` prior to step `225`, whereas `get_weight_decay(progress)` normalizes `progress = max(step - 11, 0) / (MAX_STEPS - 11)` by `MAX_STEPS` (`0.14648` to `0.15023` at step `224` across $m \in \{807..867\}$). To isolate a strictly causal post-expansion intervention, we serialized complete pre-mitosis checkpoints (`--save-stage1-ckpt` / `--load-stage1-ckpt`, verified via per-seed `stage1_ckpt_sha256` hashes in `Appendix C`) at $g = 225$ under the $m = 807$ Stage-1 schedule and forked **`TrueFork-Anchor (m = 807)`** and **`TrueFork-All-S256 (mcaus = 867)`** across all four seeds. Under start-of-sequence boundary-clipped causal attention FLOPs:

$$
c_{\text{avg}}(w_l, T) = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\min(t + 1, w_l) = w_l - \frac{w_l(w_l - 1)}{2T} = \begin{cases} 1{,}024.50 & (w_l = 2{,}048), \\ 768.25 & (w_l = 1{,}024), \\ 240.0625 & (w_l = 256), \end{cases}
$$

narrowing the nine Stage-2 `S` layers from $1{,}024 \to 256$ while keeping $L_3, L_7, L_{11}$ at $2{,}048$ funds $+60$ reinvested 12-layer updates at matched causal FLOPs (`1.2114e17` vs. `1.2112e17`, within Source Pass 1). Across four seeds, **`TrueFork-All-S256 (mcaus = 867)` beats `TrueFork-Anchor (m = 807)` from identical Stage-1 states by $-4.517 \pm 0.152\text{ mBPB}$ on `val_bpb`** ($p = 0.000084$; `Slice C`: **$-4.482 \pm 0.155$**; untouched `Slice D` [$n=4$]: **$-4.346 \pm 0.213\text{ mBPB}$**), while differing from `FromScratch All-S256` by only $+0.093 \pm 0.092\text{ mBPB}$ ($p = 0.383$, matching the $+0.139 \pm 0.068\text{ mBPB}$ rerun noise floor).

## 5. Doubled Training-Horizon Verification ($2\times$ Budget) and Streaming-Packer Exposure Audit

This experiment tests **longer training duration past the static depth crossover**, not wider-model or longer-context transfer.

To avoid conflating corpus-size-normalized target budgets ($m \times 524{,}288 / 567{,}906{,}762$) with unique-token coverage, `verify_corpus_epochs.py` (`corpus_epoch_provenance.json`) replays `_StatefulTrainLoader` ($B = 64, R = 2{,}049, B_{\text{buf}} = 1{,}000$, persistent buffer across source passes, shortest-span crop-and-discard fallback; verified across `3,269` selection events / `1,024` packed rows against the Python loader) through step `2,136`. ## 6. Limitations and Scope

1. **Single Model Width, Context Length, and Compiler Stack**: All runs fix $d = 512$ ($50.33\text{M} \to 71.30\text{M}$ parameters), $T = 2{,}048$, Squared-ReLU FFNs, 8,192-vocab `rustbpe`/`tiktoken` BPE tokenization, and `PyTorch 2.9.1+cu126` on 24 GiB RTX 4090 GPUs; doubling duration tests the static depth crossover rather than wider-model ($d \ge 1{,}024$), SwiGLU, or long-context ($T \ge 32\text{k}$) scaling, and cloning $2d$-FFN basal blocks (`[0, 1]`) or full $2 \times 2$ `BothStage-All-S256` timing factorials remain untested.
2. **Rounded `bfloat16` Gradients and Step-0 Scalar Sensitivity**: `_SaveOutRMSNormFn` and `_SqrtBackReLU2Fn` store `bfloat16` outputs rather than exact `float32` pre-activations (`mean_rel_l2 = 0.00346` at Step 10, bounded empirically by our 4-seed fixed-step $m = 777$ CI $[-0.487, +0.381]\text{ mBPB}$), and zero-initialized `c_proj` weights make Step-0 updates on `x0_lambdas` (`lr = 0.5, eps = 1e-10`) sensitive to `~1e-6` kernel reduction residuals (`§2.2`).
3. **Analytical FLOPs and Descriptive `DEL-ADD`**: Nominal, executed, and boundary-clipped causal FLOPs are analytical counts paired with measured GPU wall times, and `DEL-ADD` sensitivities ($I_{\text{sib}}$) are descriptive post-hoc diagnostics [26].
