# Accept

This update preserves the accepted contribution and adds relevant evidence rather than replacing it. The expanded 50-run record is consistent with the reported factorial means and paired contrasts. The new three-seed DEPTH=6 masking intervention directly addresses the prior review’s request for a causal check of the Layer-0/high-frequency RoPE interpretation, and the replicated Table-Neumann and BF16-state controls make the compute and precision tradeoffs more informative. The manuscript appropriately limits the interaction results to conditional transfer in the tested cells, identifies the large parameter increase from OE, treats retention ratios as descriptive, and states the one-corpus, two-depth, fixed-schedule scope. The supplement’s changed scripts and records are sufficient to inspect the intervention construction and endpoint arithmetic; this review does not claim to have reproduced GPU training.

The main remaining issue is interpretive wording in Section 4.2.2. Contrast A has a held-out mean of $+0.000950$ with the reported descriptive 95% $t$-interval $[-0.000659,+0.002558]$, so “significantly degrades” and the unqualified “3/3” framing should be replaced by “increases BPB on all three seeds” or “is directionally harmful, with an interval spanning zero.” Contrast B likewise has an interval spanning zero; its result supports “consistent with little measurable cost under this intervention,” not confirmation that the subspace is redundant. The causal difference-of-differences is also exploratory at $n=3$ and should retain that qualification wherever the mechanistic conclusion is summarized.

There is a small arithmetic wording issue in the same discussion: the stated $+0.000914$ masking component plus $+0.000722$ optimizer component sums to about $+0.001636$, which is roughly 98% of the quoted $+0.001670$ interaction, not 96.3%. Please recalculate or remove that percentage. Similar “confirms” language for the Table-Neumann null should be softened to “does not show a meaningful gain at the tested resolution,” since its confidence interval also includes zero.

With these presentation corrections, the new intervention and controls are scientifically useful partial advances. They do not establish additivity, a general scaling law, or a universal redundancy mechanism, but the revised manuscript largely acknowledges those limits and remains relevant to the pretraining problem.
