# Accept

This is a sound, reproducible empirical study of a well-defined question: whether the moment-free hashed n-gram tables of [10] and the optimizer/attention package of [11, 12] still provide conditional gains when used together. The 36-run, three-seed factorial at two depths, plus untouched held-out evaluation, is an appropriate design for that question. I independently recomputed the cell means and paired differences from `results/paper2_runs_results.json`; the reported values agree to rounding. The strongest result is at DEPTH = 8, where the optimizer added on top of OE improves held-out BPB by about $-0.001884$ (three paired seeds), and the complete OE-plus-joint arm improves it by about $-0.002625$. The code and recorded provenance are sufficient to inspect the implementation and endpoint arithmetic, although I did not rerun the GPU training.

The novelty is empirical rather than algorithmic. SCONE/Over-Encoding [16], the moment-free OE update [10], and the equilibration, cooldown, Neumann-Gram, and post-QK-Norm components [11, 12] all predate this submission; the related MuonEq and nGPT work is also correctly relevant (arXiv:2603.28254 and arXiv:2410.01131). The factorial transfer result is nevertheless useful to researchers deciding whether these previously separate families can be deployed together. The paper should present this as a bounded composition/transfer study, rather than as a new optimizer or architecture.

Several qualifications are decisive for interpretation:

- The difference-of-differences interaction intervals span zero at both depths for both the optimizer and the full joint package. Thus the data do not establish additive or synergistic interaction, nor do they show substitution. They establish conditional improvements relative to native OE, most clearly at DEPTH = 8. The DEPTH = 6 validation interval for the joint conditional effect also includes zero, and the post-QK-Norm increment is harmful on Seed 47 at DEPTH = 8. Abstract and conclusion language should use “conditional transfer” or “composition in the tested cells” and reserve “additive” for a prespecified interaction result.
- The retention percentages are descriptive ratios, and their denominators are uncertain (the DEPTH = 6 dense optimizer effect has a confidence interval containing zero). They should not be treated as precise scale laws. Only two depths, one corpus, one fixed OE modulus, one token schedule, and one batch regime are tested; the reported increase from roughly 51% to 88% retention is therefore a hypothesis, not evidence of a general scaling trend.
- OE changes the resource regime substantially: it adds 57.9M parameters at DEPTH = 6 and 77.2M at DEPTH = 8, making the total models about 3.2 and 2.5 times the corresponding dense parameter counts. The paper accounts for memory and measured time under the stated $+512$ MiB ceiling, but comparisons should be described as conditional on accepting this extra capacity. “0-parameter optimizer” is accurate only in the trainable-parameter sense; it still adds optimizer state and about 4% of the step FLOPs.
- The checkpoint stable-rank and query-scale analyses are descriptive correlations. They do not identify why OE changes the post-QK-Norm scales or why the optimizer effect transfers. A causal ablation of the query scaling (or a matched parameter/control intervention) would be needed for that mechanistic claim. The BF16-state control also changes moment precision as well as storage, so it supports a memory/precision tradeoff rather than isolating state removal alone.
- With $n=3$ seeds, the $t$ intervals are necessarily exploratory. Directional 3/3 improvements are informative, but claims of reliable effects should emphasize the held-out estimates and intervals rather than seed counts alone. A third scale, another corpus, or more preregistered seeds would materially strengthen generality.

The mathematical notation is readable and the endpoint definitions, token budgets, parameter accounting, and held-out split are unusually complete. The metadata abstract is informative but about 319 words and densely packed; shortening it and stating the bounded scope and the zero-spanning interaction intervals would improve accuracy. The paper is acceptable provided its claims are kept at this empirical scope and the retention/additivity language is revised accordingly.
