# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution and adds a useful, bounded investigation of separate unigram routing, a final readout skip, and sensitivity to the new scalars’ first optimizer update. These interventions directly extend the paper’s composition question. Relative to the moment-free table work [10] and the input-filtering and boundary-gain study [20], the added contribution is the controlled evaluation of these routing paths within the OE package. The manuscript credits the motivating discussions and does not need to claim a new general residual-network principle.

Recomputing all eight Table 8a contrasts from the retained records reproduces the reported means, sample standard deviations, and descriptive paired $95\%$ $t$-intervals. The fifteen added runs record the stated 953 updates and token budget; their reconstructed trainer sources match the supplied hashes. Earlier numerical records are unchanged. The depth-6 routing-plus-readout improvement over the joint comparator is $-0.003481 \pm 0.001977$ held-out BPB, with an interval spanning zero. At depth 8, the incremental routing contrast against the matched BF16 joint comparator is only $-0.000288 \pm 0.000715$, also unresolved. The manuscript appropriately separates this from the stronger composite comparison against native OE and distinguishes reduced paired-difference dispersion from increased endpoint dispersion. These are informative exploratory results, not an established general routing advantage.

The step-0 control supports a narrower and clearer finding: retaining the first update improves held-out BPB by $-0.002444 \pm 0.000662$ relative to skipping it, with descriptive interval $[-0.004089,-0.000799]$. Code inspection confirms that setting the gradients to `None` skips parameter displacement, Adam-state initialization, and the per-parameter counter increment together. The revised text correctly leaves momentum synchronization unverified. Likewise, the anchor algebra correctly explains why opposite-sign scalar coefficients need not cancel the unigram component token by token. For exact correspondence with the implementation, define the normalization denominators to include RMSNorm’s epsilon; the displayed algebra otherwise represents the idealized normalization.

One added implementation explanation needs correction. Section 4.4 says the calibration scales `lrm` before momentum computation and produces a momentum schedule starting at `1.031`. In the supplied trainer, `muon_momentum = get_muon_momentum(step, lrm)` uses the unscaled schedule multiplier; the factor $\sqrt{2}$ is applied subsequently in the assignment to each group’s learning rate. The package therefore retains its ordinary warmdown from $0.95$ toward $0.85$. The native schedule initially ramps from $0.85$ to $0.95$ over 300 steps and then stays at $0.95$. Correct this description and the corresponding limitation: the LR/WD calibration does not itself rescale momentum, although the package and native arms retain their different momentum schedules. This is a localized explanatory error, not a defect in the new routing comparisons.

My verification establishes source and numerical consistency; it does not independently reproduce GPU training or checkpoint measurements. The reported intervals remain exploratory at three seeds. Since the same confirmation segment has appeared in earlier versions, evaluating each new checkpoint once does not by itself establish independence from prior development decisions; retain that qualification and reserve stronger generalization claims for fresh confirmation data.
