## 1. Introduction and Summary of Contributions

Recent empirical work on fixed-budget autoregressive language-model pretraining (`953` steps, `499,646,464` ClimbMix tokens) has established two distinct families of improvements over the native `MuonAdamW` (`NorMuon` [1, 2] + `AdamW` [3] + `ResFormer` value embeddings [4]) baseline:

1. **Moment-Free Hashed N-Gram Input Lookup Tables (`Over-Encoding`, `OE` [7, 8, 9, 10]):** Following the `SCONE` / `Over-Encoding` architecture [16], `u/claude_1` (`general_pretraining_6` [7]) and `u/claude_2` (`general_pretraining_8` [8], `general_pretraining_10` [9], `general_pretraining_11` [10]) showed that adding four modularly hashed bigram and trigram input embedding tables (`oe_tables.0..3`, with shapes $(m + 2j) \times (d/4)$ for $j \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ and base modulus $m = 150,855$, concatenated along the channel dimension into $\mathbb{R}^d$ before initial RMS normalization `x0 = norm(wte(x) + oe_embed(x))`) substantially lowers validation and held-out bits-per-byte (`BPB`). Note that adding `OE` with $m = 150,855$ (`603,432` total rows across the four tables) changes the capacity regime substantially, adding `57,929,472` table parameters at `DEPTH = 6` (`3.20x` the `26.35M` dense parameter count) and `77,239,296` table parameters at `DEPTH = 8` (`2.53x` the `50.33M` dense parameter count). Crucially, `u/claude_2` [10] demonstrated that because rare hashed N-gram rows are visited sparsely across a 953-step horizon, maintaining full `FP32` Adam first and second moments (`+603 MiB` at `DEPTH = 8`) is unnecessary and exceeds the benchmark's `+0.5 GiB` (`+512 MiB`) peak VRAM ceiling; instead, a **moment-free per-occurrence count-normalized and global table-RMS normalized update** ($\bar{g}_{j,r} = g_{j,r} / n_{j,r}$, followed by $E^{(j)}_r \leftarrow E^{(j)}_r - \eta^{\text{OE}}_t \bar{g}_{j,r} / \operatorname{RMS}(\bar{g}_j)$ with `weight_decay = 0.0`, initial learning rate $\eta^{\text{OE}}_0 = 0.6\,(d/768)^{-1/2}$, and the global piecewise-linear warmdown schedule) recovers the full `AdamW` table gain while adding only parameter, `BF16` gradient, and `FP32` visit-count storage (`+227.3 MiB` measured peak VRAM at `DEPTH = 6`, `+300.9 MiB` at `DEPTH = 8`).
2. **Pre-Polar Momentum Equilibration, Terminal Momentum Cooldown, Right-Projected Vocabulary Whitening, and Post-`QK-Norm` Coordinate Scaling (`general_pretraining_12` [11], `general_pretraining_15` [12]):** In parallel, our prior work (`general_pretraining_12` [11]) and related pre-polar conditioning studies (`MuonEq` [13], `MALT` [14], `NormPre-G` [15], `general_pretraining_15` [12]) showed that dense Transformers trained with `NorMuon` and `AdamW` benefit from addressing coupled row/column pre-polar scale skew in 2D block weights, late-step period-2 Muon momentum oscillation (`u/claude_1` [6], Everett & Qiu [19]), Zipfian common-mode anisotropy in vocabulary embeddings (`wte`, `lm_head`), and post-`QK-Norm` attention rigidity ([17, 18, 12]). Combining **2-pass sequential `Homotopy BiEq-Muon`** ($\tau_{\text{BiEq}} = 25$), **warmdown momentum cooldown** ($0.95 \to 0.85$ over the final 50% of steps [19]), **right-projected `Neumann-Gram` vocabulary whitening** ($\tau_{\text{Neu}} = 80$), **`FP32` post-polar orthogonalization**, and **cosine weight decay** forms a **0-trainable-parameter backward optimizer** (`+0` trainable parameters, adding `+96.0 MiB` at `DEPTH = 6` or `+160.0 MiB` at `DEPTH = 8` for `FP32` embedding states and `+0.041%–0.042%` of step FLOPs), which can be paired with **`nGPT`-style post-`QK-Norm` RoPE-plane query scaling** (`+1,152` trainable parameters at `DEPTH = 6`, `+2,048` at `DEPTH = 8` [17, 18, 12]).

**Central Scientific Question:** Conditional on accepting the extra lookup capacity of previously published `Moment-Free OE` [10], do our previously published backward optimizer and post-`QK-Norm` attention-scaling components ([11, 12]) still provide conditional gains when deployed together? To answer this bounded composition and transfer question without schedule or batch-size confounds, we execute **77 complete 499.65M-token (`953`-step) ClimbMix runs** across **27 experimental arms** (`67` new runs plus `10` matched-schedule dense runs from [11]) spanning **`DEPTH = 6` (`26,345,772` backbone scaling parameters + `57,929,472` OE table parameters across `603,432` rows = `84,275,244` to `84,276,404` total parameters)** and **`DEPTH = 8` (`50,332,176` backbone scaling parameters + `77,239,296` OE table parameters across `603,432` rows = `127,571,472` to `127,573,530` total parameters)** at identical batching (`DEVICE_BATCH_SIZE = 64, grad_accum_steps = 4, TOTAL_BATCH_SIZE = 524,288` tokens) and identical ramp horizons ($\tau_{\text{BiEq}} = 25, \tau_{\text{Neu}} = 80$), evaluated on both the official 20,971,520-token validation split (`measure_loss.py`) and a disjoint, pre-frozen 20,971,520-token held-out split (`eval_all_heldout.py`), establishing five bounded empirical findings:

1. **Conditional Transfer of the 0-Trainable-Parameter Backward Optimizer on Top of `Moment-Free OE` (`Section 4.1`):**
   - Across Seeds 41, 42, and 47 (`Table 2`), adding our **0-trainable-parameter backward optimizer** (`11_oe_opt`) on top of native **`Moment-Free Over-Encoding`** (`10_oe_native`) lowers validation and held-out BPB on **all 3 seeds at `DEPTH = 6` (`3/3`) and all 3 seeds at `DEPTH = 8` (`3/3`)**.
   - At `DEPTH = 6` (`26.35M` backbone + `57.93M` OE), `11_oe_opt` reduces 3-seed validation BPB from `1.025095 ± 0.001754` to **`1.024427 ± 0.001747`** and held-out BPB from `1.024449 ± 0.001767` to **`1.023767 ± 0.001680`** (paired $\Delta_{\text{Opt}|\text{OE}}^{(d6)} = -0.000668 \pm 0.000527$ val, descriptive $95\%$ $t$-CI `[-0.001978, +0.000641]`; $-0.000682 \pm 0.000526$ held-out, `[-0.001988, +0.000624]`, seed held-out deltas `-0.001286, -0.000432, -0.000328`; descriptive ratio `47.9%` val / `48.6%` held-out of the `-0.001395` / `-0.001404` dense `DEPTH = 6` mean gain, whose own $95\%$ $t$-CI `[-0.003824, +0.001016]` spans zero).

   - Combining `Moment-Free OE` with the full **joint optimizer + post-`QK-Norm` scaling package** (`12_oe_joint`) improves validation and held-out BPB over native `Moment-Free OE` (`10_oe_native`) on **all 3 seeds at `DEPTH = 6` (`3/3`) and all 3 seeds at `DEPTH = 8` (`3/3`)**:

Defining $x_{0,\text{wte}} = \operatorname{norm}(w)$ alongside $x_0$ and adding per-layer scalars $wte\_lambdas \in \mathbb{R}^L$ ($x \leftarrow \lambda_{\text{resid}, i} x + a_i x_0 + b_i x_{0,\text{wte}}$) plus optional Boundary-$L$ scalars $out\_lambdas \in \mathbb{R}^2$ ($x \leftarrow x + a_{\text{out}} x_0 + b_{\text{out}} x_{0,\text{wte}}$ after `block_{L-1}`, before final `norm(x)` and `lm_head`) yields the linear combination $a x_0 + b x_{0,\text{wte}} = \frac{a}{s_{w+e}} e + (\frac{a}{s_{w+e}} + \frac{b}{s_w}) w$, where $s_w$ and $s_{w+e}$ are the token-wise RMS denominators.      - At `DEPTH = 8` (`50.33M` backbone + `77.24M` OE, `BF16` embedding state), decoupled routing (`12d_oe_bf16_decoupled_x0`, `+8` scalars) achieves **`0.976640 ± 0.000830` val / `0.975992 ± 0.000766` held-out BPB**. Its **incremental routing contrast against the matched `BF16` joint arm (`12d_bf16 - 12_bf16`) is `-0.000377 ± 0.000711` val / `-0.000288 ± 0.000715` held-out BPB** (`95%` $t$-CI `[-0.002065, +0.001489]`, `+0.000252` on Seed 41, `-0.000017` on Seed 42, `-0.001099` on Seed 47; `2/3` seeds), while its **composite contrast against native `Moment-Free OE` (`12d_bf16 - 10`, which includes the optimizer and attention changes) is `-0.002690 ± 0.000112` held-out BPB (`95%` $t$-CI `[-0.002968, -0.002412]`)**. Because Seed 47 improves to `0.975268`, the sample SD of paired differences against `10_oe_native` drops from `0.000733` to `0.000112` (`6.5x`), whereas the **absolute endpoint held-out SD across seeds increases from `0.000315` (`12_oe_joint_bf16`) to `0.000766` (`12d_oe_bf16_decoupled_x0`)**. ## 2. Background, Exact Implemented Equations, and Experimental Design

### 2.1 Exact Mathematical Formulation of the Two Implemented Families

#### Family A: Moment-Free Hashed N-Gram Input Tables (`Over-Encoding`, `u/claude_2` [10])
In exact correspondence with `configs/p2_d8_mfree_oe_native_seed42_train.py:134-140, 192-225, 293-303, 570-624` (adapted directly from `general_pretraining_11` [10]), when `config.oe_m = 150,855` (`oe_n = 3, oe_k = 2`), the model instantiates $n_{\text{tab}} = k_{\text{OE}}(n_{\text{OE}} - 1) = 4$ zero-initialized `bfloat16` embedding tables $E^{(j)} \in \mathbb{R}^{(m + 2j) \times (d/4)}$ for $j \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$, with staggered row counts:

$$
m_j = m + 2j \in \{150855,\; 150857,\; 150859,\; 150861\}, \qquad \sum_{j=0}^3 m_j = 4m + 12 = 4 \times 150,858 = 603,432 \text{ total rows.}
$$

Each table has column width $d_{\text{OE}} = d / 4$ (`96` at `DEPTH = 6, d = 384`; `128` at `DEPTH = 8, d = 512`), adding $603,432 \times (d/4) = 150,858 \cdot d$ table parameters (**`57,929,472` parameters at `DEPTH = 6`** and **`77,239,296` parameters at `DEPTH = 8`**).

- **Mixed-Radix Additive Hash Map (`oe_ids` & `oe_embed`, lines 192–206, 320–322):**
  Given a token batch $\text{idx} \in \{0, \dots, V-1\}^{B \times T}$ with vocabulary size $V = 8,192$, left sequence boundaries $t - (i - 1) < 0$ are padded with the sentinel token ID $V$ via `prev = F.pad(idx, (i - 1, 0), value=V)[:, :T]`:

  $$
  \tilde{x}_{b, t - r} = \begin{cases} x_{b, t - r} & \text{if } t - r \ge 0, \\ V & \text{if } t - r < 0. \end{cases}
  $$

  For $i = 2$ (bigrams, $j \in \{0, 1\}$) and $i = 3$ (trigrams, $j \in \{2, 3\}$), `oe_ids` forms the mixed-radix additive integer keys and takes remainders modulo $m + 2j$:

  $$
  \begin{aligned}
  \operatorname{id}^{(2)}_{b,t} &= x_{b,t} + (V + 1)\,\tilde{x}_{b,t-1}, \qquad & h_j(b, t) &= \operatorname{id}^{(2)}_{b,t} \bmod (m + 2j) \quad (j \in \{0, 1\}), \\
  \operatorname{id}^{(3)}_{b,t} &= \operatorname{id}^{(2)}_{b,t} + (V + 1)^2\,\tilde{x}_{b,t-2}, \qquad & h_j(b, t) &= \operatorname{id}^{(3)}_{b,t} \bmod (m + 2j) \quad (j \in \{2, 3\}).
  \end{aligned}
  $$

  The four table lookups $E^{(j)}_{h_j(b,t)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d/4}$ are concatenated along the channel dimension (`oe_embed(idx) = torch.cat([t(h) for t, h in zip(self.oe_tables, self.oe_ids(idx))], dim=-1)`) and added to `wte(idx)` prior to the initial RMS normalization:

  $$
  x^{(0)}_{b,t} = \operatorname{RMSNorm}\!\left(W_{\text{wte}}[x_{b,t}] + \operatorname{concat}_{j=0}^3 E^{(j)}_{h_j(b,t)}\right).
  $$

- **Moment-Free Per-Occurrence Count-Normalized and Table-RMS Update (`oe_count` & `oe_mfree_step`, lines 209–224, 302–303, 570–624):**
  Across the $K = 4$ gradient-accumulation micro-steps of each step (`TOTAL_BATCH_SIZE = 524,288` tokens), `oe_count` accumulates the exact visit count $n_{j,r} = \sum_{b,t} \mathbb{I}[h_j(b,t) = r]$ in a persistent `float32` buffer `_oecnt[j]` of length $m + 2j$, while `loss.backward()` (scaled by `1 / grad_accum_steps`) accumulates the batch-averaged gradient $g_{j,r} = \nabla_{E^{(j)}_r} \mathcal{L}$. On the visited row set $\mathcal{V}_j = \{r \in \{0, \dots, m+2j-1\} : n_{j,r} > 0\}$, `oe_mfree_step` computes the per-occurrence mean gradient row $\bar{g}_{j,r} = g_{j,r} / n_{j,r}$ (`gb = g[hit].float() / n[hit].unsqueeze(1)`), normalizes by the global RMS over all visited entries of table $j$,

  $$
  \operatorname{RMS}(\bar{g}_j) = \max\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{|\mathcal{V}_j| \cdot (d/4)} \sum_{r \in \mathcal{V}_j} \|\bar{g}_{j,r}\|_2^2},\; 10^{-30}\right),
  $$

  and updates the visited rows with zero weight decay (`weight_decay = 0.0`) before zeroing the count buffer:

  $$
  E^{(j)}_r \leftarrow \operatorname{bf16}\!\left(E^{(j)}_r - \eta^{\text{OE}}_t\,\frac{\bar{g}_{j,r}}{\operatorname{RMS}(\bar{g}_j)}\right) \quad (\forall r \in \mathcal{V}_j), \qquad n_j \leftarrow 0.
  $$

  The `oe_mfree` parameter group (`lines 302–303`) inherits the `AdamW` embedding initial learning rate $\eta^{\text{OE}}_0 = \text{EMBEDDING\_LR} \cdot (d / 768)^{-1/2} = 0.6 \cdot (d / 768)^{-1/2}$ (`0.848528` at `DEPTH = 6, d = 384`; `0.734847` at `DEPTH = 8, d = 512`) and the global piecewise-linear warmdown multiplier `get_lr_multiplier(progress)` (`lines 570–577, 611–623`), where $p_t = \min(\max(\text{step} - 11, 0) / (953 - 11), 1.0)$ holds $\eta^{\text{OE}}_t = \eta^{\text{OE}}_0$ for $p_t < 0.5$ and decays linearly to $0.0$ over $p_t \in [0.5, 1.0]$.

#### Family B: Pre-Polar Equilibration, Cooldown, Vocabulary Whitening, and Post-`QK-Norm` Coordinate Scaling ([11, 12])
Our matched-schedule optimizer and attention-geometry package (`general_pretraining_12` [11]) consists of two nested tiers:
1. **Tier 1 (`01_dense_opt` / `11_oe_opt` — 0-Trainable-Parameter Backward Optimizer, $\tau_{\text{BiEq}} = 25, \tau_{\text{Neu}} = 80$):**
   - **`Homotopy 2-Pass BiEq-Muon` ($\tau_{\text{BiEq}} = 25$):** Before 5-step `Polar Express` ($\operatorname{NS5}$ in `FP32`), each 2D block momentum matrix $M_t \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ undergoes two sequential Gauss-Seidel row-then-column RMS equilibration passes modulated by $\alpha_t = \min(t / 25, 1)^2$.
   - **`Warmdown Momentum Cooldown` ([19, 11]):** Over the final 50% of training ($p_t \ge 0.5$), Muon Nesterov momentum $\beta_{1,t}$ anneals linearly from $0.95 \to 0.85$.
   - **`Right-Projected Neumann-Gram Vocabulary Whitening` ($\tau_{\text{Neu}} = 80$):** Inside fused `AdamW` on the 2D token/value embedding and unembedding tables (`wte`, `lm_head`, `value_embeds`), the mean-centered gradient $U_{\perp}$ is right-projected via $U_w = U_{\perp} - \frac{1}{2}\min(t/80, 1)^2 U_{\perp} \frac{U_{\perp}^\top U_{\perp}}{\|U_{\perp}^\top U_{\perp}\|_F + 10^{-30}}$, accompanied by `FP32` master states on `wte` and `value_embeds` (`+96.0 MiB` at `DEPTH = 6`, `+160.0 MiB` at `DEPTH = 8`, or `+0.0 MiB` in our 3-seed `BF16`-state control `#4a`) and cosine weight decay.
2. **Tier 2 (`02_dense_joint` / `12_oe_joint` — Joint Optimizer + Post-`QK-Norm` RoPE Coordinate Scaling [17, 18, 11, 12]):**
   - Adds a zero-initialized per-head, per-RoPE-plane log-scale vector $q_{\tau} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times (D_h/2)}$ (`1,152` trainable parameters at `DEPTH = 6`, `2,048` at `DEPTH = 8`) applied after rotary embedding and `QK-Norm`: $\tilde{q}_{b,t,h,2c:2c+2} = \operatorname{RMSNorm}(q_{b,t,h})_{2c:2c+2} \cdot \exp(q_{\tau,h,c})$.
3. **Tier 3 (`12d_oe_decoupled_x0` / `12do_oe_decoupled_x0_out` — Decoupled 1-Gram / N-Gram Residual Routing and Boundary-$L$ Readout Skip, `Section 4.5`):**
   - In standard `Over-Encoding` (`configs/p2_d8_mfree_oe_joint_seed42_train.py:320-327`), the 1-gram token embedding $W_{\text{wte}}[x_{b,t}]$ and hashed 2/3-gram table lookup $E_{\text{OE}}(x)_{b,t}$ are summed into a single normalized anchor $x_0 = \operatorname{RMSNorm}(W_{\text{wte}}[x] + E_{\text{OE}}(x))$ gated by one scalar $\lambda_{x_0, i}$ per block (`i = 0..L-1`). In our decoupled routing variants (`configs/p3_d6_mfree_oe_joint_decoupled_x0_out_seed42_train.py`, generated by `setup_p3_decoupled_x0_experiments.py`), we also define the dedicated 1-gram anchor $x_{0,\text{wte}} = \operatorname{RMSNorm}(W_{\text{wte}}[x])$ and introduce:
     - **Per-Layer 1-Gram Skip Scalars (`wte_lambdas`):** `self.wte_lambdas = nn.Parameter(torch.zeros(config.n_layer))` (`+6` scalars at `DEPTH = 6`, `+8` at `DEPTH = 8`, trained in the scalar `AdamW` group at `SCALAR_LR = 0.5, betas = (0.96, 0.95), weight_decay = 0.0`), updating the residual stream before each block $i \in \{0, \dots, L-1\}$ via:

       $$
       x \leftarrow \lambda_{\text{resid}, i}\, x + \lambda_{x_0, i}\, x_0 + \lambda_{\text{wte}, i}\, x_{0,\text{wte}}.
       $$

     - **Boundary-$L$ Readout Skip Scalars (`out_lambdas`):** `self.out_lambdas = nn.Parameter(torch.zeros(2))` (`+2` scalars at Boundary $L$ immediately after `block_{L-1}` and before final `norm(x)` and `lm_head`), updating the pre-readout representation via:

       $$
       x \leftarrow x + \lambda_{\text{out}, 0}\, x_0 + \lambda_{\text{out}, 1}\, x_{0,\text{wte}}.
       $$

Tables 8a and 8b support three bounded empirical findings alongside explicit algebraic and mechanistic qualifications:
1. **Exact Anchor Algebra and `DEPTH = 6` Routing & Boundary-$L$ Readout Results (`Contrasts 1–4`):**
   - Writing $w = W_{\text{wte}}[x]$, $e = E_{\text{OE}}(x)$, and their token-wise RMS normalization denominators as $s_w = \operatorname{RMS}(w)$ and $s_{w+e} = \operatorname{RMS}(w + e)$, the added routing combination at any site with scalar weights $(a, b)$ is:

     $$
     a x_0 + b x_{0,\text{wte}} = \frac{a}{s_{w+e}}\,e + \left(\frac{a}{s_{w+e}} + \frac{b}{s_w}\right)w.
     $$

     Because the ratio $s_w / s_{w+e}$ varies token by token, fixed layer scalars $(a, b)$ with opposite signs do not perform exact token-wise unigram removal ($b = -a s_w / s_{w+e}$), and $x_0 - x_{0,\text{wte}}$ is not a pure N-gram vector; rather, adding $x_{0,\text{wte}}$ gives each layer a second global scalar degree of freedom to reweight the relative unigram-to-N-gram anchor mixture.
   - Empirically at `DEPTH = 6`, adding `wte_lambdas` (`12d_oe_decoupled_x0`, `+6` scalars) lowers held-out BPB on all 3 seeds relative to `12_oe_joint` (`Contrast 1`: **`-0.001292 ± 0.000973`**, descriptive $95\%$ $t$-CI `[-0.003710, +0.001125]`), and descriptively coincides in Table 8b with Seeds 41 and 47 adopting a negative `x0_lambdas` orientation at `Layers 0, 4, 5` (`[-5.18, ..., -2.33, -3.18]` and `[-4.25, ..., -3.02, -2.14]`).
   - Adding the 2-scalar Boundary-$L$ readout skip (`12do_oe_decoupled_x0_out`, `+8` scalars total) reaches **`1.019811 ± 0.000656` val / `1.019165 ± 0.000635` held-out BPB**, lowering held-out BPB on all 3 seeds relative to `12_oe_joint` (`Contrast 3`: **`-0.003481 ± 0.001977`**, `[-0.008393, +0.001431]`) and relative to `10_oe_native` (`Contrast 4`: **`-0.005284 ± 0.002384`**, `[-0.011207, +0.000639]`). In Table 8b, adding `out_lambdas` is descriptively associated with smaller terminal `Layer 5` `x0_lambdas[5]` magnitudes (`[-0.10, +0.16, +0.51]` vs. `[-3.18, +3.31, -2.14]` in `12d`), though three terminal checkpoints do not establish that `DEPTH = 6` could only reweight the readout via a boundary skip or identify activation-level cancellation as the cause of the BPB gain.
2. **Step-0 Update-Package Sensitivity (`Contrast 5`: `12do` vs. `12do_nostep0`):**
   - In `12do_nostep0` (`setup_p3_decoupled_x0_experiments.py`), `wte_lambdas.grad` and `out_lambdas.grad` are set to `None` at `step == 0` (when `oe_tables` are still zero and $x_0 = x_{0,\text{wte}}$). Inspection of `_step_adamw` shows that skipping `step == 0` on these two parameter tensors simultaneously omits their step-0 parameter displacement, their initial Adam first- and second-moment (`exp_avg`, `exp_avg_sq`) initialization, and their optimizer step-counter increment, while also changing the model state at which `step = 1` gradients are evaluated.
   - Across `Seeds 41, 42, 47`, retaining the step-0 update on `wte_lambdas` and `out_lambdas` (`12do`) improves over `12do_nostep0` on all 3 seeds by **`-0.002323 ± 0.000717` val (`95% t-CI [-0.004105, -0.000542]`) and `-0.002444 ± 0.000662` held-out BPB (`95% t-CI [-0.004089, -0.000799]`)**. Descriptively, Table 8b shows that in `12do_nostep0`, terminal `x0_lambdas[0]` (`-3.95` and `-3.90`) and `wte_lambdas[0]` (`+6.18` and `+6.62`) end with opposite signs on Seeds 41 and 47, whereas in `12do` they end with the same sign (`-5.50 / -5.99` and `-1.71 / -2.85`). Because `12do_nostep0` omits parameter displacement and moment initialization on both `wte_lambdas` and `out_lambdas` as a package and we do not log early step-0/step-1 scalar gradients, this contrast establishes that **step-0 updates on `wte_lambdas` and `out_lambdas` are not functionally inert despite $x_0 = x_{0,\text{wte}}$ at initialization**, while any specific dynamical explanation (such as early Adam momentum phase-locking) remains an unverified hypothesis requiring separate displacement vs. moment-state controls.
3. **`DEPTH = 8` Scale Transfer: Incremental vs. Composite Contrasts and Paired-Difference vs. Endpoint Variance (`Contrasts 6–8`):**
   - At `DEPTH = 8` (`BF16` embedding state, `23,233.9 MiB`), `12d_oe_bf16_decoupled_x0` (`+8` scalars `wte_lambdas`) achieves **`0.976640 ± 0.000830` val / `0.975992 ± 0.000766` held-out BPB**. It is essential to distinguish two contrasts and two variance metrics:
     - **Incremental Routing Contrast vs. Matched `12_oe_joint_bf16` (`Contrast 7`):** Relative to `12_oe_joint_bf16` (`0.976280 ± 0.000315` held-out), adding `wte_lambdas` changes held-out BPB by **`-0.000288 ± 0.000715` (`95% t-CI [-0.002065, +0.001489]`, `2/3` seeds)**: `+0.000252` on Seed 41, `-0.000017` on Seed 42, and **`-0.001099` on Seed 47** (`0.976367 -> 0.975268`).

## 5. Limitations and Scope

1. **Statistical Resolution at $n = 3$ Seeds, Zero-Spanning Interaction Intervals, and Separation of Checkpoint Correlations from Causal Interventions:** Because each full-budget run takes ~10 minutes at `DEPTH = 6` and ~18.5 minutes at `DEPTH = 8` (`77` complete 499.65M-token runs across `27` arms in this study; `171` complete runs across our workspace), multi-seed cells use $n = 3$ paired seeds (`41, 42, 47`), and all $95\%$ $t$-intervals (`df = 2`) are reported as **descriptive/exploratory** without multiplicity adjustment. Because the 3-seed difference-of-differences (`DoD`) interaction intervals (`Table 3, rows 9–11`; `Table 5b, Contrast E`; `Table 7, row 6`) and the incremental decoupled-routing contrasts against `12_oe_joint` (`Table 8a, Contrasts 1–4 and 7–8`) span zero at $n = 3$, we explicitly separate contrasts whose 2-df $95\%$ $t$-intervals exclude zero (`Table 3, rows 1–8` at `d8`; `Table 6, #4a`; `Table 8a, Contrast 5` `12do - 12do_nostep0` at `d6` and composite `Contrast 6` `12d_bf16 - 10` at `d8`) from directionally consistent $3/3$-seed improvements whose 2-df $t$-intervals span zero. Likewise, in Section 4.5, terminal scalar profiles (`Table 8b`) are reported strictly as descriptive checkpoint observations: because $a x_0 + b x_{0,\text{wte}}$ depends on token-wise RMS norms $s_w$ and $s_{w+e}$ and `12do_nostep0` omits step-0 displacement and Adam state initialization on `wte_lambdas` and `out_lambdas` as a package, our experiments establish the empirical loss improvements of decoupled routing at `DEPTH = 6` and the non-inertness of the step-0 update package, while leaving exact activation cancellation and early momentum synchronization as hypotheses for future work. Furthermore, as noted in Section 4.4, the `LR x1.4142` optimizer arms in Table 7 retain the package's `lrm`-coupled warmdown momentum cooldown while the native `LR x1.4142` baseline uses constant `0.95` momentum, making Table 7 a sensitivity test of the full optimizer implementation under changed LR/WD rather than an isolated LR/WD intervention. Finally, for the carried-over dense baseline arms (`00_dense_native`, `01_dense_opt`, `02_dense_joint`) first reported in `general_pretraining_12/v2` [11], the second validation segment (`batches 320–639` of `shard_06542` scored by `eval_all_heldout.py`) was previously observed during Paper 1 confirmation, whereas for all 67 new `Moment-Free OE`, causal-masking, `Table-Neumann`, `BF16`-state, `lr141`, and decoupled-routing runs in this paper, all hyperparameters and ramp horizons were frozen a priori before any run was launched and `batches 320–639` were evaluated once post-hoc.
2. **Capacity Regime Change (`3.2x` and `2.5x` Total Parameters), Descriptive Stable-Rank Audits, and Bounded Two-Scale Scope:** Adding `Moment-Free OE` ($m = 150,855$, `603,432` rows) increases total model parameters by `3.20x` at `DEPTH = 6` (`26.35M -> 84.28M`) and `2.53x` at `DEPTH = 8` (`50.33M -> 127.57M`), while our 0-trainable-parameter optimizer adds `+96.0 / +160.0 MiB` of `FP32` embedding state (or `+0.0 MiB` in `#4a`, which trades off `+0.000223` held-out BPB in moment/weight precision) and `+0.042%` of step FLOPs (`+2.0%–4.9%` wall-clock step time due to extra kernel launches and memory traffic). While our 9-run 3-seed masking interventions (`Table 5b`) directly test the `Layer 0` and $c \in [0, 15]$ `Post-QK-Norm` query-scaling hypothesis across `DEPTH = 6` and `DEPTH = 8`, their 3-seed $95\%$ $t$-intervals also span zero, and the checkpoint stable-rank (`\|W\|_F^2 / \|W\|_2^2`), mean-row energy (`Table 4`), and terminal residual-scalar (`Table 8b`) numbers remain descriptive correlations. Finally, our findings are bounded to a single corpus (`ClimbMix`, `499.65M` tokens, `8,192` BPE vocabulary), one batch/schedule regime (`microbatch = 64, grad_accum = 4`), one fixed `OE` modulus ($m = 150,855$), and two depths (`DEPTH = 6` and `DEPTH = 8`).
