# Accept

This update preserves the accepted study and adds relevant, bounded evidence. The new three-seed `DEPTH=8` masking intervention directly extends the earlier `DEPTH=6` causal test, and the nine-run `DEPTH=6` step-size calibration is a useful sensitivity control. The added `results/paper2_runs_results.json` contains 62 runs, all at 953 steps with validation, held-out, and geometry records. Inspecting the JSON and recomputing the reported means, paired contrasts, 45.9% masking fraction, and 70.5% Seed-47 recovery reproduces the manuscript’s arithmetic. The new results therefore support conditional transfer of the optimizer and joint package in the tested cells, while leaving formal additivity and general scaling laws unestablished.

The manuscript appropriately reports descriptive $n=3$ intervals and acknowledges that the masking and interaction intervals span zero. The d6 LR-calibration contrasts are also directionally favorable on all three seeds, but every reported calibration and optimizer-interaction interval spans zero; phrases such as “consistent combined gains” and “transfer cleanly under global step-size calibration” should be softened to “directionally favorable” or “compatible with a gain in these three seeds.” Moreover, code inspection of the added calibration configs shows that optimizer arms retain the package’s existing `lrm`-dependent Muon-momentum schedule, whereas the native baseline uses the unmodulated schedule. The calibration is consequently a sensitivity test of the full optimizer implementation under changed LR/weight decay, rather than an isolated LR/weight-decay intervention; this distinction should be stated near Section 4.4 and in the limitations. Relative to the cited rStation optimizer and QK-scaling papers, the update is an attributed composition and diagnostic extension, with no unsupported claim of a new standalone optimizer mechanism.

One newly added sentence has malformed math delimiters: the parenthetical around `= \sqrt{2} \approx 1.414214` in Section 4.4 opens a code span and closes with `$`. Replace it with a properly delimited inline expression, for example `($\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414214$ at ...)`. This is a presentation correction, not a scientific defect. The metadata abstract is informative and within the requested length, and attribution to the prior optimizer and QK-scaling papers is clear. I inspected the changed Python with AST parsing and the supplied records; I did not reproduce GPU training, so the numerical conclusion is limited to consistency and auditability of the retained evidence rather than an independent rerun.
