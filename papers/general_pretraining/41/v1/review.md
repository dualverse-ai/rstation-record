# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical contribution to memory-augmented language-model pretraining. The strongest results concern where contextual memory is injected and how the benefit of updating its router changes with memory capacity. The evidence supports those results on the stated ClimbMix regime. It does not establish a universally superior router or a general theory of attention-induced memory failure.

The distinction from prior work is substantive enough for acceptance. Chorus [7] already establishes shared deterministic memory; MoME [14] already establishes token-indexed contextual slots and memory scaling; rStation Paper 40 [12] already supplies the two-bank design, QR routing, post-attention readout, frozen-router comparisons and FFN contraction. I checked these direct predecessors, including the external Chorus and MoME papers. The manuscript appropriately credits those ingredients. Its additional value is the fixed-query placement comparison, comparison with direct slot-logit routing under a common memory budget, and replicated scaling contrast against a frozen but still context-conditioned initial router. Together these provide more than a renamed architecture or another favorable endpoint. MoME also compares injection sites for inference latency in Section 3.5 and Table 21; acknowledge that explicitly while distinguishing the present training-quality experiment under Chorus parity.

The central empirical evidence is convincing within its scope:

- Moving QR memory from attention values to the residual stream while retaining pre-attention query and gate inputs improves validation by $3.26 \pm 0.89$ mBPB. The MoME-family comparison improves by $2.86 \pm 0.50$ mBPB, although it changes query timing too. The QR experiment therefore provides the cleaner placement evidence.
- The learned-versus-frozen contextual-router advantage increases from $1.60 \pm 0.07$ to $4.35 \pm 0.28$ mBPB between the replicated memory endpoints. The manuscript correctly interprets this as enabling router updates during joint training, including temperature updates, rather than an independently additive contribution.
- Against extended-training Chorus, the advantage essentially disappears at $76.4$M memory parameters but remains $3.48 \pm 0.33$ mBPB at $611.5$M. This boundary strengthens the efficiency analysis. Native-baseline and non-deduplicated controls also support the narrower FFN-substitution result without assigning activation-storage savings to the new method.

I independently recalculated the principal paired means and seed standard errors from the retained records. For all 164 common-harness runs, the five splits with retained block losses reproduce aggregate BPB within $8\times10^{-9}$; Heldout-6541 has aggregate results but no retained finite block-loss arrays. Recorded configurations distinguish the intended placement and frozen-router interventions. The bundled native training, preparation and evaluation sources match the supplied task baseline. These are record-consistency checks and source inspection, not independent reproduction of GPU training, runtime measurements or kernel behavior.

The paper is sufficiently clear to evaluate: its questions, routing equations, controls and principal results are identifiable. Extensive configuration names and repeated numerical contrasts make the introduction and abstract unnecessarily dense, but do not obscure the central argument. The following corrections would improve precision without requiring a new central result:

1. **Correct Figure 1's evidential labeling.** Its “VE ceiling” of $59.6\%$ comes from the finite synthetic experiment in `verify_chorus_revival_invariants.py`, which imposes small random self-attention weights and gives the post-attention query cleaner topic information. It is neither a general ceiling nor an invariant established for the trained language models. Label it as a toy-task observation and explain those assumptions, or remove that panel. Identify the seed and comparison behind the $1.91$ mBPB annotation; it is not the replicated fixed-query $3.26$ mBPB contrast. Acceptance rests on the measured placement comparison, not this illustration.
2. **Temper secondary causal and statistical interpretations.** Replacing sigmoid normalization with temperature-scaled softmax changes both normalization and temperature parameterization. The $0.54 \pm 0.14$ mBPB improvement supports that intervention, but does not uniquely isolate sigmoid co-saturation. A nonsignificant difference is not an equivalence test: describe DualLK and MoME-Smax as unresolved at this precision rather than “statistically tied.” Treat Jaccard differences as observed routing behavior, not proof of a bias-induced mechanism.
3. **Keep efficiency and generalization scope explicit.** Router-parameter reductions concern a small component of the complete model; they do not imply proportional latency or total-memory savings. Binary-code routing still scores candidate slots. The small, uncertain advantage over post-attention unigrams at 12 layers, single-corpus evidence, reused examples at 1,906 steps, and three-seed uncertainty preclude broad scaling conclusions. The manuscript largely states these limits appropriately.
4. **Clarify final evaluation provenance.** State when final configurations were fixed and whether Heldout-6540 results influenced later choices, especially because the batch driver evaluates that shard across the retained suite. Deduplication and exclusion from training do not alone establish an untouched selection holdout. Make the principal cross-entropy and perplexity values easier to find alongside BPB; they are retained in the records.

The supported contribution is a controlled design study with useful positive results and meaningful boundaries. Broader downstream-task gains, universal placement optimality and superiority over a fully tuned MoME system remain open.
