# Accept

This is a technically careful, bounded empirical study of optimizer geometry for the supplied language-model pretraining task. The main contribution is a sequential two-pass row/column equilibration operator before finite-step Newton–Schulz orthogonalization, with a homotopy schedule. The paper also evaluates a right-projected Gram update for AdamW vocabulary tables and terminal momentum cooldown. These components are clearly distinguished from prior one-pass MuonEq-R/RC [17], MALT [14], NormPre-G [15], nGPT coordinate scaling [20], and previously reported momentum-cooldown observations [9,21]. The sequential Gauss–Seidel construction is a meaningful extension of MuonEq's simultaneous equilibration, although it is an incremental optimizer modification rather than a new general theory.

The empirical design is substantially stronger than a single favorable run. The manuscript reports 97 full-budget runs, three seeds for the principal depth-8 comparisons, an untouched held-out score, matched controls, a Seed-42 $2\times2\times2$ factorial, a three-seed cooldown/Neumann factorial, and transfer to a depth-6 model. The central comparisons are internally reproducible from the supplied records: two-pass BiEq at $\tau_{\rm BiEq}=80$ beats common-backbone MuonEq-RC on all three seeds, and $\tau_{\rm BiEq}=25$ improves the FP32-norm control on all three held-out seeds (mean difference $-0.001073$ BPB, reported 95% $t$ interval $[-0.002118,-0.000029]$). The matched cooldown-plus-Neumann addition improves both BiEq and MuonEq-R on all three seeds. The paper is appropriately candid that the comparison with row-only MuonEq-R is seed-heterogeneous and has a confidence interval spanning zero.

The mathematical description is mostly sound and maps to the implementation. The dimensions in the sequential equilibration equations are consistent, and the right-space projection in Lemma 2 is correctly written as $P=\hat\mu^\top\hat\mu$, $U_\parallel=UP$, and $U_\perp=U(I-P)$. The zero-row and exact-projection mean-preservation statements are properly qualified by the $\epsilon_0\to0$ limit. Static inspection of all supplied Python files parses successfully; the JSON result ledgers agree with the manuscript means and held-out values. I did not rerun the GPU training jobs, so the review verifies the released records and code structure rather than independently reproducing the 97 runs.

Two corrections are needed for a final version. First, the Neumann-Gram FLOP audit is undercounted. The custom AdamW kernel applies the update whenever `p.ndim == 2`, which includes `wte`, four `value_embeds` tables, and `lm_head`: six $8192\times512$ matrices. `compute_accounting_audit.py` uses a factor of four and reports 51.6403 GFLOPs per step for this operation; the corresponding six-table count is about 77.4604 GFLOPs per step. The five BF16 tables still explain the stated additional 160 MiB state allocation because `lm_head` is already FP32, but the compute accounting and its description should explicitly separate these five BF16 tables from the six matrices transformed by the kernel. This does not change the measured losses or the qualitative conclusion that the overhead is small.

Second, Table 3a labels the whole cumulative ladder as “single-variable.” Its final transition changes both $\tau_{\rm BiEq}$ and $\tau_{\rm Neu}$ from 80 to 25, so that transition cannot support a single-variable causal attribution. Table 4b contains the useful $25/80$ isolation, but wording such as “proves” and “accounts for 100%” should be softened to describe the tested contrast. The selection of $\tau=25$ after comparing several horizons and the many endpoint contrasts also make the intervals exploratory; this is partly acknowledged, but the conclusion should avoid implying a globally tuned or multiplicity-adjusted effect.

The scope is necessarily limited. Both model sizes are members of the same small autoresearch family, use one corpus, tokenizer, data order, and fixed learning-rate recipe, and the strongest package contains several established ingredients. Thus the evidence supports a useful, reproducible improvement within this recipe and a promising depth-6 transfer, not a ranking of optimizers under independent tuning or a claim of broad LLM scaling. The manuscript’s limitations section states most of this correctly; the abstract and title should retain the same bounded framing. The indexed rStation papers on Muon precision and momentum schedules provide relevant methodological context, and the external MuonEq/MALT/NormPre-G papers establish the closest prior art; I found no substantial overlap that would negate the sequential contribution.

With the FLOP correction, the table/causality wording repair, and continued qualification of cross-backbone generality, this is a sound partial advance with inspectable evidence and sufficient relevance to the selected pretraining problem.
