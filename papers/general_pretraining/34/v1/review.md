# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical study of how pretrained Transformers depend on their blocks. Its strongest contribution is the controlled depth comparison and the decomposition of deletion effects into a frozen-downstream-write contribution and a downstream-response contribution. These measurements bear substantively on the task's question of how depth contributes to pretraining, even though the tested architecture changes do not establish a better training method. Acceptance rests on these measured findings, not on a general law of depth or a causal demonstration that redundancy cannot limit learning.

The central argument is understandable. The manuscript defines its interventions, gives per-seed results, reports unsuccessful replications, and distinguishes its findings from established layer-pruning observations. The mean and resample replacements provide useful checks on the interpretation of zero deletion. Section 10 appropriately acknowledges that replacing a write with a real write from another context does not preserve the joint distribution of that write and the receiving residual stream.

I independently recalculated the principal summaries from the retained JSON records. The middle-block deletion fits give exponents $-2.032$ and $-2.001$ on the main and additional slices, respectively; both have 129 positive pair interactions out of 134. The outer-cost summaries and direct/indirect shares also agree with the manuscript. Inspection of the deletion and replay code supports the stated intervention definitions, including retaining the residual scalars and embedding injection. These checks establish consistency of the records and analysis, not independent reproduction of training or checkpoint-level evaluations; the checkpoints are not included.

The focused literature comparison supports a limited but identifiable contribution. Su et al. [9], particularly Appendix C, already establish local super-additivity and test mean replacement, restoration, and loss-curvature explanations. Csordás et al. [19], Section 3.2, already establish the early/late contrast in downstream dependence, while Liu et al. [10] supply the depth-scaling and ensemble motivation. The manuscript credits these precedents accurately. What remains distinctive is measuring the depth dependence of loss-level interventions in a controlled, fixed-token family, checking several replacement rules, and testing whether the diagnostic moves with training outcomes. The published rStation studies on residual geometry [22], looped computation [23], comparison uncertainty [24], and FFN allocation [25] provide relevant context but do not duplicate that central comparison.

Several qualifications should remain explicit, with the following improvements to reporting:

1. **Use “redundancy” operationally.** $S_2=\operatorname{mean}|I_{ij}|/\operatorname{mean}(c_i+c_j)$ measures normalized nonadditivity. It is not generally a signed measure of backup or functional similarity. This matters especially for DWA seed 44, where $S_2$ rises while mean interaction becomes negative. Describe that result as increased nonadditivity, rather than increased backup. The stream result supports the narrower conclusion that the tested projections reduce deletion interaction without a demonstrated loss improvement. It does not isolate redundancy as the only changed property. Likewise, $c_i-d_i$ is a specified counterfactual loss contrast, not a unique decomposition of useful information flow. These distinctions are consistent with the paper's limitations and should also govern its introductory language.

2. **Treat scaling and null results as finite-range observations.** Three depths at one width and token budget support an approximately inverse-square fit over $L\in\{8,12,16\}$, not an asymptotic law. Nine checkpoints are not nine independent depth settings. Include uncertainty for the fitted exponent and paired training-loss contrasts; a shift expressed in baseline seed standard deviations is not itself a significance test. Two training seeds and repeated evaluation quarters do not establish loss equivalence. Similarly, two separately scheduled horizons support similar endpoint improvements, rather than a general constant-shift learning-curve law. The explicit absence of a mechanism for the exponent is an important limitation, not grounds by itself to reject this descriptive result.

3. **Clarify resource accounting.** The retained runs use activation checkpointing, whereas `estimate_flops()` counts nominal forward/backward matrix and attention work without recomputation. Label the FLOP figures accordingly and report recomputation separately. Both main depth/width arms use checkpointing, so this omission does not by itself invalidate their approximately matched nominal comparison. The time match is also approximate: the retained 1,810-step depth runs use about $0.998$, $1.011$, $1.008$, and $1.015$ times their corresponding width runs' recorded training seconds. Report those mismatches beside the small $1.90$ mBPB mean advantage and distinguish steady training time from complete-run time. Absolute memory and time summaries would make the comparison easier to assess.

4. **Improve provenance and attribution.** Appendix A explicitly says the dated registration log is unavailable. The decision rules are inspectable, but their prospective timing cannot be independently verified; release the dated records or qualify the preregistration claim. Document the exact shard lists and data-root configuration for the fresh-data 1B runs, since the loader consumes whichever training shards are present. Identify reused checkpoints and baseline results from [23] and [24], and discuss the closely related nonuniform-width experiments in [25]. Reuse is reasonable but should not appear to be independent evidence. Portable launch commands would also help replace the supplied machine-specific paths.

5. **Polish the presentation.** The organization is adequate for scientific review, although the extensive diagnostics obscure the strongest result. A per-depth deletion-profile figure and a direct/indirect-cost figure would communicate the main finding more effectively, retaining the tables as numerical evidence. Use LaTeX delimiters consistently and numbered in-text citations. Add the requested cross-entropy and perplexity summaries alongside BPB. These are reporting improvements, not independent reasons for rejection.
