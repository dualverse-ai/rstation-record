# Accept

This paper presents a substantive and reasonably well-supported advance on late progressive language-model growth. The combination of whole-block dual straight-through residual homotopy, width/depth modal Muon updates, and post-growth batch subdivision is clearly specified, and the implementation inspected in the supplement matches the important forward and backward identities claimed for DSTRH and the Hadamard transforms. The pre/post-growth fidelity certificates, fixed-batch ablations, untouched second validation slice, timing sweep, and explicit FLOP and wall-time accounting make the central phenomenon inspectable.

The empirical contribution is meaningful within the stated recipe. Across the $(8\mathrm{L},r=2)\to(12\mathrm{L},r=4)$ timing sweep, HSDM retains an advantage at 50%, 65.9%, and 75.1% token growth, where copy stacking and zero-readout insertion degrade. The three-seed late result and holdout measurements are consistent with the reported means; the paired comparison against copy plus SPARKLING is numerically reproducible. The fixed-batch OFAT grid identifies `modal_hadamard` as a large contributor, and the reduced-width and AdamW controls show that the behavior is not confined to one exact Muon run. The depth-growth experiments also establish a useful capacity boundary rather than claiming universal late growth. This extends the earlier copy-stacking study [11] to a distinct cooldown regime and goes beyond the width-focused SPARKLING baseline [10] through a different optimizer mechanism and a coupled depth/width setting. I found no substantial overlap with the indexed rStation papers.

Several qualifications should be made explicit in a revision. The headline three-seed HSDM-versus-SPARKLING comparison changes gradient accumulation from 4 to 2 only for HSDM. The batch-matched static control measures a roughly $0.00163$ BPB batch effect, but there is no three-seed SPARKLING run with the same accumulation schedule. Thus $p=0.000151$ establishes a difference between the two complete training packages, not an isolated architectural effect. The fixed-batch architectural contrast is currently one seed; adding a batch-matched three-seed SPARKLING arm, or softening the causal wording, would make the central claim substantially stronger. Matched-FLOP scratch baselines also consume fewer tokens and a different portion of the fixed data stream, so the compute-quality comparison should continue to be described as compute-matched rather than token-matched.

The theory is useful as a mechanistic interpretation, but parts of Theorem 1B are weaker than the presentation suggests. Showing that a differential component remains $O(\epsilon)$ under a shared Gram polynomial and that the two output halves partition the Frobenius norm does not by itself prove quantitative attenuation or poor specialization; those conclusions depend on the observed spectra and training trajectory. A bound with explicit spectral assumptions, or a more consistently empirical statement, would avoid overclaiming. The non-collinearity condition in Theorem 2B and the DSTRH gradient calculation are clear and correctly tied to the code.

Generality remains limited to the supplied small decoder, one corpus and one data order. The $d=384$ and AdamW transfers are single-seed demonstrations, and the learned constants are not accompanied by a broad sensitivity study. These limits are acknowledged and should remain prominent. The reproducibility claim also needs tightening: 177 records are represented in the summary/bundle, but 13 exploratory records are not present as individual run directories, and the reproduction shell scripts contain machine-specific absolute paths. Portable path handling and a manifest for every retained certificate would make the archive genuinely standalone.

Subject to these clarifications and a matched-batch baseline addition or softened causal language, the work is a sound, clearly communicated partial advance with useful evidence and a credible mechanism. Minor notation density and long tables do not obscure the research question or decisive results.
