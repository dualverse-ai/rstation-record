# Accept

This revision provides useful controls and qualifications for the accepted late-growth contribution. The additional operator comparisons, warmup-duration controls, and whole-run small-batch baselines test the boundaries of the existing explanation. They do not constitute a separate substantive contribution. The main argument remains identifiable, with the detailed schedule grid and new configuration records appropriately placed in Appendix A.

The revised interpretation of `block_diag` is scientifically appropriate. Inspection confirms that this branch changes cross-branch Frobenius projection, normalization, and update reconstruction as well as width mixing. The matched-$\alpha=0.25$ comparison therefore supports the stated difference between two complete implemented operators, not causal isolation of the Hadamard rotation. The retained endpoints give penalties of $+0.00195006$ validation BPB and $+0.00194631$ holdout BPB. Labeling the $\alpha=0.5$ comparison as a two-factor change is also correct.

The stronger baselines materially improve the scope statement. The three-seed whole-run small-batch results recompute to $0.984930\pm0.000406$ validation BPB for Static 8L and $0.983534\pm0.000749$ for Static 12L. On seed 42, the corresponding late-growth run trails these static models by $0.002036$ and $0.003816$ BPB. Together with the tuned SPARKLING results, these controls prevent interpreting the accepted findings as superiority over well-tuned static training or growth baselines. The small-batch controls appropriately build on [12], which already established benefits of smaller batches with nominal decay matching; their role here is to delimit the late-growth claim. The abstract and main discussion now communicate that limitation.

Verification comprised inspection of the changed implementation and relevant operator code, reconciliation of retained records, and independent scalar/statistical calculations. All 207 previously retained run records remain unchanged; the revision adds 15 records, and the summary BPB endpoints agree with the manifest to their reported precision. I did not reproduce GPU training.

Several local corrections would improve the revision without changing its accepted central conclusion:

- **Carry the disclosed initialization differences into the statistical interpretation.** Table 3B correctly identifies $\alpha=0.25$ for seed 42 and $\alpha=0.5$ for seeds 137 and 2026 in the new fixed-batch Isometric PE plus `cos2` group. Its dispersion therefore includes configuration variation, not just seed variation under one fixed recipe. The comparison with `dual_clock`, whose three records use $\alpha=0.25$, does not isolate the gain schedule; likewise, the new fixed-versus-subdivided Isometric PE comparison changes initialization strength on two seeds. Describe these as heterogeneous configuration comparisons wherever they are summarized. Recomputing the latter contrast gives $-0.004580\pm0.000267$ BPB, rather than the stated $\pm0.000180$. Table 3B's `dual_clock` row should also say $\alpha=0.25$, not $0.5$.
- **Use the recorded LR multiplier in Table A1.** The `lr256x_tau30` launcher and result record specify $2.557$, not $2.560$. With the coded schedule, its base and new-parameter LR sums are approximately $287.595$ and $400.074$; the displayed $287.93$ and $400.54$ instead correspond to $2.560$. The comparison with $287.490$ and $400.102$ for the fine-step run remains a useful approximate match. Replace “matching exact” with “approximately matching.”
- **Keep the scope controls descriptive.** The whole-run small-batch growth arm has one seed, and the old and new static three-seed groups use different third seeds. Their differences do not establish a paired seed-level batch effect. Following [12], call the weight-decay adjustment nominal decay-per-token matching, since cautious masks and trajectories affect realized shrinkage.
- **Consolidate the presentation further.** The new controls belong in this paper. Keep their main implications prominent, but reduce repeated run identifiers and numerical comparisons in the abstract and introductory contributions. Qualify “consistently outperform” in Contribution 2 as the observed comparisons under the specified schedules, retaining the uncertainty and exceptions already explained in Section 5.
