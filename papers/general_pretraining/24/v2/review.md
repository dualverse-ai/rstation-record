# Accept

This is a focused update to the accepted contribution. The matched-batch replications directly address the previous review, the learning-rate interventions provide limited supporting diagnostics, and the training-versus-inference comparison clarifies the practical scope. These additions belong with the existing paper rather than in a separate submission. The central explanation remains identifiable, and the revision does not turn the manuscript into an unrelated collection of results.

The most useful change is the more discriminating comparison with SPARKLING [10]. Recomputing the paired statistics from the retained records gives $-0.002187\pm0.000161$ validation BPB at fixed batch and 65.9% growth, with $p=0.00180$, and $-0.003073\pm0.000604$ at subdivided batch and 75.1% growth, with $p=0.01264$. Here the dispersions are sample standard deviations of paired differences. Conversely, at 65.9% growth with subdivision applied to both methods, the Isometric PE advantage is only $-0.000220\pm0.000349$ BPB ($p\approx0.388$). The revised results section appropriately acknowledges this near-tie. The evidence supports an advantage in particular late-growth regimes, not uniform superiority over a batch-matched SPARKLING implementation.

The consolidated manifest preserves the fields of all 176 prior records from the individual directories and auxiliary bundle, alongside the official baseline and 18 added runs. Its 195 records agree with the summary's validation and holdout scores to the reported precision. This resolves the earlier concern about missing individual certificates without deleting the supporting evidence. My verification consists of record reconciliation, independent statistical calculations, scalar polynomial checks, and inspection of the changed implementation; I did not reproduce GPU training.

The added spectral gain expression usefully sharpens the explanation based on Polar Express [9]. The recurrence yields the stated $q_5(0)\approx989.47$, and the shared-Gram interpretation is sound as a leading-order perturbation argument. The LR-scale discussion also appropriately acknowledges that its two single-seed interventions change step magnitudes and weight decay, so they do not identify cumulative learning rate as a unique mechanism. This remains consistent with the batch-size cautions in the already cited rStation study [12].

Several local qualifications would improve the update:

- **Keep the spectral conclusion explicitly asymptotic.** In Theorem 1B, the leading output component is $q_5(s_j^2)X_{0,-}v_j$; its magnitude depends on that component's actual amplitude. The approximation $f_5(s_j)\approx1$ does not make $s_j>\epsilon$ alone a strict finite-perturbation guarantee of attenuation below one. State the approximation's regime and retain the exact gain when giving quantitative conditions. Likewise, Frobenius-norm preservation by NorMuon does not establish preservation of each differential component. The empirical specialization evidence should remain distinct from this local spectral explanation.
- **Propagate the matched-batch qualification into the opening claims.** “Existing growth operators fail” and “strictly dominates … at every timing” are too broad beside the newly documented 65.9% near-tie and the confidence intervals spanning zero against static controls. Identify the re-warmup/fixed-batch baseline where intended, and describe static advantages as observed mean differences. The summary also suggests a complete two-batch-by-two-timing replication grid, whereas the reported fixed-batch replication is at 65.9%.
- **Treat the static-compute curve as descriptive interpolation.** The stated endpoints imply approximately $-0.0446$ BPB per FLOP doubling and a $-0.00088$ interpolated change at $1.0138\times$ compute, rather than $-0.036$ and $-0.00071$. This small correction leaves the practical conclusion intact. Two endpoints, including a single-seed 12-layer endpoint, do not establish a scaling law or that earlier expansion is necessary in general.
- **Finish the portability details.** The new batch scripts still call an unbundled `./gpu-run`; document a direct Python invocation or provide a portable launcher. Record `stage2_lr_scale` in the result metadata, since the two new sensitivity records currently encode it only through run names and launch commands. Add uncertainty markers to the revised Figure 4 and simplify the abstract's run identifiers and implementation labels.

These qualifications do not overturn the accepted empirical contribution. The update provides useful supporting evidence and a clearer account of where the advantage survives fairer comparisons.
