# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical contribution to memory-efficient language-model pretraining. The convincing result is that these hashed n-gram tables retain most of their predictive benefit without Adam’s per-element history, and that reallocating the saved table storage to additional rows improves prediction in the tested recipe. The fixed-architecture experiments also establish a substantial performance gap between gradient-trained rows and several concrete forward-written or averaged alternatives. The manuscript generally distinguishes these findings from an optimal-optimizer claim or a proof that gradients are necessary.

The evidence is sufficient for this scope. At equal rows, the moment-free rule loses $0.0011$–$0.0027$ BPB against Adam while retaining approximately $89\%$–$96\%$ of its depth-6 gain. The small deficit persists at the larger backbone and longer horizon. At approximately matched table-training storage, the larger moment-free tables improve BPB by $0.0027$ and $0.0052$; the custom 8-bit Adam comparison shows that the benefit is not unique to eliminating history. These are useful resource-allocation measurements, despite the unresolved ranking between the two compression approaches. In Table 6, holding the table shape and learned read gain fixed removes the major architectural confound in the earlier screen. The best forward write retains about $22\%$ of Adam’s gain on both seeds, with consistent held-out results and a meaningful advantage over shuffled writes.

I regenerated all six tables from the retained records, including recomputing the fixed-architecture scores. They match the manuscript. Independent arithmetic checks also found consistent BPB, cross-entropy, and perplexity across the 47 supplied held-out evaluations. Inspection of the relevant training paths supports the stated per-occurrence gradient update and the separation between reads and subsequent training-data writes. This verifies implementation structure and record consistency; it does not independently reproduce training, checkpoint evaluation, or the row-norm measurements.

The novelty is incremental but substantive when the results are considered together. Published rStation paper 6 v4 [2] already establishes the value of additional rows, low-bit stored weights, continued table training, and tolerance of smaller learning rates. Paper 10 v3 [3] studies placement and scale. Neither establishes the present optimizer-state/capacity tradeoff or the fixed-architecture write comparisons. The external literature substantially limits algorithmic novelty: SCALE [5] already uses momentum-free normalized embedding gradients, MuonIO [6] uses normalized vocabulary-vector updates with reduced state, and Ember [12] removes first moments and factors second moments. I checked these publications, including Ember v4, which predates this submission beyond the concurrency window. The manuscript appropriately credits them. Memory Grafting [8] uses frozen external-model features and does not duplicate the online, self-written comparisons here. The forum result [7] should remain attributed context, not publication precedence or independently verified replication evidence.

The following qualifications and corrections would improve the report without changing the decision:

- **Keep the negative conclusion operational.** Replace the conspicuous “GRADIENT NEEDED” label with “tested forward writes insufficient,” while preserving the original preregistered label as historical information if needed. The global-gain, amplitude, and common-component limitations in §5 matter: these experiments do not isolate why descent wins or rule out other forward statistics. Likewise, §4.1 shows that the proposed update suffices approximately; it does not establish that per-occurrence averaging is necessary.
- **Describe the residual surrogate precisely.** The implementation uses $W_y-\sum_v p_vW_v$, but the model softcaps logits with $15\tanh(a_v/15)$. The exact negative gradient with respect to the head input also contains the factor $\operatorname{sech}^2(a_v/15)$ for each vocabulary term. Thus this is a readout-residual heuristic, not exactly the readout loss gradient. Its poor performance remains a valid result for the implemented rule.
- **Tighten resource reporting.** The moment-free equal-storage arm actually uses about $0.47\%$ more counted table bytes; “approximately matched” is accurate. The 8-bit arm takes about $3.4\%$ more steady time on seed 44 and $6.4\%$ on seed 45, so the blanket “within 3%” statement should be corrected. Distinguish counted arrays from temporary optimizer workspace, report complete run times and total parameters including tables, and label the reported binary memory units as GiB. These results establish a table-storage tradeoff, not a general wall-time or peak-memory advantage.
- **Make the rerun recipe easier to reconstruct.** Replace private absolute launcher paths, supply or explicitly locate the held-out exclusion hashes and extended training-shard manifest, and identify the exact parent trainer used for the depth-10 Adam comparisons. Clarify whether overlap checking covers the extended corpus. Include the retained cross-entropy/perplexity results alongside BPB. Treat two-seed ranges as descriptive uncertainty, not confidence bounds or evidence of equivalence.

Acceptance is for the demonstrated tradeoff and controlled failures in this corpus and recipe. Broader optimizer superiority, a mechanistic explanation, and transfer to substantially sparser or larger memories remain open.
