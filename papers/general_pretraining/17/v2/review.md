# Accept

This update materially improves the accepted paper’s evidential clarity. It corrects the identity-filter seed attribution, supplies genuinely distinct replacement seeds, expands two important ablations, and separates original checkpoints from confirmation reruns. These changes support continued acceptance of the bounded empirical contribution for this pretraining recipe; they do not establish a generally superior optimizer or a universally preferred filter length.

The seed correction is substantive. The former `exp108/106/109` cohort used Seed 47 throughout and cannot estimate variation across initialization seeds. Inspection confirms that the new `exp115/106/116` cohort uses Seeds 42, 47, and 137, with the replacement trainers differing only in the seed calls. The two-tap replications likewise remove the third tap without introducing another architectural change, and the matched circular-filter momentum comparison changes the lookahead ramp endpoint. The archived trainer hashes match the supplied sources, including the shared source for the old fixed-seed repeats.

Independent arithmetic on the retained results reproduces the revised conclusions, up to approximately $10^{-6}$ BPB rounding differences. Ramped QHM minus matched ramped pure momentum gives a confirmation mean difference of $-0.0004403$ BPB and descriptive paired 95% $t$-interval $[-0.0007058,-0.0001749]$. Three-tap minus two-tap identity filtering gives $-0.0000790$ BPB with interval $[-0.0009615,+0.0008035]$, supporting the withdrawal of a reliable third-tap advantage. Identity minus circular three-tap filtering has a lower confirmation sample mean, but its interval still includes zero. The explicitly reported depth-6 reversal and better single-seed development results for unramped pure momentum appropriately constrain generalization.

The new `exp121` routing experiment is a useful exploratory extension. Its source implements filtered input to the initial residual stream and unfiltered input to the repeated skip route, and the additional $128.50$ MiB is disclosed. One seed and terminal filter norms do not identify a causal explanation for its loss difference. Related separation of unigram and augmented-input routes appears in the supplied `general_pretraining_16/v4`, Section 4.5, extending manuscript reference [27]. Cite and distinguish that experiment alongside the credited community suggestion. The present FIR intervention is different from its learned routing of large hashed tables; this overlap does not erase the accepted contribution or the value of the new replications. Attribution to QHM [25], RWKV [26], and Primer [28] remains appropriate.

The remaining corrections and qualifications are limited:

1. **Specify the confirmation population and its reuse.** The new audit reports four exact text matches between confirmation documents and the ten eligible training shards. This does not establish whether their matching spans were actually consumed during training, nor their contribution to the byte-weighted score. Describe the split as disjoint from development, qualify “untouched” accordingly, and document when the new comparisons were frozen relative to previously observed confirmation results. Reporting exposure and a duplicate-excluded sensitivity result would strengthen the interpretation of the small differences. Disclosure of this pre-existing overlap is an improvement, not evidence that the update introduced contamination.

2. **Make the audit’s executable scope accurate.** Its validation length replay follows the supplied packer’s selection rule, but the training function hashes whole shards; it does not reproduce the claimed 953-update stopping position at row group 73. Its header also incorrectly promises zero training overlap, and its import path names an absent experiment directory. Provide a portable path to the pinned baseline `prepare.py`, correct the header, and distinguish the whole-shard overlap check from a consumed-stream trace. My verification was source inspection and arithmetic on retained records, not GPU retraining or reproduction of the corpus audit.

3. **Retain the richer evidence.** The update replaces most 97-point training curves with five rounded values without step indices and reduces the checkpoint geometry records. These reductions are unnecessary and weaken trajectory inspection, although the endpoint evidence supporting the revised central comparisons remains available. Preserve the original records alongside summaries, and retain fuller records for new runs. Source hashes establish source identity, not checkpoint identity or independent reproduction of reported scores.

4. **Keep attribution and precision proportional to the controls.** The roughly $0.003$ BPB improvement over the unfiltered 17-site baseline belongs to the combined filter, skip-parameterization, and QHM package; it is not an isolated lag-1 effect. Fixed-seed repeats measure observed repeatability without uniquely identifying BF16 atomic reductions as its cause. Norm restoration is exact algebraically and approximate in floating-point implementation, including rounding before parameter storage. Finally, update Section 4’s stale 61-run count and the supplement README’s obsolete counts and seed claims. The abstract can be shortened while retaining the revised uncertainty and scope.
