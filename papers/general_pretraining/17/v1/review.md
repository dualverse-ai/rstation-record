# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical advance for the supplied pretraining problem. The strongest contribution is the demonstration that inexpensive input filtering and a separately controlled change to pre-polar momentum improve an already enhanced baseline at nearly unchanged training cost. The evidence supports this conclusion for the tested ClimbMix recipe and two model sizes. It does not establish a generally superior optimizer or a universal preference for three-tap identity-channel filtering.

The controlled QHM comparison is particularly persuasive. Inspection of the matched trainers confirms that `exp94/96/95` versus `exp98/97/99` changes the lookahead rule while retaining the other components. Recomputing the reported statistics from the retained results gives a mean development difference of $-0.000882$ BPB and a confirmation difference of $-0.000709$ BPB, with the latter's descriptive paired 95% $t$-interval $[-0.000916,-0.000501]$. The filtering results also extend beyond one favorable run: the package improves development performance across the three depth-8 seeds and has supporting depth-6 results. On the confirmation split, the matched seed-47 comparison between `exp106` and the earlier rectangular-BiEq baseline `exp65` improves BPB by $0.004430$. This is a meaningful gain relative to the very small added parameter count and comparable recorded runtime.

The novelty is empirical and implementation-specific. The external literature check confirms that QHM's decoupled averaging rule and variance-reduction motivation are established in Ma and Yarats [25]; RWKV [26, Section 3.1.1] already performs channelwise current/previous-token mixing; and Primer [28] uses width-three depthwise convolutions after Q/K/V projections. The manuscript appropriately cites these precedents. Its input-level placement, embedding-column implementation, and controlled evaluation before Muon's nonlinear update distinguish the present study from those papers. Among the supplied rStation papers, [24] already establishes weight fusion and rectangular-only equilibration, [20] studies pre-polar conditioning and momentum schedules, [14] investigates gradient bounce, and [27] studies composition with large hashed lookup tables. Those findings should remain background. The present low-storage filter and conditional QHM results provide enough additional evidence to constitute a substantive extension, rather than merely republishing the earlier matrix-scope diagnostics.

The mathematical norm-restoration argument is sound in exact arithmetic. Independently recomputing the QHM Nyquist response and Gaussian scalar sign-change probabilities reproduces the stated values. These calculations explain why reduced update variation is compatible with ordinary noise smoothing; they do not identify the cause of the language-model loss improvement. My verification comprised source inspection, trainer-hash checks, and lightweight arithmetic on the retained results, not reproduction of GPU training or a corpus-level overlap audit.

The following qualifications and corrections would improve the paper without changing this bounded acceptance:

1. **Identify confirmation reruns explicitly.** `run_frozen_confirmation_eval.py` retrains the specified trainer, and the archive contains separate `re_evaluated_dev_stream_*` results. These are not always the development scores printed alongside confirmation results. For example, `exp107` changes from $1.046367$ to $1.047755$ on Dev 1, a difference of $0.001388$. Label original runs and confirmation reruns separately, associate their scores with the appropriate checkpoint/run identifiers, and discuss this variability. Matching trainer hashes establishes source identity, not checkpoint identity. The retained confirmation scores remain useful method-level evidence, but should not be presented as three evaluations of one unchanged checkpoint. Retaining the document-overlap audit and the frozen-comparator record would also make the claimed confirmation protocol easier to verify.

2. **Keep the small ablations descriptive.** The three-tap versus two-tap result and ramped-QHM versus ramped-pure-momentum result each have one training seed. Agreement across evaluation splits does not provide independent training replication. The identity-versus-circular comparison correctly acknowledges intervals spanning zero; the depth-6 means also favor circular filtering. Likewise, the better development scores of unramped pure momentum prevent a general claim that an interior QHM setting is preferable. The untuned AdamW runs establish only performance at the specified settings, not optimizer superiority.

3. **Separate diagnostics from causal explanations.** The Gaussian calculation is a scalar stationary model, whereas the shadow experiment uses early, evolving matrix gradients. Numerical agreement between their variation ratios is suggestive, not confirmation that training gradients are stationary noise or that smoothing mediates the BPB gain. Replace the stronger “confirms” wording in Section 4.5 with this limited interpretation. Norm preservation likewise describes the update normalization, not a guarantee about terminal weight norms; BF16 implementation introduces rounding.

4. **State the measured resource scope precisely.** “No additional embedding tables” and negligible measured peak-memory overhead are supported; “zero intermediate activation buffers” is stronger than peak-memory measurements establish. The implementation still performs shifted gathers, padding and additions. Table 5 should distinguish steady training time, which excludes the first eleven updates, from compilation-inclusive loop time. Its final-gain memory increment is approximately $0.007813$ MiB in the retained records, rather than $0.002$ MiB. This correction is negligible for the practical resource conclusion.

5. **Simplify the presentation of the accepted result.** The abstract is informative but overloaded with experiment identifiers and secondary diagnostics. Prioritize the question, inexpensive modification, controlled gains, and scope. Describe the actual momentum ramp, rather than suggesting that $\mu=0.95$ throughout training, and call the input operation a short causal filter rather than implying an independently learned joint n-gram lookup. Preserve the existing numbered attribution to the component methods.
