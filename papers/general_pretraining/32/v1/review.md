# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical contribution to the pretraining task. Its strongest evidence concerns how the location of copied layers affects growth under controlled attention layouts, and how retaining the learned input filter improves coarse-to-fine continuation. The manuscript makes the important qualifications explicit: the strongest growth result concerns a combined training procedure; the isolated topology contrast misses the stated family-wise threshold; and checkpointed growth loses to a longer static model at matched analytical executed FLOPs. These qualifications make the conclusions substantially more credible.

The independent checks support the principal numerical results. Recomputing paired statistics from the retained records reproduces the eight-seed topology difference of $-2.384\pm0.773$ mBPB, the combined-procedure difference of $-3.317\pm0.661$ mBPB, and their Holm-adjusted $p$-values of $0.0530$ and $0.00614$. The static executed-FLOP control reproduces $+2.857\pm0.334$ mBPB for grown minus static loss. The FFN placement and transfer contrasts also agree with the reported statistics. For coarse-to-fine continuation, the filter contrast reproduces $-17.187\pm1.370$ mBPB, and the ratio of mean contrasts and its jackknife SE reproduce $89.6\%\pm3.6\%$. These are checks of retained measurements, supported by inspection of the relevant implementation; I did not reproduce GPU training, re-evaluate checkpoints, or independently regenerate the corpus fingerprints. Matching recorded checkpoint hashes and pre-transition losses supports the described forks but does not itself verify the checkpoint contents.

Novelty is incremental but sufficient for this scoped study. OpenELM [10, §2.1] already specifies increasing layer-wise FFN multipliers, and Du et al. [6, Appendices H.2–H.3] already compare retained connections, interleaving, and partial stacking, including middle versus back layers. Their Appendix J also preserves repeating hybrid blocks. The manuscript appropriately attributes those ideas. The published rStation growth studies [1]–[4] likewise establish growth benefits and schedule sensitivity. Consequently, neither upper-layer widening nor connection-preserving copying should be counted as a new principle. The additional value here is the controlled comparison with equal splice count, unchanged destination global-attention depths, identical starting states, and matched compute, together with the narrower filter-retention experiment. The per-block coarse residual wrapper is also meaningfully distinguished from the persistent hierarchy in Hourglass [7]. The evidence supports local design guidance and a useful failure diagnosis, with generalization beyond this small ClimbMix setting remaining open.

The scientific argument is identifiable and sufficiently clear. Table 0 usefully separates static allocation tests, actual checkpoint forks, combined training procedures, and independently trained continuations. The layer maps and transition equations make the decisive interventions understandable. The main text is nevertheless unnecessarily repetitive: the introduction, results, and discussion repeatedly enumerate the same contrast identifiers and statistics. Keep the main text focused on the three questions and their implications, and move more per-seed vectors and implementation labels to the appendices. The metadata abstract would benefit from the same compression. This is a presentation improvement rather than a reason to reject the argument.

Please address the following reporting and reproducibility corrections:

- **Correct the coarse-to-fine compute total.** All sixteen retained four-arm continuation records report `cumulative_flops` of approximately $3.01449\times10^{16}$, whereas Table 0, §5.2, and Table 4 describe $2.87\times10^{16}$. This is about a $5\%$ discrepancy. Reconcile the estimator and state the actual budget consistently. The four arms share the recorded total, so their within-family contrasts remain valid; comparisons with the quarter-budget static and growth runs should not be described as exactly matched.
- **Correct the transition-shock range in §5.2.** The stated $+0.0853$–$+0.0956$ nats is not the range of individual retained `delta_loss_keep_filter` measurements. For example, the seed-42 KeepFilter run records $+0.118636$ nats. Its four-seed mean is indeed $+0.095632$ nats, consistent with Table 4. Distinguish means across runs from individual same-state counterfactual measurements.
- **Repair the documented training setup.** Both `train_multigrid.py` and `train_loop_grow.py` resolve `shard9_provenance.json` relative to their own directories, but the supplied manifest is in `experiments_p5/`. Setting `PYTHONPATH=experiments_p5` does not fix that lookup. Document the required placement or use an explicit shared path, and explain how to obtain the baseline `prepare.py` and `attention_backend.py` dependencies. The README's claim that these trainers import shared evaluation utilities from Phase 5 does not match their local evaluation definitions.
- **Maintain the inferential scope throughout.** The six-test adjustment covers that declared family, not the whole development campaign. Treat $U6$ and $U8$ as supporting estimates, and avoid reading nonsignificant comparisons as equivalence. The filter ratio remains conditional on retained outer gain and independently trained Stage-1 trajectories. Likewise, novel-bigram and long-repeat subsets describe where gains occur; they do not independently establish compositional reasoning or an induction mechanism. The observed endpoint SD reduction should remain descriptive.

The known FFN allocation principle, the empirical topology comparison, and the bounded continuation diagnosis should retain distinct roles in the contribution statement. Acceptance rests on their controlled evidence and appropriately limited conclusions, rather than the number of runs or a claim of general pretraining superiority.
