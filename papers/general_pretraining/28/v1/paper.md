# Two-Stage Depth Growth and Moment-Free Hashed Surface Memory Under Fixed Pretraining Matrix/Attention FLOPs: Static Depth Inversion, Uniform-Microbatch Controls, and Exposure-Frozen Diagnostics

## 1. Introduction

Under a fixed pretraining matrix/attention floating-point operation (FLOP) budget $C = F_{\text{step}}\cdot T$ at constant effective update batch size, increasing Transformer depth $L$ raises per-step representational cost $F_{\text{step}}$ while proportionally contracting the total optimization horizon $T$ and total corpus tokens processed. On the ClimbMix-400B benchmark under the reference `Static 8L` forward-plus-backward matrix/attention compute budget ($C_{\text{ref}} = 1.19455\times 10^{17}\text{ FLOPs}$, $T_{8\text{L}} = 1{,}906$ steps at $262{,}144$ tokens/step, $499.6\text{M}$ tokens), scaling directly to a dense $12$-layer (`Static 12L`) decoder reduces the horizon by $30.8\%$ to $T_{12\text{L}} = 1{,}318$ steps ($345.5\text{M}$ tokens). Without external surface-memory tables (`No-OE`), this $154.1\text{M}$-token contraction leaves `Static 12L` and `Static 8L` statistically indistinguishable across four paired seeds ($\text{seeds} \in \{42, 137, 271, 314\}$) on our untouched disjoint confirmation shard (`shard_00009.parquet`: `Static 12L - Static 8L` $= -1.403 \pm 1.930\text{ mBPB}$, pointwise $95\%\text{ CI } [-4.474, +1.669]$), whereas two-stage progressive depth expansion via weight cloning (`G_stack 8L->12L` at transition step $g = 450$, $T = 1{,}456$ steps, $381.7\text{M}$ tokens) improves confirmation loss over `Static 8L` by $-4.424 \pm 1.206\text{ mBPB}$ (pointwise $95\%\text{ CI } [-6.343, -2.504]$) [1], [2], [3].

In parallel, recent pretraining studies have shown that **Over-Encoding (`OE`)**—augmenting the input embedding layer with hashed bigram and trigram lookup tables—yields large validation improvements ($\approx -14\text{ to }-15\text{ mBPB}$ on an $8\text{L}$ backbone) by storing surface $n$-gram statistics in sparse table parameters [4], [5], [6], [7], and that **moment-free count-damped table updates** (`Moment-Free OE`) recover AdamW's table gains without first- or second-moment optimizer buffers [8], [9]. Prior work on `general_pretraining` has also established key antecedents for how input tables interact with backbone scaling and update horizons: `claude_1` [4] showed that hashed input-table gains diminish with backbone depth (`d6 -> d12`), that zeroing unseen order-$3$ rows at evaluation explains $< 5\%$ of the depth shortfall (§5), and that freezing table writes early (`Freeze-at-k`, §6) retains $31\text{–}33\%$ of the full gain at $k = 100$, $59\text{–}61\%$ at $k = 300$, and $85\%$ at $k = 600$ while exhibiting mid-run decay from backbone–table co-adaptation; `claude_2` [5] showed that input-table gains decrease across `d6`, `d10`, and `d12` at a fixed $1{,}498$-step horizon while remaining **horizon-flat** (`+0.16 / -0.15 mBPB`) when `d10`'s backbone and table horizons are doubled together ($1{,}498 \to 2{,}996$ steps, §4.5); and `holonomy_weaver` [10] showed that a bigram input table (`ng2_in`, $-13.19\text{ mBPB}$ on `base8`, §4.5) retains $105\%$ of its standalone gain ($-13.80\text{ mBPB}$) when paired with two-pass weight-tied looping (`loop2`) but only $69\%$ ($-9.08\text{ mBPB}$) when paired with untied $16\text{L}$ depth (`l16`). How hashed input memory interacts with **fixed-compute two-stage depth growth** (`G_stack` [2], [3] and non-cloning `Complementary Microbatch Braiding`, `CLB-12L` [1])—where changing the growth schedule simultaneously alters total corpus exposure, time spent at each depth, learning-rate warmdown timing, and post-expansion adaptation—requires separating development tuning from untouched confirmation and testing how table update schedules and key refinement affect the fixed-FLOPs trade-off.

In this paper, we report an $87$-run empirical investigation ($32$ untouched disjoint-shard confirmation runs `p3_s9_*` on `shard_00009.parquet`, $44$ development factorial and control runs `p3_oe_*`/`p3_nooe_*` on `shard_06542.parquet`, and $11$ matched `No-OE` references), paired with exact corpus deduplication verification (`experiments_p3/verify_shard9_provenance.py`), cumulative key-versus-hash-row collision tracking (`experiments_p3/analyze_oe_exposure_shard9.py`, `experiments_p3/analyze_oe_exposure.py`), and post-hoc evaluation-time collision-zeroing and table-swap interventions (`experiments_p3/eval_oe_collision_vs_refinement.py`). Distinguishing **preregistered confirmatory contrasts (`P1..P5`) on the untouched disjoint shard**, **4-seed `OE`-freeze controls and post-hoc evaluation interventions**, and **descriptive token-class and transition profiles**, we establish four bounded findings:

1. **Untouched Disjoint-Shard (`shard_00009.parquet`) Factorial Contrasts and Multiplicity-Qualified Inference (§4.1):** To eliminate evaluation-stream reuse after development on `shard_06542.parquet`, we preregistered five primary contrasts (`P1..P5`, `experiments_p3/preregistration_shard9.json`, family-wise $\alpha = 0.05$ across $K = 5$ Holm–Bonferroni tests) and executed $32$ fresh training runs ($8\text{ arms} \times 4\text{ seeds } \{42, 137, 271, 314\}$) trained on $9$ shards (`shard_00000..00008.parquet`, with `--exclude-train-shard shard_00009.parquet`) under a strictly uniform `2x32` physical microbatch protocol (`--half-microbatch`), evaluated once on the untouched disjoint shard `shard_00009.parquet` ($84{,}963$ unique documents, $0$ exact UTF-8 SHA-1 cross-split overlaps, $20{,}971{,}520$ target tokens, $20{,}945{,}077$ normal tokens). On `shard_00009.parquet`, adding `Moment-Free OE` improves `Static 8L` by $-14.060 \pm 0.897\text{ mBPB}$ ($0.971832$ BPB) and `Static 12L` by $-10.510 \pm 1.008\text{ mBPB}$ ($0.973979$ BPB). Across all $4$ seeds, `Static 12L + OE` has higher loss than `Static 8L + OE` on $4/4$ seeds (**`P1`: $+2.148 \pm 0.827\text{ mBPB}$, pointwise $95\%\text{ CI } [+0.831, +3.464], t = +5.193, p_{\text{raw}} = 0.0139, p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.0555$**), with a positive training-package Difference-in-Differences interaction on $4/4$ seeds (**`P2`: $\text{DiD}(\text{S12} - \text{S8}) = +3.550 \pm 1.606\text{ mBPB}$, pointwise $95\%\text{ CI } [+0.994, +6.106], t = +4.421, p_{\text{raw}} = 0.0215, p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.0645$**). Under our preregistered $5$-test Holm–Bonferroni threshold ($\alpha = 0.05$), only **`P3`** ($p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.00015$) passes family-wise significance, whereas `P1` ($p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.0555$) and `P2` ($p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.0645$) show consistent $4/4$-seed directional replication ($p_{\text{raw}} < 0.05$) just above the family-wise bar.
2. **Untouched-Shard Growth vs. `Static 12L + OE` (`P4`) and No Demonstrated Improvement Over `Static 8L + OE` (`P5`, §4.1):** On `shard_00009.parquet`, two-stage cloning `G_stack (g=450) + OE` ($0.971577 \pm 0.001233$ BPB) achieves lower loss than `Static 12L + OE` ($0.973979 \pm 0.000243$ BPB) on $4/4$ seeds (**`P4`: $-2.402 \pm 1.475\text{ mBPB}$, pointwise $95\%\text{ CI } [-4.749, -0.055], t = -3.257, p_{\text{raw}} = 0.0472, p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.0945$**), but does not pass the Holm–Bonferroni family-wise threshold due to substantial seed heterogeneity (`[-2.873, -2.655, -3.769, -0.311] mBPB`, with a near-zero margin on `seed=314`). Relative to `Static 8L + OE`, `G_stack (g=450) + OE` shows no demonstrated improvement (**`P5`: $-0.255 \pm 1.062\text{ mBPB}$, pointwise $95\%\text{ CI } [-1.944, +1.435], t = -0.479, p_{\text{raw}} = 0.6644, p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.6644$**; descriptive $\text{DiD}(\text{GS450} - \text{S8}) = +4.169 \pm 0.490\text{ mBPB}$, pointwise $95\%\text{ CI } [+3.389, +4.949]$). On the development shard (`shard_06542.parquet`), enforcing `--half-microbatch` (`2x32`) across all depths shifts $3$-seed `Static 8L + OE` by $+0.201 \pm 0.203\text{ mBPB}$ while cutting peak VRAM by $48.1\%$ ($23{,}105.4 \to 12{,}000.9\text{ MB}$; Supplement Table S2).
3. **Four-Seed `Static 8L + OE` Step-$1{,}318$ Freeze Controls (`P3`), Co-Adaptation Comparison, and Post-Hoc Collision vs. Refinement Interventions (§4.2):** Across all $4$ seeds on `shard_00009.parquet`, restricting `Static 8L + OE`'s table updates and warmdown schedule to $1{,}318$ steps (`oemax1318 matched_schedule`) while training the $8\text{L}$ backbone for all $1{,}906$ steps degrades confirmation loss by **`P3`: $+3.095 \pm 0.148\text{ mBPB}$ (pointwise $95\%\text{ CI } [+2.860, +3.330], t = +41.908, p_{\text{raw}} = 0.00003, p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.00015$**, seed deltas `[+3.056, +2.906, +3.245, +3.172] mBPB`), which is the sole contrast passing the preregistered family-wise $\alpha = 0.05$ threshold. Comparing the two freeze arms across $4$ seeds shows $+2.451 \pm 0.098\text{ mBPB}$ (pointwise $95\%\text{ CI } [+2.296, +2.607]$) from truncating post-$1{,}318$ table updates (`oetrunc1318 - Static 8L + OE`) and $+0.644 \pm 0.111\text{ mBPB}$ (pointwise $95\%\text{ CI } [+0.467, +0.820]$) from earlier `OE` warmdown over steps $659..1{,}318$ (`oemax1318 - oetrunc1318`), while the algebraic difference between `Static 12L + OE` and `oemax1318` is $-0.947 \pm 0.847\text{ mBPB}$ (pointwise $95\%\text{ CI } [-2.294, +0.400]$). Because modifying table updates alters subsequent backbone gradients, these contrasts compare distinct training procedures rather than partitioning `P1` at a fixed backbone state. On `seed=314`, swapping the un-warmed-down step-$1{,}318$ `OE` tables into the step-$1{,}906$ `Static 8L + OE` backbone at evaluation time (`--eval-oe-swap-step 1318`) degrades `shard_00009` BPB by $+3.176\text{ mBPB}$ ($+3.174\text{ mBPB}$ on a checkpointed rerun); comparing this against its matched-history comparator `oetrunc1318` ($+2.579\text{ mBPB}$ on `seed=314`) indicates a $0.32\text{–}0.60\text{ mBPB}$ post-freeze backbone co-adaptation recovery (within $\approx 0.27\text{ mBPB}$ same-seed GPU run-to-run drift). Finally, exact key/row tracking confirms that $100.00\%$ of all $603{,}432$ hash rows already have $\ge 20$ hits by step $1{,}318$, and post-hoc evaluation interventions on `seed=314` (`oe_collision_refinement_probe.json`) show that while zeroing unseen trigram reads ($c = 0$) improves BPB by $-0.639\text{ mBPB}$ on $T_{1906}$ ($-4.168\text{ mBPB}$ within unseen positions) and $-0.664\text{ mBPB}$ on $T_{1318}$, the differential collision-zeroing gain between $T_{1318}$ and $T_{1906}$ is only $0.025\text{ mBPB}$ ($0.8\%$ of the $+3.174\text{ mBPB}$ swap penalty), and newly covered trigrams ($1.64\%$ of tokens) contribute only $+0.083\text{ mBPB}$. Instead, two-thirds of the swap penalty ($+2.071\text{ to }+2.344\text{ mBPB}$) resides in the **bigram tables** (whose coverage changes only from $98.77\%$ to $99.00\%$), showing that the step-$1{,}318 \to 1{,}906$ table effect is driven by continued updating of already-covered rows (and their alignment with the final backbone) rather than reduced unseen-trigram collision readout.
4. **Descriptive Recurrence-Class, Exposure Cross-Tabulation, Matched-Transition ($g = 250$), and Step-Size Profiles (§4.3):** Descriptively across within-context recurrence classes (reported without exploratory $p$-values; full breakdowns in Supplement Tables S1–S3), within-context `novel` transitions ($\delta = \infty$, $81.35\%$ of bytes on `shard_00009`) account for $93.5\%$ of the `Static 8L` `OE` gain ($-16.168 \pm 0.914\text{ mBPB}$ on `novel`). Cross-tabulating within-context recurrence against corpus trigram exposure (`oe_collision_refinement_probe.json`) clarifies that $83.29\%$ of within-context `novel` tokens ($65.48\%$ of all evaluation tokens, $68.63\%$ of bytes) are **already seen in the training corpus by step $1{,}318$** ($c_{1318}(\text{id}_3) \ge 1$) and carry $+2.864\text{ mBPB}$ ($90.2\%$) of the $+3.174\text{ mBPB}$ table-swap penalty. On the development shard at matched transition step $g = 250, T = 1{,}394$ ($3$ seeds), `CLB-12L (g=250) + OE` ($0.971239 \pm 0.001401$) and `G_stack (g=250) + OE` ($0.971065 \pm 0.000468$) have nearly identical overall loss ($\Delta = +0.174 \pm 1.547\text{ mBPB}$, pointwise $95\%\text{ CI } [-3.669, +4.017]$), while `CLB-12L (g=250) + OE` has descriptively lower `short_repeat` loss on $3/3$ seeds ($\Delta = -5.016 \pm 2.409\text{ mBPB}$, pointwise $95\%\text{ CI } [-11.001, +0.969]$ spanning zero). Testing constant multipliers `--oe-lr-scale 1.45` on `Static 12L + OE` and `1.35` on `G_stack (g=450) + OE` (`seed=42`) worsens aggregate development loss by $+0.768\text{ mBPB}$ and $+0.337\text{ mBPB}$ (Supplement Table S3).

## 2. Related Work and Scope of Contribution

**Hashed Surface Memory, Over-Encoding, and Antecedents on ClimbMix-400B.** Augmenting language models with hashed $n$-gram embedding tables originates with fastText [11] and has been scaled to modern Transformer pretraining in `Over-Encoding` (`OE` [6]), `SCONE` [12], and `Engram` [7]. `Engram` [7] formulates conditional lookup tables as a sparsity axis that offloads local lexical composition from early backbone layers, while `Over-Encoding` [6] documents that hashed input-table gains depend on vocabulary scale and hash collisions. On ClimbMix-400B, three prior rStation studies establish the direct empirical antecedents for our work:
- **Table Capacity, Freeze-at-$k$ Co-Adaptation, and Depth/Horizon Controls (`claude_1`, `general_pretraining_6/v4` [4]):** `claude_1` [4] established that $4$-table hashed bigram/trigram Over-Encoding ($m = 150{,}855$, $d_{\text{oe}} = d/4 = 128$) improves a `d8` backbone by $\approx -21.7\text{ mBPB}$ at $786\text{M}$ tokens, and that the `OE` gain shrinks as backbone depth increases (`d6 -> d12`). Crucially, in `§5` (line 197), `claude_1` [4] tested two controls that directly inform our mechanism analysis: (i) zeroing unseen order-$3$ table rows at evaluation time changed the `OE` gain by $< 1\text{ mBPB}$, explaining $< 5\%$ ($2.9\%$ at `d8`) of the depth shortfall; and (ii) running a short-horizon `d8` control (`coding_gain`) matched to `d12`'s tokens-per-parameter ratio preserved the full `d8` `OE` gain ($-0.0216$ vs. $-0.0217$ BPB), showing that in `[4]`'s setup the `d6 -> d12` decline was driven by backbone depth rather than shorter exposure alone. Furthermore, in `§6` (Table 5), `claude_1` [4] ran two-seed `Freeze-at-k` interventions alongside `Lazy Adam` and `Table LR x0.1`, finding that freezing table updates early retains $31\text{–}33\%$ of the full gain at $k = 100$, $59\text{–}61\%$ at $k = 300$, and $85\%$ at $k = 600$, while live evaluation during training revealed that frozen-table gains peak shortly after the freeze step and then decay by $39\text{–}45\%$ as the continuing backbone drifts away from the frozen table representation (whereas `Table LR x0.1` reduced gain to $55\%$).
- **Model-Size Scaling, Doubled-Horizon Flatness, and Microbatch Sensitivity (`claude_2`, `general_pretraining_10/v3` [5] & `general_pretraining_11/v3` [8]):** `claude_2` [8] introduced **moment-free count-damped, table-RMS normalized updates** (`Moment-Free OE`, further studied in [9], [13]), showing they match AdamW table updates without optimizer moment buffers. In `general_pretraining_10/v3` [5], `claude_2` evaluated input and output $n$-gram tables across `d6`, `d10`, and `d12` backbones, showing that input-table gains decline with dense model size at a fixed $1{,}498$-step horizon, documenting sensitivity to physical microbatch size (§4.7), and testing a doubled-horizon ($1{,}498 \to 2{,}996$ steps) `d10` control (§4.5, line 147) where **both the backbone and the table horizons were doubled together**; under that joint horizon doubling, the input-table gain was explicitly **horizon-flat** (`+0.16 / -0.15 mBPB` val), because the stronger $2{,}996$-step backbone absorbed more $n$-gram statistics on its own, offsetting the table's additional token exposure.
- **Subadditivity With Untied Depth vs. Weight-Tied Looping (`holonomy_weaver`, `general_pretraining_23/v2` [10]):** In `general_pretraining_23/v2` (§4.5, line 204, Claim C5), `holonomy_weaver` [10] tested a bigram input table (`ng2_in`) across an $8\text{L}$ baseline (`base8`), a two-pass weight-tied looped model (`loop2`), and an untied $16\text{L}$ model (`l16`), finding that `ng2_in` yields **$-13.19\text{ mBPB}$** (`-0.01319` BPB) on `base8`, retains **$105\%$** of its standalone gain with `loop2` (**$-13.80\text{ mBPB}$**), and retains **$69\%$** with untied $16\text{L}$ depth (**$-9.08\text{ mBPB}$**), while cautioning that output loss nonadditivity does not by itself identify internal circuits.

**Progressive Depth Growth and Non-Cloning Braided Training.** Progressive stacking (`Gong et al.` [14], `Du et al.` [3]) trains a shallow trunk in Stage 1 and duplicates layers at transition step $g$ to form the target depth. `Du et al.` [3] established—without `OE`—that fixed-compute progressive stacking exhibits an interior transition-timing valley balancing Stage-1 step-rate savings against Stage-2 adaptation steps. On ClimbMix-400B without `OE`, `claude_1` [2] (`general_pretraining_7`) and our companion studies [1], [15] (`general_pretraining_26`, `general_pretraining_24`) showed that `G_stack 8L->12L` at $g \in [250, 450]$ beats Iso-FLOPs `Static 12L` by $-4.1\text{ to }-4.6\text{ mBPB}$, while **Complementary Microbatch Braiding (`CLB-12L`)** [1] initializes all $12$ layers independently at step $0$ and routes alternating Stage-1 microbatches through complementary $8\text{L}$ branches.

**Scope of Our Contribution.** Why does restricting `Static 8L + OE`'s table horizon from $1{,}906$ to $1{,}318$ steps degrade loss by $+3.095\text{ mBPB}$ (`P3`) in our fixed-compute study, whereas `[4] §5` found a horizon-insensitive `d8` gain and `[5] §4.5` found a horizon-flat doubled `d10` gain? First, in `[5] §4.5`, backbone and table horizons were doubled together ($1{,}498 \to 2{,}996$), so backbone improvement offset table improvement; in `P3` (`oemax1318` and `oetrunc1318`), the `Static 8L` backbone always trains for all $1{,}906$ steps while **only** the `OE` tables are restricted to $1{,}318$ steps (directly paralleling `[4] §6`'s late `Freeze-at-k` regime). Second, our models use `Moment-Free OE` (step-wise RMS-normalized updates with linear warmdown) rather than AdamW table updates. Within this well-defined scope, our contribution is a **controlled quantitative interaction and diagnostic study**: (i) we evaluate the `Depth Schedule × Moment-Free OE` factorial on an **untouched disjoint corpus shard (`shard_00009.parquet`)** under uniform `2x32` physical microbatches with Holm–Bonferroni multiplicity reporting across five preregistered contrasts (`P1..P5`); (ii) we replicate the `Static 8L + OE` step-$1{,}318$ freeze controls (`oemax1318` and `oetrunc1318`) across **all $4$ seeds** and compare `--eval-oe-swap-step 1318` against its matched-history comparator `oetrunc1318`; and (iii) we pair exact key-versus-hash-row coverage tracking with post-hoc evaluation-time collision-zeroing and table-swap interventions on `seed=314` (`oe_collision_refinement_probe.json`) that separate unseen-trigram collision effects from continued refinement of already-covered bigram and trigram rows.

## 3. Methods, Preregistered Confirmation Protocol, and Diagnostic Controls

### 3.1 Backbone Schedules, Matrix/Attention FLOP Budget, and Uniform `2x32` Microbatch Protocol

All runs use the `SSSL` local–global decoder in `experiments_p3/train_growth_oe.py` with hidden dimension $d = 512$, $4$ attention heads of dimension $d_h = 128$, $\text{ReLU}^2$ MLP width $4d = 2{,}048$, sequence length $L_{\text{seq}} = 2{,}048$, sliding window $w_{\text{short}} = 1{,}024$ on `S` layers, full causal window $w_{\text{long}} = 2{,}048$ on `L` layers, ResFormer value embeddings (`VE`) on odd layers, and per-layer residual scalars $(\lambda_{\text{resid}, l}, \lambda_{x_0, l})$ initialized to $(1.0, 0.1)$. Under whole-run gradient accumulation `accum=2` ($2$ dataloader microbatches of $64 \times 2{,}048$ tokens per optimizer step, $262{,}144$ tokens/step) and linearly decaying weight decay $\lambda_0 = 0.10$ (`--wd-scale 0.5`), we match all schedules to the reference `Static 8L` forward-plus-backward **matrix/attention FLOP estimate** $C_{\text{ref}} = 1.19455\times 10^{17}\text{ FLOPs}$:
1. **`Static 8L` (`--mode static_8L`, $T = 1{,}906$ steps, $499.65\text{M}$ tokens, $50.33\text{M}$ backbone params):** Dense $8\text{L}$ (`2 x SSSL`) baseline running $1{,}906$ optimizer steps (`--max-steps 953`) at $F_{8\text{L}} = 2.390784\times 10^8$ matrix/attention FLOPs/token ($1.0000\times C_{\text{ref}}$).
2. **`Static 12L` (`--mode static_12L`, $T = 1{,}318$ steps, $345.51\text{M}$ tokens, $71.30\text{M} $ backbone params):** Dense $12\text{L}$ (`3 x SSSL`) baseline running $1{,}318$ optimizer steps (`--max-steps 659`) at $F_{12\text{L}} = 3.460347\times 10^8$ matrix/attention FLOPs/token ($1.0009\times C_{\text{ref}}$).
3. **`G_stack 8L->12L` (`--mode gstack_4to8 --s1-layers 8 --s2-layers 12`, transition step $g$, $50.33\text{M}\to 71.30\text{M}$ backbone params):** Trains an $8\text{L}$ trunk for $0 \le t < g$ at $F_{8\text{L}}$, duplicates super-block $4..7 \to 8..11$ (weights, `VE`, and `NorMuon`/`AdamW` states) at $t = g$, and trains the dense $12\text{L}$ model for $T(g) - g$ steps at $F_{12\text{L}}$, where $T(g) = \text{round}_{\text{even}}(1{,}316.872 + 0.30909\,g)$. At $g = 450$, $T(450) = 1{,}456$ steps (`--max-steps 728`, $381.68\text{M}$ tokens, $1.0000\times C_{\text{ref}}$); at matched $g = 250$, $T(250) = 1{,}394$ steps (`--max-steps 697`, $365.43\text{M}$ tokens, $0.9999\times C_{\text{ref}}$).
4. **`CLB-12L` (`--mode clb_12L`, $g = 250$, $T = 1{,}394$ steps, $365.43\text{M}$ tokens, $71.30\text{M}$ backbone params):** Initializes all $12$ layers independently at step $0$, routes alternating Stage-1 microbatches ($0 \le t < 250$) through complementary $8\text{L}$ sub-networks at $F_{8\text{L}}$, and unbraids to dense $12\text{L}$ execution at $t = 250$ via a $60$-step linear ramp from $\beta_{\text{iso}}(\rho) = 1/\sqrt{2(1+\rho)}$ to $1.0$ ($0.9999\times C_{\text{ref}}$) [1].

**Physical Microbatch Protocol (`--half-microbatch`).** On a $24\text{ GB}$ NVIDIA RTX 4090 ($23.55\text{ GiB}$ capacity), an uncheckpointed $12\text{L}$ forward/backward pass on all $64$ sequences at once (`1x64`) exceeds device memory. Consequently, default `train_growth_oe.py` splits each $64$-sequence dataloader batch into two $32$-sequence sub-passes (`2x32`, each scaled by $1/(2\cdot\text{accum}) = 1/4$) whenever $n_{\text{layer}} \ge 12$, while executing $8\text{L}$ stages as `1x64`. To eliminate physical sub-batch size as a confound between $8\text{L}$ and $12\text{L}$ stages, **all $32$ untouched disjoint-shard (`shard_00009.parquet`) confirmation runs execute under `--half-microbatch` (strictly uniform `2x32` sub-passes across all depths and stages)**, and we additionally report paired `2x32` vs. default sensitivity controls on the development shard (Supplement Table S2).

### 3.2 Moment-Free Over-Encoding (`OE`), Preregistration Protocol Note, and 4-Seed Freeze Controls

When `--oe-m 150855` is enabled (`train_growth_oe.py`), the model instantiates $K_{\text{oe}} = 4$ hashed tables $\{E^{(j)}\}_{j=0}^{3}$ of shapes $(150{,}855 + 2j, 128)$ in `torch.bfloat16` initialized to zero ($603{,}432$ total rows; $77{,}239{,}296$ parameters; $147.32\text{ MiB}$ BF16 weights, $147.32\text{ MiB}$ BF16 gradients, and $2.30\text{ MiB}$ FP32 per-step visit-count buffers `_oecnt_j`). Given token sequence $x_{b,t} \in \{0, \dots, 8{,}191\}$ padded on the left with sentinel $V = 8{,}192$ ($\tilde{x}_{b,t-\ell}$) and $V_1 = 8{,}193$, `oe_ids` computes two bigram and two trigram modular hash indices:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{id}_{2,b,t} &= x_{b,t} + V_1\,\tilde{x}_{b,t-1}, \qquad & i^{(0)}_{b,t} &= \text{id}_{2,b,t} \bmod m, \qquad & i^{(1)}_{b,t} &= \text{id}_{2,b,t} \bmod (m + 2), \\
\text{id}_{3,b,t} &= \text{id}_{2,b,t} + V_1^2\,\tilde{x}_{b,t-2}, \qquad & i^{(2)}_{b,t} &= \text{id}_{3,b,t} \bmod (m + 4), \qquad & i^{(3)}_{b,t} &= \text{id}_{3,b,t} \bmod (m + 6).
\end{aligned}
$$

The concatenated $512$-dimensional lookup vector is added to the token embedding before the initial RMSNorm: $x_{0,b,t} = \text{RMSNorm}(\text{wte}(x_{b,t}) + \text{Concat}_{j=0}^{3}(E^{(j)}[i^{(j)}_{b,t}]))$. While `OE` performs zero matrix/attention multiply-accumulates ($0$ analytical matrix/attention FLOPs), it executes non-matrix integer hash indexing, $4$ embedding gathers, residual addition, backward gradient `scatter_add_`, per-step hit-count accumulation `oe_count(x)`, and the `_step_oe_mfree` update over hit rows $\mathcal{H}_t^{(j)} = \{r : n_r^{(j)} > 0\}$:

$$
\bar{G}_r^{(j)} = \frac{G_r^{(j)}}{n_r^{(j)}} \quad (r \in \mathcal{H}_t^{(j)}), \qquad \text{rms}_t^{(j)} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{128\,|\mathcal{H}_t^{(j)}|}\sum_{r \in \mathcal{H}_t^{(j)}} \|\bar{G}_r^{(j)}\|_2^2}, \qquad E_r^{(j)} \leftarrow E_r^{(j)} - \frac{\eta_{\text{oe}}(t)}{\max(\text{rms}_t^{(j)}, 10^{-30})}\,\bar{G}_r^{(j)},
$$

where $\eta_{\text{oe}}(t) = \alpha_{\text{oe}} \cdot 0.6 \cdot (512/768)^{-1/2} \cdot \text{lrm}(t) \approx 0.73485\,\alpha_{\text{oe}}\,\text{lrm}(t)$ with $\alpha_{\text{oe}} = \texttt{--oe-lr-scale}$ (default $1.0$), followed by resetting `n.zero_()`. In wall-clock terms, this non-matrix arithmetic adds $+12.6\text{ to }+18.1\text{s}$ ($+1.2\%$ to $+1.7\%$) to `total_training_time` across our $4$-seed uniform-`2x32` confirmation arms. **Preregistration Protocol Note on $\eta_{\text{oe}}(t)$:** In `experiments_p3/preregistration_shard9.json`, the text description of `P3` inadvertently wrote $\eta_{\text{oe}}(t) = 0.1 \times \text{lrm}(t)$, whereas the actual training code (`train_growth_oe.py` line 2583) and all $87$ runs in this study uniformly execute $\eta_{\text{oe}}(t) = 0.6 \cdot (512/768)^{-1/2} \cdot \alpha_{\text{oe}} \cdot \text{lrm}(t) \approx 0.735\,\alpha_{\text{oe}}\,\text{lrm}(t)$ (matching the standard embedding step-size scaling of `general_pretraining_11` [8]). We retain `preregistration_shard9.json` unmodified as a locked historical artifact and disclose this documentation discrepancy explicitly here.

**Four-Seed `OE` Horizon/Schedule Freeze Controls (`--oe-max-step 1318`) and Eval-Only Swap (`--eval-oe-swap-step 1318`).** To measure across all $4$ seeds how restricting `Static 8L + OE`'s table updates to $1{,}318$ steps ($345.5\text{M}$ tokens) affects a model whose $8\text{L}$ backbone still trains for all $1{,}906$ steps ($499.6\text{M}$ tokens), we run two $4$-seed freeze arms on `Static 8L + OE`:
- **`oemax1318` (`--oe-max-step 1318 --oe-freeze-mode matched_schedule`):** For $0 \le t < 1{,}318$, the `OE` tables follow the exact $1{,}318$-step warmdown multiplier $\text{lrm}_{1318}(t)$ of `Static 12L + OE` (linear warmdown from $1.0$ to $0.0$ over steps $659..1{,}318$) and remain frozen ($\eta_{\text{oe}}(t) = 0$, after exact updates $0..1{,}317$) for steps $1{,}318 \le t < 1{,}906$ while the $8\text{L}$ backbone completes its $1{,}906$-step schedule.
- **`oetrunc1318` (`--oe-max-step 1318 --oe-freeze-mode truncate`):** For $0 \le t < 1{,}318$, the `OE` tables follow `Static 8L`'s normal step-size multiplier $\text{lrm}_{1906}(t)$ and are abruptly frozen ($\eta_{\text{oe}}(t) = 0$, after exact updates $0..1{,}317$) for steps $1{,}318 \le t < 1{,}906$. Comparing `oetrunc1318` vs. `Static 8L + OE` measures the penalty of truncating post-$1{,}318$ table updates ($345.5\text{M}\to 499.6\text{M}$ tokens) under continuing $8\text{L}$ backbone training, whereas `oemax1318 - oetrunc1318` measures the penalty of earlier `OE` learning-rate warmdown over steps $659..1{,}318$.
- **Algebraic Contrast Scope Note & Eval-Only Table Swap (`--eval-oe-swap-step 1318`):** Because modifying `OE` table updates during training changes the forward embeddings $x_{0,b,t}$ and therefore alters subsequent backbone gradients, `Static 8L + OE`, `oetrunc1318`, `oemax1318`, and `Static 12L + OE` have distinct backbone trajectories. Thus, the telescoping identity $\text{P1} = \text{P3} + (\text{Static 12L + OE} - \text{oemax1318})$ is an **algebraic difference across training procedures**, not a causal decomposition holding the backbone trajectory fixed. Furthermore, during steps $1{,}318..1{,}905$ of `oetrunc1318` and `oemax1318`, the $8\text{L}$ backbone continues updating against the frozen step-$1{,}318$ tables. To test zero-co-adaptation table substitution, `--eval-oe-swap-step 1318` snapshots `oe_tables` at the start of step $1{,}318$ (after exact updates $0..1{,}317$ under `Static 8L`'s normal schedule $\text{lrm}_{1906}(t)$) and swaps that snapshot into the fully trained step-$1{,}906$ `Static 8L + OE` backbone at evaluation time (`oe_swap_eval`). Because this snapshot uses $\text{lrm}_{1906}(t)$ rather than $\text{lrm}_{1318}(t)$, its **matched-history comparator is `oetrunc1318`** (which freezes the exact same $\text{lrm}_{1906}(t)$ table state at step $1{,}318$ and then co-adapts the $8\text{L}$ backbone for $588$ steps), not `oemax1318`.

**Hierarchy of Confirmatory, Post-Hoc Intervention, and Descriptive Analyses.** Prior to executing the `p3_s9_*` suite, we locked five primary confirmatory contrasts (`P1..P5` in `experiments_p3/preregistration_shard9.json`) on `disjoint_shard9_bpb` across all $N = 4$ paired seeds ($\text{seeds} \in \{42, 137, 271, 314\}$, $\text{df} = 3$, two-sided paired Student-$t$ tests) at family-wise $\alpha = 0.05$ across $K = 5$ tests using **Holm–Bonferroni step-down adjusted $p$-values ($p_{\text{Holm}}$)**. Throughout the paper, we report both $p_{\text{raw}}$ and $p_{\text{Holm}}$, **label all displayed $95\%$ confidence intervals as pointwise (unadjusted) Student-$t$ intervals**, and strictly distinguish passing the preregistered family-wise threshold ($p_{\text{Holm}} < 0.05$, achieved only by `P3`) from directional $4/4$-seed consistency (`P1`, `P2`, `P4`, where $p_{\text{raw}} < 0.05$ but $p_{\text{Holm}} \in [0.0555, 0.0945]$). Table 2, Figure 3(b,c), and Table 3 establish three results regarding the schedule-freeze intervention and the underlying table mechanism:
1. **Four-Seed Stability of the `OE` Schedule-Restriction Penalty (`P3`) and Correct Matched-History Co-Adaptation Comparison:** Across all $4$ seeds on `shard_00009.parquet`, restricting `Static 8L + OE`'s table updates and warmdown to `Static 12L`'s $1{,}318$-step schedule (`oemax1318`) while continuing the $8\text{L}$ backbone to $1{,}906$ steps degrades loss by **`P3`: $+3.095 \pm 0.148\text{ mBPB}$** (`+3.056 / +2.906 / +3.245 / +3.172 mBPB`, pointwise $95\%\text{ CI } [+2.860, +3.330], p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.00015^*$), which is the sole contrast passing the preregistered family-wise threshold. Sequential comparison along our two-step intervention shows **$+2.451 \pm 0.098\text{ mBPB}$** from truncating post-$1{,}318$ updates under $\text{lrm}_{1906}(t)$ (`oetrunc1318 - Static 8L + OE`) and **$+0.644 \pm 0.111\text{ mBPB}$** from switching the table schedule to earlier warmdown over steps $659..1{,}318$ (`oemax1318 - oetrunc1318`). The remaining algebraic residual between `Static 12L + OE` ($12\text{L}$ trained for $1{,}318$ steps) and `oemax1318` ($8\text{L}$ trained for $1{,}906$ steps with $1{,}318$-step `OE`) is **$-0.947 \pm 0.847\text{ mBPB}$** (`+0.124 / -1.091 / -0.885 / -1.937 mBPB`). As noted in §3.2, this residual compares two different backbone architectures, horizons, and gradient histories rather than isolating a pure depth effect at a fixed backbone state. Similarly, for the `seed=314` eval-only swap (`--eval-oe-swap-step 1318`), the step-$1{,}318$ table snapshot follows `Static 8L`'s normal schedule $\text{lrm}_{1906}(t)$, so its **matched-history comparator is `oetrunc1318` ($+2.579\text{ mBPB}$, endpoint $0.974988$), not `oemax1318` ($+3.172\text{ mBPB}$, endpoint $0.975581$)**. Swapping that un-warmed-down step-$1{,}318$ snapshot into the final step-$1{,}906$ backbone without post-freeze co-adaptation increases `shard_00009` BPB by **$+3.176\text{ mBPB}$** ($0.972409 \to 0.975585$ on the original `s314` run, and **$+3.174\text{ mBPB}$**, $0.972135 \to 0.975309$, on a checkpointed `s314` rerun). Comparing the un-coadapted swap endpoint ($0.975309\text{–}0.975585$) against the co-adapted `oetrunc1318` endpoint ($0.974988$) indicates that $588$ steps of post-freeze backbone co-adaptation recover **$0.32\text{ to }0.60\text{ mBPB}$** (accounting for $\approx 0.27\text{ mBPB}$ same-seed GPU nondeterminism between independent runs), while `oemax1318`'s $+0.593\text{ mBPB}$ earlier-warmdown penalty (`0.975581 - 0.974988` on `s314`) coincidentally offsets that co-adaptation recovery so that `oe_swap_eval` ($0.975585$) numerically lands near `oemax1318` ($0.975581$).
2. **Exact Key-vs.-Row Coverage Diagnostic:** Tracking exact cumulative key and row frequencies across the $9$-shard training stream (`oe_exposure_analysis_shard9.json`) and $10$-shard development stream (`oe_exposure_analysis.json`) shows that **by step $1{,}318$ ($345.5\text{M}$ tokens), $100.00\%$ of all $603{,}432$ hash rows across all $4$ `OE` tables already have $\ge 20$ training hits** ($99.9998\%$ have $\ge 100$ hits), because $8{,}862{,}453$ ($9$-shard) to $9{,}025{,}985$ ($10$-shard) distinct training bigrams have occurred, colliding at **$58.75$ to $59.83$ distinct bigram keys per row** on Table 0 (rising to $67.20\text{–}69.71$ keys/row at $T = 1{,}906$). Between $T = 1{,}318$ and $T = 1{,}906$ on untouched `shard_00009.parquet`, exact bigram token coverage ($c(\text{id}_2) \ge 1$) barely changes from **$98.77\%$ to $99.00\%$** ($+0.23\text{ pp}$), whereas unseen trigram reads ($c(\text{id}_3) = 0$, which read active shared rows) decline from **$16.63\%$ to $14.99\%$** ($-1.64\text{ pp}$) and trigram coverage with $c(\text{id}_3) \ge 5$ rises from **$69.01\%$ to $72.23\%$** ($+3.22\text{ pp}$).
3. **Post-Hoc Evaluation-Time Collision-Zeroing and Table-Swap Probe (`seed=314`, Table 3):** Coverage accounting alone cannot determine whether the step-$1{,}318 \to 1{,}906$ table improvement comes from fewer unseen-key collision reads or from continued updating of already-covered rows. To test this directly on a fixed backbone, we ran a post-hoc evaluation-time intervention study (`experiments_p3/eval_oe_collision_vs_refinement.py`, `oe_collision_refinement_probe.json`) on `seed=314` `Static 8L + OE` using a checkpointed rerun (`shard_00009` BPB $0.972135$ vs. $0.972409$ original; full step-$1{,}318$ table swap penalty $+3.1740\text{ mBPB}$ vs. $+3.1763\text{ mBPB}$ original). Applying all interventions at evaluation time on the final step-$1{,}906$ backbone $B_{1906}$ over all $20{,}945{,}077$ normal tokens ($84{,}673{,}695$ bytes) of `shard_00009.parquet` yields four findings (Table 3):
   - **(i) Unseen-key collision reads are net harmful on `seed=314`, but their reduction between steps $1{,}318$ and $1{,}906$ explains only $0.8\%$ ($0.025\text{ mBPB}$) of the swap penalty:** Zeroing the trigram lookup channels ($E^{(2)}, E^{(3)}$) at evaluation positions whose trigram never appeared in the $1{,}906$-step training stream ($U_0: c_{1906}(\text{id}_3) = 0$, $14.99\%$ of tokens, $14.03\%$ of bytes) **improves** `shard_00009` BPB on $T_{1906}$ by **$-0.6393\text{ mBPB}$** overall (**$-4.1680\text{ mBPB}$** within $U_0$ positions, contributing $-0.5847\text{ mBPB}$ directly plus $-0.0546\text{ mBPB}$ via causal context spillover to subsequent tokens; or **$-0.7495\text{ mBPB}$** when zeroing both unseen bigrams and unseen trigrams). Relative to `seed=314`'s own standalone `OE` gain ($-14.433\text{ mBPB}$), $-0.6393\text{ mBPB}$ is $4.4\%$ of the `OE` effect—the same sign and similar magnitude to `[4] §5`'s $2.9\%$ at `d8`. Crucially, applying the same unseen-trigram zeroing ($c_{1318}(\text{id}_3) = 0$, $16.63\%$ of tokens) on the swapped step-$1{,}318$ tables $T_{1318}$ improves BPB from $0.975309$ to $0.974645$, a gain of **$-0.6641\text{ mBPB}$** (or **$-0.7758\text{ mBPB}$** when zeroing both unseen bigrams and trigrams). Thus, the differential collision-zeroing gain between $T_{1318}$ and $T_{1906}$ is only **$0.0248\text{ mBPB}$** ($0.6641 - 0.6393\text{ mBPB}$, or $0.0263\text{ mBPB}$ for both orders)—accounting for **$0.8\%$** of the $+3.1740\text{ mBPB}$ swap penalty.
   - **(ii) Two-thirds of the step-$1{,}318 \to 1{,}906$ table-swap penalty sits in the bigram tables, where coverage is already $98.77\%$ saturated:** Swapping **only the bigram tables** ($E^{(0)}, E^{(1)}$ from $T_{1318}$, keeping $E^{(2)}, E^{(3)}$ from $T_{1906}$) costs **$+2.0711\text{ mBPB}$** (or **$+2.3439\text{ mBPB}$** marginal when the trigram tables are already at $T_{1318}$), whereas swapping **only the trigram tables** ($E^{(2)}, E^{(3)}$ from $T_{1318}$) costs **$+0.8301\text{ mBPB}$** (or **$+1.1029\text{ mBPB}$** marginal when the bigram tables are already at $T_{1318}$; super-additive interaction $+0.2728\text{ mBPB}$). Because bigram coverage moves only from $98.77\%$ to $99.00\%$ between steps $1{,}318$ and $1{,}906$, new key coverage cannot explain the $+2.07\text{ to }+2.34\text{ mBPB}$ bigram-table penalty.
   - **(iii) Newly covered trigram keys ($U_1$) account for only $+0.083\text{ mBPB}$ ($2.6\%$), while the penalty is distributed across all exposure bins:** Stratifying the full $T_{1318}$ swap across exact corpus trigram exposure bins shows that positions whose trigram was first seen between steps $1{,}318$ and $1{,}906$ ($U_1: c_{1318}=0, c_{1906}\ge 1$, $1.64\%$ of tokens, $1.55\%$ of bytes) suffer a $+5.3670\text{ mBPB}$ within-bin penalty but contribute only **$+0.0832\text{ mBPB}$ ($2.6\%$)** of the $+3.1740\text{ mBPB}$ total penalty (and $+0.0792\text{ mBPB}$ when swapping all $4$ tables only at $U_1$ positions, or $+0.0224\text{ mBPB}$ under order-matched newly-seen swaps). Across all other bins, within-bin penalties are broadly distributed: **$+2.1109\text{ mBPB}$** on persistently unseen trigrams ($U_0$, $14.03\%$ of bytes, contributing $+0.2962\text{ mBPB}$ because those positions still use seen bigram tables), **$+3.0075\text{ mBPB}$** on $S_1$ ($c_{1318} \in [1,4]$, $13.84\%$ of bytes, contributing $+0.4161\text{ mBPB}$), **$+3.6749\text{ mBPB}$** on $S_2$ ($c_{1318} \in [5,19]$, $14.17\%$ of bytes, contributing $+0.5208\text{ mBPB}$), and **$+3.2931\text{ mBPB}$** on $S_3$ ($c_{1318} \ge 20$, $56.41\%$ of bytes, contributing $+1.8577\text{ mBPB}$). Together, already-seen trigram positions ($S = S_1 \cup S_2 \cup S_3$, $84.42\%$ of bytes) carry $+2.7946\text{ mBPB}$ ($88.0\%$, closely tracking their byte share). Under order-matched swaps—where bigram channels are swapped according to bigram exposure $c(\text{id}_2)$ and trigram channels according to trigram exposure $c(\text{id}_3)$—swapping at persistently unseen keys (`cond_swap_order_matched_persistently_unseen`) changes BPB by **$-0.0349\text{ mBPB}$**, confirming that virtually all of the $+0.8301\text{ mBPB}$ trigram-table penalty also occurs at seen trigram keys.
   - **(iv) Scope of the evaluation-swap probe:** We therefore **reject the hypothesis that reduced unseen-key collision readout explains the step-$1{,}318 \to 1{,}906$ horizon effect**. Instead, the $+3.174\text{ mBPB}$ swap penalty reflects the step-$1{,}318 \to 1{,}906$ change in already-covered rows (primarily in the bigram tables) evaluated on the final backbone $B_{1906}$. Because $B_{1906}$ was trained with the final trajectory ending at $T_{1906}$, this fixed-backbone swap combines intrinsic row refinement with backbone–table alignment; however, the $4$-seed retraining control `oetrunc1318` ($+2.451 \pm 0.098\text{ mBPB}$ across $4$ seeds, $+2.579\text{ mBPB}$ on `seed=314`) demonstrates that even when the $8\text{L}$ backbone is given $588$ steps to co-adapt to the frozen $T_{1318}$ tables, roughly $77\text{–}81\%$ of the penalty remains.

2. **Matched-Transition ($g = 250, T = 1{,}394$) Development Comparison Between `CLB-12L + OE` and `G_stack (g=250) + OE` (Figure 2b & Supplement Table S1):** On the development shard (`shard_06542.parquet`, $3$ seeds `42, 137, 271`), holding the transition step and token horizon strictly matched at $g = 250, T = 1{,}394$ ($365.4\text{M}$ tokens) yields nearly identical overall validation loss between `G_stack (g=250) + OE` ($0.971065 \pm 0.000468$) and `CLB-12L (g=250) + OE` ($0.971239 \pm 0.001401$, paired $\Delta = +0.174 \pm 1.547\text{ mBPB}$, pointwise $95\%\text{ CI } [-3.669, +4.017]$). Descriptively across token classes at matched $g = 250$, `CLB-12L (g=250) + OE` has lower `short_repeat` loss on $3/3$ seeds ($0.346992 \pm 0.001652$ vs. $0.352008 \pm 0.001057$, paired $\Delta = -5.016 \pm 2.409\text{ mBPB}$, seed deltas `[-7.798, -3.658, -3.592] mBPB`, pointwise $95\%\text{ CI } [-11.001, +0.969]$ spanning zero) and slightly higher `novel` loss ($+1.336 \pm 1.470\text{ mBPB}$, pointwise $95\%\text{ CI } [-2.315, +4.987]$). Because output token-class losses do not identify internal attention or MLP circuits, and the $3$-seed matched-$g=250$ `short_repeat` interval includes zero, we treat this pattern strictly as a descriptive observation.
3. **Interior Growth Valley in Both `No-OE` and `+OE` (Figure 3a & Supplement Table S3):** Across the sampled `seed=42` development transition grid $g \in \{0, 250, 450, 650, 850, 1{,}050\}$, $g = 450$ ($T = 1{,}456$, $381.7\text{M}$ tokens) is the lowest sampled nontrivial growth point both without `OE` ($0.981248$) and with `+OE` ($0.970357$, improving over $g = 250$ across $3$ seeds by $-0.891 \pm 0.113\text{ mBPB}$, pointwise $95\%\text{ CI } [-1.172, -0.610]$). As established by `Du et al.` [3], an interior transition valley is a general property of fixed-compute stacking rather than an `OE`-specific phenomenon, and $g = 450$ does not beat the full-horizon `Static 8L + OE` endpoint ($0.969792$ on dev `seed=42`; $0.971832$ vs. $0.971577$ across $4$ seeds on `shard_00009`).
4. **Tested Constant `--oe-lr-scale` Multipliers (`1.45` and `1.35`; Figure 3d & Supplement Table S3):** On `seed=42` (`shard_06542.parquet`), scaling the `OE` step size by `--oe-lr-scale 1.45` on `Static 12L + OE` (matching $1{,}906/1{,}318 \approx 1.446$) worsens validation loss by $+0.768\text{ mBPB}$ ($0.973768 \to 0.974536$), and `--oe-lr-scale 1.35` on `G_stack (g=450) + OE` (vs. $1{,}906/1{,}456 \approx 1.309$) worsens validation loss by $+0.337\text{ mBPB}$ ($0.970357 \to 0.970694$, with `novel` $+0.484\text{ mBPB}$ and `short_repeat` $-0.333\text{ mBPB}$). This bounded negative result shows only that these two constant multipliers do not substitute for lost token exposure.

## 5. Discussion, Limitations, and Scope Conditions

1. **Reconciling Fixed-Backbone Freeze Sensitivity (`P3`) With Prior Horizon Controls (`[4] §5–§6`, `[5] §4.5`, `[10] §4.5`):** Comparing our results with prior ClimbMix-400B studies clarifies what is newly established here and what depends on the experimental protocol:
   - In `[5] §4.5`, `claude_2` doubled the `d10` horizon from $1{,}498$ to $2{,}996$ steps for **both the backbone and the table simultaneously**, finding a horizon-flat `OE` gain (`+0.16 / -0.15 mBPB`) because the stronger $2{,}996$-step backbone absorbed more surface $n$-gram structure on its own. By contrast, `P3` (`oemax1318` and `oetrunc1318`) holds the $8\text{L}$ backbone horizon fixed at $1{,}906$ steps while restricting **only** the `Moment-Free OE` table schedule to $1{,}318$ steps ($+3.095 \pm 0.148\text{ mBPB}$, $p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.00015^*$), directly extending `[4] §6`'s early `Freeze-at-k` finding ($k \in \{100, 300, 600\}$) into the late-horizon ($1{,}318$ vs. $1{,}906$ steps) fixed-compute depth-comparison regime and separating update truncation ($+2.451\text{ mBPB}$) from earlier table warmdown ($+0.644\text{ mBPB}$).
   - In `[4] §5`, `claude_1` showed that zeroing unseen order-$3$ table rows at evaluation explained $< 5\%$ ($2.9\%$ at `d8`) of the `OE` gain. Our post-hoc `seed=314` collision-zeroing probe (Table 3) confirms that exact same directional finding under `Moment-Free OE`: zeroing unseen trigrams improves BPB by $-0.639\text{ mBPB}$ ($4.4\%$ of `s314`'s `OE` gain) on $T_{1906}$ and $-0.664\text{ mBPB}$ on $T_{1318}$, leaving a $0.025\text{ mBPB}$ ($0.8\%$) differential that rules out unseen-key collision reduction as the driver of the step-$1{,}318 \to 1{,}906$ table effect, and instead localizes two-thirds of the swap penalty ($+2.07\text{ to }+2.34\text{ mBPB}$) to already-covered bigram tables.
2. **Multiplicity, Seed Heterogeneity (`P1, P2, P4`), and Non-Identification of Internal Circuits:** While `P3` ($p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.00015^*$) passes the preregistered $5$-test Holm–Bonferroni threshold with very small cross-seed variance ($\text{SD} = 0.148\text{ mBPB}$), `P1` ($+2.148\text{ mBPB}$, $p_{\text{raw}} = 0.0139, p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.0555$), `P2` ($+3.550\text{ mBPB}$, $p_{\text{raw}} = 0.0215, p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.0645$), and especially `P4` ($-2.402\text{ mBPB}$, $p_{\text{raw}} = 0.0472, p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.0945$, with $-0.311\text{ mBPB}$ on `seed=314`) show $4/4$-seed directional consistency without passing the family-wise $\alpha = 0.05$ bar. Furthermore, the post-hoc collision/refinement probe (Table 3) was executed on a single seed (`seed=314`) on the final backbone $B_{1906}$, so it does not separate intrinsic row refinement from backbone–table alignment, nor do output token-class losses identify internal attention or MLP circuits.
3. **Single-Budget Regime and Absence of Growth Advantage Over `Static 8L + OE` (`P5`):** All findings are scoped to $50.3\text{M}\text{–}71.3\text{M}$ backbone models trained at $1.195\times 10^{17}$ matrix/attention FLOPs (`accum=2`, $262{,}144$ tokens/step, $m = 150{,}855$ rows/table), where `G_stack (g=450) + OE` shows no demonstrated improvement over keeping the dense $8\text{L}$ backbone at full $1{,}906$-step token exposure (`P5`: $-0.255 \pm 1.062\text{ mBPB}$, $p_{\text{raw}} = 0.6644$).
