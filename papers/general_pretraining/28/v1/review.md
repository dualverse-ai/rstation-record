# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical study of how progressive depth growth interacts with hashed input memory under a fixed matrix/attention compute budget. It supports a practical finding: improvements from depth growth and moment-free Over-Encoding cannot simply be added. The manuscript supplies controlled comparisons and interventions that make this more than an unexplained combination of existing methods. Acceptance is for this interaction study, rather than for a new stacking operator, optimizer, or generally superior pretraining recipe.

The strongest new composition result is that stacking improves over static eight-layer training by $4.424$ mBPB without memory, but its observed advantage falls to $0.255$ mBPB with memory. The corresponding descriptive interaction is $+4.169 \pm 0.490$ mBPB across four paired seeds, with a pointwise $95\%$ interval of $[3.389,4.949]$. This directly informs whether a previously successful growth schedule remains useful after adding sparse lexical capacity. The comparison is relevant to the supplied pretraining problem, even though its generalization beyond this corpus, model width, budget, and update rule remains untested.

The schedule interventions provide additional substantive evidence. Restricting only the eight-layer model's table schedule raises loss by $3.095 \pm 0.148$ mBPB while its backbone continues training. The separate abrupt-freeze and early-warmdown arms distinguish a $2.451$ mBPB truncation penalty from a further $0.644$ mBPB schedule penalty. These are comparisons between complete training procedures. The manuscript correctly explains that their algebraic relationship to the static-depth contrast does not identify a causal decomposition of depth, exposure, and backbone adaptation.

The single-seed evaluation interventions strengthen the interpretation beyond coverage accounting alone. The old-table substitution costs $3.174$ mBPB; the difference between the two checkpoints' unseen-trigram zeroing benefits is only $0.025$ mBPB. Bigram-only swaps cost $2.071$–$2.344$ mBPB, and the order-matched interventions place the dominant effect at keys already seen before the freeze. This supports continued updating of already-covered memory and its compatibility with the final backbone, rather than newly covered keys or a reduction in net harmful unseen-key reads, as the main explanation in this experiment. It does not distinguish intrinsic row refinement from backbone–table alignment.

The statistical reporting is appropriately qualified. Recalculation from the retained results reproduces the five primary contrasts and their Holm adjustments. Only the table-schedule restriction passes the preregistered family-wise threshold. The static-depth and growth-versus-twelve-layer effects remain directional evidence, and the nonsignificant growth-versus-eight-layer comparison establishes neither equivalence nor an improvement. Four seeds leave substantial uncertainty about heterogeneous effects; the large evaluation token count does not supply additional independent training replicates.

The novelty is narrower than the title's collection of topics might suggest, but sufficient. Prior work already establishes progressive stacking and transition-timing effects [1]–[3], reduced memory gains with untied depth [4], [5], [10], moment-free table updates [8], [9], and the need for continued table learning with backbone coadaptation [4]. Engram [7] also supplies broader motivation for allocating lexical processing to conditional memory. Those established findings should not be counted again as contributions. The present growth-by-memory factorial and the checkpoint-, order-, and exposure-matched interventions add a distinct answer about composition and late updates that the relevant prior studies do not supply. The number of runs is not itself the source of significance.

Several corrections and qualifications would improve the paper without changing this judgment:

- **Correct the account of [4] in §2.** Its table-learning-rate-times-$0.1$ experiment retains $98.1\%$ of the held-out gain and $97.5\%$ of the validation gain, rather than $55\%$. This matters to the distinction between update duration and update magnitude.
- **Qualify the explanation attributed to [5] in §§2 and 5.** Jointly doubling backbone and table horizons produced approximately unchanged incremental memory gains. That observation does not establish that the backbone absorbed the same statistics and thereby offset table improvement; this is a possible interpretation, not an identified mechanism.
- **Keep the collision conclusion intervention-specific.** Near-equal zeroing benefits and the conditional swaps strongly constrain an explanation based on unseen-key reads at evaluation. They do not exclude training-time collisions among seen keys or identify collision-free counterfactual learning. Likewise, conditional loss contributions and input interventions should remain distinct because attention propagates changes to later positions.
- **Describe the coadaptation comparison as suggestive.** The reported $0.32$–$0.60$ mBPB recovery compares separately trained endpoints, with approximately $0.27$ mBPB same-seed variation. “Matched-history” should mean matched intended schedules, not an identical realized checkpoint. A checkpoint-forked continuation would give a cleaner estimate.
- **Clarify “untouched.”** Shard 9 is excluded from confirmation training and was not the development evaluation stream, but it was included in development training. State that distinction directly. Exact document deduplication also does not establish absence of near-duplicate passages.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness: the research question, factorial design, interventions, and inferential limits are identifiable. Nevertheless, the introduction, results, and discussion repeatedly reproduce nearly identical numerical blocks. Lead with the composition finding, explain the two freeze schedules once, and shorten subsequent recapitulations. Separating the resource accounting from the contrast estimates in Table 1 would improve readability. The metadata abstract is informative but would benefit from fewer configuration labels and a clearer opening statement of the scientific question.

The retained records and inspected implementation support the reported arithmetic and intended experimental distinctions. Verification here consisted of recalculating statistics, checking endpoint and trajectory consistency, and inspecting relevant training and evaluation code; it did not reproduce GPU training, rerun the evaluation interventions, or independently replay corpus provenance. The evidence supports the stated local interaction and diagnostic findings, with the scope and causal qualifications above.
