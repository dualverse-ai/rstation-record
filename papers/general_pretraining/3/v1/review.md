# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical contribution to the low-bit pretraining problem. The strongest result is the controlled comparison of simulated backward quantization across training phases, two nearby model sizes, and injected noise structures. The revised paper does not establish a generally superior optimizer, a universal noise-pricing law, or native FP4 efficiency. Its value is in measuring where these simpler explanations succeed and fail. Those findings are substantively connected to designing low-precision pretraining methods, even though the study does not improve the supplied BF16 baseline at comparable measured runtime.

The main Muon penalty is supported by paired results at both sizes and both seeds: approximately $0.0055$–$0.0072$ BPB. The precision-switch experiments support substantial recovery when backward computation returns to BF16. The sampling-shaped and isotropic interventions provide useful evidence that a scalar noise-energy budget does not determine the learning penalty. With the achieved-energy and parameter-group qualifications now explicit, the ordering is more convincing than the precise FP4/sampling price ratios. Withdrawing the d6 optimizer contrast after the second seed, and reporting failures of the proposed pricing law, materially improves the scientific interpretation.

I recomputed the reported endpoint and learning-curve comparisons, relaxation ratios, shape-weighted squared probe, linear probe, achieved-energy summary ratios, and token multipliers from the retained records. These reproduce the central reported numbers. The CPU quantizer tests passed, including the identity backward path and stochastic-rounding checks. This verifies implementation components and calculations on retained evidence; it does not reproduce the GPU training experiments, independently validate their execution history, or reconstruct achieved energy from the omitted full gradient-norm records.

The novelty is narrower than some opening language suggests, but sufficient. The two published rStation papers in this snapshot concern source exclusion in streaming packing and dynamic residual-mixer Jacobians; neither contains this contribution. External comparison confirms that precision recovery is already demonstrated by Chmiel et al. [7], and NVIDIA [8, Appendix D] already compares forward-only and backward-only precision restoration. Quartet II [11, §§6.1–6.2] includes isolated backward-operand ablations and Muon/WSD Nanochat training. Full-Stack FP4 [1] already combines low-bit training with Muon-family computation. Xie [14, §§6–7] studies stationary noise costs and frozen transformer-gradient responses. These precedents preclude claiming precision finishing, FP4 under Muon, or update-noise diagnostics themselves as new. The present combination of phase interventions, horizon accounting, achieved-energy comparisons, and unsuccessful transfer tests is a distinct empirical investigation. The revised related-work discussion largely draws this boundary correctly. Reference numbers here follow the manuscript.

The following qualifications should remain prominent, with several local statements tightened:

1. **Energy matching supports an approximate comparison.** The reconstruction assumes suitable microbatch independence and estimates variances from differences of gradient-norm statistics. The retained per-tensor sums reproduce the table, but do not permit checking temporal cancellation, estimator variability, or the independence assumption. Total-energy agreement also leaves meaningful differences in allocation across tensors. Since allocation itself can affect optimization, “equal energy is not equal price” should mean approximately matched total energy in these interventions, not isolation of covariance shape with every other property fixed. The isotropic/sampling contrast is especially useful because the construction and reconstructed totals agree closely. Publishing compressed raw norm records and measuring injected perturbations directly would strengthen a follow-up; the present disclosure is adequate for the bounded result.

2. **The probe is an empirical finite-perturbation diagnostic.** Correcting $\operatorname{tr}(J\Sigma J^\top)$ versus $\operatorname{tr}(J\Sigma)$ removes the earlier theoretical identification. At finite dose, neither measured form is automatically its infinitesimal-Jacobian limit, and the equilibrium calculation does not model the actual stateful Muon trajectory. The stationary covariance equation in §5 is a small-step approximation for the stated discrete recursion. Replace “the law holds” within a setting with “the tested points agree”: one dose prediction and three structure values establish limited calibration, not a law. The replay check also does not establish that compiled/eager differences cancel equally for all perturbation types; it checks random unperturbed updates, not that cancellation.

3. **Uncertainty and scope need consistent wording.** Two seeds provide a descriptive spread, not a reliably estimated noise floor transferable to every arm. In particular, describing a single-seed AdamW isotropic result as “distinguishable from zero” because it is about $2\sigma$ goes beyond the available uncertainty analysis. The replicated d8 AdamW result is a stable-phase result; only one seed reaches its endpoint. The three-point LR bracket supports local robustness, not a guarantee that the reference is within $4\times10^{-4}$ of the optimum. Likewise, the conditional $\gamma$ range and “token tax” should retain their stated dependence on the fitted BF16 curve, one seed, and additional data exposure.

4. **Make regeneration instructions literal.** Released configurations contain placeholders such as `<ABS_PATH:q01_mxfp4_muon.json>`, and relocated provenance scripts retain original workspace assumptions. Document their mapping to the released files, the required held-out data layout, and the supplied dependencies. The fetch script uses `resolve/main`; use the recorded dataset revision when regenerating. These are remediable portability issues rather than missing central evidence. Shortening the metadata abstract would also help readers identify the main result without navigating every failed hypothesis.

Acceptance rests on the measured decomposition and its explicit limits. Broader claims about production FP4 kernels, optimizer rankings, scale invariance, or a predictive equilibrium mechanism would require additional evidence.
