# Accept

This update resolves the previous review’s principal clarifications while preserving the accepted empirical contribution and its experimental evidence. The baseline paragraph now correctly distinguishes the validation offsets, $0.001201$ and $0.001725$, from the $0.000524$ difference between the two BF16 references. It appropriately avoids interpreting two seeds as establishing ordinary baseline variability.

The noise-meter discussion now separates adjacent-microbatch correlation from the dependence induced by evaluating a recently trained-on batch at updated weights. Making the dose and target (b) conditional on both assumptions, and describing the $5.8\%$ estimator difference as a consistency comparison rather than a bias bound, improves the scientific interpretation. The precise attribution makes the adjacent-covariance observation easier to trace; I did not independently reproduce that measurement. These qualifications preserve the distinction between conditional noise estimates and the direct paired loss comparisons.

The added sign-aligned quantizer test is a useful verification improvement. I ran the supplied CPU tests successfully: MXFP4 yielded sign-aligned scores of $+0.48$ and $-0.66$, and the injected rounding-probability bias of $0.0025$ yielded $z=42.2$. The reported BF16-output relative biases for INT4 and INT3 were approximately $7.4\times10^{-4}$ and $2.6\times10^{-3}$. These checks support the new diagnostic claims on the specified tensor; they do not reproduce training or establish unbiasedness for arbitrary numerical inputs. The INT-format qualification does not undermine the main MXFP4 results: INT4 appears only in the already-void T1 test, and INT3 was unused in training.

Inspection confirms that the two revised provenance scripts now honor the documented workspace and revision variables, with the upstream revision pinned by default. The retained manifest records that same revision. The packed experimental records are unchanged; their only packed-file modifications are these two scripts. I did not rerun dataset downloads or GPU experiments. No new central contribution or expanded novelty claim requires reopening the accepted literature assessment.

One minor wording correction remains in §3: it is the **pooled signed mean of the per-element z-scores** that can cancel a magnitude bias across positive and negative inputs. Individual per-element scores, and sufficiently powerful two-sided aggregate tests, are not intrinsically blind to such bias. Replace “the per-element bias z-scores cannot see” with a statement about cancellation in their pooled signed mean and the limited sensitivity of the previous checks. This does not affect the validity of the added sign-aligned test or the acceptance decision.
