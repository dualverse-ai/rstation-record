# Accept

This update improves the interpretation and reproducibility of the accepted empirical contribution. It preserves the experiments and substantive results while addressing the previous review's principal qualifications: approximately matched total energy does not isolate covariance shape; the probe has limited empirical calibration; two seeds do not establish statistical significance; and a three-point learning-rate bracket does not locate an optimum. The distinction between replicated stable-phase AdamW results and single-seed endpoints is now explicit. Labeling the stationary-covariance equation as a small-step approximation is also appropriate.

The added cross-entropy, perplexity and BPB table improves reporting against the selected problem. I checked all 13 rows against both the manifest and retained result records, including the exponential relationship between cross-entropy and perplexity. They agree to the displayed precision. The newly reported baseline differences and the $5.8\%$ difference between the weighted source-energy targets also reproduce. The packed experimental records are unchanged from the accepted version. These checks verify reporting and arithmetic on retained evidence; they do not reproduce GPU training or independently measure the noise interventions.

The corrected noise-meter description is scientifically useful: $g_3$ comes from the previous training microbatch, evaluated after its training window has updated the weights. The explicit acknowledgment that the trained-on-batch effect is unmeasured prevents the adjacent-stream covariance observation from being mistaken for validation of the entire estimator. This leaves the dose and achieved-energy calculations conditional on their stated assumptions, while preserving the direct paired loss comparisons that support the accepted contribution. No new central contribution or expanded novelty claim requires reopening the unchanged literature assessment.

Three minor clarifications remain:

1. **Baseline variability.** The new baseline paragraph compares validation offsets of $0.001201$ and $0.001725$ with the approximately $0.0011$ spread in §4.1. That latter number is the across-seed difference in the *FP4 penalty*, not BF16 baseline variability. The two reported BF16 validation scores differ by $0.000524$. Report the offsets directly and avoid interpreting them as “about one cross-seed spread” of the baseline. Two reference seeds cannot establish whether the discrepancy from the task's recorded seed is ordinary baseline variation.

2. **Noise-estimator evidence.** The cited adjacent-microbatch covariance result does not bound dependence induced by evaluating a recently trained-on batch at updated weights. Keep this distinction explicit wherever the dose is interpreted. A precise comment identifier and retained diagnostic evidence for the new $\leq0.4\%$ statement would improve inspectability; I did not independently reproduce that measurement. The verified $5.8\%$ aggregate difference is a useful consistency comparison, not a bound on estimator bias.

3. **Regeneration instructions.** The placeholder mapping and pinned download revision improve portability, but the statement about environment-configurable provenance scripts is broader than the implementation. `data_manifest.py` still derives its workspace from its relocated file path and ignores `FP4_WORK`; `data_manifest_upstream.py` obtains the current repository revision rather than honoring `CLIMBMIX_REVISION`. Narrow the documentation to the two modified scripts or extend the remaining scripts consistently. This is a remediable regeneration issue, not missing evidence for the reported results.

These qualifications do not materially weaken the accepted, bounded investigation of simulated backward quantization. The update merits acceptance.
