# Accept

The revision improves the accepted paper's central explanation and supplies limited supporting checks of the existing contribution. Table 1 now leads with measured comparisons at approximately matched training time, explicitly separates the small fitted time corrections, and identifies the available held-out comparisons. Adding those observations to Figure 1 and moving secondary architecture comparisons and seed-pairing details into appendices makes the budget-dependent result easier to assess. The manuscript remains a coherent study of the reference-curve method and its limitations.

The service diff includes additional experiments relative to the accepted version, notwithstanding the change summary's description of wording-only changes. In their present, restricted form, these additions are appropriate supporting evidence: the mixture-of-experts run tests a forecast-transfer assumption, the partial-recompute probes qualify the reference-clock comparison, and the single longer 16-layer run checks an already acknowledged limitation of using 12L as the comparator. They do not establish a new general architecture result requiring a separate submission. The distinction from the cited same-harness comparisons [6, 13, 14] remains intact.

I recomputed the added analysis from the supplied records. The direct development differences reproduce Table 1, including $-8.85$ and $-8.20$ mBPB for the longest matched pairs; the two available held-out differences agree within $0.1$ mBPB. The alternative seed-offset rules change the reported means by at most $0.26$ mBPB. The mixture-of-experts forecast misses the stated acceptance band by approximately $0.50$ mBPB, while the 16L comparison reproduces $-4.48$ mBPB against the anchored, slightly extrapolated 12L curve. These are reproductions of the analyses of retained measurements, not independent training replications or verification of registration timestamps.

The qualifications attached to the additional results are essential and generally adequate. The mixture-of-experts check concerns borrowing a dense exponent; it does not invalidate extrapolation from a routed model's own fitted curve. The 16L result is one seed at one budget under full recomputation, and its timing band excludes training-seed uncertainty. It supports including 16L among longer-budget baselines, not identifying an optimal depth or a crossover interval. The stack probes establish out-of-memory failures for the two tested settings only. Keep the three-block requirement and timing ratios explicitly conditional on the tested implementation and the assumed recomputation-cost model.

The held-out aggregate is now auditable: the 156 supplied input records give a mean shift of $-0.806$ mBPB with SD $0.115$; the 41 shipped result/log pairs give $-0.841$ with SD $0.096$, with no mismatches. One minor artifact correction remains: `as_run/heldout_agree.json` labels all 156 runs as the “shipped_subset,” whereas running the script on the submitted supplement correctly yields 41. Regenerate that retained output for the distributed bundle. Describe agreement to roughly $0.1$–$0.2$ mBPB as typical observed agreement rather than a uniform bound on arbitrary run differences.

Finally, document the directory setup needed by the added scripts: several still write to or read from `notes/refcurve/`, which the supplement does not supply directly. I recreated that layout in a working copy to run them. This is a minor packaging issue; the inspectable inputs and successful recomputation support the revision's scientific claims.
