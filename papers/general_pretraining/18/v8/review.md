# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution, corrects the single-factor ablation comparisons, and adds useful exploratory evidence about how hub allocation interacts with value pooling. The explicit distinction between sample standard deviation and variance, and the qualification that two seeds do not establish a population ordering, improve the interpretation. Removing the incorrect reference [15] and updating [13]–[14] improve attribution; the discussion appropriately avoids ranking their results across different experimental settings.

The six new arms have inspectable implementations and retained endpoints. Recalculation confirms the reported two-seed means and sample standard deviations to the displayed precision. For example, the pooled-value `co_hub3_mlp` endpoints give $2.13654\pm0.00446$ BPB, while `sep_hub4` gives $2.13670\pm0.00067$. Source inspection supports the stated mechanism: the new estimator perturbs the MLP writer during hub suffix passes, pools its covariance statistics across writer and hub passes, and caches the hub estimate for subsequent use. The configured allocations retain 64 direct draws. These checks concern source and retained numerical evidence; I did not reproduce GPU training, timings, or memory measurements.

The following corrections and qualifications are warranted, but do not overturn the accepted scientific contribution:

- **Correct the replacement historical baseline in Section 4.1.** The newly quoted $2.28862\pm0.00185$ BPB for `delayed_trace_cowm_P64_fast_d1` does not match its cited record. The retained seed endpoints are $2.309428$ and $2.299822$, giving $2.304625\pm0.006792$ BPB, consistent with the existing Table 9. Thus the citation repair is appropriate, but its replacement number needs correction. The direction of the comparison is unaffected.

- **Describe the new experiments as a partial factorial screen, and temper “shows why.”** Raising hub allocation also reduces writer allocation. Moreover, the `sep_hub4` writer allocation is `[9,8,6,5,5,4]`, whereas `co_hub4_mlp` uses `[10,8,6,5,4,4]`; their difference does not isolate co-perturbation. The separate-hub, non-pooling controls at hub3/hub4 also lack the second-seed coverage of the six added arms. The results show smaller observed two-seed spreads for the tested pooling packages, not an established causal explanation or general variance reduction. At hub3, co-perturbation improves the pooling endpoint on seed 42 but slightly worsens it on seed 137. The poor non-pooling `co_hub4_mlp` result, which is commendably retained, further limits generalization. State these boundaries alongside the favorable sequence of seed-42 results.

- **Retain the evidence underlying the added resource columns.** The new per-seed records contain development BPB, holdout BPB and mean step time, but omit the peak-memory measurements printed in Table 10, configuration snapshots, learning curves and operation-accounting records. The runner already returns richer records; preserving them would make the additions more auditable. Until such records are supplied, the new memory values are not independently supported by the retained endpoints.

- **Qualify the new provenance explanation.** Recording both seed-42 $\gamma=1$ executions is useful. However, the added provenance note attributes their $0.002502$ BPB difference specifically to nondeterministic atomic accumulation in Triton/FlashAttention without a retained diagnostic establishing that cause. Present this as a possible explanation, or supply a controlled repeat demonstrating it. Distinguish execution variability at a fixed seed from variability across training seeds.
