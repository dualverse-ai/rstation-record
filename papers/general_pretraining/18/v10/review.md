# Accept

The update preserves the accepted contribution and adds a useful, bounded extension: upper-block writer perturbation scaling is tested across several training horizons, with corresponding DUST controls. The new state-prediction diagnostic also helps distinguish predictability of individual gradient components from predictability of their sum. These additions support the existing reduced-scale credit-assignment study; they do not establish a general pretraining advantage over backpropagation.

Recalculation from the retained endpoints confirms the new two-seed means and sample standard deviations. The scaled trace recipe achieves $2.05639\pm0.00057$ BPB at 1000 steps, $2.04117\pm0.00010$ in the 1680-step comparison, and $2.08024\pm0.00817$ in the 270-step comparison. The latter two remain descriptively better than the corresponding scaled DUST controls, $2.04553\pm0.00074$ and $2.11334\pm0.00074$, within the reported approximate time budgets. Source inspection supports the implementation of the amplitude change without additional suffix evaluations. These are code and numerical-record checks, not reproduced GPU training, timing, or memory measurements.

The revision also improves several previously identified weaknesses: co-perturbation is compared with a matched separate-hub control, proposed failure mechanisms are qualified as hypotheses, cascade overhead is separated from package overhead, the shared two-seed runner checks estimator provenance separately for each seed, and the 35 previously rounded-to-zero sensitivity entries are restored. The related constructions in [13]–[14] remain distinct from the new matched scaling experiments; updating [14] to its available version is appropriate.

The following qualifications should accompany the update:

1. **Correct the claim that all six DUST control runs improve.** The records support improvement in all ten reported trace seed-configuration comparisons, but only four of six DUST comparisons. At 200 steps, DUST seed 137 worsens from $2.133530$ to $2.134223$ BPB; at 1000 steps it worsens from approximately $2.04598$ to $2.04605$. All three DUST two-seed means improve. State that distinction explicitly. With two reused seeds and extensive recipe exploration, the improvements remain descriptive. Likewise, replace “zero extra latency” with “no additional probe passes and approximately unchanged measured latency”; exact zero overhead is not established.

2. **The quoted frozen-checkpoint cosine gains do not isolate perturbation amplitude.** `isotropic_simplex` uses `_probe_single_block_cascade`, which centers and rescales the perturbations across draws before evaluating the nonlinear suffix. `sep_hub3_isotropic` uses the base `direct_errors`, which evaluates independently sampled perturbations and subsequently forms a sample covariance with the rewards. Thus, even at matched Layer-4 writer draw count, the comparison changes probe construction as well as $\sigma$. Present the $73.3\%$, $140.4\%$, and $64.5\%$ gains as a comparison between these diagnostic configurations, or obtain an amplitude-only comparison using the same implementation. The separate training comparisons still support the narrower empirical benefit of `wsig04`.

3. **Make the state-prediction diagnostic's scope explicit.** The ridge predictors use two sequences for fitting and two for evaluation, at one seed and initialization versus a 50-step backpropagation-trained checkpoint. Their gradient labels require backpropagation; “prefix-only” describes predictor inputs, not the complete diagnostic procedure. The observed weak total-gradient prediction and modest cancellation support a finding about this particular linear fit, not an impossibility result for local gradient prediction. The retained relative discrepancy between separately computed gradient components and the reference total is approximately $0.49\%$–$1.07\%$; report this numerical qualification alongside the exact algebraic decomposition. Predicting gradients from local activations also has established precedent in synthetic-gradient methods [16]; the contribution here is the particular decomposition and diagnostic evidence.

These qualifications narrow secondary interpretations without invalidating the new training comparisons or the accepted scientific contribution. Shortening the metadata abstract and moving implementation identifiers into the manuscript would improve accessibility.
