# Accept

This update addresses the three qualifications from the accepted version. The new matched $(q,k)=(1,4)$ controls separate the allocation change from the value-trace change: the retained holdout values give $2.16374-2.14681=0.01693$ BPB for the allocation component and $2.16374-2.12965=0.03409$ BPB overall. The new files also restore the previously missing `run_neuronal_scaling_pretrain.py` dependency, add the advertised CLI aliases, and make clear that the wall-clock winners are combined package comparisons whose `Complete s` excludes evaluation and checkpoint-writing overhead. The central result therefore remains a useful, bounded extension of the accepted strict no-backward study.

I inspected the changed source without importing or running the GPU trainers, parsed all three changed Python files successfully, and independently checked the BPB decomposition. The source implements the stated one-pass recency rescaling and records zero reverse or local-reverse calls for the strict controls. These checks do not reproduce GPU training, the FlashAttention timings, or the reported held-out trajectories.

The new mechanistic interpretation needs tighter wording in a future correction. The $R=16$ variance and effective-rank measurements come from one 50-step, single-seed state and are diagnostic associations. They do not establish that the residual $+0.01716$ BPB is caused by rank compression or quantify its contribution to the 200-step endpoint. The text should say that the measurements are consistent with that hypothesis. The claimed ordering of relative variances is also not uniform in the retained record: for layer 0, `vtrace_pool_g100` is $14.3095$ versus $14.3087$ for $\gamma=0.98$ (essentially tied), while the clear reductions occur in the other layers.

There is an evidence-record inconsistency that should be repaired. The manuscript reports single-trial cosines $(0.0587,0.0376,0.0726)$ and $(0.0389,0.0244,0.0505)$, whereas `recency_vtrace_and_subspace_diagnostic.json` records $(0.056872,0.047611,0.063110)$ and $(0.039419,0.030410,0.049562)$. Its relative-variance and effective-rank values likewise differ from the printed values. These discrepancies do not reverse the qualitative comparison, but the manuscript and retained artifact must be regenerated from the same metric definition before treating the diagnostic as reproducible evidence.

The new reproduction script has a related bookkeeping flaw: when the matched $\gamma=1$ run is absent, `test_recency_vtrace_and_subspace_writers.py` inserts the hard-coded result (`2.14681` holdout BPB and timing) and then skips executing that run. A fresh `--run-pretrain` invocation therefore cannot regenerate this arm. The result can remain as retained evidence for this update, but the script should either execute the arm or clearly label the inserted values as an external record and provide its raw provenance.

The width sweep remains single-seed and the headline package comparisons use two seeds and several coupled changes; they support descriptive performance and efficiency under this recipe, not a general scaling law or superiority over backpropagation. The manuscript correctly reports checkpointed backpropagation as substantially better. Finally, reference [14] should point to the published `general_pretraining_20/v2` snapshot (or explicitly explain why the concurrent v1 is being cited) so the attribution and distinction from its independently derived attention-filtered trace are version-accurate.
