# Accept

The revision preserves the accepted contribution and makes a substantive correction to the exploratory co-perturbation results. The training runner now patches the estimator binding actually used by the suite and checks the returned estimator class. The replacement results appropriately withdraw the earlier favorable co-perturbation interpretation. The historical baseline is corrected to $2.30462\pm0.00679$ BPB, and the distinction between fixed-seed execution variability and training-seed variability is clearer.

Recalculation from the retained endpoints confirms the reported means and sample standard deviations for all ten arms in the revised allocation and highway-cascade screens. For example, without value pooling, separate-hub `hub3` achieves $2.13414\pm0.00216$ BPB, co-perturbation gives $2.14574\pm0.00137$, and the two cascade variants give $2.14819\pm0.00060$ and $2.14990\pm0.00421$. The allocations support 64 direct draws and the stated 58.5 equivalent-forward accounting. The retained diagnostic values also support the narrower observation that improved frozen-checkpoint cosine need not translate into improved training endpoints. These are source and numerical-record checks; I did not reproduce GPU training, timing, or memory measurements.

The new negative control is relevant to the accepted credit-assignment study. Its explicit acknowledgment of same-step reverse-depth dependencies prevents zero derivative calls from being mistaken for removal of backward locking. Guided ES [15] appropriately receives credit for surrogate-guided exploration; the added contribution is the particular Transformer diagnostic and training comparison. The related rStation studies [13]–[14] concern different delayed-credit constructions and do not supply matched controls for this experiment.

The following qualifications and reproducibility improvements remain useful:

- **Keep the proposed failure mechanism provisional.** Section 2.6 attributes degradation to persistent rank-one bias, hub perturbation variance and pooled reward centering. The endpoints establish the observed performance of these packages, but do not isolate those causes. In particular, the Layer-4 frozen diagnostic compares different hub allocations and writer perturbation scales: the `hub2` diagnostic uses writer $\sigma=0.4$, while the `co_hub3_mlp` diagnostic uses $\sigma=0.2$. Describe the explanation as a hypothesis. Likewise, “strictly unbiased isotropic simplex probing” should identify its conditional or linearized target; isotropic covariance alone does not establish unbiasedness for the nonlinear, delayed training gradient. The negative conclusion should remain limited to the tested configurations and horizons.

- **Make cache validation specific to each seed.** In `_run_2seed_arms_suite`, the class check reads arm-level `step_accounting`. When both cached seeds are stale, refreshing `s42` updates that shared class field, allowing stale `s137` to pass the next skip check. Store and validate provenance separately for each seed, or invalidate the whole arm before either rerun. This does not demonstrate that the submitted endpoints are stale, but the current guard cannot guarantee their provenance when upgrading an existing results file. Retaining each run's full configuration and history would further strengthen auditability.

- **Separate package overhead from cascade overhead.** The quoted approximately $29.7$ ms/step compares the MLP-pooled cascade with separate-hub `hub3`, changing both co-perturbation and scheduling. Against the matching non-cascade `co_hub3_mlp` control, the observed two-seed mean increment is approximately $25.8$ ms/step. Label the former as total package overhead.

- **Preserve small diagnostic values in retained evidence.** The newly applied six-decimal rounding converts 35 nonzero site-energy entries in `pure_forward_width_diagnostic.json` to zero, including values of order $10^{-7}$. Preserve these in scientific notation or at full precision. This does not alter the revised training comparisons, but unnecessarily weakens the existing sensitivity audit.

These issues do not overturn the accepted contribution or the bounded empirical conclusions of this update. The two-seed spreads remain descriptive, and the paper appropriately retains the superiority of checkpointed backpropagation in its tested setting.
