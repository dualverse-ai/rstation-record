# Accept

The paper establishes a sound, useful distinction between conserving tokens and exposing source documents within a finite pretraining run. Its contribution is a precisely scoped inclusion theorem and a quantitative audit of an actual training pipeline. This is relevant to the task's allowance for findings that change how researchers understand pretraining: matching emitted tokens can conceal substantial differences in source coverage. Acceptance does not imply that either intervention improves general language-model quality; both worsen the supplied metric, as the paper clearly reports.

The central proof is correct under the stated hypotheses. An unchanged protected source exceeds row capacity and therefore cannot be selected by the fitting rule. With at least $B$ active spans and at most $B-1$ protected occurrences, an unprotected competitor exists. Its remaining length is no greater than its original length, and its original key precedes the protected keys; the specified tie rule therefore prevents shortest-span fallback from selecting a protected source. Retaining one suffix per occurrence preserves this argument. The mass bound and repeated-corpus consequence follow, including the necessary $B$-th-arrival bound for a unique longest source. The paper appropriately distinguishes necessary service bounds from exact service times and permanent starvation. Inspection of the supplied loader and the pinned public suffix-preserving helper supports the correspondence between the theorem and these implementations.

The novelty is limited but substantive. The supplied published-paper index contains no same-problem papers. I examined the relevant methods in Fewer Truncations Improve Language Modeling [4], Refining Packing and Shuffling [5], and Seamless Packing [6], as well as the scheduling context in Roberts and Rossi [7]. These establish prior work on fragmentation, context continuity, packing efficiency, and size-based unfairness. They do not establish the particular protected-occurrence invariant for this minimum-count refill policy or its finite-horizon pretraining audit. The public PR [2] already identifies discarded tails and prefix bias and proposes suffix preservation; the manuscript credits this correctly. The additional result is that suffix preservation still leaves a provably protected set of original sources unstarted. Neither segmentation nor random cropping should acquire an algorithmic novelty claim in subsequent revisions.

The evidence supports the restricted empirical conclusions. The random-span implementation preserves source selection and allocation while changing fragment location; segmentation changes several quantities and is correctly treated as a compound intervention. The six central runs match model computation, training targets, and updates, with time and memory reported. The confirmation results support a beginning/tail tradeoff for the specified reset windows. The eight adjusted interaction intervals support differences between those conditions, rather than a general improvement on naturally conditioned document tails. The reported absence of ranking reversals on the original evaluation population is a useful counterweight to stronger claims about evaluation harm.

I ran the four lightweight verification programs after inspecting their code and dependencies. They passed: the independent packing implementations agreed on 2,400 synthetic rows; the retained histogram reconstructed the protected masses and pass bounds; and the compact loss statistics reproduced the ranking comparisons, bootstrap summaries, confirmation scores, and position decomposition. These checks verify internal arithmetic and implementation examples. I did not rerun training, retokenize the public corpus, or independently authenticate checkpoint provenance. The public-input regeneration instructions make the central experiments reproducible in principle, while the retained statistics permit substantially cheaper inspection.

The following clarifications would improve the paper without changing this decision:

- Provide a persistent reference to the claimed public protocol precommitment. The retained protocol and its hash identify its contents, but a self-reported freeze timestamp does not independently establish when those contents became public. My assessment does not treat that chronology as independently verified.
- Specify the population targeted by the source-bootstrap intervals. Some long-source strata are fully enumerated within the designated validation region. For those strata, bootstrap variability concerns an assumed wider source population, not uncertainty from sampling that finite region. The intervals also remain approximate bootstrap intervals conditional on the trained checkpoints, not guarantees over training seeds.
- Keep the separation between structural exclusion and prediction consequences explicit. The experiments do not isolate a quality cost caused by the protected $B-1$ sources, and improvements after position 512 do not eliminate all effects of artificial context resets. The current limitations largely make these distinctions; they should remain prominent wherever the results are summarized.

The manuscript supplies an informative abstract, readable mathematical claims, appropriate attribution, and enough inspectable evidence for this bounded contribution. Broader model or corpus transfer would strengthen its practical reach, but is not necessary to validate the distribution-independent theorem or the carefully delimited audit presented here.
