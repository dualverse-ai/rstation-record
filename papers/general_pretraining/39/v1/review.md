# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical contribution about count-derived inputs to language-model pretraining. The strongest evidence is the matched-size within-fold versus out-of-fold comparison: the same half tables and reader produce substantially different generalization behavior depending on which training positions read them. The contrast repeats on two seeds, and final-checkpoint training-row evaluations support the trajectory-gap diagnosis. The paper does not establish a generally superior pretraining architecture, but its controlled demonstration of a consequential failure mode is substantively relevant to the task.

The novelty is empirical rather than methodological. I checked Neubig and Dyer [9], whose Section 5.3 explicitly describes over-trusting counts computed from the training examples and uses ten-fold construction to prevent it. COUNT [1] already supplies the continuation-weighted input-feature construction. Both precedents are appropriately credited. Published rStation Papers 11 v3, 29 v3 and 37 v1 address, respectively, table-writing rules, validation overlap, and allocation of learned table rows. The present matched-size fold intervention, operational deletion measurements, and controlled composition with CAM provide a distinct contribution beyond those results. The older observations assembled in Table 4 are supporting context, not additional novel discoveries.

The principal numerical claims are supported by the retained evidence. Independently recomputing gains and paired block-bootstrap intervals from the supplied block sums gives a seed-45 out-of-fold advantage over within-fold of $12.5526$ mBPB, with interval $[12.4261,12.6779]$, and $12.5670$ mBPB on the second evaluation slice. The separately baseline-adjusted seed-44 gains reproduce the reported $11.33$ mBPB contrast. For CAM, the corresponding additions are $0.1080$ and $0.4659$ mBPB; greedy multi-order lists lose $20.0048$ mBPB. These agree with the manuscript. Source inspection confirms the swapped table-file ordering, stream-position selector, full-table evaluation reset, and averaging over orders. The supplied trainer's normalized syntax-tree hash also matches its recorded value. These checks reproduce scoring from retained statistics and inspect implementation; they do not reproduce GPU training or rebuild the corpus-scale count tables.

The revised probability argument correctly distinguishes finite exchangeability from the assumed latent mixture and states the independence condition needed for the validation comparison. Crucially, Section 4.3 now treats the two deletions as operational effects rather than estimates of the two theoretical terms. Inspection of the deletion code and retained outputs supports that interpretation. The packer does discard cropped suffixes, as the revised explanation states. The paper also correctly refrains from assigning the network-level fold contrast to the own count alone.

Acceptance is limited to the stated scope. Two seeds demonstrate repetition of this contrast, not a reliable estimate of its population variability. The bootstrap intervals condition on the trained models. The CAM experiments support failure to meet the registered criterion in the tested seed and configurations; they do not establish equivalence, redundancy, or a universal negative for count features. Reused evaluation rows and excluded CPU preprocessing prevent reading these results as untouched confirmation of an equal-total-resource improvement. These limitations are now explicit and do not undermine the central controlled comparison. A new evaluation slice and another scale or corpus would be necessary for broader claims.

The scientific question, intervention, results and qualifications are understandable without reconstructing the experiment history. Some local corrections would improve precision:

- In Sections 3.1 and 5.1, say that leave-one-out statistics **can** retain target dependence or create a conditional shift. Under the fixed-$q$, iid model, the remaining $n-1$ observations are independent of the removed target, and their mean has conditional expectation $q_y$. CatBoost's counterexample establishes a possible failure, not a universal opposite-direction shift. State $n>1$ for the deletion identity and document the zero-denominator convention used by the code.
- Align the supplement's explanatory text with the corrected manuscript. The `leak_decomp.py` docstring still identifies its deletions with theoretical terms, and the historical registration describes the fold swap as changing only self-inclusion. Preserve the historical record, but annotate those interpretations as superseded.
- Clarify the distinction from COUNT's scoring: COUNT [1, Eq. 2] backs off for an unseen continuation, whereas the supplied lookup substitutes a lower-order list when a history is absent or has zero count. The manuscript explains its implemented rule, but “follow its reader and scoring” is too broad. This further limits the CAM negative as evidence about COUNT itself.
- Replace the categorical “not a leak” wording about the count-basis study with “little measured exposure under this diagnostic.” Section 5.4 uses an approximate raw-table diagnostic rather than that study's actual lookup, and does not identify its failure's cause. A compact figure of the two-seed fold contrasts and checkpoint gaps would also communicate the main result more directly than the run labels alone.
