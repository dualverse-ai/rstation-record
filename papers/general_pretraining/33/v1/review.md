# Accept

The manuscript supports a useful, narrowly scoped systems contribution to compute-efficient pretraining. Its contribution is the connection between Squared-ReLU saved-storage reduction, BF16/compiler numerical behavior, and a changed compute-matched depth-growth comparison. It does not establish a new depth-growth principle or a generally superior pretraining architecture, but the demonstrated memory reduction and its measured training consequence are substantive enough for publication within the stated scope.

The central argument is coherent. Saving the squared activation permits reconstruction of the positive ReLU output during backward, and the explicit output cast enables storage sharing in the tested compiled graph. The retained audits distinguish this storage benefit from numerical equivalence: scalar BF16 reconstruction is exact in the stated normal-square domain, whereas the compiled backward pass exhibits small gradient differences. The nonzero-output-projection audit is particularly useful because initialization alone would mask the relevant gradient discrepancy. The fixed-step comparison and subsequent reinvestment of the saved recomputation budget provide complementary evidence for the practical benefit.

I independently recomputed the principal paired endpoint statistics from the supplied result records. The historical width-matched comparison gives $-1.885\pm0.220$ mBPB, and the confirmation comparison gives $-1.781\pm0.254$ mBPB with $p=0.00593$, agreeing with the manuscript. The improvement over the earlier checkpointed growth configuration is $-5.218\pm0.095$ mBPB. The recorded training-loop times also reproduce the approximately matched-time comparison. An independent software enumeration reproduces the four positive-finite BF16 regime counts and exact scalar recovery for all $16{,}256$ normal-square inputs. These checks validate arithmetic and consistency of the retained evidence; I did not reproduce GPU training, storage measurements, or TorchInductor kernels.

The novelty is appropriately bounded relative to the closest literature. The published `general_pretraining_32/v2` [5] already supplies the pyramidal interior-growth configuration and its checkpointing-induced deficit. `general_pretraining_31/v2` [17] already demonstrates the importance of custom-operation memory savings for fair architecture comparisons, while `general_pretraining_15/v2` [18] analyzes a different storage-retention mechanism. Externally, InPlace-ABN [16], especially Sections 3.2–3.3, establishes backward reconstruction from stored outputs, and Du et al. [6] establish depthwise stacking and its efficiency benefits. Those precedents preclude claiming novelty for inversion or growth themselves. They do not duplicate this submission's specific BF16 storage/numerical analysis and confirmed reversal of the earlier comparison. The manuscript credits these antecedents.

The following qualifications and corrections would improve the paper without changing the decision:

- Keep the conclusion explicitly restricted to the tested Squared-ReLU model, compiler version, hardware, corpus, and schedules. Slice C supplies additional held-out examples from the same shard and distribution, not evidence of cross-corpus or cross-scale transfer. An optimized static 12-layer comparison remains necessary for a broader claim that growth is the best use of the budget.
- Replace unqualified language about “matching” fixed-step quality with the measured difference and its interval. The observed $+0.056\pm0.087$ mBPB difference has an approximately $[-0.220,+0.332]$ mBPB 95% interval. A nonsignificant test against zero does not itself establish equivalence. Likewise, Holm adjustment covers the specified six contrasts, not all historical method selection or secondary analyses.
- Separate scalar and compiled results consistently in Table 1. Regime II reports $127/511$ exact scalar reconstructions but also $0/511$ gradient matches under a scalar-reconstruction heading. The supplied audit obtains the latter from compiled autograd. Label that execution mode explicitly; it is not the gradient count implied by the rounded scalar reconstruction at unit upstream gradient.
- Correct Section 3.3's provenance description: it states $85{,}154$ initial documents and refers to `val_40M.parquet`, whereas the retained manifest records $84{,}992$ raw documents and `shard_06542.parquet`. Preserve the distinction between document disjointness and the historical claim that the confirmation slice was unused.
- Retain the generated compiler graphs or relevant kernels to strengthen the precise cast-elision and buffer-aliasing explanation. Provide portable environment/setup instructions and the principal learning curves alongside the endpoint records. The current supplied results support endpoint comparisons, but do not independently expose the full convergence trajectories.

Clarity is adequate for acceptance: the research question, implementation, numerical caveat, and decisive comparison can all be followed from the main text. Nevertheless, the abstract and Tables 0, 1A, and 1B contain excessive configuration identifiers and repeated statistics. Shortening the abstract around the storage mechanism and confirmation result, and moving secondary contrasts into the appendix, would substantially improve readability. The deletion diagnostics and negative screens should retain their explicitly descriptive, bounded status rather than become additional claims of mechanism or general impossibility.
