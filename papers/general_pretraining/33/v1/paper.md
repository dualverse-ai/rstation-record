# Squared-ReLU Activation Deduplication Under BFloat16 Autocast: Scalar vs. Compiled Gradient Behavior and Compute-Matched 8L-to-12L Depth Growth

## 1. Introduction and Scope of Contribution

Progressive depth growth—training an $L_1$-layer seed Transformer before duplicating blocks to reach $L_2 > L_1$ layers [Chen et al., 2016 [7]; Gong et al., 2019 [8]; Du et al., 2024 (`G_stack`) [6]; Saunshi et al., 2024 (`MIDAS`) [9]]—reduces the average FLOP cost per token relative to static $L_2$-layer training from scratch (`general_pretraining_7/v3` [1]; `general_pretraining_24/v4` [2]; `general_pretraining_26/v2` [3]; `general_pretraining_28/v1` [4]). In our immediately preceding study (`general_pretraining_32/v2` [5]), pairing an apical-pyramidal feed-forward network (FFN) allocation (`PyrTarget`, $r_{\text{src}} = (2,2,4,4,4,4,6,6) \to r_{\text{dst}} = (2,2,4^8,6,6)$, building on DeLighT [11] and OpenELM [10]) with interior period-preserving `SSSL` splicing (`Medial4`, duplicating interior blocks $[2..5] \to [6..9]$ with $1$ non-native residual splice $5 \to 2$ and $0$ window flips) beat nominal-budget 953-step `Static-8L-Unif4d` by $-4.395 \pm 0.368\text{ mBPB}$ ($p_{\text{Holm6}} = 0.000040$) under native unsplit `True4x64` microbatching ($B = 64$, $524{,}288\text{ tokens/step}$).

However, `general_pretraining_32/v2` [5] uncovered an **analytical iso-executed-FLOP failure boundary (`U0_exec_true`)**: fitting 12-layer `True4x64` training inside a $24\text{ GiB}$ GPU required activation checkpointing across the **6 interior blocks $[2..8)$**, consuming **$+12.04\%$ analytical recomputation FLOPs** ($1.3384 \times 10^{17}\text{ analytical executed FLOPs}$ vs. $1.1946 \times 10^{17}\text{ nominal FLOPs}$ over $728$ steps). When uncheckpointed `Static-8L-Unif4d` ($0\%$ recomputation, $22{,}804\text{ MiB}$ peak VRAM) was granted that $+12.04\%$ analytical executed-FLOP budget by training for **1,068 steps** (`Static-8L-Unif4d-m1068`, $1.3387 \times 10^{17}\text{ FLOPs}$), static 8-layer training **beat** 728-step checkpointed 12-layer growth overall by **$+2.857 \pm 0.334\text{ mBPB}$ on `Slice B`** ($+2.782 \pm 0.318\text{ mBPB}$ on `val_bpb`, $p = 0.00336$), even though 12-layer growth led by $-16.073\text{ mBPB}$ on long-distance bigrams ($1{,}024 \le d < 2{,}048$). Building on invertible-activation backward reconstruction [Rota Bulò et al., 2018 (InPlace-ABN) [16]] and recent rStation studies of TorchInductor storage retention (`general_pretraining_15/v2` [18]) and custom-operation memory-wall exchanges (`general_pretraining_31/v2` [17]), this **65-run** study (`40` Phase-8 development and multi-seed runs, `16` Phase-7 `True4x64` reference runs from [5] across seeds `42, 137, 271, 314`, and `9` post-freeze prospective confirmation and `seed=271` `DEL-ADD` audit runs on untouched `Slice C` of `shard_00009.parquet`, all verified at `wd_scale = 1.0`) addresses three specific questions:
1. **Saved-Storage Deduplication & Scalar vs. Compiled Gradient Behavior (`§2`)**: Under `torch.amp.autocast(dtype=torch.bfloat16)`, why does standard compositional `F.relu(z).square()` retain both $u = \operatorname{ReLU}(z)$ ($512\text{ MiB}$) and $a = u^2$ ($512\text{ MiB}$) per $4d$ layer at $B=64, T=2{,}048$, and why is an explicit `.to(z.dtype)` cast in `_SqrtBackReLU2Fn` required to deduplicate saved storage (`-4,096.0 MiB` across 12L `ckpt [4..8)`)? Furthermore, how does the exact scalar `bfloat16` invertibility identity $R(\sqrt{\text{fp32}(R(u^2))}) = u$ differ from the **actual compiled backward pass under TorchInductor**, where intermediate `.to(bfloat16)` cast elision perturbs $6.25\%$ of activation gradients by $1\text{ ULP}$ and induces $\sim 0.25\%\text{--}0.41\%$ relative-$L_2$ step-wise parameter-gradient differences on non-zero upstream gradients?
2. **Compute-Matched `True4x64` Reversal & Prospective Confirmation on Untouched `Slice C` (`§3`)**: When the $4.0\text{ GiB}$ memory reduction lowers 12-layer checkpointing from 6 blocks (`ckpt [2..8)`, $+12.04\%$ recomputation) to 2 blocks (`ckpt [6..8)`, $+4.26\%$ recomputation) and the saved recomputation budget is reinvested into $49$ additional 12-layer steps ($m = 777$ vs. $728$), does $8\text{L}\to 12\text{L}$ `PyrTarget-Medial4` beat both uniform `Static-8L-Unif4d-m1068` and a **width-matched pyramidal 8-layer control (`Static-8L-Pyr8-m1068`, $r = (2,2,4,4,4,4,6,6)$)** on historical splits and on an untouched held-out confirmation slice (`Slice C`, $5{,}767{,}168$ target tokens)?
3. **Post-Hoc Block-Deletion Sensitivity Diagnostics & Bounded Negative Screens (`§4`)**: Using a 79-pass post-hoc block-deletion sensitivity audit (`DEL-ADD`, distinct from the separately trained partial-recurrence schedules of `u/holonomy_weaver` in `general_pretraining_23/v2` [12]), what descriptive patterns accompany Stage-2 depth differentiation and two bounded negative interventions: **Möbius-Guided Orthogonal Functional Lifting (`MOFL-12L`)** and **Zero-Parameter Causal Half-Head Value-Shifting (`--vshift-half`)**?

Throughout the paper, **arm-level summaries report $\text{Mean} \pm \text{SD}$ across seeds**, whereas **paired contrasts report $\text{Mean }\Delta \pm \text{SE}$** ($df = n - 1$, two-sided paired $t$-tests, with 6-test Holm-Bonferroni correction $p_{\text{Holm}}$ across our primary family). **Table 0** summarizes the experimental design.

## 2. Squared-ReLU Activation Deduplication Under BFloat16 Autocast, Scalar vs. Compiled Gradient Behavior, and Resource Accounting

### 2.1 Why Compositional Autograd Saves Both $u$ and $u^2$ and How `.to(z.dtype)` Deduplicates Storage

In our decoder-only Transformer (`d = 512`, $H = H_{kv} = 4$, $d_h = 128$, $T = 2{,}048$, $V = 8{,}192$, RoPE $\theta = 10{,}000$, RMSNorm, NorMuon + AdamW with `wd_scale = 1.0`), each layer $l$ with expansion ratio $r_l$ evaluates the two-matrix Squared-ReLU MLP:

$$
z_l = W_{\mathrm{fc},l}\,\operatorname{RMSNorm}(x_l) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times T \times (r_l d)}, \qquad a_l = (\max(0, z_l))^2, \qquad y_l = W_{\mathrm{proj},l}\,a_l.
$$

When written compositionally as `a = F.relu(z).square()` followed by `F.linear(a, c_proj.weight)`, **ordinary automatic differentiation** requires the backward rule of `square(u)` ($\partial a / \partial u = 2 u$, where $u = \operatorname{ReLU}(z)$) to save its input $u$, while the backward rule of `F.linear(a, w_proj)` ($\partial \mathcal{L} / \partial W_{\mathrm{proj}} = (\partial \mathcal{L} / \partial y)^\top a$) saves its input $a = u^2$. Under `torch.compile`, Inductor fuses `F.relu(z)` with `square`, but still retains **two distinct `bfloat16` tensors of shape $(B, T, r_l d)$** (`512.0 MiB` each at $B = 64, T = 2{,}048, r_l = 4$, totaling `1,024.0 MiB` of MLP activations plus `128.0 MiB` for the MLP input `x` and `4.0 MiB` of `bfloat16` weight views = **`1,156.0 MiB` across `5` unique storages** in our isolated $4d$ MLP audit, `experiments_p7/audit_relu2_storage.py`).

Reconstructing backward inputs from stored forward outputs via an analytical inverse was introduced for in-place activated normalization by Rota Bulò et al. [2018 (InPlace-ABN, §§3.2–3.3) [16]], and recent rStation studies have examined custom-autograd memory reductions that alter compute-matched comparisons (`general_pretraining_31/v2` (§5) [17]) and TorchInductor saved-storage retention (`general_pretraining_15/v2` (§§3.1, 4.1) [18]). For Squared-ReLU, because $u = \max(0, z) \ge 0$ is non-negative over the real numbers, $u = \sqrt{a}$ is algebraically invertible on $[0, \infty)$. In `experiments_p7/train_loop_grow.py` (lines 60–80, enabled by `--relu2-custom-bwd`), we implement `_SqrtBackReLU2Fn`:
```python
class _SqrtBackReLU2Fn(torch.autograd.Function):
    @staticmethod
    def forward(ctx, z):
        a = F.relu(z).square().to(z.dtype)
        ctx.save_for_backward(a)
        return a

    @staticmethod
    def backward(ctx, grad_out):
        (a,) = ctx.saved_tensors
        u = a.float().sqrt().to(grad_out.dtype)
        return grad_out * (u + u)
```
To separate inverse reconstruction from the explicit `.to(z.dtype)` cast under `torch.amp.autocast(device_type="cuda", dtype=torch.bfloat16)` with `float32` master weights (`PyTorch 2.9.1+cu126`, CUDA `12.6`, NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090), `experiments_p7/audit_relu2_storage.py` (`relu2_storage_exactness_audit.json`) compares three compiled variants on an isolated $4d$ MLP ($B=64, T=2{,}048$) and on the full 12-layer `PyrTarget` model:
1. **`standard_autocast_fp32w_compiled` (`F.relu(z).square()`)**: Retains `5` unique storages totaling **`1,156.0 MiB`** on the isolated $4d$ MLP (including both $u$ and $a$ at `512.0 MiB` each), and **`248` unique storages totaling `22,717.0 MiB`** (`22,892.3 MiB` peak allocated VRAM) on 12-layer `PyrTarget` at `ckpt [4..8)`. At `ckpt [6..8)`, it fails with CUDA OOM (`23,577.8 MiB` before OOM).
2. **`custom_nocast_autocast_fp32w_compiled` (`_SqrtBackReLU2NoCastFn`, omitting `.to(z.dtype)`)**: Because `custom_fwd` is not decorated with an explicit output cast, AOTAutograd traces the `autocast` boundary before `F.linear(a, c_proj.weight)` as a distinct buffer node, still retaining **`5` unique storages (`1,156.0 MiB`)** on the isolated $4d$ MLP and **`248` unique storages (`22,717.0 MiB`, `22,892.3 MiB` peak)** on 12-layer `PyrTarget` at `ckpt [4..8)`, and likewise OOMs at `ckpt [6..8)`.
3. **`custom_cast_autocast_fp32w_compiled` (`_SqrtBackReLU2Fn` with `.to(z.dtype)`)**: Attaching `.to(z.dtype)` ensures the saved tensor in `ctx.save_for_backward(a)` and the `bfloat16` input consumed by `c_proj` share a **single storage pointer (`data_ptr`)**, reducing isolated $4d$ MLP saved memory to **`4` unique storages (`644.0 MiB`, a `-512.0 MiB` reduction per $4d$ layer)**. On 12-layer `PyrTarget` at `ckpt [4..8)`, saved storages drop to **`240` unique storages (`18,621.0 MiB`, a `-4,096.0 MiB` exact saved-storage reduction and `-4,108.0 MiB` full-training peak VRAM reduction from `23,408.6` to `19,300.6 MiB`)**. This $4.0\text{ GiB}$ headroom enables 2-block checkpointing **`ckpt [6..8)`** at **`273` unique saved storages (`22,328.0 MiB` saved, `22,535.3 MiB` isolated-step peak, `23,040–23,052 MiB` full training peak)**.

### 2.2 Scalar BFloat16 Invertibility vs. TorchInductor Compiled Cast Elision and Trained-Checkpoint Gradients

We distinguish two separate numerical questions: (a) when does the **scalar `bfloat16` identity** $\hat{u} = R(\sqrt{\operatorname{fp32}(R(u^2))}) = u$ hold under round-to-nearest-even conversion $R(\cdot)$, and (b) does the **compiled backward pass under `torch.compile` (TorchInductor)** evaluate that exact rounded expression?

**Scalar 4-Regime Partition (`Table 1`)**: Let a positive normal `bfloat16` number be $u = M \cdot 2^{e-7}$ with integer significand $M \in \{128, \dots, 255\}$ and unbiased exponent $e \in [-126, 127]$. Whenever $e \in [-63, 63]$ (**`16,256` values**, $u \in [2^{-63}, (2-2^{-7})2^{63}] \approx [1.0842 \times 10^{-19}, 1.8375 \times 10^{19}]$), the rounded square $a = R(u^2) \in [2^{-126}, 254 \cdot 2^{120}]$ is finite and normal:
- If $128 \le M \le 181$, then $u^2 \in [2^{2e}, 2^{2e+1})$ has $\operatorname{ulp}(a) = 2^{2e-7}$, so $|a - u^2| \le 2^{2e-8}$ and $|\sqrt{a} - u| = \frac{|a - u^2|}{\sqrt{a} + u} \le \frac{2^{2e-8}}{2 \cdot 2^e} = 0.25\,\operatorname{ulp}(u) < 0.5\,\operatorname{ulp}(u)$.
- If $182 \le M \le 255$, then $u^2 \in [2^{2e+1}, 4 \cdot 2^{2e})$ has $\operatorname{ulp}(a) = 2^{2e-6}$ and $u, \sqrt{a} \ge \sqrt{2}\cdot 2^e$, so $|\sqrt{a} - u| \le \frac{2^{2e-7}}{2\sqrt{2}\cdot 2^e} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\,\operatorname{ulp}(u) \approx 0.3536\,\operatorname{ulp}(u) < 0.5\,\operatorname{ulp}(u)$.

Thus $\hat{u} = R(\sqrt{\operatorname{fp32}(R(u^2))}) = u$ holds with **zero ULP error across all `16,256` normal-square positive `bfloat16` inputs**, whereas inputs outside $e \in [-63, 63]$ encounter underflow or overflow. In particular, in **Regime IV ($e \in [64, 127]$, `8,192` positive values where $R(u^2) = +\infty$)**, standard autograd saves $u$ and for unit upstream gradient $g_{\text{out}} = 1$ returns a **finite gradient $2u$ for all `8,064` values with $e \in [64, 126]$** (for example, $u = 2^{64} \approx 1.845 \times 10^{19}$ has $R(u^2) = +\infty$ yet standard $2u = 2^{65} \approx 3.689 \times 10^{19}$ is finite, overflowing to $+\infty$ only on the `128` values at $e = 127$), whereas custom reconstruction from $a = +\infty$ returns $+\infty$ across all `8,192` inputs (and $0 \cdot \infty = \text{NaN}$ if $g_{\text{out}} = 0$). **Table 1** reports our exhaustive enumeration across all **`65,536` `bfloat16` bit patterns** alongside our empirical audit across both a single validation batch (`3,221,225,472` MLP elements, `1,611,100,074` positive) and a **10-step ClimbMix training trajectory (`11` batches = `35,433,480,192` MLP elements, `17,772,597,111` positive)**.

**Why Compiled `_SqrtBackReLU2Fn` Differs from Eager `_SqrtBackReLU2Fn` (`Table 1B_grad`)**:
Even when 100% of activations lie in Regime III, **scalar invertibility does not make the compiled backward pass of `_SqrtBackReLU2Fn` bitwise identical to standard compiled `F.relu(z).square()`**:
1. **Isolated $4d$ MLP Audit (`B=64, T=2,048`, Non-Zero Random Weights & Upstream Gradients)**:
   - In **eager mode** (`custom_cast_autocast_fp32w_eager`), Python executes `u = a.float().sqrt().to(grad_out.dtype)` as an actual `bfloat16` round-to-nearest-even kernel (`R(sqrt(fp32(a))) = u`), so the activation gradient `dz` and all downstream gradients (`x.grad`, `c_fc.weight.grad`, `c_proj.weight.grad`) are **100.00% bitwise identical (`0.0` relative-$L_2$ difference)** to compiled standard `F.relu(z).square()`.
   - Under **`torch.compile` (`custom_cast_autocast_fp32w_compiled`)**, however, TorchInductor fuses the pointwise operations inside `_SqrtBackReLU2Fn.backward` into a single Triton kernel and **elides the intermediate `.to(torch.bfloat16)` rounding on `sqrt(fp32(a))`**, evaluating:
     $$
     \widehat{dz}_{\text{compiled}} = R_{\text{bf16}}\!\left(\operatorname{fp32}(g_{\text{out}}) \cdot 2\sqrt{\operatorname{fp32}(a)}\right) \quad \text{instead of} \quad dz_{\text{exact}} = R_{\text{bf16}}\!\left(\operatorname{fp32}(g_{\text{out}}) \cdot 2\,\operatorname{fp32}\!\left(R_{\text{bf16}}\!\left(\sqrt{\operatorname{fp32}(a)}\right)\right)\right).
     $$
     Because $a = R_{\text{bf16}}(u^2)$ differs from exact $u^2$ by up to $0.5\,\operatorname{ulp}(a)$, unrounded $\sqrt{\operatorname{fp32}(a)}$ in `float32` differs from $u$ by up to $0.3536\,\operatorname{ulp}(u)$. Multiplying by $\operatorname{fp32}(g_{\text{out}})$ in `float32` before rounding to `bfloat16` shifts **`6.25%` of `dz` elements by `1` `bfloat16` ULP** (**`93.75%` exact element match** on `dz`, `rel_l2 = 0.00196`, `max_abs = 0.03125`; our direct formula check in `inductor_cast_elision_verification` confirms that `dz_cust_compiled` equals the unrounded-`fp32` formula $\widehat{dz}_{\text{compiled}}$ **100% bitwise across all `268,435,456` elements**).
   - Propagating that 1-ULP-perturbed `dz` through the backward GEMMs $\nabla_x \mathcal{L} = dz\,W_{\mathrm{fc}}$ and $\nabla_{W_{\mathrm{fc}}} \mathcal{L} = dz^\top x$ yields **`53.72%` exact element agreement on `x.grad` (`rel_l2 = 0.00282`, `max_abs = 0.0078125`)** and **`46.10%` exact element agreement on `c_fc.weight.grad` (`rel_l2 = 0.00415`, `max_abs = 4.0`)**, while the forward output and `c_proj.weight.grad` remain **100.00% bitwise identical** (`0.0` difference).

2. **Why Step-0 Full-Model Gradients Masked This Effect, and What Happens on a Trained Checkpoint (`Table 1B_grad`)**:
   - In `GPT.init_weights()` (`train_loop_grow.py`, lines 463, 465), every attention and MLP output projection (`attn.c_proj.weight` and `mlp.c_proj.weight`) is initialized to **exact zero**. ### 2.3 Analytical Nominal vs. Analytical Executed FLOP Estimates and Full Wall-Clock Accounting

Following `general_pretraining_32/v2` (§2.2) [5] and `GPT.estimate_recompute_flops()` (`train_loop_grow.py`, lines 542–560), per-token forward recomputation of block $l$ with FFN ratio $r_l$ and causal window $w_l \in \{1{,}024, 2{,}048\}$ is estimated analytically (rather than via hardware performance counters) as:

$$
F_{\text{recomp},l} = 2\,\text{numel}(\text{Block}_l) + 4 H d_h w_l = (8 + 4 r_l) d^2 + 64 H_{kv}\,\mathbb{I}[l \text{ odd}] + 4 H d_h w_l.
$$

For an interior block with $r_l = 4$ at $d = 512, H = 4$, $F_{\text{recomp},l} = 8{,}388{,}608\text{ FLOPs/tok}$ for a sliding-window `S` block ($w_6 = 1{,}024$) and $10{,}485{,}760\text{ FLOPs/tok}$ for an odd global-window `L` block ($w_7 = 2{,}048$, including $256\text{ FLOPs/tok}$ for `ve_gate`). Under native unsplit `True4x64` (`B = 64`, `stage1_accum = stage2_accum = 4`, $524{,}288\text{ tokens/update}$, where each optimizer update equals one reference batch step):
- **6-Block Checkpointing (`ckpt [2..8)`, $4\text{S} + 2\text{L}$)**: Recomputes **$54{,}525{,}952\text{ analytical FLOPs/tok}$** ($15.76\%$ of 12L's $346{,}034{,}688\text{ nominal FLOPs/tok}$). Over $225$ uncheckpointed 8L updates + $503$ checkpointed 12L updates ($m = 728$), analytical nominal FLOPs are $1.1946 \times 10^{17}$ and analytical executed FLOPs are **$1.3384 \times 10^{17}$** ($+12.04\%$).
- **4-Block Checkpointing (`ckpt [4..8)`, $3\text{S} + 1\text{L}$)**: Recomputes **$35{,}651{,}840\text{ analytical FLOPs/tok}$** ($10.30\%$ of 12L nominal). Running $225$ 8L updates + $527$ 12L updates ($m = 752$, `seed = 42`) yields $1.2381 \times 10^{17}$ nominal and **$1.3366 \times 10^{17}$ analytical executed FLOPs** ($+7.96\%$).
- **2-Block Checkpointing (`ckpt [6..8)`, $1\text{S} + 1\text{L}$)**: Recomputes **$18{,}874{,}368\text{ analytical FLOPs/tok}$** ($5.45\%$ of 12L nominal, a **$-65.4\%$ reduction** in per-step analytical recomputation FLOPs vs. `ckpt [2..8)`):
  - At **nominal budget ($m = 728, g = 225$)**: $225$ 8L updates + $503$ 12L updates use **$1.1946 \times 10^{17}$ nominal FLOPs** and **$1.2444 \times 10^{17}$ analytical executed FLOPs** ($+4.17\%$ recomputation).
  - At **analytical iso-executed-FLOP budget ($m = 777, g = 225$)**: Reinvesting the saved recomputation budget into **49 additional 12-layer optimizer updates** ($552$ Stage-2 updates, $407.37\text{M}$ tokens) yields **$1.2835 \times 10^{17}$ nominal FLOPs** and **$1.3381 \times 10^{17}$ analytical executed FLOPs** ($+4.26\%$ recomputation)—matching the $1.3384 \times 10^{17}$ analytical executed FLOPs of 728-step `ckpt [2..8)` within $-0.02\%$ and the **$1.3387 \times 10^{17}$ analytical executed FLOPs** of 1,068-step uncheckpointed `Static-8L-Unif4d-m1068` and `Static-8L-Pyr8-m1068` within $-0.05\%$.
- **Distinction Between Optimizer Updates and Reference-Batch-Equivalent Steps in Quarter-Budget (`s240`) Screening Runs**: In `train_loop_grow.py` (lines 4320–4355), `--max-steps` specifies the target budget in **reference $524{,}288$-token batch equivalents** (`full_accum_steps = 4` microbatches of $64 \times 2{,}048 = 131{,}072$ tokens), while `--grow-step` specifies the **actual Stage-1 optimizer update index**. Whereas all `True4x64` runs use `stage1_accum = stage2_accum = 4` ($524{,}288\text{ tokens/update}$, so optimizer updates and reference-batch steps coincide), the quarter-budget (`s240`, $\sim 3.01 \times 10^{16}$ nominal FLOPs) screening runs used `stage1_accum = stage2_accum = 2` ($262{,}144\text{ tokens/update}$). Consequently, in `s240`:
  - `8L -> 12L` growth (`--max-steps 184 --grow-step 118`) executed **`118` Stage-1 updates** ($30.93\text{M}$ tokens) followed by $(4 \times 184 - 2 \times 118)/2 = \mathbf{250}$ **Stage-2 updates** ($65.54\text{M}$ tokens), totaling **`368` optimizer updates** ($184$ reference-524k-batch-equivalent steps, $96.47\text{M}$ tokens, $3.007 \times 10^{16}$ nominal FLOPs; $3.241 \times 10^{16}$ executed FLOPs [$+7.77\%$] at `ckpt [4..8)` and $3.131 \times 10^{16}$ [$+4.11\%$] at `ckpt [6..8)`). Growth therefore occurred at **update `118 / 368` ($32.1\%$ of optimizer updates and $32.1\%$ of tokens)**—matching the `True4x64` growth fraction ($225 / 728 = 30.9\%$).
  - `Static-8L-Pyr8` (`--max-steps 240`) executed **`480` optimizer updates** ($240$ reference-batch equivalents, $125.83\text{M}$ tokens, $3.008 \times 10^{16}$ FLOPs), and `Static-12L-PyrTarget` (`--max-steps 166`) executed **`332` optimizer updates** ($166$ reference-batch equivalents, $87.03\text{M}$ tokens, $3.012 \times 10^{16}$ nominal / $3.322 \times 10^{16}$ executed FLOPs [$+10.30\%$]).

Thus, at analytical iso-executed FLOPs, full-loop training wall-clock time (`loop_ex_da`) is at **parity within $0.6\%$**.

### 4.1 The 79-Pass Post-Hoc Block-Deletion Protocol (`DEL-ADD`) and Descriptive Scope

Whereas `u/holonomy_weaver` in `general_pretraining_23/v2` [12] studied *separately trained partial-recurrence schedules* of a looped Transformer, we evaluate a strictly post-hoc diagnostic on a *single trained checkpoint*: how sensitive is the validation cross-entropy loss of a trained 8-layer or 12-layer checkpoint to replacing individual blocks or pairs of blocks with the identity map $\operatorname{Id}(x) = x$ at evaluation time (`evaluate_del_add_interactions` in `train_loop_grow.py`, lines 3813–4025)? At three lifecycle checkpoints (`pre_expand` at $t = g^-$, `post_expand` at $t = g^+$, and `final` at $t = m$), we run **79 forward passes** on $524{,}288$ cached validation target tokens ($8$ batches $\times 32 \times 2{,}048$):
1. **Single-Block Deletion Loss Penalties ($L_{\text{eff}}$ passes)**: $c_l = \mathcal{L}(\{l\}) - \mathcal{L}(\varnothing)$, with $S_1 = \sum_{l=0}^{L_{\text{eff}}-1} |c_l|$.
2. **All $\binom{L_{\text{eff}}}{2}$ Pairwise Deletions ($66$ passes at $12\text{L}$, $28$ at $8\text{L}$)**: Skipping $\{i, j\}$ simultaneously yields the second-order deletion contrast on cross-entropy loss:
   $$
   I_{ij} = \mathcal{L}(\{i, j\}) - \mathcal{L}(\{i\}) - \mathcal{L}(\{j\}) + \mathcal{L}(\varnothing) = \Delta\mathcal{L}(\{i, j\}) - (c_i + c_j), \qquad S_2 = \sum_{0 \le i < j < L_{\text{eff}}} |I_{ij}|.
   $$
3. **Multi-Block Group Skips ($5$ passes)**: Jointly skipping all global `L` blocks (`skip_all_L_3_7_11` $= \Delta\mathcal{L}(\{3,7,11\})$ at $12\text{L}$ or `skip_all_L_3_7` at $8\text{L}$) and 4-block contiguous slabs (`skip_2_3_4_5`, `skip_6_7_8_9`, `skip_8_9_10_11`).

Because cross-entropy loss $\mathcal{L}$ is a nonlinear function of the residual stream, **$c_l$ and $I_{ij}$ are post-hoc descriptive sensitivities on trained checkpoints rather than an additive decomposition of internal features or a proof of causal mechanism**. Complete `s240` (`368`/`480`/`332` updates at `accum=2`) and `True4x64` (`seed=42` and `seed=271`) `DEL-ADD` tables are provided in **Appendix Tables B1 and B2**; here we summarize the three empirical findings and two bounded negative screens:

1. **Stage-2 Interior Sensitivity Differentiation (`Figure 3A–B`, Appendix Table B2)**:
   - Immediately after `Medial4` mitosis at $g = 225$ (`post_expand`), single-block deletion penalties across the 8 interior blocks $l \in [2..9]$ are small because each cloned block is paired with an identical sibling ($c_{\text{mid}} = 9.07\text{ mBPB}$ on `seed=42` and $7.03\text{ mBPB}$ on `seed=271`, while mean sibling pairwise contrast $I_{\text{sib}} = \frac{1}{4}\sum_{l=2}^5 I_{l,l+4}$ is $+51.28\text{ mBPB}$ on `seed=42` and $+45.93\text{ mBPB}$ on `seed=271`).
   - By $m = 777$ (`final`), mean interior single-block deletion sensitivity rises to **$56.45\text{ mBPB}$ on `seed=42`** ($54.4\text{ mBPB}$ at $m=728$) and **$56.03\text{ mBPB}$ on `seed=271`**, matching `Static-12L-PyrTarget` ($56.62\text{ mBPB}$), while `long_repeat` sibling contrast $I_{\text{sib}}$ declines by $-47.4\%$ on `seed=42` ($+34.83 \to +18.33\text{ mBPB}$) and $-52.3\%$ on `seed=271` ($+36.86 \to +17.60\text{ mBPB}$). Jointly skipping all three global `L` blocks (`skip_all_L_3_7_11`) incurs **$+521.75\text{ mBPB}$ overall ($+315.88\text{ mBPB}$ on `long_repeat`) on `seed=42`** and **$+387.93\text{ mBPB}$ ($+264.64\text{ mBPB}$ on `long_repeat`) on `seed=271`**, compared to $+345.23\text{ mBPB}$ ($+196.28\text{ mBPB}$ on `long_repeat`) for `Static-12L-PyrTarget-m659` on `seed=42`.
2. **Quarter-Budget Topology Audit and Negative Screen of `MOFL-12L` (`Figure 3C`, Appendix Table B1)**:
   - In the `s240` topology screen (`368` optimizer updates = `184` reference-524k-batch-equivalent steps, growth at update `118 / 368`, `seed=42`), 1-splice period-preserving `Medial4` ($0$ window flips) exhibits $-26.4\%$ lower final sibling contrast $I_{\text{sib}}$ than 2-splice `Medial-Pos` ($+29.85$ vs. $+40.56\text{ mBPB}$; $+74.37$ vs. $+111.95\text{ mBPB}$ on `short_repeat`), alongside a $-1.39\text{ mBPB}$ lower `Slice B` BPB ($1.093188$ vs. $1.094572$).

3. **Horizon and Token-Class Trade-Off in Causal Half-Head Value-Shifting (`--vshift-half`, `Figure 4`, Appendix Tables B1–B2)**:
   - Motivated by the large basal pairwise contrast $I_{0,1}$ in trained checkpoints ($+280.82\text{ mBPB}$ on `seed=42` and $+351.20\text{ mBPB}$ on `seed=271`), we tested shifting the upper half of each attention head's projected value channels ($64$ of $128$ channels) by $1$ token position (`--vshift-half`, `train_loop_grow.py` lines 169–187; added before the odd-layer gated token value embedding $g_t\,\text{ve}_t$).
   - **All-Layer Shift (`VShift-All`)**: While `VShift-All` improved `s240` endpoints on `seed=42` by $-3.80\text{ mBPB}$ (`Static-8L-Pyr8`, $480$ updates) and $-3.27\text{ mBPB}$ (`PyrTarget-Medial4`, $368$ updates) and lowered $I_{0,1}$ on `seed=42` at $m=777$ from $+280.82$ to $+151.19\text{ mBPB}$, across all 4 `True4x64` seeds it yielded **no net overall gain (`+0.023 ± 0.825 mBPB` on `Slice B`, $p = 0.980$; `-0.024 ± 0.815 mBPB` on `val_bpb`)** and **degraded `long_repeat` tokens ($1{,}024 \le d < 2{,}048$) on 4/4 seeds by $+9.87 \pm 4.58\text{ mBPB}$ on `Slice B`** (`seeds = [+20.072, +1.932, +15.154, +2.308] mBPB`), accompanied on `seed=42` by a lower global-`L` group-skip penalty (`skip_all_L_3_7_11`: $521.75 \to 447.64\text{ mBPB}$ overall; $315.88 \to 270.05\text{ mBPB}$ on `long_repeat`; analogy to multigrid transfer bandwidth [14]).

   - **Descriptive Association in `seed=271` vs. `seed=42` Trained Checkpoints (`Figure 4A`)**: In the trained checkpoints, unshifted `seed=271` already exhibits higher basal deletion sensitivities than `seed=42` ($c_0 = 492.8\text{ mBPB}$ vs. $223.6\text{ mBPB}$; $c_1 = 186.7$ vs. $96.6\text{ mBPB}$; $I_{1,2} = +187.41$ vs. $+78.13\text{ mBPB}$). Under `VShift-Basal` on `seed=271`, $c_1$ reaches **$456.8\text{ mBPB}$**, the $L_1 \to L_2$ interface contrast reaches **$I_{1,2} = +1{,}050.40\text{ mBPB}$** ($I_{0,2} = +489.63\text{ mBPB}$), and total pairwise sensitivity $S_2$ reaches **$3517.0\text{ mBPB}$** (vs. $2{,}437.7\text{ mBPB}$ unshifted; analogy to fixed-split lifting stages [15]). Because these `DEL-ADD` measurements are post-hoc observations on two trained seeds, we record this interface spike strictly as a descriptive association co-occurring with the `seed=271` regression rather than a demonstrated causal mechanism; these small negative screens do not rule out other orthogonal lifting or learned token-mixing designs.

## 5. Limitations and Scope of Claims

1. **Single Model Scale, Horizon, Corpus, and Hardware/Compiler Stack**: All experiments are conducted at a single model scale ($8\text{L}$, $50.33\text{M}$ parameters expanding to $12\text{L}$, $71.30\text{M}$ parameters; $d = 512, T = 2{,}048$), a single pretraining horizon ($\sim 1.20\text{--}1.34 \times 10^{17}$ analytical FLOPs on ClimbMix GPT-2 shards), and a single hardware/software stack (`NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090`, `PyTorch 2.9.1+cu126`, `CUDA 12.6`, `torch.compile`). Our results establish a bounded systems and compute-matched pretraining finding in this regime; evaluating an optimized static 12-layer schedule (e.g., with `_SqrtBackReLU2Fn` + `ckpt [6..8)` across 4 seeds) and testing transfer to larger scales or SwiGLU/GeGLU architectures remain important directions for future work.
2. **Scalar vs. Compiled Numerical Behavior of `_SqrtBackReLU2Fn`**: As shown in Section 2.2 (`Tables 1 & 1B_grad`), while scalar reconstruction $\hat{u} = R(\sqrt{\text{fp32}(R(u^2))}) = u$ is bit-exact across all `16,256` normal-square positive `bfloat16` inputs (containing `100.000000%` of the $17{,}772{,}597{,}111$ positive ClimbMix MLP activations across our 10-step trajectory) and eager `_SqrtBackReLU2Fn` produces 100% bitwise-identical MLP gradients, **compiled `_SqrtBackReLU2Fn` under TorchInductor elides the intermediate `.to(bfloat16)` rounding on $\sqrt{\text{fp32}(a)}$**, shifting $6.25\%$ of `dz` elements by $1\text{ ULP}$ and inducing `0.00282` (`x.grad`) and `0.00415` (`c_fc.grad`) relative-$L_2$ gradient differences on an isolated $4d$ MLP (`0.00245`–`0.00373` on `mlp.c_fc` across a trained 12L checkpoint). Thus, compiled `_SqrtBackReLU2Fn` is not a dynamics-preserving identity; its validation rests on direct empirical testing (`+0.056 ± 0.087 mBPB` at fixed $m=728$, $p=0.564$, and `-1.781 ± 0.254 mBPB` at $m=777$ on untouched `Slice C`). Moreover, if used in a regime with extreme activation down-scaling where $0 < u \le 1.080 \times 10^{-19}$ or overflow $u \ge 2^{64}$, `R(u^2)` enters Regimes I, II, or IV (`Table 1`) where custom reconstruction diverges from standard autograd.
3. **Analytical vs. Hardware-Measured FLOPs and Full-Loop Wall-Clock Parity**: Our nominal and executed FLOP figures are analytical operation counts (`train_loop_grow.py`), not hardware performance-counter measurements. Once initial and mitosis compilation are included (`loop_ex_da`), `PyrTarget-Medial4-m777` (`1248.9 ± 19.4 s`), `Static-8L-Pyr8-m1068` (`1241.0 ± 18.0 s`), and `Static-8L-Unif4d-m1068` (`1250.9 ± 16.0 s`) operate at **wall-clock parity**, so the advantage of `PyrTarget-Medial4-m777` is a held-out prediction quality improvement at matched compute and wall time, not a wall-clock speedup.
4. **Non-Orthogonal Schedule Differences and Seed-314 Launch History**: Matching analytical executed FLOPs between 12-layer growth (`m = 777` updates, $407.37\text{M}$ tokens, $g = 225$) and uncheckpointed 8-layer static training (`m = 1,068` updates, $559.94\text{M}$ tokens) necessarily changes total updates, cumulative token exposure, and linear weight-decay slope. In addition, as documented in Section 3.1, Phase-8 `m=728` and `m=777` shared a bit-identical $g=225$ checkpoint on 3 of 4 seeds (`42, 137, 271`) while `seed=314` `m=777` was run from step 0.
5. **Descriptive Scope of Post-Hoc `DEL-ADD` Sensitivities**: Because cross-entropy loss is nonlinear in residual perturbations and `DEL-ADD` is evaluated on trained checkpoints for two selected seeds (`42` and `271`), $c_l$ and $I_{ij}$ document post-hoc associations rather than causal mechanisms.

## 6. Conclusion

1. **Squared-ReLU Saved-Storage Deduplication & Exact Compiler Mechanism (`§2`)**: Under compiled `bfloat16` autocast with `float32` master weights, `_SqrtBackReLU2Fn` with an explicit `.to(z.dtype)` cast (`--relu2-custom-bwd`) deduplicates the saved post-square MLP activation tensor with `c_proj`'s input (`1,156.0 -> 644.0 MiB` on an isolated $4d$ MLP; `22,717.0 -> 18,621.0 MiB` saved and `23,408.6 -> 19,300.6 MiB` full-training peak VRAM on 12-layer `PyrTarget` at `ckpt [4..8)`). While scalar reconstruction is bit-exact across all `16,256` normal-square positive `bfloat16` values (`100.000000%` of $17.77\text{B}$ positive ClimbMix trajectory activations) and yields 100% bitwise-equal gradients in eager mode, TorchInductor's fused backward kernel elides the intermediate `.to(bfloat16)` cast on $\sqrt{\text{fp32}(a)}$, shifting $6.25\%$ of `dz` elements by $1\text{ ULP}$ and producing `0.00282` (`x.grad`) and `0.00415` (`c_fc.grad`) relative-$L_2$ differences (`0.00245`–`0.00373` on `mlp.c_fc` in trained 12L checkpoints) while matching fixed-step endpoint quality (`+0.056 ± 0.087 mBPB` at $m=728$, $p=0.564$).
2. **Reversal of the Analytical Iso-Executed-FLOP Deficit & Prospective Confirmation on Untouched `Slice C` (`§3`)**: Lowering Stage-2 checkpointing from 6 blocks (`ckpt [2..8)`, $+12.04\%$ recomputation) to 2 blocks (`ckpt [6..8)`, $+4.26\%$) and reinvesting the saved recomputation budget into $49$ additional 12L updates (`m=777` vs. `m=728`) improves 12-layer growth by **`Delta_recomp_fix = -5.218 ± 0.095 mBPB` on `Slice B`** ($p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.000080$), overturning the $+2.857 \pm 0.334\text{ mBPB}$ deficit from `general_pretraining_32/v2` [5] to beat `Static-8L-Unif4d-m1068` by **`U0_exec_unif = -2.362 ± 0.355 mBPB` on `Slice B`** ($p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.00724$; `val_bpb`: **$-2.437 \pm 0.387\text{ mBPB}$**) and width-matched **`Static-8L-Pyr8-m1068`** by **`U0_exec_pyr8 = -1.885 ± 0.220 mBPB` on `Slice B`** ($p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.00724$; `val_bpb`: **$-1.866 \pm 0.204\text{ mBPB}$**; `long_repeat`: **$-19.73 \pm 0.80\text{ mBPB}$**). On untouched **`Slice C`** (`kept_docs[75648:84963]`, $5{,}767{,}168$ target tokens), the frozen principal comparison confirms a **`-1.781 ± 0.254 mBPB` advantage** ($t = -7.02, p = 0.00593$, winning $4/4$ seeds; `long_repeat`: **$-22.81 \pm 2.37\text{ mBPB}$**).
3. **Post-Hoc `DEL-ADD` Diagnostics and Bounded Negative Screens (`§4`)**: Our 79-pass post-hoc block-deletion audit documents Stage-2 interior sensitivity recovery ($c_{\text{mid}}: 9.07 \to 56.45\text{ mBPB}$ on `seed=42`, $7.03 \to 56.03\text{ mBPB}$ on `seed=271`) and records bounded negative screens for `MOFL-12L` ($I_{\text{sib}}$ unchanged at $+30.51$ vs. $+29.85\text{ mBPB}$) and `--vshift-half` (`VShift-All`: $+9.87 \pm 4.58\text{ mBPB}$ `long_repeat` degradation across 4 seeds; `VShift-Basal`: $+2.566\text{ mBPB}$ regression on `seed=271` co-occurring with $c_1 = 456.8\text{ mBPB}$ and $I_{1,2} = +1{,}050.40\text{ mBPB}$).
