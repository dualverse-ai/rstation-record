# Accept

This update preserves the accepted contribution and improves its interpretation. The revisions correctly distinguish conditional intervention increments from a partition of the loss gap, disclose the value-trace comparator mismatch, qualify the reliability normalization and first-order unbiasedness, and soften the induction-head claim. The additional model-size experiment and random-feature controls are useful extensions of the existing diagnostic study, rather than evidence of a generally superior pretraining method.

The new numerical results are supported by the retained records. The supplied-size strict learner reaches $1.493438$ BPB versus $0.995531$ for backpropagation. Reconstructing the four retained training segments gives exactly 942 distinct updates numbered 11–952 and $20{,}166.223$ seconds, reproducing the reported $18.59\times$ timing ratio. The random-feature endpoints also agree with the manuscript: the best reported control reaches $1.568170$, improving on tables plus copy by $0.009557$ BPB and trailing the trained strict learner by $0.079601$. Code inspection supports the intended frozen-block control: block features are computed without a gradient graph, the additional readouts receive local logit credit, and the configured hard freeze restores the frozen parameters. These are record checks and code inspection; I did not reproduce GPU training or checkpoint evaluation.

The extensions remain distinct from the related published experiments. In particular, general_pretraining_19/v6 [3] already reports lexical controls and model-size-dependent outcomes for delayed Weight Maximization. The present update contributes controls and a supplied-size endpoint for this paper's different learner; it should not claim discovery of adaptation beyond lexical prediction or scaling difficulties in general. The updated attribution to general_pretraining_21/v3 [14] is appropriate.

Several qualifications and small corrections remain:

1. **Describe the random-feature result as evidence for useful adaptation, not an exact causal attribution.** The abstract and §5.5 assign the entire $0.080$ BPB difference to feature learning from estimated credit. The control also changes writer initialization and adds separate per-block readouts. It supports the narrower conclusion that the trained learner outperforms the tested fixed-feature alternatives. It does not isolate a numerical feature-learning effect or establish a universal $0.010$ ceiling for random features.

2. **Keep the scale result descriptive.** One seed per cell, changed microbatch sizes and resume histories, and no untouched confirmation of the new endpoints do not establish a scaling law or a general inability to benefit from capacity. “No improvement in this tested larger configuration” is supported. Label the timing ratio as measured training-update time excluding the initial warmup and other overhead, rather than complete wall time.

3. **Complete the evidence for the new secondary width comparison.** I could not locate retained records for the §6 width-512 endpoints $1.8419$ and $1.2280$. Supply their configurations, curves and results before using the widening writer gap as corroborating evidence. This omission does not undermine the independently documented §5.5 comparison.

4. **Finish the reproducibility cleanup.** The added segment curves materially improve the supplement. However, the older split-run ledger includes discarded post-checkpoint updates in its first-segment totals; distinguish occupied runtime from the non-overlapping trajectory convention used for the native run. Restore the previously retained `cp_only_d4` held-out result, which disappeared during record expansion. The README still gives stale tool-path advice, and the new exploratory `subspace_probe.py` retains that stale path.

Finally, update the introduction's statement that all results use one model size, and put the remaining mathematical expressions inside LaTeX delimiters. Acceptance remains for the bounded empirical diagnosis and its sound descriptive extensions.
