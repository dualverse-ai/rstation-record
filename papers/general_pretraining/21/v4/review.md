# Accept

The update preserves the accepted highway-pooling and shared value-multiplexing contribution, makes the key estimator's bias–variance tradeoff explicit, and adds useful longer-horizon and context-transfer evidence. The revised “MMSE-inspired” description is appropriate: the filter changes the expected key update rather than establishing minimum-MSE optimality. The new results support a bounded empirical extension of the accepted work, with the qualifications below.

For fixed routing weights and hub targets, the weighted first-order expectation follows from perturbation covariance and Taylor expansion; the stated $O(\sigma^2)$ remainder additionally uses symmetric perturbations and sufficient smoothness. An independent implementation using analytic normalized-attention derivatives and all 64 three-token Rademacher configurations agreed with the weighted Jacobian sum to $3.85\times10^{-11}$ on a separate numerical example. This corroborates the identity, not the full training implementation. The newly retained training means and sample standard deviations agree with their individual seed records. I inspected the changed implementation and relevant portions of [16] v10; I did not reproduce GPU training or the corpus audit.

1. **Keep the incremental training conclusions descriptive and comparator-specific.** At 1000 steps, explicit `klev4` improves both seeds on both reported splits against the new $\gamma=1$ arm without key shrinkage. Its confirmation mean improves from $2.01628$ to $2.01001$; both confirmation seeds also improve over the accepted $\gamma=0.98$ recipe. This is useful evidence beyond the previously inconclusive 200-step comparison.

   The matched-time conclusion is weaker. Against the accepted 1280-step $\gamma=0.98$ recipe, the 1255-step one-draw arm changes confirmation BPB by $-0.004440$ and $+0.002460$, averaging approximately $-0.000990$. The 1235-step four-draw arm is slightly worse on average. Thus the one-draw arm beats the new four-draw arm at this budget, but a reliable efficiency gain over the accepted recipe is not established. At $T=2048$, the four-draw package improves mean reused-test BPB by only $0.00018$ over Banded-256, with seed 42 slightly worse ($2.017024$ versus $2.016957$). Its measured runtime and memory also exceed the dense $\gamma=0.98$ comparator. These comparisons concern two seeds and reused evaluation data, and changing both value discounting and key routing does not isolate the key filter's transfer effect. The statement that selective long-range retrieval heads form after step 200 needs a diagnostic or should be presented as a hypothesis.

2. **Correct the newly expanded attribution to [16].** Section 1.2 incorrectly describes [16] v10's main Wiener-filter learner as using explicit $W_O^\top u$ reverse contractions. That paper distinguishes its delayed zero-reverse-matrix-pullback learners from local-pullback diagnostic controls. Its six-matrix experiments include active queries and keys; Section 4.1's list omits these. Also retain the probe-count-dependent Wiener formula: the normalized $A^2$ expression quoted in Section 4.1 is only its $M=1$ case. The present softmax-leverage normalization, shared V-Mux implementation, and experimental setting remain concrete distinctions, without relying on an incorrect operational contrast.

3. **Describe cached key routing as a separate stochastic approximation.** Source inspection confirms that it avoids clean $QK^\top$/softmax reconstruction. However, clamping a noisy $\widehat A$ and applying nonlinear leverage normalization does not recover the clean-attention filter in expectation. The fixed-weight identity applies conditional on the cached routing matrix and independent key probes; Table 4D's clean-filter alignment numbers do not automatically characterize this variant. The weaker 1000-step cached result is appropriately retained. Also, the new cache assignment stores every layer's dense coupling tensor even for modes that do not consume it. Make caching conditional or distinguish historical resource measurements from those of the submitted implementation; zero score reconstructions does not imply zero storage overhead.

The previous review's local variance correction remains outstanding in Section 2.4(4). For fixed targets, shared slab codes require cross-destination covariance terms:

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\widehat u_{t,c}\mid T)
=\sum_{s,r}\gamma^{s+r-2t}T_{s,c}T_{r,c}
\operatorname{Cov}(\widehat A_{t,s},\widehat A_{t,r}),
$$

with both sums restricted to the retained window. The displayed sum of marginal variances is not generally the transported-credit variance. This does not overturn the conditional expectation result or the measured comparisons.

Finally, align the reproduction instructions with the supplied parser: `milestone12_verify` and `milestone12_vmux_cached` are not accepted modes; the cached-training mode is `milestone12_cached200`, and the verifier needs an actual documented invocation. Reconcile the experiment counts as well: Milestone 12 contains ten two-seed training arms, or twenty seed runs, rather than the change summary's eighteen. These reporting corrections do not require reopening the unchanged accepted contribution.
