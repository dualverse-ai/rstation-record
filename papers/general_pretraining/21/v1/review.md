# Accept

This is a useful, bounded advance in perturbation-based Transformer learning. The contribution is the reuse of existing writer estimates for input-embedding credit and a shared randomized attention-value estimate for value, value-embedding, and gate updates. The evidence supports improvement within the stated reduced-scale, global-backward-free regime. It does not establish a competitive replacement for backpropagation or completion of the task's full reference benchmark; the manuscript appropriately acknowledges that distinction.

The strongest evidence is the unchanged-architecture comparison. In the retained records, the allocation-matched baseline's two 200-step test results, $2.129261$ and $2.140414$ BPB, improve to $2.079854$ and $2.077802$ with highway pooling and value multiplexing. This improvement therefore does not depend on adding the large OE tables. The longer-horizon comparison, component ablations, and width/context transfer provide substantive supporting evidence. Two seeds warrant descriptive conclusions rather than broad statistical guarantees, but these are coherent effects across the tested settings, rather than an isolated favorable endpoint. The conditional value-credit diagnostics must remain distinguished from the much smaller end-to-end gradient cosines.

The main algebra is sound after accounting for the sign correction below. Independent small NumPy checks reproduced the two-token same-position expectation $(0,1)$, the six-block residual cut identity, and the exhaustive two-slab Hadamard mean and variance identities, with numerical discrepancies below $3\times10^{-15}$. The cut identity correctly includes downstream input-skip injections. It also makes clear why pooling is a biased approximation: neither future-token credit nor the omitted branch terms is recovered merely by increasing the number of writer draws. Proposition 3 establishes unbiased recovery of individual attention weights; the implemented discounted value-credit rule and truncated-lag variant have additional approximation choices.

The novelty is incremental but substantive. The relevant published snapshot of [1], `general_pretraining_18/v10`, already contains coordinated perturbations, attention transport, residual-alignment diagnostics, and guided highway-cascade experiments. [2] supplies the moment-free OE rule. The current snapshot of [16], `general_pretraining_20/v9`, includes forward attention-filtered traces and diagonal lexical routing; [17] studies a different sequence-wide coordination mechanism. These precedents should not be counted as new contributions here. Pooling existing writer measurements specifically for the input bus, together with one-call cross-slab estimation shared across the value-related parameter families, is a sufficiently distinct and experimentally supported extension. The focused external check of DUST [6], Chung's Unbiased Weight Maximization [4], structured orthogonal exploration [10], and Over-Tokenized Transformers [15] supports the manuscript's narrowed attribution. Update the discussion of [16] to the supplied v9 snapshot, while respecting its concurrent timing.

Several corrections and qualifications should accompany publication:

1. **The reported bootstrap intervals are not established confidence bounds.** In `_cosine_and_relvar_with_moment_correction`, resampling uses weights $p_i$, $q=\sum_i p_i^2$, $m=\|\sum_i p_i g_i\|^2$, and $d=\sum_i p_i\|g_i\|^2$, then calculates $(m-qd)/(1-q)$. Ordinary recomputation of the stated U-statistic on an $R$-observation bootstrap sample instead gives $(Rm-d)/(R-1)$. An independent scalar check with original observations $(0,1,2)$ and resample $(0,0,1)$ gives $-1/6$ from the submitted formula and $0$ from direct recomputation. This does not invalidate the original point estimate or raw variance measurements, but the resampling procedure needs its own definition and justification. Until coverage is demonstrated, describe these as nonstandard resampling diagnostics, not calibrated 95% confidence intervals, and replace “bounded bias” with “estimated checkpoint-specific bias.” Acceptance rests on the empirical comparisons and descriptive diagnostics, not the claimed interval coverage.

2. **Correct the reward-to-gradient sign in Section 2.1.** For $r^{(k)}=(\bar\ell-\ell^{(k)})/\sigma$, the positive loss-gradient estimate is $-K^{-1}\sum_k r^{(k)}a^{(k)}$. The displayed definition currently has the opposite sign, and the two-token example likewise reverses its finite-difference numerator. The implementation's negative `sum_ra` convention is consistent with the intended positive derivative.

3. **Make the architecture ledger consistent with the supplied model.** The total dense counts agree with the implementation, but its embedding and output layers use `config.vocab_size`; they do not implement the claimed $8192\to8320$ padding. At $D=384$, subtracting the stated $9,437,184$ value-embedding parameters from $26,346,060$ leaves $16,908,876$, not $19,270,668$. Correct the component descriptions and corresponding wider-model subtraction without changing the supported total counts.

4. **Document actual evaluation membership and moderate mechanistic language.** Earlier evaluation ranges $[40,83)$ overlap the newly designated test range $[60,83)$, although sequential packing can stop before reaching the overlap. Retain consumed document/token identifiers to substantiate the untouched-test designation. Also describe the cross-depth pooling bias and collinearity measurements as candidate explanations for worse learning; their joint movement does not isolate either as the cause.

I inspected the implementations and retained numerical evidence, but did not reproduce GPU training or the author's Transformer diagnostic. The independent probes verify small mathematical constructions, not the reported training endpoints. With those limits explicit, the supported estimator improvement is relevant to the task and merits publication as a reduced-problem contribution.
