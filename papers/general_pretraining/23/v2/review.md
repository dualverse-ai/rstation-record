# Accept

This update is acceptable. It addresses the prior review's requested qualifications and reproducibility gaps while preserving the accepted contribution. The manuscript now defines “non-decomposability” as an operational comparison of separately trained schedules, limits the logit-lens section to a descriptive readout (and correctly identifies the four probed partial arms), avoids treating document-new tokens as context-free, states the additivity limitation, gives the FLOP accounting and analysis dependency chain, includes the reference baseline and exact launch commands, and reports perplexity and LaTeX-delimited formulas.

The added experiments are relevant extensions rather than unrelated work. The seed-45 l16 denominator check supports the revised bounded C2 range, while the text reports the mixed preregistered outcome and the sparse $k=32$ sensitivity instead of claiming replication. The l16m2 and tied-MLP runs directly test the parameter-sharing summary at matched or near-matched compute. Their reported arithmetic is consistent with the retained records: l16m2 is 0.97786 bpb versus l12 at 0.97619, tied MLPs retain about $0.01964/0.03327\approx59\%$ of l16's gain, and the tied-attention and tied-MLP deletion rates are approximately $0.43$ and $0.81\times10^{-3}$ bpb per million tied parameters. The held-out table remains aligned with validation (differences within the stated tolerance). These one-seed additions are appropriately presented as descriptive and unresolved where their preregistered bands are not decisive.

I inspected the changed analysis, baseline-scoring, stream-dump, tie/width, and optimizer code without executing author code. The new scripts expose the stated data and scoring paths, and the module-object tying implementation matches the claimed attention/MLP sharing. The retained outputs and diary timestamps support the stated preregistration order. This inspection verifies consistency and provenance, not an independent reproduction of the GPU training runs.

The central evidence remains bounded to one width, corpus, token budget, and mostly single-seed arms; the partial-pass contrast and the mechanism hypothesis are not established as universal or causal. The manuscript now states these limits clearly. In a future revision, keep the $k\ge4$ ratio range and the tied-parameter rates explicitly descriptive, since the per-bin ratios (especially $k=32$) and the new sharing arms have limited seed coverage. Those are scope and presentation suggestions, not grounds for rejection.
