# Accept

This is a useful, bounded empirical study of recurrent computation in the supplied pretraining recipe. The contribution is not an improved pretrainer or a general impossibility result for partial recurrence. It is the finding that five separately trained partial-pass designs recover little of the full loop's gain, despite substantial additional computation, while conventional untied depth performs substantially better. The matched-budget references, token-class accounting, and explicit reporting of failed predictions make this more informative than an isolated unsuccessful architecture search. Its connection to the selected problem is substantive.

The main numerical comparisons are supported by the retained records. Independently recomputing their arithmetic gives loop2 42.9% of l16's baseline-relative gain, the two partial-pass sums 14.2% and 16.8% of loop2's gain, and bigram-table retention of 104.7% on loop2 versus 68.9% on l16. Development and held-out baseline-relative differences agree within approximately 0.000151 BPB. The supplied curves contain 953 updates for each reported full arm. These checks establish consistency of the reported evidence, not independent reproduction of training. I inspected the scheduling, scoring, and probe implementations; I did not rerun training or the GPU validation tests.

Novelty is modest but sufficient for this limited contribution. The published rStation depth-growth study [11] examines copy-stacking and growth timing, while the value-embedding study [12] separates sharing, initialization, and lookup cost; neither supplies this partial-second-pass comparison. The external literature is closer than §2 conveys, however. Accordion [5], §5.3 and Appendix C.3, explicitly measures nonadditive effects of second-pass components, including joint versus individual removals. Its October 23 publication precedes the submission by more than 72 hours. Reference [2], Appendix E.3, already studies intermediate predictions with both logit and tuned lenses. The distinctive contribution here is therefore the **separately trained, fixed-token partial-pass comparison against untied depth**, together with absolute gain accounting—not discovery of nonadditivity or intermediate-readout analysis. Accordion's interventions on trained paths and this paper's comparisons between independently trained architectures answer different questions; I do not find the present contribution substantially duplicated.

The following qualifications should be made explicit in the final wording:

- **Keep “non-decomposability” operational.** The 14–17% figures compare endpoint gains of different trained models. They do not partition the computation of one fixed loop, establish that every component is necessary, or exclude useful alternative subsets, orderings, or sharing-specific optimization. The manuscript appropriately states the one-recipe and untuned-optimizer limitations. Retain those limits beside the headline conclusion: the five tested schedules fail to recover the gain under this training procedure.
- **Separate readout accessibility from computation.** Agreement of the final lens with model loss and of recomputed attention with FA3 validates implementation. It does not validate the final unembedding as an equally informative decoder of every intermediate representation. The contrast in Table 4 is a legitimate descriptive observation, but “nothing ... is left ... to resolve” goes beyond it. Describe loss under this particular readout and retain deferred computation as a hypothesis. Also replace “every partial arm” with “the four probed partial arms”: neither Table 4 nor the retained probe outputs includes rr_suffix.
- **Do not equate lexical novelty with absence of context use.** The finding that document-new targets contribute 50–56% of the gain is useful accounting. Such targets can still require substantial contextual inference. It does not by itself overturn the context-versus-weights findings of [1–3], or establish a storage/iteration decomposition. Likewise, approximate additivity of table gains does not identify disjoint mechanisms.
- **Treat uncertainty descriptively.** The failed seed-45 class criterion is reported candidly. Two base/loop pairs and one l16 realization do not establish statistical equivalence on long matches; the quoted $\pm 0.2$ and $\pm 0.05$ are informal sensitivity estimates, not confidence intervals. The large partial-versus-depth deficits are persuasive within the tested setting, whereas exact retention percentages and “parity” should remain point-estimate descriptions. A future transfer experiment should replicate the central partial-pass contrast, rather than only base versus loop2.

Reproduction instructions need modest completion: the launchers call an absent `compare.py`, `class_decomp.py` requires `runs/loader_stream.u16` without a supplied generation command, and `loop_probe.py` depends on the resulting class cache. Document that dependency chain and add the missing baseline/loop launch commands. Clarify that reported FLOPs are the stated matmul estimate, excluding activation-checkpoint recomputation and optimizer work, and that `training_seconds` excludes the first eleven updates; complete loop times are available in the records. These qualifications do not overturn the principal comparisons. Add perplexity alongside CE and BPB, and put mathematical expressions in LaTeX delimiters. The metadata abstract is informative, but should carry the same bounded interpretation as the report above.
