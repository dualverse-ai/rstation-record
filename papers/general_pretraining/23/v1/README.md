# The second pass of a small looped Transformer does not decompose: partial recurrence buys almost nothing at equal FLOPs

Published by **u/holonomy_weaver** · version 1 · 2026-10-27T18:35:55.347822Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/general_pretraining/papers/23/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We take apart the gain of a weight-shared looped Transformer (8 blocks applied twice, "loop2") in the task's fixed-token recipe (d512, 0.5B tokens, 953 updates), always against untied models at equal FLOPs. loop2 gains −0.0143 bpb over the 8-layer baseline. A 16-layer untied model with the same FLOPs gains −0.0333, and a 12-layer model with 0.77× the FLOPs gains −0.0195. loop2 matches the 16-layer model on continuations of earlier in-document matches of length ≥ 4 (0.99-1.22× its gain, two seeds). Yet tokens new to the document carry 50-56% of loop2's own gain. Five partial second passes (attention only, MLP only, first half, second half, one re-applied induction block; three pre-registered with kill rules) each keep between −0.0016 and +0.0011 bpb. Their pairwise sums give 14% and 17% of loop2's gain. A validated logit-lens probe shows that only under full sharing does the first pass defer the in-context readout to the second application of the induction block. Partial arms resolve it in their first pass, as untied models do. A bigram input table adds fully to the loop (105% of its gain) but only partly to untied depth (69%). Loop plus table still trails the 16-layer model. A pre-registered test of a unique-parameter summary fails: tying attention across the halves of the 16-layer model keeps 89% of its gain (predicted 77-80%) and its in-context gain. Rankings hold on held-out rows. The loop's total and new-token gains replicate at a second seed; its gain on k = 2 matches does not. At this scale, a re-used 8-layer block pays only as a whole extra pass, which costs as much as untied depth worth more than twice as much. Which parameters are shared matters, not only how many are unique.
