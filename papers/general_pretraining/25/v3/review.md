# Accept

This revision appropriately qualifies the accepted contribution. The changed title and Section 4.4 distinguish an embedding that reproduces the gain from an established mechanism, and explicitly acknowledge that the foreign-document control changes match frequency as well as document identity. Section 4.11 adds a bounded training-budget sensitivity study of the existing method and comparators. With its descriptive scope and removal of extrapolation advice, this is supporting evidence for the accepted contribution, rather than a separate substantive contribution requiring another paper.

The new population definition is scientifically important. Document distance of at most 8K does not imply availability to a lookup that resets at chunk boundaries. Section 4.11 now separates sources inside and outside the target's chunk and, for B, separates sources inside and outside its 2K row. This supports a more precise result: on copies whose source is inside the chunk but before B's row, the observed loss fraction removed is approximately stable across the three budgets, whereas the fraction falls on copies visible to B. The A results likewise distinguish declining gains on accessible copies from small gains on inaccessible copies. The manuscript clearly labels this partition as post hoc and keeps the original registered predictions attached to their original distance-only population.

I reproduced Tables 9 and 10 from the supplied aggregates, including B's fractions of .452/.446/.442 on dev-val and .453/.446/.440 on dev-held-out. The twelve B records with chunk visibility preserve the corresponding row-record counts, bytes and losses after summing over visibility. Inspection of the record-generation code supports the stated chunk-boundary rule. The merged run results retain all previous result objects unchanged, the packed class totals are unchanged, and the earlier training curves differ only by the documented rounding. These checks establish internal consistency and table arithmetic; they do not independently reproduce training, per-token classification and scoring, or the chronology of prediction registration. The new class totals do not support an independent document bootstrap of the class-specific trends.

The limitations are proportionate to the added evidence: separately completed schedules, additional training exports at the longest budget, development-set evaluation, and only one seed at 1,906 updates. In particular, the successful $\pm15\%$ prediction rule cannot distinguish constant fractional from constant absolute gain when the base loss changes only 5–8%; the text now states this. Neither the three observed budgets nor their ratios establish an extrapolatable scaling law or a causal account of how attention learns to replace the feature. The unchanged matched-FLOPs ranking remains a 953-update result because local-global models were not repeated at other budgets.

The overall argument remains identifiable, and moving prediction histories and effective-training-length calculations into the supplement keeps the added section focused. The fraction-of-loss comparison is appropriately credited to [19]; that paper's cross-corpus prediction study does not duplicate this within-recipe training-budget qualification.

Minor improvements:

- Use “approximately stable at the tested budgets” instead of “constant” in the abstract and concluding description. This accurately describes the measurements without suggesting a statistical equivalence result.
- In the abstract, specify that the 17–29% shrinkage concerns the A layout's full-table and structure-only arms. Generic “8K-model gains” can be mistaken for evidence about the local-global layouts as well.
- Complete reference [2], whose arXiv record is accessible: Yunao Zheng, Xiaojie Wang, Lei Ren and Wei Chen, *ROSA-Tuning: Enhancing Long-Context Modeling via Suffix Matching*, arXiv:2602.02499 (2026).
