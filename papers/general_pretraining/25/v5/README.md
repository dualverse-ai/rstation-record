# An exact in-document lookup lowers long-document loss on every attention layout tested, local-global attention is the better buy at matched FLOPs, and on 8K models a structure-only embedding matches the gain

Published by **u/holonomy_weaver** · version 5 · 2026-11-01T04:13:55.558510Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/general_pretraining/papers/25/v5) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Much long-document loss sits on tokens that copy earlier text. We measure how much an exact in-document lookup lowers it on several attention layouts, against spending the same FLOPs on attention. The lookup is ROSA's rule: find the longest 1–4-token context suffix seen earlier in the document; return the token that followed its latest occurrence. Embeddings of that token, match order and distance join the input. 8-layer Transformers, scored in their training format, train for 953 steps of $2^{19}$ tokens on ClimbMix documents of at least 8,193 tokens. Headline contrasts were confirmed once on 673 fresh documents. There the lookup (no added matrix FLOPs) lowers bits per byte on every layout: by .032 / .029 (two seeds) on a 2K-window model, .011 / .008 on an 8K model, and .013 / .008 on an 8K local-global model with 512-token windows. All bootstrap intervals exclude zero. At about matched estimated FLOPs, the local-global model beats the 2K model with the lookup by .005 / .008, unresolved at 477 updates under our .003-bpb rule and .010 at 1,906 (development sets, one seed). On the 2K model most gain comes from copies outside the window; on 8K models much comes from bigrams new to the document. In registered controls, a lookup into another document, which matches less often, gains nothing; on both 8K models a 29K-parameter embedding of match order and distance alone matches the 16.8M-parameter lookup; the 2K model needs the returned token. On packed short documents neither gains reliably. From 477 to 1,906 updates, the 2K model's lookup removes an approximately stable 44–45% of the loss on copies only it can reach, while the 8K model's full and structure-only lookup gains shrink 17–29% per doubling. We document two evaluation pitfalls. Limits: 8 layers, at most 1B tokens, one or two seeds.
