# Accept

The revision adds bounded supporting evidence for the accepted contribution. The match-rate control (TT), earlier-field resampling control (GR), and output-probability decomposition address the existing question of why match-structure features improve predictions on order-0 targets. They neither introduce a separate architecture nor establish a new mechanism. Their relationship to claim 3 is clear, and the manuscript appropriately reports the unresolved controls and seed disagreement. These closely related diagnostics belong with the existing paper rather than in separate thin submissions.

The previous review's requested corrections are substantially addressed: the 477-update comparison is described as unresolved under the stated tolerance, the removal-curve statement is restricted to the sampled grid, and Figure 3c now displays document-bootstrap intervals. The accepted comparisons and central explanation remain identifiable.

The added evidence supports the following limited conclusions:

- **TT:** With thinned fields, the order-0 gain retains approximately .58/.56 of the full-table gain at seed 44 and .39/.32 at seed 45. This demonstrates attenuation under the specified random-thinning intervention while retaining positive gains. Both registered readings remain unresolved; the foreign-document comparator still has only one training seed.
- **GR:** The retained shares are .648/.649 at seed 44 and −.071/−.068 at seed 45. Reporting this disagreement, rather than claiming that positional alignment is either necessary or unnecessary, is appropriate.
- **Output decomposition:** The identity $\log p(y)=\log P(g(y))+\log p(y\mid g(y))$ provides a valid descriptive decomposition using the same data-defined groups for both models. I reproduce group-term shares of .096/.104 and .107/.100, with the reported document-bootstrap intervals. Thus approximately 89–90% of the aggregate order-0 improvement lies in the within-group conditional log-probability term. This locates the improvement in the output distribution without identifying its internal cause.

I reproduced the TT, GR, output-decomposition and revised block-mask summaries from the retained aggregates. The repacking preserves the earlier class and document totals, and the earlier run-result objects are unchanged. The decomposition's retained total losses agree with the earlier records within approximately $2.2\times10^{-5}$ relative error. These checks establish numerical consistency; they do not reproduce training or checkpoint inference, independently validate every target classification, or authenticate registration chronology. Document-bootstrap intervals remain conditional on the trained checkpoints and sampled interventions.

Several wording and presentation improvements would make the new material more precise:

1. In claim 3 and the TT paragraph, replace “the lower match rate therefore accounts for part of that null” with the directly supported observation that thinning own-document fields reduces their gain, while those fields still outperform the foreign-document arm at the tested marginal rate. The experiment does not uniquely partition the foreign arm's shortfall into causal components: thinning changes field availability and its relationship to the text, and interactions remain possible. Also distinguish the training removal probability .3664 from the evaluation probabilities adjusted separately to each set's foreign match rate.
2. In the GR paragraph, “keep the document's mix of match orders and distances” is too strong. Sampling uniformly from each position's preceding prefix overweights early fields and changes their distribution and correlations. The registration already acknowledges this; bring that qualification into the main text. Evaluation also conditions on one fixed resampling pattern shared across rows.
3. Replace “better ranking” in claim 3 and the decomposition paragraph with “higher conditional target log-probability within its group.” Log-probability can improve without any change in token rank; no rank statistic is reported.
4. Section 4.4 remains understandable but is becoming disproportionately long. Consolidate its controls by scientific question, retain Table 6c and the decisive decomposition result, and move registration times, prediction probabilities and repeated numerical narration to the existing supplement. Remove the stale statement that the block-mask test is “new in this version.” Further closely related additions should come through a consolidated presentation rather than continued appended diagnostics.

These qualifications preserve the supported empirical findings and do not undermine the accepted central contribution.
