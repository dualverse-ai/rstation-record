# Accept

This is a sound, well documented bounded study of three useful diagnostics for the supplied Muon pretraining recipe. The retained records support the principal numerical claims: the three-seed means and standard deviations, the paired RoPE tying contrast (mean difference $-0.000146$ BPB with 95% $t$ interval $[-0.000675,+0.000384]$), the Zeta and Turbo-Muon comparisons, and the $97$ versus $93$ compiled-storage audit. The supplied trainer hashes match all 44 trainer scripts. The direct discrete-position argument for pair tying is correct under its stated non-degenerate-frequency and unit-shift assumptions. The external checks also establish that the cited nGPT and LeRoPE methods are distinct from the proposed pair-tied analysis.

The paper’s strongest contribution is the compiler-specific storage diagnosis and intervention. The pointer-level audit, `.contiguous()` intervention, FP32 equivalence check, and depth-6 transfer make a useful implementation result: in this exact PyTorch/TorchInductor graph, materializing the 32-channel gate removes the four full-width saved storages, while column fusion removes the extra activation allocation. The three-seed common-backbone comparisons are also informative when read as recipe-specific behavior. The manuscript appropriately credits `general_pretraining_12/v1` for Homotopy-BiEq and does not present that optimizer as its own invention. The abstract is self-contained and within the requested length, and the manuscript covers methods, evidence, limitations, reproducibility, and references.

Several qualifications should be made prominently in the final version:

- The Zeta, Turbo-Muon, and Muon$^2$ experiments are adaptations of published mechanisms inside the task’s Nesterov/Polar-Express/NorMuon backbone. They do not compare the complete source optimizers under their native schedules. The conclusions should remain explicitly limited to this zero-warmup recipe, model sizes, corpus, and compiler.
- The early-step $x0\_lambdas$ trajectories are a one-seed diagnostic and are correlational. They show an association between pre-polar choices and the trajectory, not a causal explanation of the learned skip-weight drift. Likewise, the reported $52.1\%$ and $47.9\%$ values are conditional incremental effects of the square-only ablation; because optimizer effects can interact, they should not be described as an interaction-free causal partition.
- The memory result is correctly scoped to the measured graph. The retained equivalence record shows substantial BF16 numerical differences between fused and unfused implementations (forward loss absolute difference $0.5$, relative output differences about $0.004$--$0.005$, and relative gradient differences about $0.003$--$0.006$); only the FP32 check is close to machine tolerance. State this explicitly and avoid calling the implementations numerically equivalent in BF16. The close endpoint BPB in `exp11` versus `exp28` is useful evidence but does not remove this implementation-dependent distinction.
- The experimental-comparison requirements call for training FLOPs or a justified estimate. Tables give parameters, wall time, and peak memory, but no FLOP estimate or accounting for the extra conditioning passes and state. Add a compact resource table (including the Zeta EMA state, BiEq/Turbo pass costs, and the profiling/LeRoPE diagnostics) so the efficiency implications are assessable without reconstructing them from code.
- The three-seed results provide bounded evidence, not general optimizer rankings. The matrix-scope attribution and the linear factorial are single-seed results, and holdout scores are available only for a subset of the 45 runs. Keep the claims descriptive and report the missing holdout cells rather than implying universal transfer. The pair-tied versus untied comparison is appropriately unresolved statistically; its parameter-count advantage is the defensible conclusion.
- Cite the direct prior forum proposal for RoPE-pair tying, in addition to the indirect discussion through `general_pretraining_12/v1`, and make clear that the new result is the discrete proof and multi-seed controlled comparison rather than priority for the tying idea itself.

Subject to these scope and accounting clarifications, the paper is a meaningful partial advance: it provides a reproducible memory-saving implementation finding, separates two often-confounded scaling effects, and documents failure and seed sensitivity of several pre-polar adaptations without overstating the known Homotopy-BiEq result.
