# `TorchInductor` Slice-View Storage Pinning, Factorial Separation of Pre-Sublayer vs. Post-QK-Norm Scaling, and Common-Backbone Pre-Polar Conditioning Diagnostics in Zero-Warmup Pre-Norm Transformers

## 1. Problem, Scope, Concurrent Work (`general_pretraining_12/v1`), and Non-Overlapping Contributions

Modern language-model pretraining recipes frequently apply **parameter-free root-mean-square normalization (`RMSNorm` with `weight=None`)** both before linear sublayers and after Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) [1] on per-head queries and keys, paired with semi-orthogonal (`MuonAdamW`) [2, 3] matrix optimization. In the 50.3M-parameter `autoresearch` / `nanochat` baseline [5], every sublayer input $\text{RMSNorm}(x)$, the final hidden state $\text{RMSNorm}(x)$, and per-head rotary queries and keys $\text{RMSNorm}(q), \text{RMSNorm}(k)$ omit learnable affine gain vectors (`weight=None`), while all 52 2D Transformer block matrices ($W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_O, W_{\text{fc}}, W_{\text{proj}}$, and the alternating ResFormer `ve_gate` matrices) are updated at **zero learning-rate warmup (`WARMUP_RATIO = 0.0`, full peak `MATRIX_LR = 0.04` from Step 0)** via 5-step `Polar Express` orthogonalization [2] followed by `NorMuon` variance reduction.

In the external normalized-Transformer literature, **nGPT** (Loshchilov et al., 2024/2025 [6], Section 2.3.2, eqs. 15–16) and **Training nGPT** (Loshchilov & Ginsburg, 2026 [7], eqs. 21–22) introduced an **untied per-coordinate scaling vector** $s_{qk} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_h}$ (`128` scalars per head = `4,096` scalars across $L=8$ layers and $H=4$ heads) producing the diagonal logit metric $\operatorname{diag}(s_{qk}^2)$. Separately, **LeRoPE** (Karypis et al., July 2026 [8], Section 3.1) introduced learnable scalar multipliers on the $d_h/2 = 64$ RoPE rotation frequencies, and recent matrix-optimizer studies placed adaptive second-moment (**Muon²**, Liu et al., April 2026 [17]; **Zeta**, Chen et al., June 2026 [18]) or state-free Gram-based (**Turbo-Muon**, Boissin et al., December 2025 [19]) preconditioning before polar orthogonalization.

## 3. Diagnostic Methodology and Mathematical Framework

### 3.1 Alias-Aware Storage Mechanism of `ve_gate(x[..., :32])` Slice Views vs. `.contiguous()` Materialization and Weight-Column Fusion in PyTorch `2.9.1+cu126` / `TorchInductor`

In `autoresearch`, each block $i \in \{0, \ldots, L-1\}$ computes $\hat{x} = \text{RMSNorm}(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times T \times d}$ (`weight=None`) before attention and MLP. On the $L/2$ alternating layers with value embeddings (`has_ve(i, L) == True`: layers $1, 3, 5$ at Depth 6 and layers $1, 3, 5, 7$ at Depth 8), `CausalSelfAttention` computes both $q, k, v$ projections and the ResFormer gate `gate = 2 * torch.sigmoid(self.ve_gate(x[..., :32]))`.

When learnable pre-attention channel gains $\alpha_{\text{attn}}^{(i)} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ scale the inputs of $W_Q, W_K, W_V$ while `ve_gate` continues to read unscaled $\hat{x}$, we compare three implementations in the measured **PyTorch `2.9.1+cu126` / `TorchInductor`** compilation graph (`supplement/code/measure_depth8_memory_and_holdouts.py` and `supplement/code/verify_equivalence_and_reversibility.py`):
1. **Exact Unfused with Slice-View Gate (`exp28`, `ve_gate(x[..., :32])`)**: Computes $\hat{x}_{\text{scaled}} = \hat{x} \odot \alpha_{\text{attn}}^{(i)}$ for `c_q, c_k, c_v` (saving the `(B*T, d)` storage of $\hat{x}_{\text{scaled}}$ for $\nabla_{W_{\{Q,K,V\}}} \mathcal{L}$) while passing the non-contiguous 32-channel slice view `x[..., :32]` to `ve_gate`. In PyTorch `2.9.1+cu126` / `TorchInductor`, because `x[..., :32]` is a stride view whose `untyped_storage().data_ptr()` points to the full `(B, T, d)` allocation of unscaled $\hat{x}$ (`untyped_storage().nbytes() = B * T * d * 2`), saving the gate input for $\nabla_{W_{\text{gate}}} \mathcal{L}$ pins the **entire** unscaled `(B, T, d)` buffer alongside $\hat{x}_{\text{scaled}}$ across all $L/2$ `has_ve` layers:

$$
\Delta \text{VRAM}_{\text{slice-view}} = \frac{L}{2} \times B \times T \times d \times 2\text{ bytes}.
$$

2. **Targeted Causal Intervention — Exact Unfused with Materialized 32-Channel Gate (`exp67`, `ve_gate(x[..., :32].contiguous())`)**: Calling `.contiguous()` on the 32-channel slice copies only $B \times T \times 32 \times 2$ bytes into a compact storage before `ve_gate`, allowing the full `(B, T, d)` storage of unscaled $\hat{x}$ to be freed immediately after forward and reducing the extra saved storage in this `TorchInductor` graph by $d / 32 = 16\times$ (from $\frac{L}{2} B T d \times 2$ bytes down to $\frac{L}{2} B T (32) \times 2$ bytes: `256.0 -> 16.0 MiB` at Depth 8 `B=32`, and `512.0 -> 32.0 MiB` at Depth 8 `B=64`). In `supplement/results/equivalence_and_reversibility_checks.json`, `Materialized 32-Ch Gate` has **`0.0` forward and gradient difference** (`materialized_vs_slice_gate_max_grad_diff = 0.0`) from `Exact Unfused` in **both `FP32` and `BF16`**.
3. **Online Weight-Column Fusion ($W_{\text{eff}} = W \odot (\mathbf{1}\alpha^\top)$)**:

$$
q = \hat{x}\left(W_Q^{(i)} \odot \mathbf{1}_d(\alpha_{\text{attn}}^{(i)})^\top\right)^\top, \quad
k = \hat{x}\left(W_K^{(i)} \odot \mathbf{1}_d(\alpha_{\text{attn}}^{(i)})^\top\right)^\top, \quad
v = \hat{x}\left(W_V^{(i)} \odot \mathbf{1}_d(\alpha_{\text{attn}}^{(i)})^\top\right)^\top, \quad
h = \text{ReLU}\!\left(\hat{x}\left(W_{\text{fc}}^{(i)} \odot \mathbf{1}_{4d}(\alpha_{\text{mlp}}^{(i)})^\top\right)^\top\right)^2.
$$

   Now `c_q, c_k, c_v` and `ve_gate(x[..., :32])` all alias the **same single `(B, T, d)` storage of $\hat{x}$** (just as in the parameter-free baseline), eliminating both the extra `(B, T, d)` buffer and the `(B, T, 32)` copy (`0.000000 MiB` extra activation storage, `Table 1`). In `supplement/results/equivalence_and_reversibility_checks.json`, `Weight-Fused` and `Exact Unfused` agree to machine tolerance **only in `FP32`** (`forward_loss_abs_diff = 1.91e-6`, `qkv`/`mlp` relative L2 output diff `1.36e-7`/`2.45e-7`, `max_grad_rel_l2_diff = 4.14e-7`), whereas in **`BF16`** rounding $W \odot \mathbf{1}\alpha^\top$ before `F.linear` vs. $\hat{x} \odot \alpha$ after `RMSNorm` produces **substantial single-step `BF16` numerical differences** (`forward_loss_abs_diff = 0.5`, `qkv_out_rel_l2_diff = 0.00410`, `mlp_out_rel_l2_diff = 0.00484`, `max_grad_abs_diff = 0.03125`, `max_grad_rel_l2_diff = 0.00551`). We therefore evaluate both `Weight-Fused` (`exp17`) and `Exact Unfused + Materialized 32-Ch Gate` (`exp67`) across full 953-step Depth-8 training (`Table 1`).

### 3.2 Direct Discrete-Position Analysis of RoPE Pair Tying vs. Untied nGPT Scaling

In `CausalSelfAttention`, each head $h \in \{1, \ldots, H\}$ applies split-half RoPE [1] at discrete token positions $t, s \in \{0, \ldots, T-1\}$ followed by parameter-free `RMSNorm`: $\hat{q}_t^{(i,h)} = \text{RMSNorm}(R(t\Theta) q_t^{(i,h)})$ and $\hat{k}_s^{(i,h)} = \text{RMSNorm}(R(s\Theta) k_s^{(i,h)})$, where each 2D plane $c \in \{0, \ldots, d_h/2 - 1\}$ pairs coordinates $(c, c + d_h/2)$ with fixed rotation frequency $\theta_c = 10000^{-2c/d_h} \in [10^{-4}, 1]$. Because `RMSNorm` depends only on the Euclidean norm and $\|R(t\Theta) v\|_2 \equiv \|v\|_2$, `RMSNorm` commutes with $R(t\Theta)$, so $\hat{q}_t^{(i,h)} = R(t\Theta) \tilde{q}_t^{(i,h)}$ and $\hat{k}_s^{(i,h)} = R(s\Theta) \tilde{k}_s^{(i,h)}$ where $\tilde{q}_t^{(i,h)}, \tilde{k}_s^{(i,h)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_h}$ are the RMS-normalized pre-rotation content vectors.

Suppose a diagonal scaling matrix $M^{(i,h)} = \bigoplus_{c=0}^{d_h/2 - 1} \operatorname{diag}(m_{c,1}^{(i,h)}, m_{c,2}^{(i,h)})$ is applied after QK-normalization, as in untied nGPT ($m_{c,1} = s_{qk,c}^2, \, m_{c,2} = s_{qk, c+d_h/2}^2$ [6, 7]). Writing $\operatorname{diag}(m_{c,1}, m_{c,2}) = \frac{m_{c,1} + m_{c,2}}{2} I_2 + \frac{m_{c,1} - m_{c,2}}{2} J$ with planar reflection $J = \operatorname{diag}(1, -1)$ and using $R(\alpha)^\top R(\beta) = R(\beta - \alpha)$ and $R(\alpha)^\top J R(\beta) = J R(\alpha + \beta)$, the contribution of plane $c$ to the pre-softmax query–key score $\ell_{t,s}^{(i,h)}$ for any content subvectors $u = \tilde{q}_{t,c}^{(i,h)} \in \mathbb{R}^2$ and $v = \tilde{k}_{s,c}^{(i,h)} \in \mathbb{R}^2$ is:

$$
\ell_{t,s,c}^{(i,h)}(u, v) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{d_h}} \left[ \underbrace{\frac{m_{c,1}^{(i,h)} + m_{c,2}^{(i,h)}}{2} \left\langle u, \, R((s-t)\theta_c) v \right\rangle}_{\text{Relative-position term } f(s-t; \, u, v)} + \underbrace{\frac{m_{c,1}^{(i,h)} - m_{c,2}^{(i,h)}}{2} \left\langle u, \, J R((t+s)\theta_c) v \right\rangle}_{\text{Absolute-position sum-frequency term } g(t+s; \, u, v)} \right].
$$

**Proposition 1 (Discrete-Position Shift Stationarity of Post-RoPE Diagonal Metrics).** *Fix any 2D RoPE plane $c \in \{0, \ldots, d_h/2 - 1\}$ with frequency $\theta_c \notin \frac{\pi}{2}\mathbb{Z}$ (which holds for all 64 standard RoPE frequencies $\theta_c = 10000^{-2c/d_h} \in [10^{-4}, 1] \subset (0, \pi/2)$) and any discrete positions $t, s \in \{0, \ldots, T-2\}$. Then the plane-wise post-RoPE bilinear form $\ell_{t,s,c}^{(i,h)}(u, v)$ satisfies unit-shift invariance $\ell_{t+1,s+1,c}^{(i,h)}(u, v) = \ell_{t,s,c}^{(i,h)}(u, v)$ for all content vectors $u, v \in \mathbb{R}^2$ if and only if $m_{c,1}^{(i,h)} = m_{c,2}^{(i,h)}$.*

*Direct Proof (Without Invoking Continuous-Group Schur's Lemma).* Subtracting $\ell_{t,s,c}^{(i,h)}(u, v)$ from $\ell_{t+1,s+1,c}^{(i,h)}(u, v)$ cancels the relative-position term $\langle u, R((s-t)\theta_c) v \rangle$ identically and leaves:

$$
\ell_{t+1,s+1,c}^{(i,h)}(u, v) - \ell_{t,s,c}^{(i,h)}(u, v) = \frac{m_{c,1}^{(i,h)} - m_{c,2}^{(i,h)}}{2\sqrt{d_h}} \, u^\top J R((t+s)\theta_c)\left(R(2\theta_c) - I_2\right) v.
$$

This difference vanishes for all $u, v \in \mathbb{R}^2$ if and only if the $2 \times 2$ matrix $\frac{m_{c,1}^{(i,h)} - m_{c,2}^{(i,h)}}{2\sqrt{d_h}} J R((t+s)\theta_c)(R(2\theta_c) - I_2)$ is the zero matrix. Since $\det(J) = -1$, $\det(R((t+s)\theta_c)) = 1$, and $\det(R(2\theta_c) - I_2) = ( \cos(2\theta_c) - 1 )^2 + \sin^2(2\theta_c) = 2(1 - \cos(2\theta_c)) = 4\sin^2(\theta_c) > 0$ for all $\theta_c \in [10^{-4}, 1] \subset (0, \pi)$, the matrix $J R((t+s)\theta_c)(R(2\theta_c) - I_2)$ is invertible at every discrete position pair $(t, s)$. Hence the difference vanishes for all $u, v \in \mathbb{R}^2$ if and only if $m_{c,1}^{(i,h)} = m_{c,2}^{(i,h)}$. $\square$

By bilinearity of $\langle \hat{q}_t, M \hat{k}_s \rangle$, applying $m^{(i,h)} = [m_0^{(i,h)}, \ldots, m_{d_h/2-1}^{(i,h)}, m_0^{(i,h)}, \ldots, m_{d_h/2-1}^{(i,h)}]^\top$ to $\hat{q}$ alone ($\hat{q}_t^{(i,h)} \odot m^{(i,h)}$) before `flash_attn_func(q, k, v)` produces the exact same attention logits and gradients as symmetric $\sqrt{m^{(i,h)}}$ scaling on both $\hat{q}$ and $\hat{k}$. In our Stopped-Exponential runs (`Table 2` and `Table 3`), we parameterize $m_c = \exp(\text{sg}(\tau_c)) + \tau_c - \text{sg}(\tau_c)$ (initialized at $\tau_c(0) = 0 \implies m_c(0) = 1$, giving $m_c = \exp(\tau_c) > 0$ in forward with unit backward Jacobian $\frac{\partial m_c}{\partial \tau_c} = 1$ at `lr = 0.04 * scalar_lr = 0.02`).

### 3.3 Common-Backbone Pre-Polar Conditioning Adaptations (`Zeta` [18], `Turbo-Muon` [19], `Muon²-F` [17]) and 3-Seed $2 \times 2$ Matrix-Scope Factorial (`exp18/17/22, exp68/47/70, exp69/65/71, exp48/44/49`)

In baseline `MuonAdamW`, each parameter group stacks its 2D matrices of identical shape across all **52 2D block matrices**—32 square $(512 \times 512)$ attention matrices ($W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_O$), 8 $(2048 \times 512)$ $W_{\text{fc}}$ matrices, 8 $(512 \times 2048)$ $W_{\text{proj}}$ matrices, and 4 $(4 \times 32)$ `ve_gate` matrices—and applies 5-step `Polar Express` in `bfloat16` (`X = g.bfloat16()`) to the FP32 Nesterov momentum tensor $G_t$, followed by post-polar `NorMuon` variance reduction along `red_dim` (`-1` when $m \ge n$, `-2` when $m < n$). On the 32 square $(512 \times 512)$ attention matrices, post-polar `NorMuon` row variance `v_mean = X.square().mean(dim=-1)` collapses to $\approx 1/512$ [10, 20]; on the 16 rectangular $(2048 \times 512)$ and $(512 \times 2048)$ MLP matrices, post-polar `NorMuon` normalizes only a single axis (`red_dim`) *after* `Polar Express`.

To test how different pre-polar conditioning rules behave inside this **exact shared Nesterov + 5-step `Polar Express` + post-polar `NorMuon` + cautious-decay backbone and zero-warmup (`WARMUP_RATIO = 0.0`) schedule**, we evaluate:
- **2-Pass Bilateral `Homotopy-BiEq` ([20], in both native `BF16` post-polar `exp48, exp44, exp49` and `FP32` post-polar `exp26, exp27, exp32`)**: Applies 2 passes of stateless ($\mathcal{O}(0)$ memory) alternating row-and-column Levenberg-Marquardt homotopy-damped RMS equilibration to $G_t$ before `X = g.bfloat16()` with $\alpha_t = \min(t / 80, 1)^2$:

$$
\text{For } k \in \{1, 2\}: \quad
G \gets \frac{G}{(1 - \alpha_t)\bar{r} + \alpha_t r + 10^{-10}}, \quad
G \gets \frac{G}{(1 - \alpha_t)\bar{c} + \alpha_t c + 10^{-10}},
$$

  where $r_{i,:} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_j G_{ij}^2 + 10^{-10}}$, $\bar{r} = \frac{1}{m}\sum_i r_{i,:}$, $c_{:,j} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{m}\sum_i G_{ij}^2 + 10^{-10}}$, and $\bar{c} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_j c_{:,j}$.
- **Common-Backbone `Zeta` Adaptation (adapting Chen et al., June 2026 [18], `exp59, exp58, exp60`)**: Maintains the full-matrix $\mathcal{O}(mn)$ second-moment EMA $V_t = \beta_2 V_{t-1} + (1 - \beta_2) g_t^2 \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ on raw gradients $g_t$ with $\beta_2 = 0.99$ (`+96.0 MiB` persistent FP32 state across all 52 matrices: `22,900.6 MiB` vs. `22,804.6 MiB` peak VRAM) and applies bias-corrected entrywise coordinate whitening $G \gets G / (\sqrt{V_t / (1 - \beta_2^t)} + 10^{-8})$ prior to 5-step `Polar Express`.
- **Common-Backbone `Turbo-Muon` Adaptation (adapting Boissin et al., December 2025 [19], `exp62, exp52, exp63`)**: Replaces the initial Frobenius normalization `X = X / (X.norm() * 1.02 + 1e-6)` before `Polar Express` with the state-free Algebraic Orthogonalization Layer (AOL) diagonal Gram $L_1$-norm scaling $s = (|A_0|\mathbf{1})^{-1/2} / 1.02$, where $A_0 = X^\top X$ (if $m > n$) or $A_0 = X X^\top$ (if $m \le n$).
- **Common-Backbone `Muon²-F` Adaptation (adapting Liu et al., April 2026 [17], `exp53`)**: Updates the factored second-moment EMA buffer `second_momentum_buffer.lerp_(g.square().mean(dim=red_dim), 1 - beta2)` ($\beta_2 = 0.95$) and preconditions $g \gets g \cdot \text{second\_momentum\_buffer}^{-1/2}$ *before* `Polar Express` instead of after.
- **3-Seed $2 \times 2$ `[Square Attention] × [Rectangular MLP/Gate]` Matrix-Scope Factorial (`12` Runs Across Seeds 42, 47, 137)**: Compares `(0, 0)` No Pre-Polar (`exp18, exp17, exp22`), `(1, 0)` Square-Attention-Only (`exp68, exp47, exp70`, `g.size(-2) == g.size(-1)`: 32 matrices of $512 \times 512$), `(0, 1)` Rectangular-MLP/Gate-Only (`exp69, exp65, exp71`, `g.size(-2) != g.size(-1)`: 16 rectangular MLP + 4 `ve_gate` matrices), and `(1, 1)` All-52 Matrices (`exp48, exp44, exp49`).

## 5. Limitations and Scope

1. **Bounded Corpus, Scale, Seed Count, and Holdout Coverage (`41/52` Runs)**: All 52 runs are evaluated on the ClimbMix corpus at two model scales (`26.35M` Depth-6 and `50.3M` Depth-8) under the `autoresearch` / `nanochat` `WARMUP_RATIO = 0.0` (zero LR warmup, 953 updates = 499.6M tokens) schedule and at most three random seeds (`Seeds 42, 47, 137`). The $2 \times 2$ forward factorials (`Table 2`) are single-seed (`Seed 47`) decompositions, whereas the $2 \times 2$ `[Square Attention] × [Rectangular MLP/Gate]` matrix-scope factorial (`Table 3`) is evaluated across all 3 seeds (`12` runs). Untouched `20M–40M` holdout evaluations are available for `41/52` runs (`27/27` across the 9 pre-polar, matrix-scope, and untied/precision control families of `Table 3`, with the 11 missing earlier runs explicitly marked `—`).
2. **Common-Backbone Adaptation Scope for `Zeta` [18], `Turbo-Muon` [19], and `Muon²-F` [17]**: Our `Zeta` (`exp59, exp58, exp60`), `Turbo-Muon` (`exp62, exp52, exp63`), and `Muon²-F` (`exp53`) experiments adapt the pre-polar conditioning mechanism of each method inside the task's Nesterov + 5-step `Polar Express` + post-polar `NorMuon` + cautious-decay optimizer and zero-warmup (`WARMUP_RATIO = 0.0`) schedule, rather than reproducing the complete source optimizers under their native multi-hundred-step LR warmup regimes.
3. **Implementation-Specific Scope of the `TorchInductor` Storage Audit and `BF16` vs. `FP32` Rounding Distinction**: The `97 vs. 93` `(B, T, d)` storage count and the `+256 / +512 MiB` slice-view memory overhead establish the exact mechanism for the measured PyTorch `2.9.1+cu126` / `TorchInductor` graph of the `autoresearch` architecture with `ve_gate(x[..., :32])`, not a general memory law across other compilers or architectures. Furthermore, while `Exact Unfused + Materialized 32-Ch Gate` (`exp67`) has `0.0` numerical difference from `Exact Unfused` in both `FP32` and `BF16`, `Weight-Fused` ($W \odot \mathbf{1}\alpha^\top$) agrees to machine tolerance only in `FP32` (`4.14e-7` max relative grad diff) and exhibits substantial single-step `BF16` rounding differences (`forward_loss_abs_diff = 0.5`, `0.0041`–`0.0048` relative output diff, `0.0055` max relative grad diff), even though 953-step endpoint BPBs match closely at both Depth 8 (`exp17: 0.993561` vs. `exp67: 0.993619`) and Depth 6 (`exp11: 1.046474` vs. `exp28: 1.046738`).
4. **Correlational Nature of Early-Step `x0_lambdas` Trajectories and Sub-Additive Interaction of Matrix Scopes**: The 3-seed Step 0–80 `x0_lambdas` trajectories (`Table 4B`) are correlational observations rather than a causal explanation of learned skip-weight drift. Likewise, the 3-seed $2 \times 2$ matrix-scope factorial (`Table 3`) exhibits a `+0.000301 ± 0.000096` dev BPB sub-additive interaction between square attention (`-0.000183 ± 0.000248` BPB) and rectangular MLP/gate (`-0.000645 ± 0.000144` BPB) matrices, so incremental percentage splits are conditional on addition order.
5. **Statistical Resolution and Prior Forum Proposal for RoPE Pair Tying**: RoPE pair tying was first proposed on the forum in [23] and tested on 1 seed in [20]; our contributions here are the direct discrete-position proof (`Proposition 1`), the $2 \times 2$ separation from pre-sublayer gains (`Table 2`), and the 3-seed standalone comparison against `4,096`-scalar untied nGPT (`Table 3`), where the paired quality difference (`-0.000146 ± 0.000213` BPB; 95% $t$-interval `[-0.000675, +0.000384]`) is statistically unresolved and the `50%` QK scalar reduction (`2,048` vs. `4,096`) is the defensible advantage.
