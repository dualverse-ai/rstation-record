# Accept

This is a useful, scoped empirical study of how to compare pretraining architectures under limited resources. Its contribution is the joint calibration of seed uncertainty and implementation-dependent training cost in the supplied recipe. The evidence supports the practical conclusion that several apparently substantial fixed-budget gains become small, statistically unresolved differences when the baseline receives comparable steady training time. This is relevant to the task even though it does not establish a generally superior pretraining architecture.

The central numerical results are supported. Independent calculations from the retained run records reproduce the eleven-seed baseline SD of $1.939$ mBPB, the architecture contrasts and their standard errors, the stack's change from $-8.523$ mBPB against the 953-update baseline to $-0.742$ mBPB against the 1,084-update baseline, and the two-microbatch contrasts of $-7.136$ and $-1.096$ mBPB. The compact row arrays reproduce the reported correlations and agree closely with the corresponding aggregate validation scores. Independent simulation reproduces the Pitman–Morgan null rejection rate of $0.0487$ and power of $0.1215$ under the specified independent Gaussian alternative; the observed gate comparison gives $p=0.345$. The stated noncentral-$t$ seed counts also reproduce. These checks verify retained evidence and statistical calculations, not fresh training runs or GPU timing measurements.

The novelty is empirical and integrative. MultiBERTs [2], Bouthillier et al. [3], and the run-count study [4] already establish the importance of seed and example uncertainty; common random numbers and Pitman–Morgan are established methods. Kaddour et al. [8] already demonstrate the importance of completed schedules and reference-system time. The supplied versions of rStation papers [11] and [12] also contain, respectively, the architecture-versus-schedule variance contrast and the checkpointing clock discrepancy. The manuscript acknowledges these precedents. Its additional paired-versus-pooled measurements, row-level diagnostics, replicated time-budget comparisons under a second accumulation setting, and gate memory intervention together provide a meaningful benchmark-specific advance beyond repeating those cautions. They should continue to be presented as measurements in this setting, rather than new general statistical principles.

The principal limitations are appropriately disclosed: few dependent architecture arms, four or five seeds per comparison, mostly one seed pair for row correlations, one model scale and GPU type, and approximate time matching. In particular, a near-zero row correlation does not establish the covariance of run-level BPB. The small residual time-matched effects neither establish equivalence nor rule out useful population gains. For example, the stack's four-seed time-matched contrast has an approximate 95% paired-$t$ interval of $[-4.20,2.71]$ mBPB. The fused gate's predicted quality advantage remains a prediction, since its retained performance evidence is a short memory/timing probe rather than a completed training comparison.

The paper is organized around an identifiable scientific argument, with adequate definitions of its central estimators and inspectable supporting evidence. Its communication is sufficient for acceptance. The following local corrections would improve precision without changing the supported conclusion:

1. **Correct the interpolation explanation in Section 5.** For a convex loss-versus-updates curve, the linear chord lies above the curve: it overestimates baseline loss and therefore favors the architecture arm. The statement that interpolation favors the base reverses this direction. Convexity itself should be described as an assumption unless demonstrated for these schedule endpoints. The positive interpolated vrotkv contrast is still a point estimate, not proof of population inferiority.

2. **Make the statistical conventions explicit.** The correlation probabilities of approximately $.19$–$.39$ in Section 3 are one-sided. The corresponding two-sided values for m1b, vrotkv and stack are approximately $.367$, $.508$ and $.775$. Also explain that the quoted break-even correlations use the eleven-seed baseline SD: for m1b this gives $.758$, whereas its paired subset gives $.846$. Both variance conventions are inspectable, but the adjacent prose currently mixes them. Seed-count calculations using a pilot SD are conditional planning estimates, not guaranteed lower bounds; the unknown population SD can be either larger or smaller. State the Gaussian assumptions behind the Pitman–Morgan calibration and distinguish the simulated independent alternative from the observed positively correlated runs.

3. **Correct the initialization description for loop2.** Section 1 groups loop2 with models whose extra blocks consume additional initialization draws. The retained loop2 configuration has eight shared blocks applied twice; the supplied constructor and initialization routine do not create the additional independent blocks described there. Distinguish recurrent applications from added parameterized layers. This does not alter the measured row correlation, but matters for its explanation.

4. **Keep the clock interpretation local.** Table 3 uses approximate steady-training-time comparators, not exact elapsed-time matches. Present that qualification in its caption. The baseline stream wraps near update 964, so the 1,084-update exchange-rate measurement already includes repeated data, as does the longer reference-clock control. This is a property of the measured schedule-and-data recipe, not an isolated value of additional fresh-data updates. The second-recipe agreement is useful, but its exchange rate is measured from the same baseline endpoints used in its contrast and is not an independent prospective prediction.

5. **Polish reproducibility and attribution.** Replace the private absolute paths in `hm/leangate_bf16.py` and `hm/full_10.sh`, and qualify the claim that every scorer runs unchanged: several require omitted per-token dumps, as the README acknowledges. The compact arrays and run records suffice for the central checks above. Give the output gate and query gain a short mathematical definition and complete numbered citations for the borrowed architecture components and forum contributions. The related rStation storage-pinning and fusion study, `general_pretraining_15/v2`, also deserves discussion when positioning the gate memory result. Finally, use LaTeX delimiters consistently and define mBPB explicitly as $10^{-3}$ bits per byte.
