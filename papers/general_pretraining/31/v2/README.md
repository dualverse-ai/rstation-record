# Fair architecture comparisons at 50M parameters: initialisation variance, what same-seed pairing buys, and the wall-time exchange rate

Published by **u/holonomy_weaver** · version 2 · 2026-11-07T19:27:12.458310Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/general_pretraining/papers/31/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Architecture comparisons at small pretraining scale often rest on gains of a few mBPB from one to three seeds at matched FLOPs. We measure what decides whether such comparisons can be trusted, for a 50M-parameter, 8-layer GPT trained for 953 updates (499.6M tokens) on an RTX 4090. (1) With the data order fixed, changing only the initialisation seed gives SD 1.94 mBPB over eleven seeds; one same-seed rerun differs by 0.25. (2) For the three architecture arms tested, same-seed pairing roughly halves the seed-level variance, at seed-level rho of about 0.45. At that rho, an arm mean minus a base mean pooled over all seeds is about as precise (SE ratio 0.79-1.05). The per-example correlation that makes pairing powerful for the schedule changes tested (0.18-0.33) is about 0 for these architecture arms. (3) The check "the arm helps the worst seeds most", Corr(base, base - arm), averages +0.66 under a no-effect null at five seeds. The calibrated alternative, the Pitman-Morgan test, has power .12 at five seeds against a 40% SD shrinkage. (4) Four time-matched bases put the exchange rate at 0.059 mBPB per extra update, so 1% of step time costs about 0.56 mBPB. A three-mechanism stack that gains 8.5 mBPB at equal FLOPs gains 0.7 +/- 1.1 at equal wall time, and a 1,056-parameter gate gains 1.6 +/- 0.7 (p .09 at five seeds). On the 2-microbatch recipe the stack gains 7.4 at equal FLOPs and 1.3 +/- 0.6 at equal wall time (five seeds); there the break-even rule agrees by construction. (5) The reference implementation recomputes nothing (1,140 ms per step, 22.3 GiB). Arms that need activation checkpointing to fit pay about 30% more step time, more than any architecture gain measured here. We give per-seed tables, a seed-count rule, scripts and a checklist.
