# Accept

This is a careful and technically useful study of batch size and momentum dynamics in Muon pretraining. Its strongest contribution is methodological: it identifies the feedback term that makes products of successive training gradients non-identifiable as population-gradient correlations, and supplies a discarded, data-disjoint probe together with randomized-gradient checks. The within-update split-batch estimator and the probe identities in M2 are correct under the stated conditional-independence/common-mean assumptions. The scalar counterexample makes clear why the naive estimator can have the wrong sign. The randomized-packer validation, replay checks, and synthetic tests provide meaningful support for those assumptions, while the manuscript explicitly limits the probe's distributional scope.

The central dynamical observation is supported by the supplied records. In the depth-8 probe run, the stable-phase lag-one correlations are about $-0.82$ to $-0.86$ with narrow block-bootstrap intervals, and the reported $(1+\rho_1)/2$ is correctly described as an adjacent-averaging energy share rather than a uniquely identified slow component. The manuscript also correctly separates the Muon-block secant from a full-update line-minimum measurement and reports the latter independently. This resolves the earlier cross-term and self-noise problems. The mode-mixture calculation is presented as a model-dependent interpretation, with its failure used to narrow the claim rather than to assert a universal landscape explanation.

The intervention evidence is substantial for the stated regime. Pointwise lengthening of momentum memory is reproducibly harmful at depth 8 on two seeds and transfers to depth 6; the influence-matched control and the late-only and cooldown controls prevent a simple learning-rate redistribution explanation. The batch-size study has independent implementations, held-out confirmation, multiple seeds for the main comparisons, and checks at 26M, 50M, and 135M parameters. The decay-per-token quarter-batch gains are about $0.012$--$0.014$ BPB at the three depths, and the held-out deltas agree with validation. Horizon experiments show that the advantage shrinks at $2\times$ and $4\times$ tokens, while the fork experiments compare branches from the same complete optimizer state and show that an early window's immediately banked value is much larger than its retained endpoint value. These are relevant, reproducible observations for the supplied pretraining problem, even though they do not produce a new optimizer.

The paper is appropriately distinguished from the indexed prior work. Muon scaling and critical-batch papers [4, 15, 19], WSD batch schedulers [16, 17], the LMO hyperparameter bounds [3], and the edge-of-stability study [5] provide adjacent theory or measurements, but do not provide this feedback-free temporal estimator or the retained-window and nominal-decay analysis. The authors credit the known weight-decay scaling rule [6] and state that their result is a narrower empirical characterization. The contribution is therefore a useful empirical principle and diagnostic rather than a cosmetic variation of an existing optimizer.

Several qualifications should remain prominent in a final version. The feedback-free correlation profile is directly measured in one depth-8 seed; the randomized validation is a depth-6 run, and the depth-8 profile is not independently validated. The headline wording should therefore continue to say “in this recipe/regime” rather than imply a universal property of Muon. “Decay-matched” is nominal: the supplement reports a 13--23% difference in realized cautious-decay integrals between $k=1$ and $k=4$. Sensitivity to matching realized rather than nominal shrinkage would strengthen the causal interpretation of the batch result. The full-state fork mechanism is based on one depth-6 trunk and a small number of data-order twins; it rules out inheritance from earlier history and one equilibrium-norm route, but does not identify whether slow-direction progress, polar-map nonlinearity, or another state effect carries the gain. The manuscript generally says this, and should avoid stronger causal language in the abstract or title. Finally, $k=1$ is the microbatch boundary and the smaller-batch runs incur extra optimizer work; the reported compute accounting and boundary qualification should be retained.

The bundled numerical evidence is internally consistent. Recomputing the main BPB differences from the recorded validation and held-out JSON files gives the quoted values (for example, the depth-8 $k=2$, WD$\times0.5$ contrast is $-0.00913$ validation and $-0.00922$ held out), and the diagnostic summary reproduces the probe table, alignment ledger, and fork values. The code, sufficient statistics, preregistrations, run configurations, and provenance are adequate for an independent reproduction subject to the stated data and hardware requirements. The remaining limitations concern external generality and mechanism resolution, rather than a materially unsupported central claim.
