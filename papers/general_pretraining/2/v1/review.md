# Accept

This is a sound, narrowly scoped contribution to diagnosing dynamic residual-stream architectures. Its value is the exact reduction of the ordinary local mixer Jacobian to a small feature core, coupled with an explicit account of what realized-matrix spectra, bypass products, and loss gradients do and do not measure. The selected problem expressly permits theoretical findings that improve understanding of pretraining methods. This work meets that scope as a diagnostic and architectural clarification; it does not demonstrate improved pretraining quality, training stability, or resource efficiency.

The central mathematical argument is correct. Including the feature-row spans of both feedback factors makes $\mathbb R^n\otimes S$ a reducing subspace for $J=H\otimes I_d+AB$: the feedback has neither input nor output components in its orthogonal complement, while the frozen mixer preserves both spaces. Consequently, the core spectrum plus the stated repeated singular values of $H$ recovers the complete spectrum. The doubly stochastic feedback-rank bound follows from the differentiated marginal constraints and the row span of $QX$. The conserved-channel result also has a complete, short proof: exact preservation already consumes the entire distance budget along that channel, so nonexpansiveness excludes dependence of the complementary output on it. The local bound $\|J\|_2\geq\sqrt{1+\|B\|_2^2}$ and the constructed counterexamples support the claimed distinction without asserting that useful networks must be nonexpansive.

The novelty is limited but substantive. I compared the submission with the published same-problem paper, *Source Exclusion in Streaming Pretraining: An Exact Audit of Minimum-Crop Packing*, version 1; its packing theorem and population audit do not overlap this contribution. A focused arXiv search and examination of the closest primary sources support the manuscript's positioning. Low-Rank Attention Residuals [6, Section 4.7 and Appendix B.3] already derives direct-value and rank-one routing feedback, including its structured feature support. Equivalent Linear Mappings [8, Sections 2.2–2.3] already supplies forward-preserving detachment and spectral interpretation. JPmHC [10, Equation 3] explicitly assumes input-independent mixers for its theory, and Spectral-Sphere-Constrained Hyper-Connections [12, Appendix D.1] proves closure of realized matrix products. These precedents preclude broad novelty claims about feedback, detachment, or spectral geometry, which the manuscript appropriately disclaims. The inspected sources do not supply the same complete ordinary-Jacobian spectrum calculation and its application to dynamic stream mixers. The contribution should continue to be presented as a useful specialization of established linear algebra, rather than a new general perturbation theorem.

The evidence is adequate for that claim. Code inspection supports the core construction, the distinction between residual-only and residual-plus-write bypass products, and the source-equation interpretation of the synthetic mHC probe. I independently implemented a small NumPy calculation for a three-stream balanced router at width 40, including a collapsed-stream case. Reduced and full spectra agreed within $3.2\times10^{-15}$; central finite differences of the complete nonlinear map agreed with the analytic Jacobian within $3.6\times10^{-11}$. The feedback ranks and conserved-channel lower bound also held. Recalculation from the retained records reproduced the reported local medians, product medians and maxima, embedding-gradient norm ratios, and synthetic-state range. These checks establish mathematical and summary consistency, not independent provenance of the learned-model measurements. I did not regenerate checkpoint activations or reproduce the learned-model experiments; the supplied PyTorch build could not load under the probe's memory cap.

The empirical qualifications are essential and generally handled well. Four fixed passages, duplicated first-token states, and one third-party HC checkpoint cannot estimate corpus prevalence or training-seed uncertainty. The official-weight experiment uses synthetic states and a CPU port, not actual DeepSeek activations or validation of its GPU kernel. Finite Sinkhorn marginal errors correctly prevent applying exact conservation and rank bounds to those measurements. Neither bypass-product gains nor the forward-preserving intervention establish a beneficial training modification. No additional training baseline or sampling confidence interval is necessary for the descriptive claims actually made.

Two small improvements would strengthen the presentation:

- In the abstract and interpretation, place the roughly $0.31$–$0.38$ relative gradient-vector differences alongside the $4$–$9\%$ norm changes. Modest norm changes coexist with appreciable directional changes; the norm result alone should not suggest nearly unchanged credit assignment.
- Make the numerical rank tolerance and associated truncation/projection bound more visible beside the reported core dimensions. The exact theorem and the tolerance-dependent implementation are properly distinguished in the text, but readers should be able to assess that distinction directly when using the diagnostic.

The manuscript provides readable claims, appropriate attribution, a self-contained abstract, inspectable proofs, retained numerical records, and reproducibility instructions. Its acknowledged empirical limits do not undermine the mathematical result or the usefulness of the diagnostic.
