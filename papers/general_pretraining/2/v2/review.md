# Accept

This update is acceptable. It makes the two presentation changes requested in the prior review and does not alter the contribution, methods, numerical records, or supplementary artifacts.

The added gradient-vector statistics are accurate and materially improve interpretation. The retained records give stopped/actual embedding-gradient norm ratios of $0.9089$–$0.9613$, relative differences of $0.3067$–$0.3750$, and cosines of $0.9272$–$0.9518$, with identical forward logits. Reporting these beside the 4–9% norm changes correctly shows that a modest norm effect can coexist with a meaningful directional change. The manuscript continues to state the appropriate limitation: this is a frozen, descriptive intervention on four fixed passages and does not demonstrate a useful training rule or improved pretraining.

The numerical-rank clarification is also sound. The code uses $	au=10^{-12}$ for the successive SVD truncations and feature-span selection, so the reported dimensions are tolerance-dependent numerical dimensions rather than consequences of the exact invariant-subspace theorem. The new perturbation expression is consistent with the implementation: discarded factor components are bounded by the retained-factor norms and discarded singular-value tails, while the two projection terms use Frobenius products. Weyl's inequality then gives a valid singular-value perturbation bound. The stated maxima agree with the retained records: $2.04\times10^{-12}$ over 1,152 public-HC records and $5.47\times10^{-12}$ over 72 synthetic-state records, rounded upward. The explicit exclusion of floating-point roundoff and disclaimer that these are not interval certificates are important and sufficient.

The synthetic mHC scope remains correctly qualified. These are synthetic states evaluated with a CPU port of the published equations, not captured activations or validation of the GPU kernel. Finite Sinkhorn marginal errors still prevent imposing exact doubly-stochastic rank or conservation conclusions. The learned-HC measurements remain descriptive observations from one checkpoint and four fixed passages; no new generalization or quality claim is introduced.

The complete prior assessment therefore remains applicable: the exact feature-core reduction and conserved-channel argument are mathematically coherent, the evidence supports a diagnostic contribution, and the limitations, attribution, and reproducibility material are appropriate. No full re-review of unchanged accepted material is needed.
