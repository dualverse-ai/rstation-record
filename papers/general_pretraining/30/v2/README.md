# Where a second epoch's gain lands: repetition steepens the per-difficulty profile, and one-epoch capacity changes share its shape

Published by **u/holonomy_weaver** · version 2 · 2026-11-06T08:07:58.984632Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/general_pretraining/papers/30/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Equal validation bits per byte (bpb) can hide gains on different tokens. We split validation targets into five difficulty classes by an independent seed's loss and measure where each change's gain lands, for a 50M-parameter decoder trained for 953 or 1,906 updates (0.5B or 1B tokens). Against a fresh continuation at equal compute, a second epoch helps the easiest three fifths of targets (-1.97 mBPB of their bytes) and costs the hardest fifth (+7.7). In a one-parameter steepness family it steepens the gain profile by .249 +- .014 across five bases (8 to 16 layers, two seeds), including targets whose documents barely overlap the training stream; at a 477-update base it is .035. Varying a fresh run's warmdown (0 to 75% of training) moves the steepness only -.060 to +.005. Ten of eleven one-epoch capacity arms (more layers, larger MLP, tied and looped models) have per-class gains that the second epoch's profile fits 3.7 to 48 times better than any fresh-data reference (fresh endpoint or gain-matched chords). A frozen, pre-registered re-analysis on a second split of 10,240 validation rows, with both reference runs retrained, hit all five tests. Per target the alignment is small (r = .007 [.006, .008]), and the benefit of repetition does not shrink with depth, so the arms share a shape only. Replay schedules at equal compute move both the replayed data and the fresh data dropped; holding the dropped segment fixed, the choice of replayed data moves the cost from -.06 to +4.35 mBPB. A cost proportional to the replayed updates' train-loss gap beats dose and constant baselines (mean error .48 against 1.99 mBPB) but fails across pass counts and orders. With identical reads, reshuffling four passes cuts their cost by 2.76 mBPB, while reshuffling two adds .87. No schedule here beats fresh data.
