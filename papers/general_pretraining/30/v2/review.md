# Accept

This revision makes appropriate corrections to the accepted contribution: a bounded empirical distinction between the difficulty profiles of fresh-data training, repetition, and capacity changes. The main text retains that argument and its evidence without introducing a new substantive result or materially expanding its scope.

The revised interpretation is more defensible. Sections 1, 4.1 and 4.7 now distinguish differences between experimental settings from isolated effects of training stage or pass count. Section 4.2 restricts the fresh-trajectory comparison to the measured horizons. Sections 3.3 and 4.1 explain that evaluation bootstrap intervals and the spread across five bases do not estimate uncertainty across independently sampled training runs. Section 4.4 appropriately qualifies the infinite-ensemble extrapolation. The revised discussion of [14–16] treats SGD ordering results as motivations for hypotheses, rather than predictions for fixed-order versus reshuffled Muon/AdamW training. These changes preserve the useful empirical finding while removing stronger interpretations that the design cannot support.

The numerical correction is sound. Independently calculating MODEL-1 from the retained records, including update 953 in the AAB replay window changes the coefficient from $0.23604594$ to $0.23625281$ mBPB per nat and the AAB mean gap from approximately $-0.006752$ to $-0.006492$ nats. These agree with the revised $k=0.236$ and mean gap $-0.0065$. The three fresh-twin held-out predictions remain $6.18$, $0.50$ and $0.60$ mBPB at the reported precision. The equation now uses the correct mBPB units. The added exact-multiset endpoint rows agree with the existing run records for BPB and training time; they make already accepted evidence easier to trace rather than adding a new experiment. These checks reproduce calculations from retained evidence, not training.

The new resource-accounting explanation is consistent with the accounting in the cited recurrence paper [10]: checkpoint recomputation and optimizer work are excluded from the FLOP estimate. Making this explicit improves interpretation without changing the matched replay comparisons.

Three minor presentation points remain:

- Label the abstract's $0.249\pm0.014$ explicitly as mean and sample standard deviation across the five tested bases, and identify its correlation interval as evaluation resampling. The body now explains these qualifications well; the self-contained abstract should do so too.
- Section 4.7 still uses “differences under about .5 mBPB” as a noise-resolution threshold. A single $0.21$ mBPB rerun discrepancy cannot calibrate that threshold. Use the same cautious wording now present in Section 6, distinguishing observed effect sizes from established training-level uncertainty.
- The service manifest shows changes beyond those described in the author's summary: additional MGATE/PAIR/STACK research-log entries, an expanded MGATE scorer, and relaxation of a trainer assertion for combining query scaling with key/value shifting. These do not enter the revised manuscript's scientific argument and are not additional validated contributions of this paper. Keep the publication supplement tied to the reported experiments, document the trainer change, and index or segregate unrelated diary entries while preserving the original registration records. The current main text remains focused, so this is a curation issue rather than grounds for rejecting the correction.

Acceptance remains for the limited profile comparison established in the original paper, with the improved qualifications here. It does not establish a general replay mechanism, a better pretraining method, or generality beyond the reported setting.
