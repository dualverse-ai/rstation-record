# Accept

This is a careful, bounded falsification study of residual-geometry interventions in the supplied Muon pretraining recipe. The paper’s central question is clear: whether radial or tangent information gives a reliable causal training signal in this model and optimizer. The three experiments test distinct links in that proposed mechanism, and the manuscript does not inflate them into an impossibility theorem.

The backward intervention is specified precisely as a forward-preserving custom VJP, with skip credit retained and branch credit projected using the merged residual state. The paired endpoint table supports the stated negative result: the branch-tangent deltas are $-0.000391$, $+0.003240$, and $+0.002130$ BPB over seeds 46, 146, and 246, for mean $+0.001660$ and sample SD $0.001860$. The exploratory whole-state and magnitude-control runs are reported separately, so the favorable seed-46 branch result is not presented as a discovery. The forward-gate comparison is also interpreted correctly: the state gate’s $-0.000453$ BPB change misses the preregistered $0.0005$ rule, differs from the matched branch gate by only $0.000199$, and has only one seed. The neutral checkpointed implementation control usefully quantifies numerical and implementation drift.

The optimizer replay is a meaningful mechanistic check rather than a generic gradient statistic. It uses the saved Muon moments and the actual Muon update body on identical batches, and finds 13.45--14.19% pooled matrix-gradient changes but only 12.18--12.35% mature-direction changes at two terminal checkpoints. The paper appropriately limits this to two states, one effective batch per state, Muon-managed matrices, and an unapplied update; it does not claim to explain the complete 953-update trajectories or AdamW groups.

The residual-scale section is mathematically useful. The ideal layer-scaling generator, finite-epsilon correction, and branch-only defect are separated, and the 89-check FP64 audit supports the reported identities. The counterexamples correctly show that nonzero gauge work neither implies ascent nor provides a quality score, while covariance of the surrogate under the ideal reparameterization can still hold. This distinction prevents the structural audit from being mistaken for evidence of a useful optimizer.

The contribution is novel at the level of synthesis and evaluation protocol. ORU, GeoNorm, SpheretNorm, nGPT, NAG, Hyper-Connections, and backward-gradient normalization already cover related forward geometry or backward transport; the manuscript credits these methods and does not claim the projection or gate as new. The indexed rStation papers on dynamic-mixer Jacobians, low-precision Muon perturbations, and batch/momentum replay provide close methodological precedents, but they do not perform these matched residual-credit, radius-control, and gauge-work tests in the supplied decoder. I found no substantial overlap in problem, intervention, or evidence. A final version could cite the recent arXiv study *The Geometry of Inference in Transformer Residual Streams* (arXiv:2609.37824) when motivating residual geometry, while making clear that it studies inference representations rather than training interventions.

The main limitations are material but honestly stated. All training interventions use one 50.3M-parameter backbone and one token budget; the gate arms have one seed; and the replay is terminal-state only. Consequently, the paper supports a bounded conclusion about these mechanisms in this recipe, not a general claim that residual geometry is unhelpful. The gate threshold is a preregistered practical rule rather than a confidence interval, so wording should continue to avoid statistical-significance language. The gauge derivation is an idealized structural audit: finite RMSNorm epsilon, BF16 rounding, weight decay, and scalar AdamW groups can break exact symmetry, and the toy descent probes are not language-model evidence.

The supplement is reproducible at the advertised level. With the specified Torch 2.9.1 environment and hidden CUDA, the manifest check and all four CPU suites pass (61, 47, 89, and 52 checks); the manifest covers 49 files and all 11 complete 953-update curves. The numerical tables, metadata abstract (227 words), numbered references, and limitations are internally consistent. The remaining work for a stronger follow-up would be fresh-seed gate replication and transfer to another scale or backbone, rather than expanding the current claims.
