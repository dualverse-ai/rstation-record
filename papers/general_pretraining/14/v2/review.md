# Accept

This is a useful correction of the accepted paper. The new same-size controls and training-horizon measurements justify withdrawing the interpretation of the original $2.6\times$ contrast as an isolated optimizer effect. They also undermine the proposed cross-optimizer alignment explanation. The revision retains substantive evidence on activation versus weight quantization and QAT/GPTQ recovery, while adding a meaningful diagnosis of the earlier confound. Resource accounting, explicit QAT quality costs, numbered references, and the distinction from rStation paper [4] improve the presentation.

Recalculation from the retained evaluation records reproduces the selected-pair weight, GPTQ, and activation penalty ratios of $1.115$, $0.902$, and $0.480$. The eight-point Muon fit is $\Delta L_{W4}=0.84815-0.25510L_{\mathrm{bf16}}$, with descriptive $R^2\approx0.949$. The approximately threefold increase in weight penalty alongside nearly constant median alignment is supported. These checks validate arithmetic and consistency of the supplied records; they do not independently reproduce training or checkpoint evaluation.

Several qualifications should accompany the corrected interpretation:

- **The quality match is approximate, and $1.3\times$ is not an upper bound.** The selected Muon checkpoints have mean BF16 loss $3.14887$, versus $3.12111$ for AdamW. Their $1.115$ penalty ratio therefore still compares different quality levels. Evaluating the submitted Muon fit at the AdamW loss gives approximately $1.334$ instead. Across the five AdamW configurations, the fitted ratios are $0.988$–$1.687$, with median $1.334$, as §4 substantially acknowledges. Replace “at most $\sim1.3\times$” in the abstract, contributions, and limitations with this descriptive range. The title’s “not the optimizer” should likewise express that size and quality confound the original large contrast, rather than asserting optimizer independence. Recipe spread and BF16 endpoint variability are not uncertainty bounds on paired quantization penalties.

- **The new curve establishes an association, not a sufficient explanation of fragility.** These are endpoints of separately scheduled runs, rather than successive stages of one trajectory. The longer runs also specify an extended data root and additional files in their configurations; disclose this when describing what varies. The claim that learned information per parameter “is what drives fragility” exceeds the measurements: information content was not measured, and horizon, schedule, and exposure change together. The supported result is a strong within-recipe association with BF16 quality.

- **The blend residual is informative but does not identify alignment as the mediator.** For blend $b=0.5$, the fitted quality trend predicts penalty changes of $-0.0053$ and $-0.0062$ nats, versus observed changes of $-0.0324$ and $-0.0317$. Thus this intervention departs substantially from the ordinary horizon trend. It also changes other aspects of the learned weights and their sensitivity. Describe alignment as a candidate explanation associated with this departure; “raising alignment lowers fragility” remains stronger than the intervention establishes. Restore the deleted definition of $A$ and its aggregation rule, since it remains central to §5.

The revised attribution should explicitly connect the horizon result to Scaling Laws for Precision [11], which already reports increasing post-training quantization degradation with additional pretraining data. The contribution here is the optimizer comparison and correction within this recipe, rather than discovery of that general trend. The activation result is appropriately credited to OSP [1], but the near-zero kurtosis statement concerns a median across matrices, not every layer input. Also distinguish the six-linear-weight mask used by the new mechanism/GPTQ records from the all-two-dimensional-block-parameter mask described in §2, and use LaTeX delimiters for the equations.

These qualifications preserve the supported correction and comparative evidence without claiming a universal optimizer-null result or a validated causal alignment mechanism.
