# Accept

The revision improves the interpretation and reproducibility of the accepted empirical contribution without materially expanding the manuscript's claims. The central argument remains clear: reallocating part of Chorus's memory budget to contextual token rows improves prediction against the tested learning-rate baselines, with limited evidence about why contextual reads help. The existing distinction from related work [2], [3], and [7] is preserved. The main text remains organized around this argument rather than becoming a collection of additional experiments.

The substantive corrections are appropriate. Sections 2 and 5 qualify optimization as a finite search, and the abstract and introduction now distinguish the cosine-router comparison from the fresh confirmation results. Section 7 correctly describes the frozen-router experiments as joint-training contrasts, identifies both upstream gradient pathways removed by detachment, and withdraws the inappropriate uncertainty comparison. Section 8 now defines the per-occurrence estimand as total loss change divided by affected occurrences, explains pooling across read sites and replay outside the intervention quintile, and limits the interpretation to sensitivity to context-matched reads. Section 12 removes the unsupported explanation of the difference between memory budgets. These changes strengthen scientific accuracy without requiring new evidence.

The retained evidence supports those corrections. All 74 previously supplied run records and configurations are unchanged. Running the inspected collection script on a working copy regenerated exactly the retained `numbers.json`. Independent arithmetic gives cosine minus binary routing of $-1.998 \pm 0.307$ mBPB on validation and $-1.944 \pm 0.312$ on the secondary split, with uncertainty calculated across seeds. The retained intervention records agree with the revised normalization and frequency pattern. Source inspection supports the revised descriptions of pooling, replay, and microbatch EMA updates. I did not reproduce training or checkpoint interventions; checkpoints and per-row evaluation arrays remain unavailable, as the revised reproduction section now states explicitly.

There is a discrepancy in the change summary worth correcting. Despite “No new experiments,” the supplement adds two single-seed bigram-keyed bank runs and a second seed for the 76M reference-recipe binary bank. It also adds optional `bigram` and `token_prevhash` addressing code and further experiment plans. The original token-addressing path remains the default, and these additions do not change the reported manuscript comparisons. I regard the extra records as limited exploratory/supporting material, not an established new substantive contribution. They do not warrant a separate thin paper or reversal of acceptance. Clearly label their status and keep conclusions about bigram routing confined to the tested configuration; the roughly $0.08$ mBPB difference between its two new arms does not establish a general absence of routing benefit.

Minor remaining corrections would make the revision more precise:

- In Section 2, distinguish the fitted minimum near $0.33\times$ from the best evaluated multiplier, $0.35\times$. A quadratic interpolation is not itself a tested rate.
- In the abstract, replace “Controls separate two parts of the routing effect” with wording consistent with Section 7's joint-training qualification, and identify those controls as single-seed results.
- Reconcile Section 11's “71 runs in the supplement” and compute accounting with the 77 retained run entries, distinguishing reported comparisons from exploratory runs. Update the change summary accordingly.
- Make the configuration-extraction and training commands share an explicit path: extraction at the supplement root creates `cfg.json` there, whereas training from `code/chorus_lk` requires `../../cfg.json` or an absolute path. The opening provenance sentence also still uses the author's workspace paths rather than the delivered supplement paths.

These are bounded reporting and usability issues. The revised central explanation and the evidence supporting the accepted contribution remain sound within the stated model, corpus, optimization, and replication limits.
