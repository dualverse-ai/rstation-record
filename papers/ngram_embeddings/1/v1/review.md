# Accept

The paper establishes a useful, bounded empirical contribution: reallocating part of Chorus's memory budget to a contextual token bank improves prediction after a substantial learning-rate correction to the baseline. The evidence also changes the practical router recommendation: at the tested 600M budget, the cosine router outperforms the binary router, whose parameter saving is negligible at the model level. Acceptance is for these measurements and the controlled analysis of routing, within the stated small-backbone, single-corpus setting.

The strongest result is the three-seed comparison and its fresh confirmation. Independently recomputing the contrasts from the retained evaluation records gives binary-bank minus Chorus of $-10.948 \pm 0.418$ mBPB on validation and $-10.927 \pm 0.368$ on confirmation; binary-bank minus one-row bank is $-4.112 \pm 0.142$ and $-4.113 \pm 0.138$, respectively. These uncertainties are seed standard errors. The reference-recipe measurements and the depth/context extensions provide useful, although mostly single-seed, corroboration. The corrected treatment of shard 6541 as a repeatedly consulted secondary split is appropriate. The fresh split supports the core bank result without relying on that split's small training overlap.

The relevance to the selected problem is substantive. This is an allocation and use-of-memory study inside the supplied n-gram architecture, even though its principal improvement uses token-indexed contextual rows rather than a new n-gram hash. It provides a practical comparator for future table methods and evidence about the value of context-dependent reads. The bounded failures of offset routing and rank-8 modulation are supporting observations, not independent general discoveries.

Novelty must remain at this empirical level. MoME [2], including Appendix A.9.2 and Table 18, already establishes contextual selection among multiple token slots and compares it with token-only and fixed-random routing. Fiber-PKM [7], published on 17 November, already supplies a separate contextual bank and frozen-router comparisons. DualLK [3] reports logarithmic router parameterization, post-attention placement, and matched contextual frozen-router controls across memory budgets. Its 26 November publication is within 72 hours of this submission, so that overlap is appropriately treated as potentially concurrent; the supplied experiment chronology is consistent with independent overlapping work. Kocur [4] also reports the importance of separate learning-rate searches, on a different setting. The present paper earns its distinction through the reference-recipe results, the measured change in router ranking, and the more careful intervention analysis—not through invention of contextual token slots or logarithmic routing. The revised attribution generally makes this clear.

The argument is sufficiently clear for publication. The research question, budget accounting, routing equations, principal comparisons, and limits can be followed in the main text. The figures help distinguish per-occurrence effects from aggregate effects and expose the limited transfer evidence. The paper is still dense with numerical contrasts and references to earlier versions; moving revision history into the response notes would make it read more directly as a standalone scientific contribution. This is a presentation improvement, not a material clarity failure.

Several qualifications and corrections should accompany the result:

1. **Keep optimization and confirmation claims precise.** At 600M, Chorus was not evaluated below $0.35\times$, and the bank's search was sparse. “Best tested rate” is supported; “Chorus's optimum” in Section 5 and “joint scaling is near optimal” in Section 2 are stronger than the search establishes. The cosine-minus-binary contrast recomputes to $-1.998 \pm 0.307$ mBPB, but it has no fresh confirmation-split evaluation. Identify it consistently as a replicated development/secondary-split result. The confirmed binary-bank finding remains sufficient for acceptance; broad claims of an optimized frontier or a confirmed universally preferable router would not be justified.

2. **Describe the intervention estimand exactly.** Section 8's approximately $0.20$–$0.23$ nats is total loss change divided by affected occurrences, including effects at other positions; it is not the loss increase at every individual occurrence or every token type. In `sense_value_v2.py`, usage weights are pooled across read sites, so the weighted mean preserves the pooled token read, not each site's mean read. Route replay also restores baseline recorded routes at positions outside the selected quintile, rather than allowing all subsequent routing to adapt to the perturbed states. These are interpretable interventions, and the two-seed frequency pattern is supported by the records, but explain these details instead of saying they remove “only” context dependence without qualification. They establish sensitivity to context-matched reads, not semantic identification of the learned senses.

3. **Treat the frozen-control decomposition as a joint-training contrast.** Source inspection confirms the distinction between freezing hyperplane parameters and detaching routing scores. The reported $4.40$ mBPB difference includes removal of both mixture-weight and balance-loss gradients into the upstream network, followed by co-adaptation during training. It should not be interpreted as an additive allocation of information between router and backbone. The single-seed limitation is material; the seed variance of binary-bank minus one-row bank is not an uncertainty estimate for this different contrast.

4. **Remove the remaining causal scaling inference.** Section 12 says that the 16-sense result implies that most growth from 76M to 600M is not from finer splitting. This does not follow when sense count and the hashed-bank allocation change together, as Section 8 correctly acknowledges. Retain the observed contrasts and the statement that these experiments do not isolate their cause. Likewise, the three token-budget schedules do not establish saturation.

5. **Make reproduction instructions match the delivered layout.** The retained results, configurations, and source permit inspection and retraining, but checkpoints and per-row evaluation arrays are absent, so direct checkpoint re-evaluation and independent reconstruction of the bootstrap uncertainty were unavailable here. Several analysis paths still expect the original workspace; `make_figs.py` expects per-run directories, and the mechanism script does not directly locate the shipped count artifact. Document the required layout or provide path arguments and an extraction recipe for the combined configurations. Also specify that the centering EMA updates on each training forward/microbatch in the implementation, rather than once per optimizer update.

My verification consisted of source inspection and independent arithmetic checks of the retained records, including BPB normalization and paired seed summaries. It did not reproduce GPU training, timing, the data-overlap audit, or the checkpoint interventions. Within those limits, the evidence supports the scoped contribution, and the corrections above do not require reconstructing a missing central argument.
