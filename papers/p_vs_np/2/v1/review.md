# Accept

This manuscript makes substantive, clearly delimited advances on exponential-threshold MCSP. Its principal contribution is an unconditional lower bound on the time needed to materialize a globally exact reduction from high-threshold MCSP to low-threshold MCSP in the specified one-work-tape model. The additional bounds for syntactic read-once nondeterministic and arbitrary oblivious read-two branching programs are meaningful restricted-model results. These contributions are relevant to the supplied problem through hardness magnification and its limitations. They do not resolve $P$ versus $NP$, and the manuscript explicitly maintains that distinction.

The central argument in Theorem 3.1 is sound. Each fixed-output monitor requires only $O(N')$ states and constant simulation overhead; global exactness supplies the pointwise identity $\mathbf 1_A=\sum_{y\in Q(A)}D_y$. Applying one universal generator at sufficiently small error to these monitors charges the number of occupied YES outputs through $\log M$, rather than through the state count at every decomposition boundary. The contradiction between sparsity of $A$ under uniform inputs and small circuit complexity of every generator output gives the claimed tradeoff. I checked the published STACS version of [2], particularly Lemma 20 and Theorem 21: it explicitly supports growing nonuniform state counts and the required dependence on time and error. Since the monitors have polynomially many states, those statements also directly support the tradeoff up to the polylogarithmic factors suppressed here. Corollary 3.3 then correctly combines $N'\le O(t)$ with the circuit-counting bound for the target YES set, including the endpoint $\mu_L=1/2$.

The branching-program arguments also withstand inspection. In Theorem 4.1, syntactic read-once behavior makes the unions of prefix and suffix variable sets disjoint at a cut node. Sparsity supplies the near-balanced sizes needed by the hash argument. The fixed-seed mixing inequality and the bounded-independence moment calculation produce one distribution that controls every covering rectangle; no unjustified union bound over all partitions is needed. In Theorem 4.6, the separated-signature argument, the matching-based coloring bound, and the shared selector across three shifts fit together correctly. In particular, the section-graph calculation in (63) and the first-moment estimate after excluding prefix collisions supply the positive entropy surplus that the counting argument requires. The promise construction in Section 3.4 usefully explains why global exactness, rather than physical transport on a restricted grid alone, is decisive.

The contribution is distinct from the relevant prior work. I compared the supplied version of [12], including its conditional composition statements and special switch quotient, and the publicly available version of [11], including its entropy construction and ordered-width argument. Their results are appropriately attributed and used as inputs. Theorem 3.1 extends beyond the special quotients and composition assumptions in [12]; the variable-order and repeated-read obstacles addressed in Section 4 are absent from [11]. The external comparison with [2], the published statement of Glinskih–Riazanov [3], and focused arXiv searches supports the stated distinction from earlier one-tape and linear-threshold read-once results. Volk [10] concerns a different explicit function and parity model. I found no substantial prior duplication of the new results in this focused check.

The evidence is primarily analytical, as it should be for these claims. Both supplied verification scripts ran successfully under the review resource limits. They reproduce the finite arithmetic, combinatorial examples, section-graph checks, and numerical diagnostics reported in the supplement. These checks are useful corroboration, not verification of the asymptotic theorems or exhaustive tests of arbitrary schedules. The manuscript generally explains that boundary accurately.

Clarity is sufficient for acceptance independently of correctness: the models, contribution, proof strategies, and limits are identifiable, and the decisive arguments are in the main text. The following minor improvements would make the paper easier to assess without changing its scientific contribution:

- Shorten the metadata abstract and reduce its concentration of constants and secondary method-boundary claims. Emphasize the fixed-output-monitor insight and the shared-selector construction before presenting detailed formulas.
- Move the lengthy normalization comparison in Section 2.2 and some multiplexer accounting in Proposition 4.2(1)(b) to supporting appendices. Keep the definitions, conclusions, and essential budget argument in the main text. A small schematic showing how the three shifted copies meet $P$, $Q$, and $C$ would help explain Theorem 4.6.
- In Lemma 3.4, replace the exact statement $\log_2(K(K-1))=2h$ by $2h+\log_2(1-2^{-h})=2h+O(1)$. This does not affect the argument.
- In the supplement README's toy example, distinguish the label-domain cardinality $K2^q=8$ from the parameter-family cardinality $2^{K2^q}=256$; the current description uses $|T|=256$ inconsistently with the manuscript. Also describe the pushforward calculation as a toy arithmetic illustration rather than a general computational verification of seed averaging.
- Qualify the fixed-schedule upper-bound statement for $s_{\min}$ to schedules on which the function is computable, or explicitly restrict that statement to covering schedules. The lower-bound argument for programs with omitted coordinates is separate and remains valid.

Acceptance is for these restricted-model results and the exact-materialization barrier, with the stated fixed-exponent, deterministic-reduction, and tape-interface assumptions. It does not establish a low-threshold one-tape decision lower bound or meet the problem's complete solve definition.
