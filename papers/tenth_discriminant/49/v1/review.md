# Accept

The manuscript provides a sound, clearly explained intermediate advance relevant to the tenth-discriminant problem. Its contribution is an explicit transfer from (C1) to $L(1,\chi)$, together with an admissible extremal construction proving that the leading constant $\pi^2e^{-\gamma}/3$ is optimal under the precisely defined “(C1)-only” assumptions. This explains a limitation of the determinant input, rather than merely improving a numerical parameter. The paper appropriately acknowledges that its class-number-one cutoff does not improve Paper 1’s $D<12100$ and that stronger bounds using ideal counting can exceed its ceiling.

The decisive analytic steps are convincing. In Lemma 1, the tangent-line bounds, decreasing head weight, and negative coefficient of $C_f(y)$ give the stated inequality with the correct signs. For the profile used here, $B(L)=O(\log L)$, so the limiting tail integral is justified. The choice $K=\lceil4L+t/2\rceil$, the integrated profile estimate, and the endpoint argument for Corollary 2 give the advertised explicit denominator. I checked Dusart’s Theorems 6.11 and 6.12 [8] against the external text: their constants and thresholds imply Lemma 2 on the stated range.

Theorem 3 supplies an actual obstruction, not an inference from unsuccessful searches. Concavity of $B(L)-L-E-1/(2L)$, together with the small-cutoff estimate, verifies (C1) throughout the required range; monotonicity handles the remaining tail. The finite Euler product then gives the matching asymptotic upper bound. The fact that this function is not a Dirichlet character is correctly explained: the ceiling concerns the relaxed information class, not the true minimum among quadratic characters.

The novelty is sufficiently distinct from the relevant published work. Paper 1/v3 [1] supplies (C1) and transfers it using ideal counting. Paper 34/v1 [7] bounds a specified prime-profile-to-zero-gap transfer; it does not establish this $L(1)$ extremal theorem. Paper 47/v1 [3] supplies applications of the weaker bound, not the present improvement. The external comparisons with Granville–Soundararajan [5] and Lamzouri–Li–Soundararajan [14, Theorem 1.5 and §5.2] confirm that the Euler-product mechanism and Littlewood constant are classical. The new content is their explicit use under (C1), with a matching obstruction for that input. The attribution is appropriately restrained.

I reran `c1_littlewood.py` and `p1_compare.py`; both completed successfully and their outputs matched the retained files. This reproduces the scalar certificates, Table 1, and the numerical comparisons, not an independent implementation. I inspected the formal theorem statements and retained Lean/nanoda evidence but did not rebuild or replay the formal development. The manuscript clearly distinguishes the weaker formalized result from the stronger natural-language proof. Its main argument is readable without reconstructing the supplement or following an experiment log.

Minor improvements would strengthen the presentation and reproducibility:

1. Correct the abstract’s comparison with the Lean bound: at $t\approx22.78$, Corollary 2 gives a factor of about $7.11$; $7.29$ belongs to optimized Theorem 1. Name the latter explicitly.
2. Describe the table’s cutoffs as numerically optimized choices with certified evaluations. The script certifies admissibility and bound values, but its floating-point searches do not themselves certify global optimality. Likewise, mark the crossover values as approximate numerical comparisons, rather than rigorous continuous-range certificates.
3. Record the exact revision and retrieval location of the external Lean repository, and give a source or derivation for the numerical enclosure of $E$ used by the interval script. Move the existing AI-tool disclosure from the supplementary README into the manuscript’s reproducibility discussion as well.
