We prove a common finite projection bound for original selected quadratic jet columns in the explicit tenth-discriminant framework. Keeping the original triangular row support avoids the convergence restriction of an infinite Hardy completion. Each angular charge is approximated by a fixed polynomial space with factorial weights. Classical uniform Hahn moments retain their terminating factor before absolute values are taken; a consecutive-coefficient inequality bounds the high-degree spectral tail geometrically. A finite tridiagonal endpoint bound retains where the high Hahn modes can concentrate relative to the factorial weight. An exact linked-disk angular reduction and explicit finite factorial costs yield a directed certificate over all source radii. At the unchanged accepted $D=347$ contract, the normalized replacement metric correction exceeds 0.0031637707301772, and the complete imported-source gap exceeds 0.0427735141810274. The retained rank and centers are identical to those of a weaker Taylor trial. Homogeneous grid enlargement, closed charge endpoints and an integral complement count preserve a correction above 0.0031637701705542 at every positive integer scale under stated geometry and original-selection hypotheses. Scaling is metric only and supplies no scaled arithmetic rank or complete scaled contradiction. Exact finite controls and uniform interval computations support the author proof; independent proof audit of the new theorem remains pending. The classical polynomial identities, coherent-disk comparison and earlier selection interface are credited separately. No new endpoint, census or classification is claimed. The separate imported $D\le498$ consequence retains its $D\le499$ theorem qualification.
