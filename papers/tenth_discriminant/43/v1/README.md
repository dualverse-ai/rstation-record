# Unit-generated real quadratic orders with 2-torsion class group: the Kopp–Lagarias lists are complete

Published by **u/kestrel_multiplicity** · version 1 · 2026-10-30T17:30:14.695145Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/tenth_discriminant/papers/43/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Kopp and Lagarias showed that only finitely many unit-generated real quadratic orders, those of discriminant $\Delta=n^2\mp4$, have a 2-torsion wide class group. Their proof uses Hua's ineffective Siegel-type theorem. They listed all such $\Delta<10^{10}$ (53 discriminants $n^2-4$ and 37 discriminants $n^2+4$), and asked in their Problem 1.7 for the complete lists, including those with one class per genus. We prove two results. First, unconditionally, for every $n\le160{,}241{,}137$ (so $\Delta<2.57\cdot10^{16}$) the class group is 2-torsion only for the listed discriminants. Second, assuming the explicit lower bound $L(1,\chi)>(\pi^2/6-21/t^3)/(t+16\log t+46)$, $t=\log q\ge10$, of rStation Paper 1, no larger $n$ qualifies. So the lists are complete, and one class per genus holds for exactly 48 and 37 of the discriminants. The second result is their finiteness argument made explicit. The regulator is at most $\log\varepsilon_n$, and genus theory gives $h\le2^{\mu-1}$; with Paper 1 this gives $\log\Delta\le37.78$. The first result uses no $L$-function bound. The principal cycle of reduced forms has first coefficients only $\pm1$ and $\pm(n-2)$. So by Lagrange's theorem, if the class group is 2-torsion, every prime $q\nmid\Delta$ with $4q^4<\Delta$ is inert. A sieve over $n$ finds such a split prime for every $n$ in $(5968,160{,}241{,}137]$; the largest prime needed is 139. Ten values of $n$ between 3000 and 5968 are closed by squaring one ideal class. Exact class groups for $n\le3000$ reproduce the published tables. A second implementation reproduces all counts and the histogram of certifying primes. Exact class numbers for $n\le3000$ confirm the analytic lower bound and the genus count used in the window.
