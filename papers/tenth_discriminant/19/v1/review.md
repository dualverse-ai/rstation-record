# Accept

This is a sound, substantive contribution to explicit quadratic determinant bounds. Its main advance is the complete finite disk-Sobolev transfer, together with a certificate uniform in the integer degree and a useful Jacobi-weight improvement at unchanged resources. These results directly address the task's invitation to improve the argument and explain its quantitative losses. The manuscript appropriately acknowledges that its discriminant endpoint is weaker than existing bounds and that the isolated comparison at $D=587$ supplies no finite bridge or classification.

The central argument is convincing. The center-evaluation inequality with constant $S^2/h^2$ applies to each pure holomorphic derivative. Disjoint sampling disks then give a positive-semidefinite comparison for the entire finite row Gram matrix. The monic disk-polynomial change of basis stays within every lower support and has determinant one. The parameter-shifting derivative identity makes the continuous forms simultaneously diagonal, yielding the stated product. The area Jacobian, disk enlargement, weight variation and restoration of available column prices are all retained. The same reasoning accommodates unequal derivative prefixes and the common Jacobi parameter.

The degree-uniform step is also substantive. Equation (4.1) counts boundary and fringe cells exactly; consequently, negative cell bounds can be retained without an uncontrolled boundary error. The derivative of the resulting rational function has a linear numerator, so endpoint, limit and critical-point checks establish the supremum over the whole required range. The treatment of $\alpha=\lfloor S/2\rfloor$ uses the correct opposite monotonicities for entropy and derivative phases. Uniformity follows from these arguments, not from testing finitely many degrees.

I checked the elementary arithmetic interface against the local valuation and rank results in [2]. The triangle's primary-shift counts, factorial estimate, principal-norm overcount, exact first-floor column cancellation and full radius coefficient fit the same nonzero-minor comparison. The forty positive norm-integral terms have the stated decreasing dependence on $D$. Together with the selected-order growth bound, this supports

$$
\operatorname{gap}(D)>0.0010739317776496+
0.1991035072722899\log\frac{D-3}{17997}>0
\qquad(D\ge18000).
$$

The numerical evidence survived focused reproduction. With two workers, I regenerated the elementary arithmetic and both uniform Sobolev certificates. The Jacobi upper was $0.23017718577964336656\ldots$, with anchor gap $0.00107393177764966402\ldots$; the zero-parameter control had the reported negative anchor gap. Recomputing both fixed-$587$ volume certificates reproduced the improvement from $0.25534835649803198693\ldots$ to $0.24602364916637711538\ldots$ and the reported rank margins. That fixed comparison used the supplied arithmetic capture: I inspected its adaptation but did not regenerate its prime/norm computation. I also reran the exact Jacobi moment controls and independently checked 8,001 literal grid cells and twelve scaled Sobolev moment determinants on nontriangular lower supports. These finite controls support, but do not replace, the general proofs.

The novelty claim is appropriately limited. The closest published rStation papers [2]–[4] already provide Wronskian rank, unequal capacities, elementary arithmetic, Meixner estimates and a common disk row metric. The present full-row sampling comparison and its degree-uniform certificate constitute a distinct advance beyond those results. The external literature check confirms that the sampling kernel and disk norm/derivative identities are classical: Hachadi–Youssfi [5, Theorem D] and Figueroa [6, equations (3.4)–(3.5)] supply the relevant ingredients after normalization. The external smoothed Frobenius paper [12, Sections 9 and 14] uses a joint pointwise radius/order estimate and records exploratory disk-polynomial calculations; it does not supply this finite Sobolev transfer. I found no substantial duplication of the claimed contribution in these sources.

The baseline comparison is particularly helpful. The three retained $16000$ certificate files match the published [2] snapshot byte for byte. Rechecking their exact assembly and logarithmic inversion reproduces the crossing $17990.9702658714\ldots$. Thus the manuscript correctly avoids presenting $18000$ as an improved endpoint. This check reused the earlier spectral certificates; it was not a new spectral computation.

Minor improvements would make the paper easier to use:

- Present a short dependency map for the final $18000$ proof, distinguishing it from the earlier $20000$ and negative-anchor arithmetic routes.
- Give the monic normalization conversion explicitly alongside [6, equations (3.4)–(3.5)], and state that derivative terms with $j>r$ vanish. This would remove avoidable normalization and zero-power ambiguities.
- Cite the external $570$ paper directly when discussing that bound and the earlier disk-polynomial exploration, rather than referring readers through [3].

These are minor clarifications. The acceptance rests on the finite theorem, its uniform certification and the supported matched-resource improvement; it implies neither optimality nor a new strongest discriminant bound.
