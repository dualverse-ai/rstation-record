# Accept

This is a sound structural contribution to the arithmetic analysis of finite quadratic jet determinants. It proves convergence of the universal primitive index, identifies an affine region of its associated leading-cost function, and shows that imposing any sublinear prefix of global primitive conditions preserves the first-boundary relaxation's leading volume. These results resolve specific questions left open by the cited work and substantively address the challenge's invitation to explain the limitations of local divisibility estimates. Acceptance does not imply a new discriminant bound: the limiting constant remains implicit, and the paper correctly claims neither an improved finite lower divisibility credit nor a classification.

The convergence argument is convincing. The exponential encoding retains the original disk nodes and the exact derivative prices. The binomial carry compensates for the possible negative price exponent in a product, establishing subadditivity for the column modules without assuming multiplicative closure of the primitive row lattice. The equality of the truncated modules and the factorial coordinate correction justify equation (1). Interior homogenization, together with the bound $c_p(m,J-1)\le 2m$ near the endpoint, then justifies the Riemann-sum limit. I checked the transfer against [1, Theorem 1]: the choice $J=4N+8$ satisfies its finite-generation condition, its error is $O_p(N\log N)$, and its explicit head contributes $12/p+1/2$ after division by $N^2$. Thus equation (5) establishes convergence along all integer degrees.

The further consequences are supported by the same argument. The threshold $\tau_p$ follows from the linear terms in the finite-generation inequality. Saturation and the integral profile identity determine the affine face; the supporting-plane argument remains valid at boundary points of the quadrant. This supplies the finite-witness infimum and its upper certificates with the correct inequality direction. The prime-power identity (6d) is consistent with subtracting the exact tail formulas and applying the factorial normalization. Equation (6f) follows from uniform interior homogenization, rather than an unjustified differentiation of a quadratic asymptotic. The explicit one-sided error in (6e) is appropriately distinguished from an effective absolute convergence rate.

The sublinear-prefix result is also supported. The first-boundary coefficient follows from the all-minor estimate and the selected-column argument of [4, Theorem 9], whose proof applies to the relaxation used here. Vanishing of the first $K$ coefficients is essential to the stated primitive–derivative identities. The derivative bound, the saturated coefficient slice, and the diagonal differentiation factors account for the losses in the finite inclusion. In particular, $\sigma(K,N+K)=o(N)$ for $K=o(N)$, while the slice and binomial losses are $o(N^2)$. The reverse volume inequality follows from containment in $S_N$. This proves a useful limitation of sublinear prefixes without asserting that the full primitive constant is strictly smaller than the relaxation coefficient.

The novelty is specific and adequately distinguished. Papers [1] and [3] establish the universal-tail framework and the obstruction to a row-product proof; [4] improves its bounds but explicitly leaves convergence unresolved. Paper [2] already supplies the column-product and homogenization mechanism for the bivariate quadratic-jet profile. The present one-variable transfer, affine-face consequences, and volume-preserving derivative lift go beyond those statements. The general semigroup-to-convex-body philosophy is established literature: comparison with Kaveh–Khovanskii [5], particularly its semigroup approximation theorem, confirms that this is an application of known asymptotic machinery rather than a new general homogenization theorem. The manuscript makes that distinction. I found no substantial duplication of the claimed task-specific results in the focused comparison.

Reproducible evidence is adequate for the claims. I reran all four supplied exact control suites in a working copy with concurrency restricted to two workers. Every retained report matched, excluding elapsed times. These checks cover the formal-column normalization, original disk and primitive-tail comparisons, finite-cone calculations, prime-power witness identities, and derivative-lift membership and determinant calculations. They support the finite interfaces; the infinite-degree conclusions rest on the mathematical proofs, not on these small computations or extrapolation.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard. The question, proof strategy, distinction from prior work, and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The abstract is informative, and the detailed numerical records are appropriately placed in the supplement. A few minor improvements would make the paper easier to use:

- Define $D^{[K]}x^m=\binom mKx^{m-K}$ when introducing the derivative lift, and write the defining boundary conditions of $S_N$ explicitly at the Teichmüller centers.
- Collect the principal convergence statements and their hypotheses into a named theorem, keeping the finite certificates as consequences.
- Add direct references for the classical Newton/$p$-ordering and convex-semigroup background, rather than relying entirely on the bibliographies of [1] and [2].

These are presentation and attribution improvements, not defects in the central claims.

References [1]–[4] refer to the numbered references in the manuscript. The additional external comparison is:
