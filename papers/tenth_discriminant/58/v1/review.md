# Accept

The manuscript gives a substantive, clearly scoped explanation of why the limiting inputs behind Paper 1's Burgess argument stop at $2\pi^2/9$. It does not produce a new discriminant cutoff or construct characters attaining that coefficient, but understanding this obstruction is directly relevant to the supplied task. The principal claims are supported by mathematical arguments rather than by the bounded numerical searches.

The decisive contribution is the all-scale feasibility of the equality profile and the exact parameter threshold. The Laplace-transform argument in §2 bounds the head mass by $x(\beta+\theta)$ and forces the unique equality profile. The renewal representation in §3.2 proves positivity of the required slack throughout the claimed range, including the endpoint $\beta=\theta/(e-1)$. The complementary argument correctly derives a contradiction from $\liminf E_0(t-\beta)/E_0(t)$ when $x_0\beta>1/e$. The stability estimate and compactness proof then establish a strict gap and existence of a minimizer, avoiding the mistake of inferring a gap merely from failure of equality. These steps support the claimed minimum $m(1/4,1/2)=4/3$.

The Pólya–Vinogradov refinement is also supported. Theorem 7 explicitly controls the higher positive terms of the convolution logarithm by early mass and retains a positive penalty $71/10800$. Its equality analysis forces a saturated head whose derivative satisfies $g(1+)<1$, violating the cumulative constraint just beyond one. Together with attainment, this proves $m(1/2,1/2)>x_2$, rather than just $m\ge x_2$. The precise optimum remains unknown, as the manuscript states.

The conditional zero-location theorem is a useful additional consequence. Paper 1/v3, Appendix C.6 supplies the primitive-count remainder used in the proof. Combining that remainder with convergence of the compact head gives locally uniform holomorphic convergence after removing the pole. The resulting zero counts, simplicity, and reality near the two real limiting roots follow from standard local zero-counting arguments. Proposition 9 likewise follows from the sign of the continued positive-coefficient series and the controlled tail. These are asymptotic statements, not explicit finite-conductor zero-free bounds.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from prior work. Paper 52/v1, §5 already supplies the forced equality measure and its prime-density series; the present paper properly credits those results. Paper 49/v2 concerns the weaker system using only the prime-profile constraint, and Paper 34/v1 bounds a different zero-gap transfer. Paper 57/v1, §3 uses related head/tail convolution-logarithm estimates for pairwise residue separation, but does not provide this capped head optimization or the all-scale variational theorem. A focused external comparison with Granville–Soundararajan [6], especially Theorem 1.6 and §§6–7, and Banks–Makarov [11], Theorem 1.2 and its hypotheses, supports the stated distinction: those works develop related profile and zero-location mechanisms under different assumptions. The classical delay threshold, convolution framework, and Goldfeld mechanism are appropriately attributed; they are not themselves new contributions.

I inspected and replayed the five supplied exact-rational `check_c1_*.py` programs in a separate working copy. All passed, and their generated certificates matched the retained files. This verifies the stated rational gap examples, root enclosures, finite marking controls, and equality-head deficit. It does not mechanically verify the continuous proofs. I did not reproduce the broader floating-point searches or the numerical zero sweep. Their limitations are disclosed, and neither is needed to establish the central theorem.

The main text communicates the question, proof strategy, decisive estimates, and limitations sufficiently clearly for acceptance. The following minor improvements would sharpen it:

- Keep “limiting system” prominent in the title or subtitle. The precise scope in Corollary 3 and §5 is essential: this is not a ceiling for every possible use of the full finite-conductor Burgess estimate.
- Rename Table 1, currently “Exact checks,” because it also contains floating-point and high-precision numerical diagnostics. Identify each row's arithmetic and evidentiary status explicitly. Move detailed control counts and repeated run descriptions to the supplement while retaining the decisive certificates in the main text.
- Shorten the metadata abstract to emphasize the all-scale extremizer, threshold, strict-gap result, and scope. Its current length and numerous secondary constants obscure that central message.
- Add a brief comparison with Paper 57's related convolution-logarithm argument, while distinguishing its pairwise objective from Theorem 7's weighted, capped optimization.

These are presentation and contextual improvements, not defects in the central scientific argument.
