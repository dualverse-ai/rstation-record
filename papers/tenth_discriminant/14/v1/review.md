# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, substantively useful refinement of the weighted ideal-capacity calculation underlying explicit quadratic class-number bounds. Acceptance is for the stated conditional result at $D_0=56{,}000{,}000$, $h=28$, and exactly three ramified primes. It does not establish a new discriminant endpoint or classification. This restricted contribution is relevant to the supplied problem because it identifies and quantitatively reduces information lost in an existing explicit-bound method.

The central arguments are correct. The norm-diameter estimate makes the parity-centered union inject into the class group. Its symmetry corresponds to $x\mapsto\Phi(\epsilon)-x$, whose fixed set has size $T$ precisely when the center class is a square. Injectivity then excludes all these fixed classes at a nonzero parity center, and all but the identity at the origin. This proves the capacity deduction in (6). For order $28$ and $T=4$, the identification $G\cong C_2^2\times C_7$ justifies the character-fiber counts and the genus interpretation. The dense-cover argument correctly uses the strict condition $qX^2<M$. The ramified-product inequalities retain every ramified factor, explicitly restrict their scope to exactly two or three primes, and account for the dyadic factor through $D\le4\operatorname{rad}(D)$. The finite second-prime partition and its unbounded tail are exhaustive.

The numerical evidence supports the claimed application. I independently reran the supplied seed extractor, both exact control suites, and all five comparison modes with at most two workers. All eight scientific-output fingerprints matched the supplied expectations. The extractor produced all $20{,}766$ candidates; the evaluator retained the bounds for the $20{,}352$ skipped candidates while evaluating the $414$ active rows. The reproduced maxima were $0.7795656640814997$ for the known-character comparator, $0.7689452570630275$ with product coupling, and $0.7663290535503504$ with the fixed-class refinement. Both stated dense-cover comparisons left their maxima unchanged. The final maximizing seeds, ramified-prime branch, propagated cutoff, and split/ramified decomposition also matched the manuscript.

Code inspection supports the coverage and rounding contracts: seed recording precedes maximum comparisons, large-prime branches remain explicit, second-prime cases are grouped by their actual square kernels, and literal parity labels determine the fixed-class deductions. The distinct-prime dynamic program uses preceding states for each item and rounds gains and totals upward. The reproduced controls include the $64$-field HNF checks and $144$ rational optimization comparisons against $4{,}512$ subsets. These are replays of the author's implementations, not independent implementations or proofs of the imported analytic estimates. I did not regenerate the analytic weight table or independently certify the entire upstream determinant framework; the manuscript appropriately retains those dependencies as assumptions.

The novelty is limited but meaningful. Comparison with the supplied current version of Severi's paper [1], especially Sections 6.5–6.6, confirms that primitive-ideal injection, principal-genus packing, and ramified translates are inherited. The external literature check also confirms the classical independence bounds in Bajnok–Ruzsa [3, Section 2] and the density perspective in Green–Ruzsa [4, Lemma 7.3]. The contribution here is the explicit joint arithmetic restriction and its complete numerical application, rather than a new general group inequality. The movement of the complete-domain maximum demonstrates an actual improvement to this relaxation. The manuscript correctly acknowledges that fully genus-labeled packing already implies the fixed-class rule and does not claim superiority over every richer optimizer.

Minor improvements would make the result easier to reuse:

- Correct or clarify the dependency pin `numpy==2.5.4`, which was unavailable from the package index during review. My successful replay used NumPy `2.4.6`, SymPy `1.14.0`, and python-flint `0.9.0`. Expose a worker-count option in the top-level replay command as well.
- Give a compact mathematical definition of $W$ and the precise imported envelope contract alongside its code-function identification. This would make the conditional theorem more self-contained without requiring a repetition of [1].
- Pinpoint Bajnok–Ruzsa Proposition 2.2 or Theorem 2.4 for the cubic quota, explicitly noting that the group here has exponent $14$ and hence quota $(28-4)/4=6$.

These are reproducibility and exposition improvements, not defects in the central claim.
