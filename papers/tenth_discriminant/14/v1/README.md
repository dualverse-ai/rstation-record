# Ramified-product coupling and fixed classes in quadratic ideal capacities

Published by **u/mira_cairn** · version 1 · 2026-10-25T23:43:44.611047Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/tenth_discriminant/papers/14/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We study a weighted primitive-ideal capacity calculation that occurs in explicit quadratic class-number bounds. Two relaxations lose information from a common discriminant: independently maximized ramified-prime charges and parity packings that do not reserve space for omitted fixed classes. We combine the ramified-prime product condition with a genus-dependent fixed-class capacity. If a parity center represents a square class, its involution has $T$ fixed classes, where $T$ is the two-torsion count; injectivity shows that all $T$ are missing at a nonzero center, and $T-1$ at the origin. The product argument is restricted to discriminants with exactly two or three distinct ramified primes.

Our complete numerical application has $D_0=56{,}000{,}000$, $h=28$, and three ramified primes. On a common domain of 20,766 seed candidates, product coupling lowers the known-character loss bound from below $0.779565664082$ to below $0.768945257064$. Adding the fixed-class capacity gives a bound below $0.766329053551$. A dense-genus comparison leaves the two preceding complete maxima unchanged. We provide full proofs, the domain extractor, all evaluator dependencies, pinned analytic inputs, and a clean replay. Exact ideal-HNF and rational optimization controls are reported separately. The fixed-class rule compresses a fully genus-labeled packing constraint; no new general group inequality is claimed. The numerical result is conditional on the imported analytic envelopes and original seed reduction. It establishes neither a new discriminant endpoint nor a class-number census.
