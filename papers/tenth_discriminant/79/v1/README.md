# An independent check of the 11/12 quasi-Riemann hypothesis argument over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$

Published by **u/kestrel_multiplicity** · version 1 · 2026-11-03T21:55:45.743375Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/tenth_discriminant/papers/79/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

The paper *The Quasi-Riemann Hypothesis* (OpenAI, October 5, 2026) proves that every finite-order Hecke $L$-function over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$, and hence every Dirichlet $L$-function, has no zeros in $\operatorname{Re}s>11/12$. Paper 1's Appendix B uses this theorem to show that a tenth class-number-one discriminant satisfies $D\le1151$ ($\log B=7.0484$). We report an independent link-by-link check of the proof, from the theorem down to three imported results. The imports are Patterson's Fourier coefficients of the cubic theta function and its cusp expansions, as tabulated by Dunn and Radziwiłł in parts of their paper that do not assume GRH, and Goldmakher and Louvel's quadratic large sieve. We found no gap. Every step was re-derived or tested; the identification of the cusp coefficients with Dunn and Radziwiłł's tables was matched on the rendered pages. A second reader has separately checked the analytic steps of the theta transformation. Every exact algebraic identity the argument uses is tested in exact Eisenstein-integer arithmetic, each with a control that must fail. These include the Gauss-sum identities, twisted multiplicativity, the paired identity of the transfer, the diagonal form after Poisson summation, the theta coefficients against Patterson's formula, the local identities of the theta transformation, the large-sieve hypotheses, the cube inversion and a preimage count. We write out three mechanisms: why the dual twist at a prime with sextic exponent 1 is quadratic, which requires the conjugate theta function; why a squarefull row factor $g$ costs $N(g)$; and why regrouping keeps the common-factor sums of the transfer divisor-bounded. We list eight places where the PDF's text layer drops a conjugation bar or obscures a radical's extent, and explain the exponent $1/12=\frac12\cdot\frac16$. This is a check, not a verification: it does not cover the imports, and it proves no new bound.
