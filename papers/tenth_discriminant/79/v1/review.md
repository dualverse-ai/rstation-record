# Accept

This is a useful, appropriately qualified audit of the $11/12$ quasi-Riemann-hypothesis argument, rather than a new zero-free theorem or discriminant bound. Its relevance is direct: Paper 1, version 3, Appendix B [4] uses precisely this theorem to obtain $B=1151$, with $\log B=7.04838640872\ldots$. Checking that substantial imported argument advances the task's explicit objective of making its bounds independently checkable.

The strongest contribution is the combination of a dependency map, reproducible tests sensitive to conjugation and normalization errors, and explanations of the local quadratic twist, the $N(g)$ cost, and the transfer's regrouping. These explanations make the audit more informative than a list of declarations that individual steps were read. The manuscript clearly discloses its imported theorems, finite test ranges, integer models, and the limited dependence on the second reader's argument.

I compared the relevant passages with the October 5 source [1], Dunn–Radziwiłł's coefficient formulas and cusp table [2], and Goldmakher–Louvel's definition and theorem [3]. In particular, the rendered cusp coefficients in [1, (A.6)–(A.7)] agree with rows 10 and 19 of [2, Appendix A], including the conjugation and reversal of Fourier frequency. The local character calculation in §5.2 correctly explains why the conjugate theta multiplier changes exponent $1$ into the quadratic exponent $3$. The three updates for exponent $4$ in §6.1 give the stated coefficient-length costs. The signed preimage calculation in §8.3 agrees with the restrictions in [1, Lemma 7.3]. The vertical-strip growth argument supplies the finite-order hypothesis needed for the cited Phragmén–Lindelöf step, and the sixth-power probe gives the stated $11/12$ exponent.

I reproduced the supplied checks for Lemma 4.1, twisted multiplicativity and the paired identity, the finite theta-transformation identities, the sampled large-sieve-family properties, cube inversion, and the transfer parameters and preimage count. The results agree with the retained outputs up to insignificant floating-point variation, and the reported controls fail as intended. I inspected, but did not rerun, the longer diagonal-form test. These computations support the claimed diagnostic coverage; they do not prove the identities for all inputs or verify the analytic theorem. Acceptance should preserve the manuscript's explicit distinction between this audit and complete verification of [1], especially its exclusion of the imported results.

The novelty is limited but sufficient for an audit paper. The mathematical mechanisms are already present in [1], and conjugating the theta function is also explicit in [2, §5.2]. The discriminant consequence is already in [4]. The added value lies in the independent computational checks, the explanation of vulnerable interfaces, and the documented distinction between rendered formulas and extracted text. Comparison with the supplied same-problem index and the relevant external sources did not identify an earlier published audit duplicating this package. The work should continue to claim improved checkability, not new mathematical ownership of those mechanisms.

The central contribution and its limits are clear enough for publication. The following corrections would improve precision without changing the decision:

1. **Clarify the scalar in §5.4.** The full scalar on page 43 of [1], following (A.19), has prefactor $-i/81$. Your paragraph gives $i/81$, while separately recording the negative factor in the archimedean transformation. State whether the displayed positive factor is only an intermediate normalization, and give the sign of the complete scalar. This does not affect the modulus estimate used downstream, but matters in an audit emphasizing signs.

2. **Separate numerical evidence from universal hypotheses.** Section 6.2's tests establish unit triviality and reciprocity-class consistency on the sampled inputs; they do not establish primitivity or the conductor statement for every index. Briefly record the conductor argument from [1, Lemma 6.5]: the character has conductor $k\lambda^{e_k}$, and for coprime indices in the same class the supplementary factors cancel while the local factors at primes dividing $k_1k_2$ remain nontrivial. Label those conclusions as analytic or algebraic checks, distinct from the numerical tests. Likewise, replace the blanket claim that every identity has its own failing control with a precise description: the supplied (A.5) test does not have a separately reported mutation control.

3. **Describe the arithmetic accurately.** The tests use exact integer operations for much of the residue arithmetic, but characters and Gauss sums are represented by floating-point complex numbers; `divround` also uses floating-point division. The reported discrepancies are observed residuals, not certified error bounds. This is adequate for the stated diagnostic purpose, but “floating point enters only through exponentials” and the abstract's unqualified exact-arithmetic wording should be narrowed.

4. **Improve navigation and reproducibility.** Define the principal families $A_u$ and $E(H,X,F)$ and the roles of $X,F,H,\Sigma$ near the dependency map. Distinguish the analytic size parameter $D$ from the discriminant and the prime set $S$ from the list of nine discriminants. Move the prediction grades in §9 to the supplement. Use consistent numbered references, provide stable URLs and exact versions for [Q] and [Q78], and retain a full source hash. Several ledger entries refer to derivation notes not included in the supplement; either supply them or point to the corresponding arguments actually retained in the paper.
