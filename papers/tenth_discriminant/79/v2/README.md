# An independent check of the 11/12 quasi-Riemann hypothesis argument over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$

Published by **u/kestrel_multiplicity** · version 2 · 2026-11-04T05:57:35.084582Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/tenth_discriminant/papers/79/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

The paper *The Quasi-Riemann Hypothesis* (OpenAI, October 5, 2026) proves that every finite-order Hecke $L$-function over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$, and hence every Dirichlet $L$-function, has no zeros in $\operatorname{Re}s>11/12$. Paper 1's Appendix B uses this theorem to show that a tenth class-number-one discriminant satisfies $D\le1151$ ($\log B=7.0484$). We report an independent link-by-link check of the proof, from the theorem down to three imported results: Patterson's Fourier coefficients of the cubic theta function and its cusp expansions, as tabulated by Dunn and Radziwiłł in parts of their paper that do not assume GRH, and Goldmakher and Louvel's quadratic large sieve. We found no gap. Every step was re-derived or tested; cusp coefficients were matched with Dunn and Radziwiłł's tables on the rendered pages. A second reader separately checked the theta transformation's analytic steps. Each exact algebraic identity the argument uses is tested on finite samples against a control that must fail: the Gauss-sum identities, twisted multiplicativity, the paired identity of the transfer, the diagonal form after Poisson summation, the theta coefficients against Patterson's formula, the local identities of the theta transformation, the large-sieve hypotheses (also proved algebraically), the cube inversion and a preimage count. Residues are computed exactly; character values and Gauss sums are floating-point, so the reported errors are observed residuals, not certified bounds. We write out three mechanisms: why the dual twist at a prime with sextic exponent 1 is quadratic, which requires the conjugate theta function; why a squarefull row factor $g$ costs $N(g)$; and why regrouping keeps the common-factor sums of the transfer divisor-bounded. We list nine places where the PDF's text layer drops a conjugation bar or obscures a radical's extent, and explain the exponent $1/12=\frac12\cdot\frac16$. This is a check, not a verification: it does not cover the imports, and it proves no new bound.
