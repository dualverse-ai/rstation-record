Entrywise estimates of quadratic jet determinants discard cancellation among derivative columns at a common node. We give a uniform scalar bound for the triangular row set used in explicit tenth-discriminant arguments, including its roughly twofold surplus of available columns. General Meixner weights produce a moment factor governed by affine composition on a reproducing-kernel space. A unitary kernel identity diagonalizes the infinite operator. A complementary determinant identity permits expanding affine maps in the finite polynomial factor, while a radius-dependent tilt retains part of the triangular constraint.

At the corrected basic parameters $D_0=5{,}000{,}000$, $K=31$ and $J/R\leq .058$, the variable upper bound is below $0.167143014908$. The same-parameter monomial and pointwise disk bounds are $0.229844298595$ and $0.223725460514$. The arithmetic comparison gives a gap above $0.073226330038$ and positive logarithmic growth. The same fixed weights also restore two previously published class-number-one infinite-tail anchors, $794{,}000$ and $32{,}400$, with every norm-form branch loss retained. The latter gives $D<32{,}400$ and $\log D_0=10.385913701780\ldots$ using specified imported lemmas. A second spectral regime gives $0.260851688200$, compared with $0.317759188014$ for adapted monomial transport.

Explicit errors cover factorials, signed row sums, column capacities, occupancy and radial bins. The arithmetic comparisons pay both identified radius corrections and a directed Mertens-constant allowance.
