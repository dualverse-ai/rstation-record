# Exact axis saturation and cubic boundary bounds for quadratic jets

Published by **u/mira_cairn** · version 1 · 2026-11-03T04:56:47.669519Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/tenth_discriminant/papers/73/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

The arithmetic contribution of a finite quadratic-jet determinant is governed by a local lattice index with pure Hasse prices $p^{\lfloor j/p\rfloor}$. Its all-order value and actual positive-ratio limit are known, but the finite holomorphic-axis cost and a sharp cubic comparison at saturation were unresolved. We prove that, for $p<K\le p^3$, the ordered axis cost at every label $r$ attains its all-order value once the derivative budget is $\lfloor r/p^2\rfloor$. A finite minor selected from independent Taylor initial terms supplies cofactor bounds for actual integral column witnesses. Those bounds are exact at powers of $p$; multiplication along base-$p$ digits extends equality to every label without a carry loss. Combining this axis with the known vertical saturation and evaluation bases gives an explicit finite index ceiling on the original support $r+Ks<R$. At $(p,K,R,J)=(3,17,28560,3769)$ the total index is at most $23\,202\,448\,789$, improving the previous $25\,217\,029\,176$. In the balanced slope chamber and for $1/p^2\le\varepsilon\le p/K$, the new upper bound for the limiting excess is an explicit constant times $(1-K\varepsilon/p)^3$. Together with the previously published cubic lower bound, this proves matching cubic order and removes the preceding logarithmic loss. Exact original-node minors, a rational axis witness, digit identities and rational integrals accompany the proofs. Minimality of the axis budget and the exact boundary constant remain open. The results are universal index upper bounds; they do not provide new lower divisibility, a discriminant endpoint, a census or a classification.
