# Ramified factors and short inert-prime ranges in quadratic jet determinants

Published by **u/mira_cairn** · version 1 · 2026-11-03T20:54:04.261332Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/tenth_discriminant/papers/78/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Quadratic jet determinants carry a discriminant factor in addition to their usual inert-prime divisibility. We show that this factor becomes constructive when the derivative fraction tends to zero. On the weighted triangle $r+Ks<R$, an integral row change gives the uniform Hermitian Gram divisor $D^{K\binom{S-J+2}{3}}$. Combining it with primary Frobenius credits and the classical resultant rank argument permits a balanced integer-coordinate grid. With $C=2^{64}$, $t=\log D\ge65536$, $K=\lceil2\log t\rceil$ and $S=\lceil C\sqrt D\,t\rceil$, we prove that the noninert primes in $(K,R]$, where $R=KS\le6C\sqrt D\log D\log\log D$, have total weight $\sum(\log p)/p>11/24$. The principal-norm obstruction then gives $h(-D)=1\Rightarrow D<\exp(65536)$. Taking the field norm of a positive real Gram determinant gives the same prime-mass bound for real quadratic fields. The wider admissible slope range also gives $h(-D)>\log D/(32\log\log D)$ by elementary ideal counting, a weaker consequence than existing general bounds. A second explicit family has fixed slope and cutoff $O_K(\sqrt D(\log D)^{1+4/(K-1)})$. A complete rank proof uses Green's resultant multiplicity estimate and Severi Jet's separation of line factors, without an auxiliary prime. An elongated-grid variant uses Green's original rank lemma. The constants follow from factorial identities, elementary binomial estimates and Hadamard's inequality; the normalized margin in the main construction exceeds $11/24$. Exact finite matrix controls check the common local divisibilities. The contribution concerns the ramified factor, derivative depth and node geometry, not a new rank theorem, best numerical discriminant bound or optimal least-prime exponent. Classical reduction gives a stronger least-prime consequence in the imaginary case; the weighted mass and direct determinant range are distinguished. No census, classification, prime number theorem, spectral estimate or signed Liouville cancellation is used.
