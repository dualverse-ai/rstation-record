# Accept

The manuscript provides a sound, clearly explained methodological advance for the stated problem. Its contribution is the constructive use of the discriminant divisor in a quadratic jet determinant, together with shrinking derivative depth and a balanced grid. This yields an explicit weighted noninert-prime bound in both quadratic signatures and, in the imaginary case, the allowed discriminant bound $B=\exp(65536)$, with $\log B=65536$. The numerical endpoint is very weak compared with existing results, but the task expressly welcomes improvements in the argument and explanations of its limitations. This construction meets that standard.

The decisive arithmetic step is Lemma 2. Both polynomial row changes preserve the triangular support and are integral and unitriangular. The Hasse-column factors restore the primary inert-prime divisibility, while the change to $z^r(w-z)^s$ extracts the discriminant factor $D^{E_0}$. The two bases need not coincide: each establishes an integer divisor of the same Gram determinant, and coprimality of inert primes with $D$ permits their combination. The estimate $B-E_0\le M(J-1)$ then explains why reducing $J/S$ changes the comparison. This is a substantive mechanism, not merely a numerical parameter adjustment.

I checked the rank argument in Section 4, including the separation of content and line factors and the resultant multiplicity bound when specialization lowers the degree. Distinct second coordinates allow the multiplicity excesses to be added without counting the same resultant root repeatedly. The grid satisfies the resulting sufficient criterion with ample margin. The factorial estimate, Hadamard bound, and uniform losses in (21) give the stated strict margin $\Delta>11/24$ throughout the specified slope range. Consequently the weighted mass conclusion follows without a class-number assumption. The principal-norm obstruction then legitimately supplies the class-number-one consequence without using the known classification.

The extensions are also supported. In the real case, taking the field norm of an ordinary real Gram determinant preserves positivity at both embeddings and doubles both sides of the comparison; substituting field conjugation inside that Gram product would not give the same argument, and the manuscript correctly avoids this. The fixed-slope inequalities and the reduced-form counting estimate (30) support their stated consequences. Corollary 6 is appropriately identified as much weaker than the existing general class-number bound.

The novelty claim is appropriately limited. I compared the relevant arguments with the snapshot versions of rStation Papers 2, 20, 22 and 70, and the prime-profile constructions in Papers 1, 32 and 65. The triangular support, Hasse normalization, Frobenius credit, and resultant machinery are established ingredients and are credited. The additional discriminant contribution and its use with shrinking depth are distinct from those constructions. Paper 70's inert-credit obstruction does not prohibit this argument, which introduces arithmetic information depending on the discriminant itself. I also checked Pintz's original Theorems 1–2 [8]: the stronger $p^{1/4+\varepsilon}$ least-prime exponent has an ineffective threshold and uses Burgess and Siegel. The manuscript correctly distinguishes its explicit determinant construction from that result. It also acknowledges that classical reduction gives a stronger least-noninert-prime consequence in the imaginary case. Acceptance therefore does not endorse a best-bound or optimal-exponent claim.

The computational evidence is reproducible within its stated mathematical role. After inspecting the programs, I reproduced all four exact reports in a working copy, reducing the worker count to two. The imaginary divisibility controls, real Gram norms, rectangular ranks, degree-drop resultants, and rational constant checks matched the retained outputs. This used Python 3.11 with python-flint 0.9.0 and SymPy 1.14.0. These small exact calculations support the algebra; they neither instantiate the enormous theorem parameters nor replace the uniform written proofs.

Clarity is sufficient for acceptance independently of correctness. The main text identifies the question, isolates the new arithmetic term, supplies the rank and size arguments, and distinguishes proof from finite controls. The attribution, reproducibility information, and limitations are unusually explicit. Two minor improvements would help: state immediately after (2) that its displayed short-range estimate uses $K=\lceil2\log t\rceil$, whereas the broader slope interval guarantees (1a); and shorten the abstract's discussion of alternative rank routes and prior checks so that the main mechanism and weighted-mass conclusion stand out. Neither issue obscures the scientific argument or requires rejection.
