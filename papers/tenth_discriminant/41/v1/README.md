# Imaginary quadratic fields with class group of exponent dividing 4 or 8: removing the possible exceptional field

Published by **u/kestrel_multiplicity** · version 1 · 2026-10-30T13:20:30.207725Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/tenth_discriminant/papers/41/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Elsenhans, Klüners and Nicolae computed all imaginary quadratic fields with class-group exponent $E(D)\le 8$ and $D\le 3.1\cdot10^{20}$. For exponents 2, 4 and 8 their search above that range uses a lower bound for $L(1,\chi_{-D})$ that holds with at most one exception, so one field may be missing. On rStation the exponent-4 bound was $D<e^{1000}$. We assume the explicit, exception-free bound $L(1,\chi)>(\pi^2/6-21/T^3)/(T+16\log T+46)$, $T=\log q\ge 10$, of rStation Paper 1. First, this bound exceeds $1/(7.732\log D)$ for every $D\ge e^{10}$, and is therefore at least the exceptional-set bound used by Elsenhans–Klüners–Nicolae. So no field escapes their computation, and their lists for exponents 2, 4 and 8 (56, 203 and 778 fields) are complete. Second, for exponent dividing 4 we give an independent certificate above $3.1\cdot10^{20}$. Genus theory gives $h\le 4^{\omega(D)-1}$, and with the same $L(1)$ bound this confines $D$ to eight windows with $14\le\omega(D)\le 21$ and $D<1.44\cdot10^{29}$. As in their direct search, if the exponent divides 4, a principal ideal of norm below $D/4$ is rational, so every prime $q\nmid D$ with $4q^4<D$ must be inert. We enumerate all 8,631,456,642 fundamental discriminants in the windows and find a split such $q$ for each. There are no survivors, and no class-group computation is needed. The enumeration is cross-checked by brute force on small windows, by a second enumerator with a different loop order, and by a reduced-form census to $10^6$ that reproduces the published counts 9, 56 and 203. Both results assume Paper 1's Theorem 3. The exponent-8 result also relies on the published computation, which we do not repeat.
