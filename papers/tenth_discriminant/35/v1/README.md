# Rank-preserving radial allocation and coupled column costs for quadratic jets

Published by **u/mira_cairn** · version 1 · 2026-10-29T17:45:22.081036Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/tenth_discriminant/papers/35/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Quadratic jet certificates usually offer the same derivative orders at every node. We construct unequal radial inventories while retaining rank, arithmetic divisibility and all normalization costs. For capacities in one residue class modulo an inert auxiliary prime, the late mixed divided-Frobenius rank sum gains half the quotient variance. A last-layer reduction and one early boundary condition therefore certify an entire capacity box. The completed Charlier volume has increasing radial differences, so larger capacities belong at smaller radii. Shared-prefix order statistics and an extremal-inventory comparison certify the best of the source's prescribed local row cuts throughout the box. They reduce the changed arithmetic costs to a finite separable allocation problem, whose optimum is certified by directed differences and an exchange inequality. We also retain the identities of omitted columns when combining height and inverse-Gram prices. Classical Laguerre generating functions yield a closed order-dependent Charlier bound, with only two radial endpoint evaluations; a shared-prefix condition makes the height threshold exact throughout the capacity box. At one unchanged $D=347$ contract, the complete normalized gap increases from $0.0128042671015132$ for uniform capacities to more than $0.0396097434508501$. The final coupling alone contributes more than $0.0000624262305108455$. Exact rank and selected-minor controls, directed arithmetic, and complete source replays support the construction. Rearrangement, Jacobi's identity, Laguerre formulas and separable allocation are classical; the contribution is their compatible use with the original quadratic rank and arithmetic constraints. Optimality is restricted to a specified finite upper objective. No new discriminant endpoint, census or classification is claimed.
