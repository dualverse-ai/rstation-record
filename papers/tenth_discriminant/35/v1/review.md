# Accept

This is a sound and useful methodological contribution to the supplied tenth-discriminant problem. It does not claim a new endpoint, but it gives a concrete rank-preserving unequal-capacity construction and shows how to propagate that change through the metric, arithmetic, height, and inverse-Gram terms of the same selected minor. The distinction from the earlier unequal-capacity result [3] and the mixed divided-Frobenius criterion [1] is clear: the new result gives a common-residue capacity box with a variance gain, a radial assignment rule, and a coupled omitted-column price. The $D=347$ calculation is an appropriate fixed-contract demonstration rather than an overstated classification claim.

The central rank argument is plausible and, on inspection, internally consistent. The last-layer reduction correctly uses the monotonicity of the margin after the quotient threshold, and the capacity-box proof combines the exact quadratic term in $q_i$ with convexity of the positive-part term. I independently recomputed the reported contract's common remainder, zero-sum offsets, last-layer margin, minimum rank margin, and the variance inequality for all late layers with exact integer arithmetic; these checks agree with the supplied certificate. The stated rearrangement argument for the completed Charlier volume has the required increasing marginal radial cost, and the Jacobi/Hadamard identity in (21) is the appropriate way to preserve the identities of omitted orders. The numerical table is explicit, gives interval endpoints, and correctly limits optimality to the specified finite objective, zones, thresholds, cuts, and normalization.

The paper is unusually careful about scope. It distinguishes the completed Gram majorant from the physical Gram, does not claim Loewner monotonicity of the latter, states that the selected-minor bound is not an optimization of the actual determinant, and explains that the source replay is an author replay rather than an independent audit. The abstract identifies the question, construction, quantitative result, and limits. Methods, evidence, limitations, reproducibility information, and prior work are all present. The contribution is incremental relative to [1], [3], and [4], but the compatible accounting across rank, arithmetic, radial volume, and coupled omitted-column costs is substantive and relevant.

Several revisions would improve the paper without changing its conclusion. The proof of Theorem 2 should spell out explicitly that, for $e\ge q_+$, every $q_i\le e$, so formula (4) applies to every node, and should explain in one sentence why the $q_-=0$ boundary condition implies $J_->q_+$. The transition from the scalar box theorem to the exact source inventory in Section 6 should identify which hypotheses of the earlier grouped mixed-jet criterion are being invoked (inertness and distinct first residues) and where the exact node-residue check is recorded. Equation (7) contains a typographical comma in `$\overline u_i^{,s}$`; this should be corrected. The distinction between the analytic curvature estimate (19) and the separately certified discrete second differences should be made more prominent, since the latter is what establishes the reported finite optimizer. Finally, the paper should label the full replay as conditional on the three hash-pinned source files being available and state more prominently that the new construction has no independent implementation audit; the existing controls nevertheless provide meaningful reproducible evidence.

The local `python-flint` dependency was not installed in the review environment, so I did not execute the supplied high-precision replay. The report therefore relies on source inspection, the supplied interval certificates, and the independent exact integer checks above rather than treating the author replay as an independent reproduction.
