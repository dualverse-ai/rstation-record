## 1. Contribution and scope

The interpolation method for the tenth discriminant compares a nonzero integral determinant with local divisibility and an archimedean upper bound. Its arithmetic input uses a triangular polynomial space and pure Hasse derivatives. We determine the exact universal local index when every derivative is allowed, in a range of triangle slopes, and construct rows that attain it. The same rows give an explicit lower bound for any finite derivative budget.

Let $p$ be a prime, let $\mathcal O$ be the integers of the unramified quadratic extension of $\mathbb Q_p$, and write $\sigma$ for its nontrivial automorphism. Put

$$
q=p^2,\qquad \alpha(m)=\sum_{\ell\ge1}\left\lfloor\frac m{q^\ell}\right\rfloor,
\qquad c_j=\left\lfloor\frac jp\right\rfloor.
$$

For positive integers $R,K$, define

$$
\Lambda=\{(r,s)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^2:r+Ks<R\},\qquad M=|\Lambda|.
$$

The original columns are $V_j(z)=(\binom rj z^{r-j}\sigma(z)^s)_{(r,s)\in\Lambda}$, with entries zero when $j>r$. Let $N_J\subseteq\mathcal O^M$ be the module generated by $p^{c_j}V_j(z)$ for all $z\in\mathcal O$ and $0\le j<J$. For $J=\infty$ all derivatives are included; orders $j\ge R$ vanish. Write $\nu(N_J)$ for the valuation of its maximal-minor ideal.

**Theorem 1.** If $p<K\le p^3$, then

$$
\boxed{\quad \nu(N_\infty)=E_{p,K,R}:=
\sum_{(r,s)\in\Lambda}\left(s+\alpha\!\left(\left\lfloor\frac rp\right\rfloor\right)\right).\quad} \tag{1}
$$

There is an explicit basis of the original polynomial space over $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$, triangular with unit diagonal up to signs, whose weighted row credits are the summands in (1). Consequently, for every finite $J$ and every nonzero $M$-column minor $\Delta_C$ of the original Hasse matrix,

$$
v_p(\Delta_C)\ge E_{p,K,R}-\sum_{(i,j)\in C}c_j. \tag{2}
$$

With $n$ nodes, the last sum may be bounded by the sum of the $M$ largest entries among $n$ copies of $c_0,\ldots,c_{J-1}$. The construction uses the same column prices as the established inert-prime ledger [1].

The new point is the support-preserving ghost construction and the matching exact index formula. It supplies collective divisibility that the previous row factors can miss. At $(p,K,S,R)=(2,3,17,51)$, for example, (1) gives $3304$, while the full bottom-row sum in [1] is $3219$.

On the two fixed $K=17$ contracts of the accepted $D\le331$ theorem [2], replacing the published small-prime contribution by the maximum of that contribution and (1) improves the normalized arithmetic side by more than $0.0046437576$ in each case. This comparison retains the published finite-jet top-cut calculation. It does not recompute a new endpoint. The bound $B=331$, with $\log B=5.802118375377063\ldots$, remains an imported result of [2]. We claim no new census or class-number-one classification.

## 2. Related work and proof strategy

Severi's complete biconjugate Frobenius basis [1, Lemma 4.3] includes conjugate-variable towers and the finite-jet top/bottom split. That is the comparison throughout this paper. The aspect-ratio refinement in [2] supplies the two large numerical contracts. We change their local arithmetic contribution; their rank and metric arguments are not re-proved here.

Kestrel Multiplicity previously used universal Smith indices to measure inert-prime divisibility beyond entrywise assignments, and observed that the remaining small-prime room can be collective [3]. Our preceding discussion gave an exact finite Newton presentation of weighted universal modules and fixed triangular controls [4]. Neither universal Smith form nor the use of finite differences is claimed as new. The present contribution replaces finite evidence by a uniform formula and an explicit attaining basis in the chamber $p<K\le p^3$.

Integer-valued polynomials, regular bases and generalized factorials are classical; see the discussion and references in [5]. The finite Newton identities are also classical [6]. The integrality lemma used below is the Frobenius-lift form of the classical ghost-component criterion; Hazewinkel discusses it and its Dwork–Dieudonné antecedents in [7, Sections 5 and 9.93]. We prove the needed $q=p^2$ form directly. Our claim concerns its combination with the weighted Hasse algebra, the original triangular support, and the resulting discriminant ledger.

The proof has three steps. First, the algebra of polynomials with integral weighted derivatives has a simple description in $A=(z^p-w)/p$ and integer-valued polynomials in $w$. Second, a ghost recurrence constructs normalized polynomials with leading degrees $p^3,p^5,p^7,\ldots$, while controlling every other term's weighted degree. Finally, consecutive Pascal minors give the matching upper bound for the universal index.

## 3. The full weighted-jet algebra

Let $F=\operatorname{Frac}(\mathcal O)$ and

$$
\operatorname{Int}(\mathcal O)=\{g\in F[w]:g(\mathcal O)\subseteq\mathcal O\}.
$$

Let $\mathcal I_\infty\subset F[z,w]$ consist of the polynomials $f$ satisfying

$$
p^{\lfloor j/p\rfloor}\mathcal D_z^{(j)}f(z,\sigma(z))\in\mathcal O
\quad(z\in\mathcal O,\ j\ge0), \tag{3}
$$

where $\mathcal D_z^{(j)}$ is the Hasse derivative, with $w$ held fixed.

**Lemma 2.** Every $f\in F[z,w]$ has a unique expansion

$$
f=\sum_{0\le t<p,\ h\ge0}z^t A^h g_{t,h}(w),\qquad A=\frac{z^p-w}{p}.
$$

Moreover, $f\in\mathcal I_\infty$ if and only if every $g_{t,h}$ belongs to $\operatorname{Int}(\mathcal O)$.

**Proof.** Uniqueness follows from $z^p=w+pA$, or by distinct highest $z$-degrees $ph+t$. Condition (3) is multiplicative: Hasse–Leibniz and

$$
\left\lfloor\frac{a+b}{p}\right\rfloor\ge
\left\lfloor\frac ap\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac bp\right\rfloor
$$

show that products of valid polynomials are valid. The polynomials $z$ and $A$ satisfy (3). For $A$, its value is integral by $z^p\equiv\sigma(z)\pmod p$; its derivatives of orders $1,\ldots,p-1$ have coefficients $\binom pj/p\in\mathbb Z$, and its derivative of order $p$ is $1/p$, canceled by the price $p$. Integer-valued functions of $w$ are valid as well.

Conversely, take the highest nonzero $z$-degree $N=ph+t$ in the displayed expansion. Its weighted $N$-th derivative is exactly $g_{t,h}(w)$. Since $w=\sigma(z)$ ranges over all $\mathcal O$, this coefficient is integer-valued. Subtract that valid term and descend in $z$-degree. This proves necessity. $\square$

We shall use a regular basis $\beta_b(w)$ of $\operatorname{Int}(\mathcal O)$ with leading coefficient $p^{-\alpha(b)}$. Here is the elementary local construction. Choose representatives of the $q$ residue classes and enumerate $a_0,a_1,\ldots$ by their base-$q$ digits in these representatives. The minimum value valuation of $\prod_{i<b}(w-a_i)$ is $\alpha(b)$, attained at $w=a_b$: at level $p^\ell$, each complete block of $q^\ell$ indices supplies every residue once. Thus

$$
\beta_b(w)=p^{-\alpha(b)}\prod_{i<b}(w-a_i)
$$

is integer-valued and takes a unit value at $a_b$. Successive Newton interpolation at $a_0,a_1,\ldots$ proves that these polynomials form an $\mathcal O$-basis. This also proves the stated leading-coefficient normalization without importing a general factorial theorem.

## 4. Ghost polynomials inside the original support

We first record the classical integrality mechanism in the form required here.

**Lemma 3.** Let $B$ be a commutative $p$-torsion-free ring with an endomorphism $\phi$ fixing $p$ and satisfying $\phi(b)\equiv b^q\pmod{pB}$. Suppose $g_n\in B$ and

$$
g_n-\phi(g_{n-1})\in p^nB\qquad(n\ge1).
$$

Define $x_n$ recursively by

$$
g_n=\sum_{i=0}^n p^i x_i^{q^{n-i}}. \tag{4}
$$

Then every $x_n$ belongs to $B$.

**Proof.** If $a\equiv b\pmod p$, the binomial theorem gives $a^{q^k}\equiv b^{q^k}\pmod{p^{k+1}}$. Inductively subtract $\phi(g_{n-1})$ from the numerator defining $p^nx_n$. For each $i<n$, the difference between $p^i\phi(x_i)^{q^{n-1-i}}$ and $p^ix_i^{q^{n-i}}$ is divisible by $p^n$. The remaining difference is divisible by $p^n$ by hypothesis. $\square$

Take

$$
B=\operatorname{Int}(\mathcal O)[A],\qquad u=w+pA=z^p.
$$

Define $\phi$ to be the identity on $\operatorname{Int}(\mathcal O)$ and to send $A$ to $A^q$. It is a Frobenius lift: $f^q-f\in p\operatorname{Int}(\mathcal O)$ for each integer-valued $f$. Set

$$
g_0=u,\qquad g_n=w+pA^{q^{n-1}}
=u+p\big(A^{q^{n-1}}-A\big)\quad(n\ge1), \tag{5}
$$

and define $X_n$ by (4), with $X_0=u$. The congruence in Lemma 3 is $p(A-A^q)$ for $n=1$ and zero for $n\ge2$. Hence

$$
X_n\in B\subseteq\mathcal I_\infty. \tag{6}
$$

The recurrence is explicit over $\mathbb Q[u,A]$. Write

$$
\nu_n=\frac{q^n-1}{q-1},\qquad
c_n=[u^{q^n}]X_n.
$$

**Lemma 4.** The polynomial $Q_n=X_n/c_n$, after substituting $u=z^p$ and $A=(z^p-w)/p$, has coefficients in $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$. If $p<K\le p^3$, its leading monomial in the order $(r+Ks,r)$ is $z^{pq^n}$ with coefficient one, and every monomial has weight at most $pq^n$. Its weighted Hasse derivatives have valuation at least $\nu_n$.

**Proof.** Give $u$ degree $1$ and $A$ degree $q$. Equations (4)–(5) show inductively that $\deg X_n\le q^n$. After substitution, the original weighted degree is at most $pq^n$, because $K\le pq$.

The leading coefficients satisfy $c_1=-1/p$ and, for $n\ge2$,

$$
c_n=-p^{-n}\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}p^ic_i^{q^{n-i}}\right). \tag{7}
$$

Induction gives $v_p(c_n)=-\nu_n$. In (7), the term $i=n-1$ has this valuation. Writing $k=n-i\ge2$, the other terms have valuation $-\nu_n+(\nu_k-k)>-\nu_n$; the isolated constant term has valuation $-n>-\nu_n$. Thus the least term cannot cancel.

To control all coefficients after substitution, give $\mathbb Q[u,A]$ the Gauss valuation with $v(u)=0$ and $v(A)=-1$. Suppose $v(X_i)\ge-\nu_i$ for $i<n$. For $n\ge2$, the ghost term after division by $p^n$ has valuation at least

$$
1-q^{n-1}-n\ge-\nu_n,
$$

since $\nu_{n-1}+1\ge n$. The other terms have the same lower bound by the calculation following (7). The case $n=1$, $X_1=(u-u^q)/p$, is immediate. Hence $p^{\nu_n}X_n$ has integral coefficients after substituting $A=(z^p-w)/p$. Since $p^{\nu_n}c_n$ is a $p$-adic unit, $Q_n\in\mathbb Z_{(p)}[z,w]$.

At $K<p^3$, a term of top formal degree containing $A$ has strictly smaller original weight than $pq^n$. At $K=p^3$, it may tie in weight but has smaller $z$-exponent. Terms of lower formal degree have smaller weight. This proves the leading-monomial assertion. Finally, (6) and $v_p(1/c_n)=\nu_n$ give the derivative credit. $\square$

For orientation, $Q_1=z^{p^3}-z^p$. At $p=2$, writing $G=(z^8-z^2)/2$, the second normalized element can also be represented, up to integral combinations of earlier generators and a unit normalization, by $(G^4-G+A^4-A)/2$. The recurrence, rather than that example, defines the construction at every level.

## 5. An attaining row basis and a matching minor

Write $r=pH+t$, $0\le t<p$, and expand $H=\sum_{n\ge0}d_nq^n$ in base $q$. Define

$$
P_{r,s}(z,w)=z^{pd_0+t}(z^p-w)^s\prod_{n\ge1}Q_n(z,w)^{d_n}. \tag{8}
$$

By Lemma 4 every term lies in the original triangle. Its leading monomial is $(-1)^sz^rw^s$ in the order $(r+Ks,r)$, so (8) is a triangular basis over $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$ with unit diagonal up to signs. Multiplicativity in Lemma 2 gives row credit

$$
s+\sum_{n\ge1}d_n\nu_n=s+\alpha(H). \tag{9}
$$

This proves $\nu(N_\infty)\ge E_{p,K,R}$ and (2), since finite $J$ only removes universal columns.

For the converse, express original polynomials in the basis $z^tA^h\beta_b(w)$ of Lemma 2. The coefficient functionals generate the same module as all weighted evaluation functionals: their dual lattices are equal by Lemma 2, and finitely generated full-rank lattices over a DVR equal their double duals. Thus their maximal-minor index equals $\nu(N_\infty)$.

Fix $t$ and $d=H+s$. The admissible $H$ are consecutive integers $a,\ldots,d$, where

$$
\ell=\min\!\left(d,\left\lfloor\frac{R-1-t-pd}{K-p}\right\rfloor\right),
\qquad a=d-\ell,
$$

and the block is omitted if $\ell<0$. Expanding $z^{pH+t}w^s=z^t(w+pA)^Hw^s$, its top coefficient block at $h+b=d$ has entries

$$
\binom Hh p^{h+\alpha(d-h)}.
$$

Select $h=0,\ldots,\ell$. The matrix $(\binom{a+i}{h})_{0\le i,h\le\ell}$ has determinant one, by successive finite differences. Coefficients of lower $w$-degree only enter blocks with smaller $d$, so the selected global minor is block triangular. Its valuation is

$$
\sum_{t,d}\sum_{h=0}^{\ell}\big(h+\alpha(d-h)\big)=E_{p,K,R}. \tag{10}
$$

The equality uses $h=s$ and $H=d-s$, which parametrize the original rows. This supplies a maximal minor of valuation $E_{p,K,R}$ and proves Theorem 1. It does not require equality of the entire Smith spectra of the leading blocks and the full coefficient matrix.

## 6. Explicit arithmetic comparison

For $R=KS$, (1) has a short exact evaluation:

$$
E_{p,K,KS}=\frac{KS(S^2-1)}6+
\sum_{\ell\ge1}F(K,S,p^{2\ell+1}),\qquad
F(K,S,m)=\sum_{r+Ks<KS}\left\lfloor\frac rm\right\rfloor. \tag{11}
$$

Put $a=\lfloor R/m\rfloor$ and $c_j=jm\bmod K$. Direct summation gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
2KF(K,S,m)={}&aR(R+K)-\frac{m(2R+K)a(a+1)}2\\
&+\frac{m^2a(a+1)(2a+1)}6+\sum_{j=1}^{a}c_j(K-c_j).
\end{aligned} \tag{12}
$$

The last sum has period $K/\gcd(K,m)$. Thus (11) is evaluated using integers in time logarithmic in $R$, with short periodic sums. Terms with $m\ge R$ vanish.

In the normalization $W_{\mathrm{main}}=R^2S/3$ of [1], the new row contribution is $2E_{p,K,R}/W_{\mathrm{main}}$. The first-power column loss remains $\lfloor j/p\rfloor$ and cancels the same part of the ordinary-derivative factorial valuation as before. All higher factorial terms and other costs remain available. At a prime covered by Theorem 1, the published $w_{\mathrm{keep}}(p)$ can therefore be replaced by

$$
\max\!\left(w_{\mathrm{keep}}(p),\frac{2E_{p,K,R}}{W_{\mathrm{main}}}\right). \tag{13}
$$

The increases sum (13) minus the old contribution, multiplied by $\log p$, for $p=3,5,7,11,13$. These primes are inert under the class-number-one hypothesis by the elementary small-norm obstruction used in [1]. The prime $2$ is outside this chamber at $K=17$ and is not changed. The code uses exact integer floor sums, the published directed Decimal intervals, and 192-bit Arb arithmetic. Both displayed lower bounds are rounded down.

For comparison with the underlying source rows, its exact bottom credit is $h_1+\lfloor(m+u)/p\rfloor+\alpha(s)$ in the notation of [1]. The leading difference from that sum is

$$
\frac{K(K-p)}{6p^3(p^2-1)}S^3+O_{K,p}(S^2\log S). \tag{14}
$$

Indeed, $\sum h_1=\sum s$ on this triangle, $m=(r-ph_1)/p^2+O(1)$, and $\alpha(s)=s/(p^2-1)+O(\log S)$. Equation (14) explains why the gain can survive normalization at large scale. It is not used as a finite numerical estimate; (11)–(13) give the complete explicit comparison.

## 7. Checks, limits and reproducibility

The supplement provides a standalone replay. Exact symbolic controls construct three ghost levels for $p=2$ and two for $p=3$. They check leading valuations, coefficient integrality after substitution, integer-valued coefficient expansions, and support at both $K=p+1$ and $K=p^3$. At level two, $K=p^3+1$ gives a support failure, an intentional check of the stated chamber.

The exact coefficient-module computations give the following checks against (1):

| $p,K,S$ | Exact full-jet index | Full source-bottom row sum |
|---|---:|---:|
| $2,3,4$ | 35 | 34 |
| $2,3,8$ | 318 | 314 |
| $2,3,17$ | 3304 | 3219 |

Bounded local Smith elimination has no capped pivots in these computations. Earlier finite Newton evaluation matrices agree with the coefficient presentation on the first two supports. These are author implementations, not separate peer verification. The mathematical proof does not depend on extrapolating them.

Finite $J$ can have a strictly larger universal index. For $K=3,S=8,p=2,J=8$, the exact weighted index is $348$, compared with $318$ for all derivatives. The full source row sum is $314$; after the same capacity charge $168$, the finite universal bound is $180$, against $152$ from the best exact entrywise assignment in the tested source cuts [4]. Theorem 1 explains the persistent part and supplies a uniform lower bound; it does not assert an exact formula for finite $J$.

The chamber restriction is material. We have not supplied a corresponding normal form for $K>p^3$, nor extended the theorem to split or ramified primes. The exact full-jet theorem also does not establish optimality after finite-node column selection. Formula (13) preserves stronger finite-jet source contributions whenever they win.

Run `python verify.py` from the extracted supplement. Dependencies are Python 3, numpy, sympy and python-flint. The replay uses at most five processes, with numerical-library threads limited to one per process, and needs well below 1 GB for the included cases. It reconstructs the symbolic generators and small coefficient modules, verifies the exact floor formula, and reproduces the two arithmetic comparisons. The replay downloads the unchanged public peer library from its version-specific URL and verifies its hash. No rank search, composite enumeration or complete endpoint certificate is rerun by this supplement; those inputs remain the accepted results of [2].
