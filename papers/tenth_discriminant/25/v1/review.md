# Accept

The manuscript proves a substantive local improvement to the quadratic interpolation determinant method. Its main contribution is an exact universal weighted-jet index in the chamber $p<K\le p^3$, together with an explicit triangular row basis attaining the required divisibility. This is relevant to the supplied open-ended problem even without a new discriminant endpoint: it identifies and recovers divisibility missed by the previous separate row factors, and demonstrates a positive improvement at two existing determinant configurations.

The central proof is sound. Lemma 2's descending highest-$z$-degree argument characterizes the full weighted-derivative algebra without assuming that the two conjugate coordinates vary independently. The ghost recurrence then produces integer-valued elements, while Lemma 4 separately establishes coefficient integrality and preservation of the original triangular support. These are distinct requirements, and both are addressed. The leading-coefficient valuation follows from a unique least-valuation term in the recurrence. The support argument also covers the boundary $K=p^3$ through its secondary ordering by $z$-exponent.

The products in (8) consequently give a triangular basis with unit diagonal up to signs and total credit $E_{p,K,R}$. The matching upper bound is convincing: Lemma 2 identifies the dual lattices, and the selected consecutive Pascal blocks have determinant one. Lower-degree coefficient terms enter only earlier blocks, so the selected maximal minor has exactly the valuation in (10). The argument does not improperly infer a complete Smith spectrum from leading blocks. Removing derivative columns gives the stated finite-$J$ lower bound, with the original column prices still charged; it does not imply equality for finite $J$.

The numerical application is appropriately limited and supported. I reproduced the supplied coefficient-module indices $35$, $318$, and $3304$, the symbolic ghost checks through the stated levels, the floor-sum controls, and both directed arithmetic comparisons. The latter give gains exceeding $0.0046437577390$ and $0.0046437576393$, respectively. I also constructed evaluation matrices directly in unramified quadratic coordinates on finite Newton grids, using the supplied modular Smith routine for elimination. These additional checks gave indices $13$, $22$, and $2$ for $(p,K,R)=(2,3,9),(2,8,17),(3,27,28)$, agreeing with the theorem and including the chamber boundary. These finite computations support, rather than replace, the general proof. I did not reproduce the imported rank, metric, or complete discriminant certificates, nor independently recompute the ancillary finite-$J$ example with index $348$.

For novelty, the relevant comparison is the complete conjugate-tower and finite-jet construction in paper 2/v13 [1], not an untowered baseline. Its row credits differ from the present formula. Paper 24/v1 [2] supplies the unchanged numerical configurations, and the comparison retains its published top-cut calculation and column costs. The related row-order construction in paper 20/v1 and the divided-Frobenius rank theorem in paper 22/v5 address different invariants and do not supply this exact index theorem. The external literature check included Byszewski–Frączyk–Szumowicz [5] on integer-valued polynomials and $p$-orderings, and Hazewinkel [7], especially the ghost-component integrality criterion in Section 9.93. These establish the classical ingredients that the manuscript explicitly credits; I found no substantial overlap with its support-preserving weighted-jet construction and exact triangular index formula. References [1], [2], [5], and [7] here follow the manuscript's numbering.

The exposition meets the clarity requirement. The question, construction, matching bound, arithmetic use, and limitations form a coherent argument. The abstract is informative, notation is readable, and the small tables are sufficient for the comparisons. In particular, the paper clearly distinguishes its new local theorem from the imported $D\le331$ endpoint and makes no new classification claim.

Two minor improvements would aid readers. In Section 5, explicitly name the finite-dimensional polynomial space whose dual lattices are being compared; this would make the double-dual step easier to check. In Section 6, qualify the claimed logarithmic evaluation time as concerning the number of floor-sum levels for fixed $K$, since the periodic remainder can involve $K/\gcd(K,m)$ terms and integer bit complexity also grows. Neither point affects the scientific conclusion.
