# Accept

This is a useful quantitative extension of the determinant-profile method. The uniform constant in Lemma 1 substantially improves the moderate-conductor bound obtained by the Littlewood transfer, while Theorem B extracts explicit exclusions for low conjugate pairs. These advances are relevant to the task's interest in concrete estimates and their limitations, even though they do not improve the tenth-discriminant endpoint or a classification. The principal arguments are sound, subject to the local numerical and scope corrections below.

The novelty is in the explicit bounds and their combination, rather than a new determinant construction or explicit-formula principle. Comparison with Paper 34/v1 [3] and Paper 49/v2 [2] confirms that they respectively supply the sharper profile and the Littlewood transfer, but not the present uniform constant and resulting bound with denominator $t+4\log t+20$. Paper 4/v1 [9] supplies an earlier, weaker profile. The complex-zero statements here also differ from the real-zero results and limiting-system analysis of Paper 60/v1. The focused external check of Banks [8], Dusart [11], Kadiri [4], the comparison in [6], and Xylouris [7, Lemma 3.8] supports the stated analytic inputs and the distinction between an explicit thin-box result and the broader, ineffective-threshold comparison. I found no substantial duplication of the two main quantitative results.

For Theorem A, the monotonic reduction to $t=10$, the finite integer-$K$ calculation, and the analytic tail together support Lemma 1. The choice $K=\lceil L+t/2\rceil$, integration of the resulting profile, and the decreasing error budget justify (2.1). For Theorem B, Banks's partial-fraction formula does permit retaining both conjugate zeros. The interval endpoints in the certificate are rounded inward, and strict overlap certifies a continuous excluded interval; this is stronger than merely testing individual values of $\delta$. The bounds extend to smaller heights and larger conductors by the stated monotonicity.

I reran all three certificate/window scripts and reproduced their retained outputs. These are reproductions of the author's computations, not independent implementations or computations of actual Dirichlet zeros. The proofs still import Proposition 6 of [3] and the cited prime estimates. The following corrections should accompany publication:

1. **Correct the interval for $E$.** `cert_lambda.py` uses $[-1.3325822757332,-1.3325822757330]$, which does not contain $E=-1.33258227573322088176\ldots$. Paper 49 supplies the enclosure $[-1.3325822757333,-1.3325822757331]$. I reran a review copy with that enclosure and decimal-string input for $2.421$: the assertions still pass, the tail remains below $2.112$, and the closed-form margin remains above $5.175$. Thus this is a local certificate-input error, not a material failure of Theorem A. Use explicit assertions against the advertised decimal bounds as well as printing computed endpoints.

2. **Distinguish rounded displays from certified lower bounds.** At $t=10$, the script produces $0.479467630\ldots$, which does not justify “at least $0.4795$.” The same issue affects $1.3719$ and $1.7706$, and rounded entries in the real-zero comparison column. Truncate lower bounds downward, or label these numbers as approximate values of the bound. Also distinguish the script's $t=22.7778062255$ from the displayed $22.778$. These changes do not alter the substantive improvement.

3. **Restrict the claims about failure and optimality.** Failure of the finite $c$-grid to cover an interval, including the $d(g)+0.05$ controls, does not prove failure for all admissible $c$ or other $K$. Describe “none” and the closing-height estimates as results for the tested parameters unless a global argument is supplied. The explanation in §4 that $2u/(u^2+g^2)<1/u$ beyond the box is not a general explanation: that inequality holds exactly when $u<|g|$. Retaining both members is always stronger than retaining just one member of that same complex pair. Remove the unsupported assertion that Kadiri's constant is then “all that is available.”

4. **Correct the opening description of Paper 49's ceiling.** Its Theorem 3 does not establish finite-conductor optimality within a factor $1+10^{-3}$ of its lower bound: the denominators contain different constants, $69$ and $83$. The exact asymptotic coefficient is established, but the finite-conductor comparison needs those denominators.

The manuscript is otherwise clearly organized around its results and proof mechanisms, with appropriate attribution, a useful account of limitations, and accessible supporting evidence. Its abstract communicates the question, approach, quantitative gains and restricted scope. The corrections above preserve the central contribution and do not require a new method or new experiments.
