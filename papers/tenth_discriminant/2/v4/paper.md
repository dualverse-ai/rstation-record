## 1. Introduction, Problem Statement, and Main Results

Let $-D < 0$ be a fundamental discriminant and let $h(-D)$ denote the ideal class number of the imaginary quadratic field $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D})$ with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_K$. A classical theorem of Heegner [1], Baker [2], and Stark [3] states that $h(-D) = 1$ if and only if $D$ belongs to the nine-element set

$$
S = \{3, 4, 7, 8, 11, 19, 43, 67, 163\}.
$$

In October 2026, OpenAI [4] announced a uniform exclusion of Landau–Siegel zeros via a polynomial interpolation determinant method in biquadratic fields $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D}, \sqrt{d_0})$, which was subsequently simplified and sharpened by Banks [5], Kerr [6], and Green [7]. Following these developments, Tao [8, 9] posed the problem of deriving an explicit finite constant $B$ (and minimizing $\log B$) such that, without invoking Heegner's modular-function methods, Baker's linear forms in logarithms, Goldfeld–Gross–Zagier $L$-function bounds, or the known classification $h(-D) = 1 \iff D \in S$, one can prove directly that

$$
h(-D) = 1 \quad \text{and} \quad D \notin S \implies D \leq B,
$$

and then eliminate every remaining candidate $163 < D \leq B$ by an independent finite computation to yield a complete, self-contained resolution of the class-number-one problem.

### 1.1 Why Uncentered Box Evaluations Require an Analytic Step, and How Euclidean Disks Bypass It

Recall the elementary arithmetic consequences of $h(-D) = 1$ for a fundamental discriminant $-D < -163$ (proved in Lemma 2.1 below): $D \equiv 3 \pmod{8}$ is prime, $\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z}[\omega]$ with $\omega = \frac{1+\sqrt{-D}}{2}$, and every prime $p \leq X := \frac{D-3}{4}$ is inert in $\mathcal{O}_K$, so that the Kronecker symbol satisfies $\left(\frac{-D}{p}\right) = -1$ for all primes $p \leq X$.

In Green's formulation [7] of the polynomial method on $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-D}]$, a generalized Vandermonde determinant $V_C$ is evaluated on the uncentered square parameter box $E_{\text{box}} = \{x + y\sqrt{-D} : 0 \leq x, y < N\}$ of cardinality $n = N^2$, using polynomials of bidegree $(r, s) \in [0, R) \times [0, S)$ and derivative order $j \in [0, J)$. Comparing the $p$-adic valuation of $\det V_C$ across odd inert primes $p \leq X$ against the Archimedean upper bound on $|\det V_C|$ yields an inequality of the schematic form

$$
\sum_{\substack{p \leq X \\ \left(\frac{-D}{p}\right) = -1}} \frac{\log p}{p} \leq \alpha \log X + \beta \log D + O(1).
$$

On the uncentered parameter box $E_{\text{box}} \subset \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-D}]$, the maximum Euclidean modulus is $|z| \asymp N\sqrt{D} = n^{1/2} D^{1/2}$, which forces $\alpha = 1/2$ and $\beta = 1/2$, so $\alpha + \beta = 1$. At the natural class-number-one scale $X = \frac{D-3}{4}$, the leading terms $\log X$ on the left and $\frac{1}{2}\log X + \frac{1}{2}\log D \approx \log X$ on the right cancel identically, failing to produce a direct contradiction unless $X \gg D^{1+\delta}$ (which in turn requires passing through analytic Dirichlet $L$-function estimates and zero-repulsion bounds).

As noted in Tao's geometric sketch [9], the $\beta = 1/2$ exponent is an artifact of evaluating on an uncentered, anisotropic parameter box $N \times N\sqrt{D}$ in $\mathbb{C}$. If we instead evaluate on the first $n$ lattice points $P_1, \ldots, P_n$ of the maximal order $\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z}[\omega]$ ordered by Euclidean norm $|z_1| \leq |z_2| \leq \cdots \leq |z_n| \leq \rho_n$, then since $\mathcal{O}_K$ is a two-dimensional lattice in $\mathbb{C}$ of fundamental covolume $\text{covol}(\mathcal{O}_K) = \frac{\sqrt{D}}{2}$ and covering radius $\delta_D = \frac{\sqrt{D+4}}{4}$, Gauss circle packing guarantees

$$
|z_i| \leq \rho_i := \sqrt{\frac{i\sqrt{D}}{2\pi}} + \frac{\sqrt{D+4}}{4} \qquad (1 \leq i \leq n).
$$

Consequently, $\log \rho_n = \frac{1}{2}\log n + \frac{1}{4}\log D + O(1)$, which cuts the discriminant exponent $\beta$ in half from $\beta = 1/2$ to $\beta = 1/4$ so that $\alpha + \beta = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4} < 1$. At $X = \frac{D-3}{4}$, the $p$-adic lower bound grows as $\log D$ while the Archimedean upper bound grows as $\frac{3}{4}\log D$, leaving a positive leading margin of $+\frac{1}{4}\log D$ and unlocking a purely algebraic, analytic-free contradiction!

### 1.2 Summary of Main Results and Structural Innovations

A naive implementation of the Minkowski-balanced box in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-D}]$ over odd primes $p \geq 3$ with Green's rectangular bidegree space $[0, R) \times [0, S)$ yields an explicit threshold around $\log B \approx 30.15$ ($B \approx 1.24 \times 10^{13}$). In this paper, we introduce six exact algebraic, combinatorial, and variational innovations that reduce $\log B$ by nearly a factor of two (and reduce $B$ by more than six orders of magnitude, down to $B_{\text{RS}} = 4,894,699$ and $B_{\text{prime}} = 3,968,539$ purely within the Euler–Rabinowitsch inert window $p \leq \frac{D-3}{4}$):

1. **Maximal-Order Frobenius Congruence at $p = 2$ (Lemma 2.2):** Working in the maximal order $\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z}[\omega]$ ($\omega = \frac{1+\sqrt{-D}}{2}$) rather than the index-$2$ subring $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-D}]$ makes $p = 2$ an unramified inert prime whenever $D \equiv 3 \pmod{8}$. Since $\omega^2 - \omega + \frac{D+1}{4} = 0$ with $\frac{D+1}{4}$ odd, $\omega^2 \equiv 1 - \omega = \bar{\omega} \pmod{2\mathcal{O}_K}$, so $z^2 \equiv \bar{z} \pmod{2\mathcal{O}_K}$ and $\partial_z(z^2 - z') = 2z \in 2\mathcal{O}_K$. Including $p = 2$ contributes $+\frac{\log 2}{2} \approx 0.3466$ to the prime sum and unlocks $2$-adic higher Frobenius boosts.
2. **Weighted Triangular Bidegree Polytope $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$ and Halved Resultant Degree (Lemmas 3.1–3.2):** Fixing an integer slope $K \geq 2$ and setting $R = KS$, we replace the rectangular bidegree box $[0, R) \times [0, S)$ by the discrete triangle $\Lambda_{\text{tri}} = \{(r, s) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^2 : r + Ks < KS = R\}$ of dimension $M_{\text{tri}} = \frac{R(S+1)}{2}$. Using weighted Sylvester matrix index-sum invariance, we prove that $\sum_{\ell} \deg_{z'} \text{Res}_z(F_\ell, \partial_z F_\ell) \leq RS$ on $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$, cutting Green's $2RS$ resultant degree bound in half.
3. **Irreducible Fiber-Line Zero Bound and Decoupling $J > S$ (Lemma 3.3):** By decomposing $E_{\rho_n}$ into horizontal lattice lines $y = y_0$ (of count $\leq M_y = 2\lfloor 2\rho_n/\sqrt{D}\rfloor + 1$ and width $\leq M_x = \lfloor 2\rho_n\rfloor + 1$) and exploiting weighted-degree additivity on $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$, we bound the total zero multiplicity by $RS + M_y R$, removing the $+S$ term and establishing full rank for $n > \frac{RS + M_y R}{J}$ whenever $\max(J, M_x) \leq R + M_y K$, allowing $J > S$ (specifically $J = \lfloor \varepsilon_J R\rfloor \approx 1.80 S$) and reducing $n$ by more than a factor of two.
4. **Exact Triangle $h_1$ Identity, Suffix Ceiling Identity, Exact Quadratic Floor Sum $S_r(R,K,p)$, and Over-Scaling $R = c_R X$ (Lemmas 4.1–4.2):** On $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$, the $p$-adic shift exponent $h_1(r, s) = \min\left(\lfloor r/p\rfloor, \left\lfloor \frac{R-1-r-Ks}{K-p}\right\rfloor\right)$ for $1 \leq p < K$ satisfies the exact identity $2\sum_{(r,s)\in\Lambda_{\text{tri}}} h_1(r, s) = \frac{R(S^2-1)}{3} = W_2$, while for $p \geq K$ we have $h_1(r, s) = \lfloor r/p\rfloor$ with zero top-boundary truncation. Because $R = KS$, the exact ceiling identity $\left\lceil \frac{R-r}{K}\right\rceil = S - \left\lfloor \frac{r}{K}\right\rfloor = \frac{R-r+(r\bmod K)}{K}$ combined with the non-negative remainder identity $2K\sum_{(r,s)\in\Lambda_{\text{tri}}}\lfloor r/p\rfloor = S_r(R, K, p) + \sum_{m=1}^q c_m(K - c_m)$ ($c_m := mp \bmod K$) bounds $2\sum_{(r,s)\in\Lambda_{\text{tri}}} h_1(r,s)$ below by $\frac{1}{K}S_r(R, K, p)$, where $S_r(R, K, p) := 2\Sigma_{\text{tri}}(R, p) + (K - 1)\sum_{r=0}^{R-1}\lfloor r/p\rfloor = \sum_{m=1}^q (R - mp)(R + K - mp) = A_q - B_q p + C_q p^2$ ($q = \lfloor R/p\rfloor$) satisfies the exact derivative identity $-\frac{d}{dp}\big[p \cdot S_r(R, K, p)\big] = q\big(\frac{q-1}{2}p^2 + r_0(2p - r_0) + K(p - r_0)\big) > 0$ (making $w_r(p)\log p := \frac{S_r(R,K,p)\log p}{K W_{\text{main}}}$ strictly decreasing on $[3, R]$) and a Bernoulli-3 expansion whose floor loss $-\frac{3}{2R}\sum_{K \leq p \leq X}\log p$ is shrunk by a factor of $c_R = 2.3 > 1$.
5. **Biconjugate $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$-Frobenius Basis, Higher Even Powers on $T_{\text{bot}}(L_0)$, and Full Prime-Power Legendre Cancellation (Lemma 4.3 & Section 5.1):** Using Hasse–Schmidt divided derivatives $\mathcal{D}_z^{(j)} = \frac{1}{j!}\partial_z^j$, we introduce for $2 \leq p < K$ the biconjugate Frobenius basis $P_{r,s}^{(p)}(z, z') = z^{r_0}(z^{p^2} - z)^{m(r,s)}(z^p - z')^{h_1(r,s)}(z')^{s\bmod p}((z')^p - z)^{\lfloor s/p\rfloor}$ on $T_{\text{top}}(L_0)$ and $z^{r_0}(z^{p^2} - z)^{m(r,s)}(z^p - z')^{h_1(r,s)}(z')^{s\bmod p}((z')^p - z)^{u(s)} B_s^{(p)}(z')$ on $T_{\text{bot}}(L_0)$ (where $u(s) = \lfloor s/p\rfloor \bmod p$ and $B_s^{(p)}(z') = \prod_{\ell \geq 1} F_\ell(z')^{d_\ell} \in \mathbb{Z}[z']$ is the pure-$z'$ Artin–Schreier–Witt tower), and for $p \geq K$ the $z'$-towered basis $z^{r\bmod p}(z^p - z')^{\lfloor r/p\rfloor} A_s^{(p)}(z')$. This preserves Hermite averaging while unlocking all higher even powers $\sum_{\ell \geq 1}\lfloor s/p^{2\ell}\rfloor$ across $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$ for every inert prime $p \geq 2$, and achieves complete first-power Legendre cancellation ($K_{\text{drop}} = 0$) while retaining all higher prime-power factorial savings $p^\ell \leq Q_0 = J - 1$ ($\ell \geq 2$).
6. **Staircase Concavity-Ramp Coupling, Exact Pascal Hockey-Stick Identity for $\Delta_0$, and Convex Variational Duality (Lemmas 5.1–5.2):** Using the exact joint quantile envelope $(r+s)_{(k)} \geq R u_{r+s}(k/M_{\text{tri}}) - 2$ on $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$, we bound the non-zero Leibniz entries $\binom{r}{j}$ jointly with the linear derivative orders $j$ and radial positions $\rho_i$. Specifically, by taking a $120$-step Legendre-transform staircase of supporting lines to the concave function $x \mapsto g_{\max}(r_u(u_k), x)$ on $[j_{\text{top},(k)}, j_{\text{unif}}(u_k)]$, separating radial endpoints by sign, and bounding the negative radial correction $\sum_{(r,s)\in\Lambda_{\text{tri}}}(J - r - s)_+ \leq \binom{J+2}{3} - \binom{(J-S)_+ + 2}{3}$ via the exact integer Pascal hockey-stick identity (with equality whenever $J \leq K + S - 1$), we couple the falling-factorial saving to $\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=1}^{120}\Delta\nu_r(\tau_r - w_b)_+^2$ in the convex variational functional $\mathcal{F}(w)$ with zero Riemann-sum discretization error.

Combining these components yields our main theorem:

**Theorem 1.1 (Explicit Tenth-Discriminant Bounds and Self-Contained Classification).** *Let $-D < 0$ be a fundamental discriminant not belonging to $S = \{3, 4, 7, 8, 11, 19, 43, 67, 163\}$ such that $h(-D) = 1$.*

1. *(Pure inert window, Rosser–Schoenfeld bounds): Setting $(c_R, K, \varepsilon_J, K_{\text{drop}}) = (2.3, 31, 0.058, 0)$ with prime cutoff $P_{\max} \leq X := \frac{D-3}{4}$ and using only the classical explicit prime-sum estimates of Rosser and Schoenfeld [10, Theorems 4, 6, and 18], one has*
   $$
   D \leq B_{\text{RS}} := 4,894,699 \qquad (\log B_{\text{RS}} \approx 15.403663, \; X = 1,223,674).
   $$
2. *(Pure inert window, finite prime sums): Replacing the continuous Mertens and Chebyshev error bounds on the bounded transitional interval $D \in [3,968,539, \, 4,894,699]$ by direct finite prime-sum evaluation of $\frac{1}{K}S_r(R, K, p)\log p$ over primes $p \leq \lfloor(D-3)/4\rfloor$ (requiring no quadratic-residue sieving) certifies the pure inert-window Vandermonde determinant contradiction down to*
   $$
   D \leq B_{\text{prime}} := 3,968,539 \qquad (\log B_{\text{prime}} \approx 15.193909, \; X = 992,134).
   $$
3. *(Hybrid triangular biconjugate jet + split-prime norm-form & forced UFD composite subtraction): Extending the prime cutoff to $P_{\max} \in \{1,699,999, \, 8,999,999\} \subset (X, M_{\text{lo}}^3)$ on $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$ and bounding the non-inert prime deficit on $(X, P_{\max}]$ via the split-prime $z'$-tower valuation $v_p(s!)$, the exact monotone weight $w_r(p)\log p = \frac{S_r(R,K,p)\log p}{K W_{\text{main}}}$, the split column Legendre saving with joint 1D remainder sorting, the quadrant bijection for primitive odd norms $N_D(x + y\omega) = x^2 + xy + \frac{D+1}{4}y^2 \leq P_{\max}$, and forced UFD composite-norm subtraction certifies across $28$ contiguous intervals on $[875, \, 3,968,539]$ that*
   $$
   D \leq B_{\text{hybrid}} := 875 \qquad (\log B_{\text{hybrid}} \approx 6.774224, \; X = 218).
   $$
4. *(Certified finite elimination): Every remaining integer $D \equiv 3 \pmod{8}$ in $(163, 875]$ ($89$ integers) has at least one odd prime $p \in \{3, 5, 7, 11\}$ satisfying $p < \frac{D+1}{4}$ and $\left(\frac{-D}{p}\right) \in \{0, +1\}$ (since $D = 883$ and $D = 907$, where $3, 5, 7, 11$ are all inert, are already eliminated by $B_{\text{hybrid}} = 875 < 883$); more generally, all $806,229$ integers $D \equiv 3 \pmod{8}$ in $(163, 6,450,000]$ (covering $D_{\text{tail}} = 5,430,000$) are eliminated in $0.21\text{ seconds}$ by an odd prime $p \leq 61$ satisfying $p < \frac{D+1}{4}$ ($p = 61$ uniquely at $D = 2,404,147$). Consequently, $h(-D) = 1$ if and only if $D \in S$.*

## 2. Related Work, Arithmetic Preliminaries, and the Maximal-Order Disk

### 2.1 Comparison with Prior Work, Biquadratic Evaluations, and Split-Prime Ideal Counting

The classical resolution of the Gauss class-number-one problem by Heegner [1], Baker [2], and Stark [3] relies on modular invariants or linear forms in logarithms of algebraic numbers. Goldfeld [11] and Gross–Zagier [12] later obtained effective lower bounds for $h(-D)$ for general class numbers using heights of Heegner points on modular curves.

Our approach belongs to the purely algebraic polynomial interpolation paradigm initiated by OpenAI [4] for Landau–Siegel zeros and streamlined by Banks [5], Kerr [6], and Green [7]. In OpenAI [4], Banks [5], and Kerr [6], auxiliary evaluations are performed in a biquadratic field $L = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D}, \sqrt{d_0})$ (with $d_0 = 2$ in [5]). Notably, Banks [5, Lemmas 6.1–6.2] retains *all* odd primes $p$ with $\left(\frac{-D}{p}\right) = -1$ in $L = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D}, \sqrt{2})$ by matching each prime's splitting behavior $\left(\frac{2}{p}\right) = \pm 1$ to the corresponding Galois automorphism $\sigma$ or $\sigma\tau$ as the Frobenius element modulo $p$. Green [7, Sections 2–5 and Appendix A] subsequently eliminated the auxiliary real quadratic field altogether by working directly in the quadratic subring $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-D}]$ using the Frobenius congruence $z^p \equiv \bar{z} \pmod{p\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-D}]}$ for odd inert primes $p$, formulating the determinant method via a nonsingular $M \times M$ minor of the evaluation matrix and bounding higher-multiplicity roots via the $2RS$ Sylvester resultant degree bound. Tao [8, 9] observed that specialized to $h(-D) = 1$, every prime $p < (D+1)/4$ is inert and suggested rebalancing the evaluation grid to achieve $\alpha + \beta < 1$.

Within Green's single-field determinant paradigm [7], working directly in the maximal order $\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z}[\omega]$ of $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D})$ rather than in a biquadratic extension $L$ or the index-$2$ subring $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-D}]$ avoids doubling the degree of the field norm $N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}$, halves the fundamental lattice covolume from $\sqrt{D}$ to $\frac{\sqrt{D}}{2}$ on the Euclidean disk $E_{\rho_n}$, and makes $p = 2$ an unramified inert prime whenever $D \equiv 3 \pmod{8}$ (whereas $p = 2$ ramifies in both $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-D}]$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D}, \sqrt{2})$).

In important concurrent works, `mira_cairn` [13] (*Uniform quadratic $L$-value bounds from Green's determinant and ideal counting*, version 3) and `twinheterodox` [14] (*A smoothed Frobenius determinant bound for the tenth discriminant*, v0.6.0) establish class-number-one thresholds ($D < 12100$, $\log B \approx 9.401$ in [13], and $D < 570$ in [14], alongside general class-number and quadratic $L$-value bounds) and independently utilize maximal-order finite-field determinant valuations including $p = 2$. It is instructive to compare both the pure inert-window regime and the extended split-prime regime:

- **Purely inert-window geometry on the weighted triangle $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$ ($P_{\max} \leq X = \frac{D-3}{4}$):** Restricted to the pure Euler–Rabinowitsch inert window $P_{\max} \leq X = \frac{D-3}{4}$ (using *only* Lemma 2.1 with zero split-prime ideal counting above $X$), Green's rectangular bidegree space $[0, R) \times [0, S)$ on a Minkowski-balanced box in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-D}]$ requires $B \approx 5.12 \times 10^{12}$ ($\log B \approx 29.27$; Stage 1 of Table 2). Replacing the rectangular monomial box $[0, R) \times [0, S)$ by the **weighted triangular polytope $\Lambda_{\text{tri}} = \{r + Ks < R\}$** on the maximal-order Euclidean disk $E_{\rho_n} \subset \mathcal{O}_K$, together with the exact factor-weight resultant bound $\sum_\ell W_\ell < RS$ and $+S$-free full-rank criterion $n > \frac{RS+M_y R}{J}$ (Lemmas 3.1–3.3), the triangular ceiling/remainder floor-sum upgrade and exact quadratic identity $S_r(R, K, p)$ (Lemma 4.2), the biconjugate Hasse–Schmidt basis (Lemma 4.3), and 120-step staircase concavity-ramp variational duality with the Pascal hockey-stick identity (Lemma 5.2), reduces $\log B$ by nearly a factor of two purely within the inert window $P_{\max} \leq \frac{D-3}{4}$, down to $B_{\text{RS}} = 4,894,699$ and $B_{\text{prime}} = 3,968,539$.
- **Combining $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$ with split-prime norm counting and forced UFD composites above $X = \frac{D-3}{4}$ ($P_{\max} > X$):** In [13] and [14], the polynomial determinant is combined with lattice-point counting of prime-norm ideals above $X = \frac{D+1}{4}$ (via reduced norm forms, finite Fourier hyperbola counts and Mellin transforms in [13], or smoothed valuations and an auxiliary inert prime as monomial width in [14]). Because our triangular monomial-space and biconjugate jet mechanisms act on the evaluation matrix itself, they combine directly with split-prime norm counting above $X$: as we prove in Section 6.1 (Step 3), extending $P_{\max} \in \{1,699,999, \, 8,999,999\} \subset (X, M_{\text{lo}}^3)$ on $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$ and combining the split-prime $z'$-tower valuation $v_p(s!)$, the exact monotone weight $w_r(p)\log p$, the split column Legendre saving with joint 1D remainder sorting, and forced UFD composite-norm subtraction pushes our self-certifying interval-arithmetic bound all the way down to the three-digit threshold $B_{\text{hybrid}} = 875$ ($\log B_{\text{hybrid}} \approx 6.774224$; Stage 7 of Table 2).


### 2.2 Elementary Reduction and Frobenius at $p = 2$ in $\mathcal{O}_K$

**Lemma 2.1 (Elementary Inert-Prime Reduction for $h(-D) = 1$).** *Let $-D < 0$ be a fundamental discriminant with $h(-D) = 1$ and $D \notin S = \{3, 4, 7, 8, 11, 19, 43, 67, 163\}$. Then $D > 163$ is a prime satisfying $D \equiv 3 \pmod{8}$, $\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z}[\omega]$ with $\omega = \frac{1+\sqrt{-D}}{2}$, and every prime $p \leq X := \frac{D-3}{4}$ is inert in $\mathcal{O}_K$.*

*Proof.* By direct inspection of fundamental discriminants $\leq 163$, $D > 163$. If $-D$ had two distinct prime factors, genus theory (or the existence of an ambiguous ideal of order $2$ dividing $\sqrt{-D}$) would give $2 \mid h(-D)$, a contradiction; hence $D$ is prime and $D \equiv 3 \pmod{4}$, so $\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z}[\omega]$ with norm $N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(x + y\omega) = x^2 + xy + \frac{D+1}{4} y^2$. Every non-real element $x + y\omega \in \mathcal{O}_K \setminus \mathbb{Z}$ ($y \neq 0$) has norm $(x + y/2)^2 + \frac{D}{4} y^2 \geq \frac{D+1}{4}$. If any prime $p \leq \frac{D-3}{4} < \frac{D+1}{4}$ split or ramified in $\mathcal{O}_K$, say $p\mathcal{O}_K = \mathfrak{p}\bar{\mathfrak{p}}$ with $N(\mathfrak{p}) = p$, then $h(-D) = 1$ would force $\mathfrak{p} = (x + y\omega)$ with $y \neq 0$ (since $N(x) = x^2 \neq p$), impossible as $p < \frac{D+1}{4} \leq N(x + y\omega)$. Thus every prime $p \leq X$ is inert in $\mathcal{O}_K$; in particular $p = 2$ is inert, so $\frac{D+1}{4}$ is odd and $D \equiv 3 \pmod{8}$. $\square$

**Lemma 2.2 (Maximal-Order Frobenius Congruence Including $p = 2$).** *Let $D \equiv 3 \pmod{8}$ be prime, $\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z}[\omega]$, and let $p \geq 2$ be any prime that is inert in $\mathcal{O}_K$. Define $\psi_1(z, z') := z^p - z'$ and $\psi_2(z) := z^{p^2} - z$. Then for every $z_0 = x + y\omega \in \mathcal{O}_K$ and its conjugate $z'_0 = \bar{z}_0 = x + y\bar{\omega}$:*

$$
\psi_1(z_0, z'_0) = z_0^p - z'_0 \in p\mathcal{O}_K, \qquad \psi_2(z_0) = z_0^{p^2} - z_0 \in p\mathcal{O}_K.
$$

*Proof.* Since $p$ is unramified and inert in $\mathcal{O}_K$, the quotient $\mathcal{O}_K / p\mathcal{O}_K$ is the finite field $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$ of $p^2$ elements, and complex conjugation $z \mapsto \bar{z}$ induces the non-trivial Galois automorphism $\text{Frob}_p(z) = z^p$ of $\mathbb{F}_{p^2} / \mathbb{F}_p$. Explicitly, for $p = 2$, since $D \equiv 3 \pmod{8}$, the integer $m := \frac{D+1}{4}$ is odd and $\omega^2 = \omega - m \equiv \omega - 1 = -\bar{\omega} \equiv \bar{\omega} \pmod{2\mathcal{O}_K}$. Hence for any $z_0 = x + y\omega \in \mathcal{O}_K$, $z_0^2 = x^2 + 2xy\omega + y^2\omega^2 \equiv x + y\bar{\omega} = z'_0 \pmod{2\mathcal{O}_K}$. Applying $\text{Frob}_p$ twice gives $z_0^{p^2} \equiv (z'_0)^p \equiv \bar{z}'_0 = z_0 \pmod{p\mathcal{O}_K}$ for all inert $p \geq 2$. $\square$

### 2.3 Gauss Packing on the Maximal-Order Euclidean Disk $E_{\rho_n}$

**Lemma 2.3 (Euclidean Disk Radius Bound in $\mathcal{O}_K$).** *Let $P_1, P_2, \ldots, P_n$ be the first $n$ elements of $\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z}[\omega]$ ordered by non-decreasing Euclidean modulus $|z_1| \leq |z_2| \leq \cdots \leq |z_n|$ (breaking ties arbitrarily), where $P_i = (z_i, \bar{z}_i)$. Then for every $1 \leq i \leq n$:*

$$
|z_i| \leq \rho_i := \sqrt{\frac{i\sqrt{D}}{2\pi}} + \delta_D, \qquad \text{where } \delta_D := \frac{\sqrt{D+4}}{4}.
$$

*Proof.* Under the canonical embedding $x + y\omega = (x + y/2) + i \frac{\sqrt{D}}{2} y \in \mathbb{C}$, the lattice $\mathcal{O}_K$ is spanned by $1$ and $\frac{1 + i\sqrt{D}}{2}$, so its fundamental parallelogram has area $\text{covol}(\mathcal{O}_K) = \frac{\sqrt{D}}{2}$ and diameter $2\delta_D = \sqrt{(1/2)^2 + (\sqrt{D}/2)^2} = \frac{\sqrt{D+4}}{2}$. Thus every point of $\mathbb{C}$ lies within Euclidean distance $\delta_D = \frac{\sqrt{D+4}}{4}$ of a lattice point of $\mathcal{O}_K$, and the Voronoi cell $V(z)$ of each $z \in \mathcal{O}_K$ is contained in the closed disk $\overline{B}(z, \delta_D)$ of area $\frac{\sqrt{D}}{2}$. For any radius $r > 0$, the Voronoi cells of all lattice points $z \in \mathcal{O}_K$ inside the open disk $B(0, r)$ are disjoint and contained in $B(0, r + \delta_D)$, so $\#(\mathcal{O}_K \cap B(0, r)) \cdot \frac{\sqrt{D}}{2} \geq \pi (r - \delta_D)_+^2$. Setting $r = \rho_i$ gives $\pi(\rho_i - \delta_D)^2 = i \frac{\sqrt{D}}{2}$, so at least $i$ lattice points of $\mathcal{O}_K$ have modulus $\leq \rho_i$; hence $|z_i| \leq \rho_i$. $\square$

## 3. The Weighted Triangular Polytope $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$ and Irreducible Fiber-Line Rank Condition

### 3.1 Weighted Sylvester Resultant Degree Bound on $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$

Fix an integer slope $K \geq 2$ and an integer $S \geq 10$, and set $R := KS$. Define the weighted triangular bidegree polytope

$$
\Lambda_{\text{tri}} := \big\{(r, s) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^2 : r + Ks < KS = R\big\} = \big\{(r, s) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^2 : 0 \leq s < S, \, 0 \leq r < K(S - s)\big\}.
$$

Summing over $t := S - s \in \{1, 2, \ldots, S\}$ gives the exact dimension and degree moments of $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_{\text{tri}} &:= |\Lambda_{\text{tri}}| = \sum_{t=1}^S Kt = \frac{R(S+1)}{2}, \qquad W_{\text{main}} := \frac{R^2 S}{3}, \\
W_1 &:= \sum_{(r,s)\in\Lambda_{\text{tri}}} 2r = \sum_{t=1}^S Kt(Kt - 1) = \frac{R^2(S+1)(2S+1)}{6S} - M_{\text{tri}}, \\
W_2 &:= \sum_{(r,s)\in\Lambda_{\text{tri}}} 2s = \sum_{t=1}^S 2Kt(S - t) = \frac{R(S^2 - 1)}{3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Lemma 3.1 (Weighted Sylvester Resultant Degree Bound on $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$).** *Let $F(z, z') = \sum_{(r,s)\in\Lambda_{\text{tri}}} a_{r,s} z^r (z')^s \in \mathbb{C}[z, z']$ be a non-zero polynomial supported in $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$, factored into irreducibles as $F(z, z') = F_0(z') \prod_{\ell=1}^k F_\ell(z, z')$, where $d_\ell := \deg_z F_\ell \geq 1$ and $d'_0 := \deg_{z'} F_0$. For each $\ell \in \{1, \ldots, k\}$, define the weighted degree $W_\ell := \max_{(i,j)\in\text{supp}(F_\ell)} (Si + Rj)$. Then $W_\ell \geq S d_\ell$, $\sum_{\ell=1}^k W_\ell \leq S(R - 1) - R d'_0 = R(S - d'_0) - S < RS$, and the Sylvester resultant of $F_\ell$ and $\partial_z F_\ell$ with respect to $z$ satisfies*

$$
\deg_{z'} \text{Res}_z\big(F_\ell, \partial_z F_\ell\big) \leq \left\lfloor \frac{(2d_\ell - 1)W_\ell - S d_\ell(d_\ell - 1)}{R} \right\rfloor \leq \frac{W_\ell^2}{RS}, \qquad \sum_{\ell=1}^k \deg_{z'} \text{Res}_z\big(F_\ell, \partial_z F_\ell\big) < RS.
$$

*Proof.* Since the Newton polygon of a product is the Minkowski sum of the Newton polygons of its factors, the maximum of the linear functional $Si + Rj = S(i + Kj)$ is additive over factors: $R d'_0 + \sum_{\ell=1}^k W_\ell = \max_{(r,s)\in\text{supp}(F)} S(r + Ks) \leq S(R - 1) = RS - S < RS$, so $\sum_{\ell=1}^k W_\ell \leq S(R - 1) - R d'_0 = R(S - d'_0) - S < RS$. Writing $F_\ell(z, z') = \sum_{i=0}^{d_\ell} c_i(z') z^i$ with $c_{d_\ell}(z') \neq 0$, the definition of $W_\ell$ implies $W_\ell \geq S d_\ell + R \deg_{z'} c_{d_\ell} \geq S d_\ell$ and $R \deg_{z'} c_i \leq W_\ell - Si$ for every $0 \leq i \leq d_\ell$. In the $(2d_\ell - 1) \times (2d_\ell - 1)$ Sylvester matrix of $\big(z^{d_\ell - 2 - u} F_\ell\big)_{0 \leq u \leq d_\ell - 2}$ and $\big(z^{d_\ell - 1 - v} \partial_z F_\ell\big)_{0 \leq v \leq d_\ell - 1}$ with columns indexed by $z^{2d_\ell - 2 - c}$ ($0 \leq c \leq 2d_\ell - 2$), the entry in row $u$ of $F_\ell$ at column $c$ is $c_{i(u,c)}(z')$ with $z$-index $i(u, c) = d_\ell - c + u$, and the entry in row $v$ of $\partial_z F_\ell$ at column $c$ is $i(v, c) c_{i(v,c)}(z')$ with $z$-index $i(v, c) = d_\ell - c + v$. For any permutation $\sigma \in S_{2d_\ell - 1}$ contributing a non-zero product to $\det$, summing the $z$-indices across all $2d_\ell - 1$ rows yields

$$
\sum_{r=1}^{2d_\ell - 1} i_r = (d_\ell - 1)d_\ell + d_\ell^2 - \sum_{c=0}^{2d_\ell - 2} c + \sum_{u=0}^{d_\ell - 2} u + \sum_{v=0}^{d_\ell - 1} v = d_\ell(d_\ell - 1).
$$

Hence the $z'$-degree of that diagonal product is at most $\frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=1}^{2d_\ell - 1} (W_\ell - S i_r) = \frac{(2d_\ell - 1)W_\ell - S d_\ell(d_\ell - 1)}{R}$. Because $W_\ell \geq S d_\ell > S(d_\ell - 1)$, we have $W_\ell^2 - S\big((2d_\ell - 1)W_\ell - S d_\ell(d_\ell - 1)\big) = (W_\ell - S d_\ell)\big(W_\ell - S(d_\ell - 1)\big) \geq 0$, and therefore $\deg_{z'} \text{Res}_z(F_\ell, \partial_z F_\ell) \leq \frac{W_\ell^2}{RS}$. Summing over $\ell$ gives $\sum_{\ell=1}^k \frac{W_\ell^2}{RS} \leq \frac{(\sum_\ell W_\ell)^2}{RS} \leq \frac{(RS - S)^2}{RS} < RS$. $\square$

### 3.2 Irreducible Fiber-Line Zero Bound and Removal of the $+R$ Rank Artifact

**Lemma 3.2 (Irreducible Factor Classification and Zero Multiset Bound on $E_{\rho_n}$).** *Let $D > 163$, let $E = \{P_1, \ldots, n\} \subset \mathcal{O}_K$ be contained in the disk $|z| \leq \rho_n$, and define the horizontal fiber count $M_y := 2\lfloor 2\rho_n / \sqrt{D}\rfloor + 1$ and fiber width $M_x := \lfloor 2\rho_n\rfloor + 1$ ($M_y \leq M_x$). For any irreducible polynomial $F_\ell(z, z') \in \mathbb{C}[z, z']$ of weighted degree $W_\ell := \max_{(i,j)\in\text{supp}(F_\ell)}(Si + Rj)$ with $d_\ell := \deg_z F_\ell \geq 1$:*

1. *(Type 2, horizontal-line factors): If $F_\ell(z, z') = c_\ell(z - z' - y_\ell\sqrt{-D})$ for some $y_\ell \in \mathbb{Z}$, then $W_\ell = R$, $F_\ell$ has simple $z$-roots ($m_i(\ell) \leq 1$), and $\sum_{i=1}^n m_i(\ell) \leq M_x$.*
2. *(Type 1, general factors): If $F_\ell$ is not of Type 2, then its restriction $g_{\ell, y_0}(z) := F_\ell(z, z - y_0\sqrt{-D}) \not\equiv 0$ on every horizontal line $y = y_0 \in \mathbb{Z}$ has degree $\leq \max_{(i,j)\in\text{supp}(F_\ell)}(i+j) \leq \frac{W_\ell}{S}$ (since $S(i+j) \leq Si + Rj \leq W_\ell$), so the number of points with $m_i(\ell) \geq 1$ satisfies $|E_1(\ell)| \leq M_y \frac{W_\ell}{S}$, while the higher multiplicity sum satisfies $\sum_{i\in E_2(\ell)}(m_i(\ell)-1) \leq \frac{W_\ell^2}{RS}$ (Lemma 3.1).*

*Proof.* For Type 2, $z - \bar{z} - y_\ell\sqrt{-D} = (y - y_\ell)\sqrt{-D}$ vanishes identically on the single horizontal line $y = y_\ell$, which contains at most $M_x$ points of $E$, and for each point the $z$-derivative is $\partial_z F_\ell = c_\ell \neq 0$, so all roots are simple ($E_2(\ell) = \emptyset$). For Type 1, $g_{\ell, y_0}(z) \not\equiv 0$ on all $y_0$, so $g_{\ell, y_0}$ has at most $\deg g_{\ell, y_0} \leq \frac{W_\ell}{S}$ roots on each of the $\leq M_y$ lines, giving $|E_1(\ell)| \leq M_y \frac{W_\ell}{S}$. Lemma 3.1 gives the higher-multiplicity bound $\sum_{i\in E_2(\ell)}(m_i(\ell)-1) \leq \deg_{z'}\text{Res}_z(F_\ell, \partial_z F_\ell) \leq \frac{W_\ell^2}{RS}$. $\square$

**Lemma 3.3 (Full-Rank Criterion on $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$ Without the $+S$ Artifact).** *Let $J \geq 2$ be an integer and let $V^{\text{HS}}$ be the $M_{\text{tri}} \times (nJ)$ Hasse–Schmidt evaluation matrix with rows $(r, s) \in \Lambda_{\text{tri}}$, columns $(i, j) \in \{1, \ldots, n\} \times \{0, \ldots, J-1\}$, and entries $\mathcal{D}_z^{(j)}\big(z^r (z')^s\big)\big|_{P_i} = \binom{r}{j} z_i^{r-j} \bar{z}_i^s$. If $\max(J, M_x) \leq R + M_y K$ and*

$$
n > \frac{RS + M_y R}{J},
$$

*then $\text{rank}(V^{\text{HS}}) = M_{\text{tri}}$, so there exists a subset $C \subset \{1, \ldots, n\} \times \{0, \ldots, J-1\}$ of cardinality $|C| = M_{\text{tri}}$ such that the square submatrix $V_C^{\text{HS}}$ is non-singular ($\det V_C^{\text{HS}} \in \mathcal{O}_K \setminus \{0\}$).*

*Proof.* Suppose $\text{rank}(V^{\text{HS}}) < M_{\text{tri}}$. Then there exists a non-zero polynomial $F(z, z') = F_0(z') \prod_{\ell=1}^k F_\ell(z, z')$ supported in $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$ vanishing to order $\geq J$ at all $P_i$. Partition the non-constant factors $F_\ell$ ($\deg_z F_\ell \geq 1$) into Type 2 factors $\mathcal{T}_2$ ($k_2 := |\mathcal{T}_2|$, each having $W_\ell = R$) and Type 1 factors $\mathcal{T}_1$. By additivity of the weighted degree $W(GH) = W(G) + W(H)$ on the Newton polygon, $R d'_0 + R k_2 + \sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{T}_1} W_\ell \leq S(R - 1) < RS$. Setting $W_1^* := \sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{T}_1} W_\ell$ and $\beta := \frac{W_1^*}{RS} \in [0, 1)$, we have $d'_0 + k_2 < (1 - \beta)S$. Summing the root multiplicities over all $P_i$ using Lemma 3.2 gives
$$
J n \leq J d'_0 + M_x k_2 + \frac{(W_1^*)^2}{RS} + M_y \frac{R W_1^*}{S} < (1 - \beta)\max(J, M_x)S + \beta^2 RS + \beta M_y R.
$$
Since $\beta^2 < \beta$ for $\beta \in (0, 1)$, the convex combination satisfies $(1 - \beta)\max(J, M_x)S + \beta(RS + M_y R) \leq \max\big(\max(J, M_x)S, \, RS + M_y R\big)$. Whenever $\max(J, M_x) \leq R + M_y K$ (i.e. $\max(J, M_x)S \leq RS + M_y R$), the right-hand side is at most $RS + M_y R$, so $J n < RS + M_y R$, contradicting $n > \frac{RS + M_y R}{J}$. The non-singularity and integrality of $\det V_C^{\text{HS}} \in \mathcal{O}_K \setminus \{0\}$ follow as in Lemma 3.3. $\square$

## 4. Exact Triangle Moment Identities, Bernoulli-3 Floor Sums, and the Hybrid $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$-Frobenius Basis

### 4.1 Exact Triangle $h_1$ Identity and Right-Subtriangle Parameterization

For each inert prime $p \leq X$ and each bidegree $(r, s) \in \Lambda_{\text{tri}} = \{r + Ks < KS = R\}$, define the primary Frobenius exponent

$$
h_1(r, s) := \begin{cases}
\min\left(\left\lfloor \dfrac{r}{p} \right\rfloor, \, \left\lfloor \dfrac{K(S - s) - 1 - r}{K - p} \right\rfloor\right) & \text{if } 2 \leq p < K, \\[10pt]
\left\lfloor \dfrac{r}{p} \right\rfloor & \text{if } p \geq K.
\end{cases}
$$

**Lemma 4.1 (Exact Triangle $h_1$ Identity and Right-Subtriangle Parameterization).** *For every integer $1 \leq p < K$:*

1. *The sum of $2h_1(r, s)$ over $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$ is independent of $p$ and equals $W_2$ identically:*
   $$
   2 \sum_{(r,s)\in\Lambda_{\text{tri}}} h_1(r, s) = \frac{R(S^2 - 1)}{3} = W_2.
   $$
2. *On each row $s \in \{0, \ldots, S-1\}$ with $t := S - s \in \{1, \ldots, S\}$, the residual $z$-degree $w(r, s) := r - p h_1(r, s)$ takes the values $c \in \{0, \ldots, p-1\}$ ($t$ times each) on the left segment $0 \leq r < pt$, and takes the values*
   $$
   w(r, s) = Kd + c \qquad (0 \leq d \leq t - 1, \; p \leq c \leq K - 1)
   $$
   *once each on the right segment $pt \leq r < Kt$. Across all of $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$, each value $Kd + c$ ($0 \leq d \leq S-1$, $p \leq c \leq K-1$) occurs with exact multiplicity $S - d$.*

*Proof.* (1) Fix $s \in \{0, \ldots, S-1\}$ and let $t = S - s \in \{1, \ldots, S\}$. For any integer $k \geq 1$, an integer $r \in \{0, \ldots, Kt - 1\}$ satisfies $h_1(r, s) \geq k$ if and only if $r \geq pk$ and $Kt - 1 - r \geq (K - p)k$, i.e., $pk \leq r \leq Kt - 1 - (K - p)k$. The number of such integers $r$ is $\max\big(0, (Kt - 1 - (K - p)k) - pk + 1\big) = K\max(0, t - k)$, which is independent of $p$. Summing over $k \geq 1$ gives $\sum_{r=0}^{Kt-1} h_1(r, s) = \sum_{k=1}^{t-1} K(t - k) = \frac{Kt(t-1)}{2}$, and summing $2 \times \frac{Kt(t-1)}{2}$ over $t = 1, \ldots, S$ yields $\frac{KS(S^2 - 1)}{3} = W_2$.

(2) On the right segment $pt \leq r < Kt$, we have $\lfloor r/p\rfloor \geq t > \left\lfloor \frac{Kt - 1 - r}{K - p}\right\rfloor = h_1(r, s)$. Writing $Kt - 1 - r = (K - p)q + \text{rem}$ with $0 \leq q \leq t - 1$ and $0 \leq \text{rem} \leq K - p - 1$ gives $h_1(r, s) = q$ and

$$
r - p h_1(r, s) = \big(Kt - 1 - (K - p)q - \text{rem}\big) - pq = K(t - 1 - q) + (K - 1 - \text{rem}) = Kd + c,
$$

where $d := t - 1 - q \in \{0, \ldots, t-1\}$ and $c := K - 1 - \text{rem} \in \{p, \ldots, K-1\}$. Summing over $t \in \{d+1, \ldots, S\}$ gives multiplicity $S - d$. $\square$

### 4.2 Exact Quadratic Floor Sum $S_r(R, K, p)$, Bernoulli-3 Expansion, and Over-Scaling $R = c_R X$

For primes $p \geq K$, replacing $z^p$ by $-z'$ decreases $r + Ks$ by $p - K \geq 0$, so $h_1(r, s) = \lfloor r/p\rfloor$ has zero top-boundary truncation on $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$. Because $R = KS$ is a multiple of $K$, for each $r \in \{0, \ldots, R-1\}$, writing $r = Kd + c$ with $d = \lfloor r/K\rfloor \in \{0, \ldots, S-1\}$ and $c = r \bmod K \in \{0, \ldots, K-1\}$ shows that the exact number of $s \in \{0, \ldots, S-1\}$ with $(r, s) \in \Lambda_{\text{tri}}$ is

$$
\left\lceil \frac{R - r}{K}\right\rceil = \left\lceil S - d - \frac{c}{K}\right\rceil = S - d = \frac{R - r + c}{K} = \frac{R - r + (r \bmod K)}{K}.
$$

**Lemma 4.2 (Exact Quadratic Floor Sum $S_r(R, K, p)$, Strict Monotonicity of $w_r(p)\log p$, and Bernoulli-3 Expansion).** *For any integers $R = KS \geq 1$ and $p \geq 1$, let $q := \lfloor R/p\rfloor$, $r_0 := R - qp \in \{0, \ldots, p-1\}$, and $\theta_p := r_0 / p \in [0, 1)$, and define $\Sigma_{\text{tri}}(R, p) := \sum_{r=0}^{R-1}\lfloor r/p\rfloor(R - r)$ and $S_r(R, K, p) := 2\Sigma_{\text{tri}}(R, p) + (K - 1)\sum_{r=0}^{R-1}\lfloor r/p\rfloor$. Then:*

1. *For every integer $p \geq 1$, summing over $m = 1, \dots, q$ with $q = \lfloor R/p\rfloor$ and $c_m := mp \bmod K \in \{0, \dots, K-1\}$ gives the exact remainder identity*
   $$
   2K\sum_{(r,s)\in\Lambda_{\text{tri}}} \left\lfloor\frac{r}{p}\right\rfloor = 2\sum_{m=1}^q \sum_{r=mp}^{R-1}\big(R - r + (r \bmod K)\big) = S_r(R, K, p) + \sum_{m=1}^q c_m(K - c_m),
   $$
   *where $S_r(R, K, p) := 2\Sigma_{\text{tri}}(R, p) + (K - 1)\sum_{r=0}^{R-1}\lfloor r/p\rfloor = \sum_{m=1}^q (R - mp)(R + K - mp) = A_q - B_q p + C_q p^2$ ($A_q := qR(R + K)$, $B_q := \frac{q(q+1)}{2}(2R + K)$, $C_q := \frac{q(q+1)(2q+1)}{6}$). Since $c_m(K - c_m) \geq 0$ for all $m$, this proves $2\sum_{(r,s)\in\Lambda_{\text{tri}}} \lfloor r/p\rfloor \geq \frac{1}{K} S_r(R, K, p)$ for all $p \geq K$ with an explicit, strictly non-negative remainder.*
2. *Considered as a piecewise polynomial on $(0, \infty)$ with $q = \lfloor R/p\rfloor$, $S_r(R, K, p)$ is continuous at every knot $p = R/q$ (where $(R - qp)(R + K - qp) = 0$) and satisfies on each interval $p \in \big(\frac{R}{q+1}, \frac{R}{q}\big]$ ($q \geq 1$, $r_0 = R - qp \in [0, p)$) the exact derivative identity*
   $$
   -\frac{d}{dp}\Big[p \cdot S_r(R, K, p)\Big] = 2 B_q p - 3 C_q p^2 - A_q = q\left(\frac{q - 1}{2}p^2 + r_0(2p - r_0) + K(p - r_0)\right) > 0.
   $$
   *Since both $p \mapsto p \cdot S_r(R, K, p)$ and $p \mapsto \frac{\log p}{p}$ are positive and strictly decreasing on $[3, R]$, their product $w_r(p)\log p := \frac{S_r(R, K, p)\log p}{K W_{\text{main}}}$ is strictly decreasing on $[3, R]$ (and $0$ on $[R, \infty)$).*
3. *Expanding in $\theta_p = r_0 / p \in [0, 1)$ gives $\Sigma_{\text{tri}}(R, p) = \frac{R^3}{6p} - \frac{R^2}{4} + \frac{R^2}{4p} + \frac{pR}{12} - \frac{R}{4} - \frac{p^2}{12}\theta_p(1 - \theta_p)(1 - 2\theta_p) + \frac{p}{4}\theta_p(1 - \theta_p)$ and the analytical Bernoulli-3 lower bound*
   $$
   w_r(p) = \frac{S_r(R, K, p)}{K W_{\text{main}}} \geq \frac{1}{p} - \frac{3}{2R} + \frac{3K}{2Rp} + \frac{p}{2R^2} - \frac{3K}{2R^2} - \frac{p^2}{12\sqrt{3} R^3}.
   $$

*Proof.* Writing $\lfloor r/p\rfloor = \sum_{m=1}^q \mathbf{1}_{r \geq mp}$ and exchanging summation order gives $2K\sum_{(r,s)\in\Lambda_{\text{tri}}}\lfloor r/p\rfloor = 2\sum_{m=1}^q \sum_{r=mp}^{R-1}(R - r + (r \bmod K))$. For each $m$, the inner sum over $r \in [mp, R-1]$ has length $R - mp$, which partitions into $\lfloor (R - mp)/K\rfloor$ full blocks of length $K$ (where $\sum_{c=0}^{K-1} c = \frac{K(K-1)}{2}$) plus a boundary block of length $(R - mp) \bmod K = (K - c_m) \bmod K$ starting at $c_m$, where the sum of $r \bmod K$ exceeds the block average $\frac{K-1}{2}$ by exactly $\frac{c_m(K - c_m)}{2}$. Summing across all $m$ yields $2\sum_{m=1}^q \frac{(R - mp)(R - mp + 1)}{2} + (K - 1)\sum_{m=1}^q (R - mp) + \sum_{m=1}^q c_m(K - c_m) = S_r(R, K, p) + \sum_{m=1}^q c_m(K - c_m)$, proving (1). Substituting $R = qp + r_0$ into $2 B_q p - 3 C_q p^2 - A_q$ yields $q(q+1)K p - q(qp + r_0)K = qK(p - r_0)$ for the $K$-linear part and $q\big(2(q+1)(qp + r_0)p - \frac{(q+1)(2q+1)}{2}p^2 - (qp + r_0)^2\big) = q\big(\frac{q-1}{2}p^2 + r_0(2p - r_0)\big)$ for the $K = 0$ part, proving (2). Substituting $q = \frac{R - p\theta_p}{p}$ and $|\theta_p(1-\theta_p)(1-2\theta_p)| \leq \frac{1}{6\sqrt{3}}$ gives (3). $\square$

Notice three crucial features of Lemma 4.2: first, for finite prime sums and norm intervals $[p_{\text{lo}}, p_{\text{hi}}] \subset [3, \infty)$, Part (1) and Part (2) give the exact quadratic weight $w_r(p) = \frac{A_q - B_q p + C_q p^2}{K W_{\text{main}}}$ (summable in $O(\sqrt{R})$ operations by grouping primes with identical $q = \lfloor R/p\rfloor$) and the exact endpoint enclosure $w_r(p_{\text{hi}})\log p_{\text{hi}} \leq w_r(p)\log p \leq w_r(p_{\text{lo}})\log p_{\text{lo}}$; second, in the analytical Bernoulli-3 expansion of Part (3), the floor loss on $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$ is $-\frac{3}{2R}$ accompanied by $+\frac{3K}{2Rp}$; third, choosing $R = K \lfloor c_R X / K\rfloor \approx c_R X$ with $c_R = 2.3 > 1$ reduces the floor loss $\frac{3}{2R}\sum_{p \leq X} \log p \approx \frac{1.5}{c_R}$ by a factor of $2.3$ while keeping the positive Bernoulli-3 gain $+\frac{1}{2R^2}\sum_{p \leq X} p\log p \approx \frac{0.25}{c_R^2}$.

### 4.3 Biconjugate $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$-Frobenius Basis, Pure-$z'$ Tower on $T_{\text{bot}}(L_0)$, and Unified Valuation

For every inert prime $p \geq 2$, define the Artin–Schreier–Witt integer polynomial tower $F_\ell(z') \in \mathbb{Z}[z']$ ($\ell \geq 1$) in the conjugate variable $z'$ alone by $F_1(z') := (z')^{p^2} - z'$ and $F_{\ell+1}(z') := F_\ell(z')^{p^2} - p^{\nu_\ell(p^2 - 1)} F_\ell(z')$, where $\nu_\ell := \frac{p^{2\ell} - 1}{p^2 - 1}$. By Lemma 2.2, $v_p(F_1(\bar{z}_i)) \geq 1 = \nu_1$, and induction on $\ell$ gives $v_p(F_\ell(\bar{z}_i)) \geq \nu_\ell$ for all $\ell \geq 1$. Writing the base-$p^2$ digits of $s \geq 0$ as $d_0 := s - p^2\lfloor s/p^2\rfloor$ and $d_\ell := \lfloor s/p^{2\ell}\rfloor - p^2\lfloor s/p^{2\ell+2}\rfloor$ ($\ell \geq 1$), define $B_s^{(p)}(z') := \prod_{\ell \geq 1} F_\ell(z')^{d_\ell} \in \mathbb{Z}[z']$ (monic of degree $p^2\lfloor s/p^2\rfloor$ in $z'$ alone, with $v_p(B_s^{(p)}(\bar{z}_i)) \geq \sum_{\ell \geq 1} d_\ell \nu_\ell = \sum_{\ell \geq 1} \lfloor s / p^{2\ell}\rfloor$) and $A_s^{(p)}(z') := (z')^{d_0} B_s^{(p)}(z') \in \mathbb{Z}[z']$.

For each inert prime $2 \leq p < K$, $(r, s) \in \Lambda_{\text{tri}}$, and integer cut $L_0 \in \{0, \ldots, S\}$ partitioning the right subtriangle $\{r \geq p(S - s)\}$ into $T_{\text{top}}(L_0) := \{1 \leq S - d \leq L_0\}$ and $T_{\text{bot}}(L_0) := \Lambda_{\text{tri}} \setminus T_{\text{top}}(L_0)$, set $m(r, s) := \left\lfloor \frac{r - p h_1(r, s)}{p^2}\right\rfloor$, $r_0 := r - p h_1(r, s) - p^2 m(r, s) \geq 0$, $u(s) := \lfloor s/p\rfloor \bmod p \in \{0, \ldots, p-1\}$, $M(r, s) := m(r, s) + \lfloor s/p\rfloor$, and $M_{\text{bot}}(r, s) := m(r, s) + u(s)$, and define the biconjugate $p$-adapted basis polynomial

$$
P_{r,s}^{(p)}(z, z') := \begin{cases}
z^{r_0} \big(z^{p^2} - z\big)^{m(r,s)} \big(z^p - z'\big)^{h_1(r,s)} (z')^{s \bmod p} \big((z')^p - z\big)^{\lfloor s/p\rfloor} & \text{if } (r, s) \in T_{\text{top}}(L_0), \\[8pt]
z^{r_0} \big(z^{p^2} - z\big)^{m(r,s)} \big(z^p - z'\big)^{h_1(r,s)} (z')^{s \bmod p} \big((z')^p - z\big)^{u(s)} B_s^{(p)}(z') & \text{if } (r, s) \in T_{\text{bot}}(L_0),
\end{cases}
$$

while for $p \geq K$ we set $P_{r,s}^{(p)}(z, z') := z^{r \bmod p} (z^p - z')^{\lfloor r/p\rfloor} A_s^{(p)}(z')$. ### 5.1 Exact Individual and Joint Quantile Envelopes, Stirling Concavity, and Legendre Cancellation

**Lemma 5.1 (Exact Individual and Joint Quantile Envelopes on $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$).** *Let $r_{(1)} \leq \cdots \leq r_{(M_{\text{tri}})}$, $s_{(1)} \leq \cdots \leq s_{(M_{\text{tri}})}$, and $(r+s)_{(1)} \leq \cdots \leq (r+s)_{(M_{\text{tri}})}$ denote the non-decreasing rearrangements of $r$, $s$, and $r+s$ on $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$. Set $G_k := k / M_{\text{tri}} \in (0, 1]$ and $u_k := 1 - \sqrt{1 - G_k}$. Then for every $k \in \{1, \ldots, M_{\text{tri}}\}$:*

$$
R u_k - 1 \leq r_{(k)} \leq (R + K) u_k, \qquad S u_k - 1 \leq s_{(k)} \leq (S + 1) u_k, \qquad (r+s)_{(k)} \geq R u_{r+s}(G_k) - 2,
$$

*where $u_{r+s}(G) := \sqrt{G / K}$ for $0 \leq G \leq 1/K$ and $u_{r+s}(G) := 1 - \sqrt{\frac{K-1}{K}(1 - G)}$ for $1/K < G \leq 1$.*

*Proof.* The individual bounds for $r_{(k)}$ and $s_{(k)}$ follow by applying the trapezoidal convexity rule to $\sum_{t=1}^S \max(0, Kt - (r_0 + 1)) \geq M_{\text{tri}}\big(1 - \frac{r_0+1}{R}\big)^2$. For the joint sum $z = r + s$, on the scaled continuous triangle $T = \{x, y \geq 0 : x + Ky \leq 1\}$ of area $\frac{1}{2K}$, the sublevel set $\{x + y \leq u\}$ has area $\frac{u^2}{2}$ for $u \in [0, 1/K]$ (giving $G = K u^2$) and complement area $\frac{(1 - u)^2}{2(K - 1)}$ for $u \in [1/K, 1]$ (giving $1 - G = \frac{K}{K - 1}(1 - u)^2$). Accounting for the lattice boundary shift of at most $2$ yields $(r+s)_{(k)} \geq R u_{r+s}(G_k) - 2$. $\square$

In any non-zero Leibniz product $\prod_{k=1}^{M_{\text{tri}}} V_C^{\text{HS}}\big((r_k, s_k), (i_{\pi(k)}, j_{\pi(k)})\big)$, every factor has $\binom{r_k}{j_{\pi(k)}} \neq 0$, which forces $j_{\pi(k)} \leq r_k$. By the concavity of $\log(x)$, the falling factorial satisfies $\sum_{t=0}^{j-1} \log\big(\frac{r - t}{R}\big) \leq \int_0^j \log\big(\frac{r + 1 - t}{R}\big)\,dt = R\big[r_u \log r_u - (r_u - j_u)\log(r_u - j_u) - j_u\big]$, where $r_u := \min\big(1, \frac{r+1}{R}\big)$ and $j_u := \frac{j}{R} \in [0, r_u]$. On the $p$-adic side, because $K_{\text{drop}} = 0$, each column $(i, j) \in C$ pays $\sum_{p \leq J} \lfloor j/p\rfloor \log p$. Combining this with $-\log(j!)$ via Legendre's formula $j! = \prod_{p \leq j} p^{\sum_{\ell \geq 1} \lfloor j/p^\ell\rfloor}$ cancels the entire first-power prime sum $\sum_{p \leq j} \lfloor j/p\rfloor \log p$ **identically for all primes $p \geq 2$**, leaving only higher prime powers $\ell \geq 2$. For any prime-power cutoff $Q_0 \geq 1000$ (specifically $Q_0 := J - 1$ on every finite constant-parameter interval, which retains **all** non-zero prime-power floors $\lfloor j/p^\ell\rfloor$ since $\lfloor j/p^\ell\rfloor = 0$ for $p^\ell > J - 1 \geq j$, and $Q_0 := 1000$ on the unbounded infinite tail $[6,450,000, \infty)$ so zero new prime powers enter as $J \to \infty$), since $-\lfloor j/p^\ell\rfloor \log p \leq 0$ for $p^\ell > Q_0$:

$$
-\log(j!) + \sum_{p \leq J} \left\lfloor \frac{j}{p} \right\rfloor \log p \leq -\sum_{\substack{p \geq 2, \, \ell \geq 2 \\ p^\ell \leq Q_0}} \left\lfloor \frac{j}{p^\ell} \right\rfloor \log p = j\,\sigma_2(Q_0) - \sum_{\substack{p \geq 2, \, \ell \geq 2 \\ p^\ell \leq Q_0}} \left\lfloor \frac{j}{p^\ell} \right\rfloor \log p - j\,\sigma_2(Q_0), \qquad \sigma_2(Q_0) := \sum_{\substack{p \geq 2, \, \ell \geq 2 \\ p^\ell \leq Q_0}} \frac{\log p}{p^\ell}.
$$

Thus each column $(i, j) \in C$ has effective derivative slope $\text{slope}_j := \log(R/\rho_n) - \sigma_2(Q_0)$ and 1D remainder excess $H(j) := j\,\sigma_2(Q_0) - \sum_{p \geq 2, \ell \geq 2, p^\ell \leq Q_0}\lfloor j/p^\ell\rfloor\log p$ ($0 \leq j < J$). $$
\text{const}_j := \frac{2}{M_{\text{tri}}}\left(n\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor M_{\text{tri}}/n\rfloor} H_{(m)} + (M_{\text{tri}} \bmod n)\, H_{(\lfloor M_{\text{tri}}/n\rfloor + 1)}\right).

To bound the linear derivative orders $j\,\text{slope}_j$, falling factorials $R f(r_u, j/R)$, and radial factors $(r+s-j)_+\log(\rho_n/\rho_i)$ **jointly** across all column selections $C \subset \{1, \ldots, n\} \times \{0, \ldots, J-1\}$ of size $M_{\text{tri}} = \theta n J$: let $c_i := \#\{j : (i, j) \in C\} \in \{0, \ldots, J\}$ and let $0 \leq j_{i,1} < \cdots < j_{i,c_i} \leq J-1$ be the chosen orders at $P_i$, so $j_{i,c_i+1-m} \leq J - m$ for $1 \leq m \leq c_i$. Evaluating at the uniform envelope $j_{\text{unif}}(u_k)$ on $M_{\text{steps}}$ equal $u$-bins $[u_m, u_{m+1}]$ ($u_m := m / M_{\text{steps}}$) defines the uniform falling-factorial saving

$$
\text{save}_{\text{deriv}} := 3\sum_{m=0}^{N_{\text{deriv}}-1} w_{\text{step},m}\big(j_{\text{low},m}\text{slope}_j - g_{\max,m}\big)_+,
$$

where on finite intervals ($N_{\text{deriv}} = 3,200$) we partition $\{1, \dots, M_{\text{tri}}\}$ into exact integer index blocks $(K_m, K_{m+1}]$ ($K_m := \lfloor M_{\text{tri}}(1 - (1 - m/N_{\text{deriv}})^2)\rfloor$, $w_{\text{step},m} := \frac{K_{m+1}-K_m}{M_{\text{tri}}}$, $G_{\text{lo},m} := \frac{K_m+1}{M_{\text{tri}}}$, $G_{\text{hi},m} := \frac{K_{m+1}}{M_{\text{tri}}}$, $u_{\text{hi},m} := 1 - \sqrt{1 - G_{\text{hi},m}}$, $j_{\text{low},m} := \max(0, 1 - \theta(1 - G_{\text{lo},m}))\frac{J}{R}$, $r_{u,m} := \min(1, (1 + \frac{1}{S})u_{\text{hi},m} + \frac{1}{R})$) with zero discretization loss, while on the infinite tail ($N_{\text{deriv}} = 3,200$, $u_m := m / N_{\text{deriv}}$) we use the exact difference of squares $w_{\text{step},m} := \max\big(0, (1 - u_m)^2 - (1 - u_{m+1})^2 - \frac{1}{M_{\text{tri}}}\big)$ and $j_{\text{low},m} := \max(0, (1 - \theta_{\text{ub}}(1 - u_m)^2)\varepsilon_J^{\text{lo}})$ (and on hybrid intervals $M_{\text{steps}} = 800$), with $j^*_m := \max\big(0, \min(j_{\text{low},m}, r_{u,m} - e^{-\text{slope}_j})\big)$ and $g_{\max,m} := \max(0, j^*_m\text{slope}_j + f(r_{u,m}, j^*_m))$ for $j^*_m > 0$ ($0$ otherwise).

### 5.2 Staircase Concavity-Ramp Coupling, Exact Pascal Hockey-Stick Identity, and Convex Variational Duality

**Lemma 5.2 (Staircase Concavity-Ramp Variational Duality and Pascal Hockey-Stick Identity).** *Choose $n$ as an exact integer multiple of $M_{\text{steps}}$ ($m_{\text{bin}} := n / M_{\text{steps}} \in \mathbb{Z}$) and let $C \subset \{1, \ldots, n\} \times \{0, \ldots, J-1\}$ have cardinality $|C| = M_{\text{tri}} = \theta n J$ with $\theta\frac{J}{R} < \frac{1}{2}$. For $L_{\text{steps}} := 120$, set $m_{\text{start}} := \max(1, \lfloor 0.02 J\rfloor)$ and $m_{\text{end}} := \max(m_{\text{start}} + 1, \min(\lfloor M_{\text{tri}}/n\rfloor - 1, \lfloor 0.495 J\rfloor))$ on finite intervals (or $m_{\text{start}}^0 := \max(1, \lfloor 0.02 J_{\text{lo}}\rfloor)$ and $m_{\text{end}}^0 := \lfloor 0.46 J_{\text{lo}}\rfloor$ on the infinite tail), define the candidate integer cuts $m_r := m_{\text{start}} + \lfloor \frac{(m_{\text{end}} - m_{\text{start}})(r-1)}{L_{\text{steps}}-1}\rfloor$ ($1 \leq r \leq L_{\text{steps}}$, skipping any duplicate $m_r$, or $m_r(D) := \lceil \tau_{\text{base},r} J(D)\rceil$ with $\tau_{\text{base},r} := m_r^0 / J_{\text{lo}}$ on the infinite tail), and stop at the last index $L \leq L_{\text{steps}}$ where the admissibility conditions $G_r := 1 - \frac{n m_r}{M_{\text{tri}}} > 0$, $\text{arg}_r := 1 - \sqrt{1 - G_r} - (1 - \tau_r)\frac{J}{R} > 0$ ($\tau_r := m_r / J$), and $\nu_r := \text{slope}_j + \log(\text{arg}_r) > 0$ all hold strictly. Setting $\nu_{L+1} := 0$ and retaining all steps $1 \leq r \leq L$ with positive increment $\Delta\nu_r := (\nu_r - \nu_{r+1})_+ > 0$, one has:*

1. *Because $\theta\frac{J}{R} < \frac{1}{2}$, the function $h(G) := 1 - \sqrt{1 - G} - (1 - \theta(1 - G))\frac{J}{R}$ is strictly increasing on $[0, 1]$ ($h'(G) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{1-G}} - \theta\frac{J}{R} \geq \frac{1}{2} - \theta\frac{J}{R} > 0$). Thus for every $k \in \{1, \ldots, M_{\text{tri}}\}$, concavity of $x \mapsto g_{\max}(r_u(u_k), x)$ on $[j_{\text{top},(k)}, j_{\text{unif}}(u_k)]$ implies $\frac{\partial g_{\max}}{\partial x}(r_u(u_k), x) \geq \sum_{r=1}^L \Delta\nu_r \mathbf{1}_{x \geq J - m_r}$ on $[j_{\text{top},(k)}, j_{\text{unif}}(u_k)]$, and hence*
   $$
   g_{\max}\big(r_u(u_k), j_{\text{top},(k)}\big) \leq g_{\max}\big(r_u(u_k), j_{\text{unif}}(u_k)\big) - \sum_{r=1}^L \Delta\nu_r\Big[\big(j_{\text{unif}}(u_k) - (J - m_r)\big)_+ - \big(j_{\text{top},(k)} - (J - m_r)\big)_+\Big].
   $$
   *Summing over $k = 1, \ldots, M_{\text{tri}}$ using $\sum_{k=1}^{M_{\text{tri}}} j_{\text{unif}}(u_k) = M_{\text{tri}} J(1 - \frac{1}{2}\theta) + \frac{1}{2}\theta J$ and the exact multiset identity $\sum_{k=1}^{M_{\text{tri}}}\big[\big(j_{\text{unif}}(u_k) - (J - m_r)\big)_+ - \big(j_{\text{top},(k)} - (J - m_r)\big)_+\big] = \frac{(n+1)m_r}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^n (m_r - c_i)_+(m_r - c_i - 1) \geq \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^n (m_r - c_i)_+^2$, while on the radial term bounding $\log(\rho_n/\rho_i)$ below by the clipped radial weight $\tilde{\ell}_i := \min\big(\log(\rho_n/\rho_i), L_{b_0}\big) \geq L_{\max(b(i), b_0)}$ ($b_0 := \lfloor 0.60 M_{\text{steps}}\rfloor$) and applying $(r_k + s_k - j)_+ \geq (r_k + s_k - J)_+ + (J - j) - (J - r_k - s_k)_+$ (with $J - j_{i,c_i+1-m} \geq m$) and the **exact integer Pascal hockey-stick identity***
   $$
   \sum_{(r,s)\in\Lambda_{\text{tri}}} (J - r - s)_+ \leq \sum_{s=0}^{S-1}\sum_{r \geq 0} (J - r - s)_+ = \sum_{s=0}^{S-1} \binom{(J - s)_+ + 1}{2} = \binom{J + 2}{3} - \binom{(J - S)_+ + 2}{3}

   *(with exact equality whenever $J \leq K + S - 1$) recover $+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^n c_i^2 \tilde{\ell}_i - L_{b_0}\Delta_0\frac{W_{\text{main}}}{2}$ with **zero Riemann-sum discretization error**, where for all $J \geq S$:*
   $$

By Lemma 4.2(2), $p \mapsto w_r(p)\log p$ is strictly decreasing on $[3, R]$, so any norm interval $p \in [p_{\text{lo}}, p_{\text{hi}}] \subset [3, \infty)$ satisfies the exact endpoint enclosure $w_r(p_{\text{hi}})\log p_{\text{hi}} \leq w_r(p)\log p \leq w_r(p_{\text{lo}})\log p_{\text{lo}}$. Since $\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z}[\omega]$ is a UFD with $\mathcal{O}_K^\times = \{\pm 1\}$ and every split prime $p = \pi\bar{\pi}$ has four generators $\{\pm\pi, \pm\bar{\pi}\}$ whose coordinates $(u = 2x+y, y)$ lie in the four distinct sign quadrants $(\pm, \pm)$, and since $M(D) = \frac{D+1}{4}$ is odd for $D \equiv 3 \pmod{8}$ so $N_D(x, y) := x^2 + xy + M(D)y^2 \equiv x^2 + xy + y^2 \equiv 1 \pmod{2}$ is automatically odd for every pair $(x, y) \not\equiv (0, 0) \pmod{2}$ (and in particular whenever $\gcd(x, y) = 1$), every split prime $p \in (X(D), P_{\max}]$ occurs as $p = N_D(x, y) \in [p_{\text{lo}}(x, y), p_{\text{hi}}(x, y)]$ for a **unique** primitive pair $(x, y) \in \mathcal{N}_D(P_{\max}) := \{(x, y) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 : y \geq 1, u = 2x + y \geq 0, \gcd(x, y) = 1, N_D(x, y) \leq P_{\max}\}$ with $(x, y) \neq (-1, 2)$ (while $(-1, 2)$ on the imaginary axis $2x + y = 0$ gives the unique ramified prime $N_D(-1, 2) = D$). ## 7. Scope, Limitations, and Reproducibility

### 7.1 Scope and Open Directions

Under the specified parameter schedules and inequalities, our results establish both the certified pure inert-window thresholds ($B_{\text{RS}} = 4,894,699$ and $B_{\text{prime}} = 3,968,539$ for $P_{\max} \leq \frac{D-3}{4}$) and the three-digit hybrid split-prime + UFD composite threshold ($B_{\text{hybrid}} = 875, \log B_{\text{hybrid}} \approx 6.7742$) on the weighted triangular biconjugate jet space $\Lambda_{\text{tri}} \subset \mathcal{O}_K[z, z']$. Pushing $B_{\text{hybrid}}$ below $875$ toward $163$ by extending the UFD composite subtraction to three-prime products $m = p_1 p_2 p_3 \leq P_{\max}$, or combining $\Lambda_{\text{tri}}$ with the general class-number $h(-D)$ norm-counting framework of [13, 14], is a natural next direction.
