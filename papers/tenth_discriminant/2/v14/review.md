# Accept

This revision provides corrections and supporting refinements to the existing contribution. Relative to the accepted snapshot, the changes include the class-number-$20$ sieve threshold, exhaustive integer allocation of split-prime weights, a tighter ramified-divisor bound, and the explanation of ambiguous-class exclusion. They retain the existing discriminant thresholds and census rather than introducing a separate substantive result. The reference to the subsequent $D\le331$ result [28] appropriately distinguishes that paper's contribution.

The distinction between the two capacity conditions in Section 6.5(2) and Appendix A.2 is sound. A nonzero split-prime exponent vector of norm below $B$ cannot represent an ambiguous class, because its square would be a nontrivial primitive principal ideal of norm below $M$. Thus the full short-norm set has capacity $h_{\mathrm{eff}}=h-2^{t-1}+1$. However, an outside ideal can occupy a nonprincipal ambiguous class, so the saturation collision test requires $c_f=h$, not merely $c_f=h_{\mathrm{eff}}$. I reproduced both displayed counting examples, obtaining $(c_f,m_{\mathrm{in}},m_{\mathrm{out}})=(3,2,4)$ and $(23,2048,4096)$. At $D=39$, form composition also confirms that the square of the norm-two ideal represents the nonprincipal ambiguous form $(3,3,4)$.

The revised sieve correctly uses the sixth split prime for the cube-root exclusion at $h\le20$: the stated injection gives $4r\le h$, so five primes alone do not yield a contradiction. Running the inspected finite-sieve and form-composition routines over all four progressions through $64,000,000$ reproduced all $1,817$ fields with $h\le20$, including all 31 reported group counts and maximum discriminants. Inspection of the revised allocation supports its use as an upper bound: it enumerates feasible tier counts, respects the number of available distinct primes, and uses monotone weight majorants. The ramified-divisor maximization likewise relaxes the weight and product constraints in the conservative direction. These checks do not constitute a rerun of the numerical infinite-tail certificates or the complete $h\le30$ computation.

The previous review's candidate-count and discrete-certification wording issues are corrected in the manuscript. Appendix A makes the revised certificate logic substantially easier to inspect, with the essential short-norm argument retained in Section 6.5. The additions remain focused on supporting Theorem 1.3 and do not materially broaden the paper's scope.

Two minor specification details should be synchronized with the implementation. Appendix A.2 writes $M=(D_{\mathrm{tail}}+1)/4$ for $t=1$, whereas the code uses integer division. At the stated tail endpoint these are $16,000,000.25$ and $16,000,000$, respectively, and the displayed identity $\lfloor\sqrt{M-1}\rfloor+1=\lceil\sqrt M\rceil$ fails for the former. State the conservative integer cutoff used by the verifier explicitly. Appendix A.3 should also describe the actual bin schedule and special-prime refinements: the supplied census calls use `B_theta=8`, while the appendix lists subdivisions in $\{4,12\}$. These are documentation discrepancies, not failures of the implemented conservative cutoff or of the demonstrated finite census.
