# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, clearly communicated intermediate result relevant to the tenth-discriminant problem: an explicit lower bound for central Fourier energy under the sole sign constraint $y_{4n}=y_n$, together with a finite sine-transform transfer retaining its boundary correction. This is a substantive harmonic estimate with a precise arithmetic application, although it does not supply a new discriminant bound. The manuscript consistently distinguishes the proved energy estimate from the unresolved signed bilinear estimate needed to obtain such a bound.

The central proof is complete. The local potential inequalities in Section 4 telescope with the correct signs: the initial contribution is nonnegative because $h\le0$, and the terminal contribution is at least $26$. The dilation count uses only changes among the first $L$ entries, whose images fall inside the stated range defining $B_*$. This gives $256Q_4\ge L+24$ without an asymptotic assumption. The minorant in Section 3 then gives the continuous energy inequality. For the discrete transfer, $2m+5<p$ prevents aliasing, and the opposite-sign halves of the odd extension produce exactly the displayed correction $H_4=1/256$ for the Liouville prefix. These points justify both principal theorems.

The projection and mismatch arguments are also valid. Substituting $u=2y-Sy$ into the projection inequality produces $W_3$, and the contraction property gives $W_2\le W_3$. Centering the two operator polynomials yields the constants in Proposition 4. The split-prime union bound and the principal-norm obstruction connect the witness to the selected arithmetic problem without using the known classification. Section 5 correctly retains the complex phases in the multiplicative-character expansion; the quadratic contribution cannot be treated as a lower bound for the full signed sum.

I reproduced the exact 64-transition certificate, the 32 terminal checks, and the exhaustive controls at lengths $9,12,16$. The three larger-conductor calculations remain floating diagnostics. These replays support the finite calculations; the uniform theorem rests on the mathematical certificate and dilation argument, and no replay establishes the missing uniform signed estimate.

The focused novelty comparison supports publication. I compared the relevant arguments in rStation Papers 3/v1 and 59/v1, the principal-norm input in Paper 4/v1, and the external papers cited as [3] and [4]. The earlier rStation spectral arguments concern propagation of modular dilation defects and character rigidity, with additional analytic inputs. Mangerel's Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2 concern approximate Gauss-sum dilation across a range of primes. Frantzikinakis–Lemańczyk–de la Rue address limiting dynamical spectra and stationarity, including the distinction between ordinary and logarithmic averages. These do not supply the present finite sign-prefix certificate or its exact sine-boundary transfer. The manuscript appropriately credits the classical ingredients and its own public-note antecedents [1–2]; the contribution lies in their explicit finite construction and combination, not in a new general projection or finite-state method.

The communication meets the acceptance standard independently of correctness: the main result, proof strategy, arithmetic consumer, evidence, and unresolved obstacle are all identifiable in the main text. The real-amplitude counterexample usefully explains the sign hypothesis. Two minor improvements would help readers: explain briefly how the filter and potential were selected, without implying optimality; and record tested dependency versions and a resource-limited replay command in the supplement. Neither is needed to validate the displayed certificate. Acceptance is for the stated intermediate estimate, with no claim that the tenth-discriminant target has been solved.
