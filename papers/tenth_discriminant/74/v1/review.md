# Accept

This is a sound, substantive follow-up on the limitations of quadratic determinant arguments. It does not improve the numerical tenth-discriminant bound, but it advances the task's stated objective of understanding which arithmetic information these arguments can exploit. The strongest contribution is Lemma 6′: for evaluation-only designs, the primary Frobenius-layer divisibility is already polynomial content and therefore does not distinguish inert from split or ramified specialization. The interval-domination extensions and the explicit class-dependent examples complement this result. The manuscript clearly separates its theorems from its finite observations and remaining conjecture. The qualifications below concern attribution, scope and presentation; they do not undermine these main results.

The argument in Lemma 6′ is convincing. Localizing at $(q)$ makes $2y-1$ invertible, and the modified polynomial $g'$ evaluates coefficientwise into $q\mathbf Z[y]$. The inequalities $q>K$ and $q>1$ ensure that every lower term of the proposed basis has smaller weight, so the triangular change preserves the original support. Contracting $q^N\mathbf Z[y]_{(q)}$ back to $\mathbf Z[y]$ before specializing is essential and is correctly included. The resulting divisibility of the resultant by $q^{2N}$ applies even to ramified specializations. The explanation of why derivative columns invalidate this particular content argument identifies a meaningful mathematical distinction, rather than merely an empirical difference between test sets.

The other local arguments also check. Lemma 3 is the usual quadratic norm/resultant identity expressed in the discriminant variable. Corollary 4 correctly uses the valuation of a norm from an unramified quadratic extension. In Lemma 5, reduction of the terms of least $(q,g_c)$-order gives a nonzero polynomial in the lift parameter over $\mathbf F_{q^2}$; the hypothesis $r_c<q$ guarantees a non-root among the $q$ possible residue parameters. Remark 6 uses the imported triangular layer basis correctly and establishes its stated lower bound, without proving equality of the exact class minima. Proposition 7 follows from the arithmetic-progression ordering and the auxiliary-prime argument. Combining it with the nonnegative Gram polynomial and Chebyshev's extremal inequality gives the condition in Corollary 8, including its factor $(D-3)/16$. The restriction to a logarithmic row range is appropriately presented as a limitation of this sufficient condition, not an impossibility theorem for larger ranges.

I compared Theorem 2 with the relevant portions of Papers 18/v1, 22/v5, 23/v1, 33/v1, 38/v1 and 48/v1. The profile and cluster arguments preserve the inputs used by the cited bounds when the normalization and other parameters are frozen. For Papers 38 and 48, the additional step is necessary: their two-sided zones cannot simply be treated as upper profiles. The decreasing capacities, common charge projections and overlapping envelopes permit the stated transfer to an earlier zone. The nonnegative Cauchy–Binet expansion then supplies the required monotonicity of each selected local Gram determinant. This verifies the extension at the level of the published arguments and certificates; I did not rerun those papers' large numerical certification jobs. The exclusion of Paper 22 §8.9 is justified, and the manuscript correctly avoids claiming that its interval-domination hypothesis is false.

The novelty is specific and should remain so. Paper 70/v3 [8] already gives the discriminant-polynomial framework, the fixed-divisor/Chebyshev mechanism, and explicitly states the expression $\kappa_q+kd+v_q(d!)$ in its §8.3 discussion of a single residue class. Thus Proposition 7 should be described as a proved specialization and extension of that discussion, rather than an independently new general cap. The external literature likewise places this cap and finite-value computation of fixed divisors within established theory: [14, Theorems 1.1 and 1.3] records the consecutive-value and arithmetic-progression results, while [15, §3] treats the relation between local ideal powers and fixed divisibility at small depth. These are relevant antecedents to §§3–4, not prior statements of the submitted determinant-content lemma. The indexed local-lattice Papers 69, 71, 72 and 73 concern universal indices over the unramified quadratic integers; their results do not establish the claimed coefficientwise content statement across discriminant specializations or the exact class-dependent witnesses here. Taken together, Lemma 6′, the extension of interval domination, and the counterexamples to unrestricted class independence constitute enough new mathematical content for publication. The detailed attribution of the Papers 38/48 argument to M. Cairn is appropriate.

The central computational evidence is inspectable and receives independent support. I used a separate implementation with Python integer arithmetic and fraction-free elimination of the integer regular-representation matrix, without importing the submitted determinant code. It reproduces the six-node witness's valuation sequence. For the printed eighteen-node example, taking a full consecutive-value window justified by a degree bound gives

$$
\varphi_{11}(4)=4,\qquad
\varphi_{11}(1)=\varphi_{11}(6)=\varphi_{11}(7)=\varphi_{11}(8)=2.
$$

This independently confirms class dependence at a prime within the row range. I also recovered the determinant polynomial of the reported worst-ratio $(K,S,J)=(2,4,4)$ design by exact finite differences. Its sampled margin is $15.0044971415\ldots$ and its gain is $9.3079207003\ldots$, giving ratio $0.6203420622\ldots$. An exact comparison of integer powers verifies that this ratio is below $0.621$. Using the maximum over integer points as a lower bound for the continuous supremum is in the correct direction for this conclusion. These are targeted reproductions, not a rerun of every random ensemble or an independent verification that this design maximizes the ratio over all 28 cases. The remaining reported counts are supported by the inspected algorithms and retained outputs. The $d+1$ evaluation rule makes their class minima exact rather than minima from an unjustified bounded search.

Clarity is sufficient for acceptance independently of correctness. The introduction states the question and scope, the main text contains the decisive proofs, and the numerical observations are organized around class dependence and its possible usefulness. The abstract is informative, although its opening would be more accessible if it first explained the competition between local divisibility and determinant size before referring to Paper 70. Section 5 is dense with controls and counts; some repeated diagnostics could move to the supplement while retaining the explicit counterexample, the gain table and the distinction between saturation and excess. This is an organizational improvement, not a missing scientific argument.

Please make the following focused clarifications:

1. **Qualify the fixed-design conclusion in §4 and §6.2.** The CRT argument proves that a fixed design has arbitrarily large prime discriminants at which all residue-class credits attain their worst-class values simultaneously. It therefore cannot obtain a strictly positive class advantage uniformly over all such discriminants. It does not show that a fixed design cannot benefit on favorable classes. The sentence saying class credits can help “only” a design chosen according to $D$ modulo $Q_k$ is too broad unless explicitly restricted to this uniform objective. The paper's own examples illustrate the distinction.

2. **Keep the layer theorem separate from the class-independence conjecture throughout the supplement.** In particular, the label “Remark 6 forbids these” in `c3big_posthoc.py` and its output is inaccurate for mixed exact class minima. Remark 6 forces the common lower threshold; different minima above that threshold would not contradict it. The main text makes this distinction correctly. The 479-pair observation is useful exploratory evidence, but 475 saturated pairs and only four excess pairs give limited information about the conjecture above saturation, as §6.1 acknowledges. The independent-per-class random model should retain its heuristic status.

3. **Make the experimental normalization self-contained.** Before the tests, collect the relations $R=KS$, $|\Lambda|=KS(S+1)/2=nJ$, the meaning of $J$, and the precise integer column prices used by the supplied code. Also identify the tabulated $R/\sqrt D$ values as nominal targets under the stated rounding rule. This would reduce unnecessary dependence on Paper 70 for reconstructing an experiment.

4. **Complete the disclosure and sharpen the novelty wording.** Explicitly disclose the AI tools used for this submission; the section entitled “Credit and disclosure” currently describes contribution ownership and checks without naming those tools. Acknowledge the already printed single-class cap in [8] and distinguish the standard resultant and fixed-divisor facts from their new application here. These changes would bring the presentation into closer alignment with the task's attribution requirements.
