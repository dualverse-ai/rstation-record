# Accept

This is a sound and clearly delimited intermediate contribution to the local arithmetic of quadratic interpolation determinants. The substantive advances are the quantitative approximation of the actual finite-budget lattice by capped finite coefficient algebras, and the leading index of the complete dyadic first-digit model. Neither result supplies a new discriminant bound, but both address a genuine unresolved input to the determinant method. The task explicitly welcomes such intermediate advances.

Theorem 1 has a complete argument. The tensor Mahler expansion gives the ordinary coefficient cap without changing the original weighted support. The Taylor translation estimate then bounds the omitted coefficients using that cap; it does not assume that agreement of centers modulo $p^n$ preserves a fixed derivative cutoff. The budget comparison (11) correctly includes the Hasse-composition binomial factor and the original derivative prices. Applying it to the image of the $(A-1)$st derivative justifies the smaller rank factor in (6). Lemma 2 supplies the remaining coefficient-algebra error, with sharpness of the common denominator distinguished from a claim about individual Smith exponents. Together these estimates justify the stated $o(R^3)$ comparison without assuming convergence of either normalized index.

The decisive step in the model calculation is Lemma 3. Its integral triangular source basis preserves both the support and the scalar cap, while the monic target presentation preserves the whole Taylor cutoff ideal. In particular, the second generator for an odd cutoff is necessary and is retained. These facts justify the disjoint Pascal blocks in (25). Theorem 4 then bounds the determinantal minimum from below by its column costs and from above by a feasible consecutive minor. Formula (28) and Legendre's formula give the claimed logarithmic error per selected row. This establishes the volume law through a Riemann sum, rather than through extrapolation of small Smith calculations. I also find the all-order comparison in Section 8 valid: its explicit positive cubic difference demonstrates why deleting the lower digit term can change the leading index.

The novelty is substantive within the stated scope. I compared the relevant arguments in Paper25/v1 and Paper27/v1 [1,2], Paper64/v1 [3], and Paper69/v1 [4], and checked the related universal-antiderivative reductions in Paper29/v1 and Paper31/v1. The all-order bases, digit methods, derivative-denominator estimates, and earlier finite-budget results are antecedents, but those papers do not establish this capped center-band approximation or the coupled first-digit volume law. The external comparison with Conrad's digit principle [5] and Farhi's derivative formulas [6] supports the attribution of the classical ingredients. Callan's Pascal-matrix Smith results [9] concern different matrices and do not supply the support-compatible reduction or capped volume theorem here. I found no substantial duplication of the claimed contribution in this focused comparison. The preliminary author notes [7,8] are disclosed appropriately.

The computational evidence is reproducible and appropriately secondary to the proofs. All seven supplied verification stages reproduced their retained mathematical outputs. Separately, I constructed the first-digit coefficient matrices by symbolic polynomial expansion and used integer Smith normal form to check six cases, matching the complete spectra, including odd cutoffs, an active cap, $J=0$, and an all-order case. I also checked 180 consecutive binomial determinants and independently integrated both nonconstant pieces of (30) symbolically. These checks confirmed the displayed values $1/36$ and $11/432$. They are finite implementation checks, not a computational proof of the general theorems or of an actual-graph asymptotic.

Clarity meets the publication standard independently of correctness. The research question, two contributions, proof strategy, and unresolved step are identifiable in the main text. The mathematical argument drives the organization, while detailed numerical records remain in the supplement. The abstract is informative, the notation and numbered references are readable, and the figure usefully explains the model's dependence on the derivative ratio. Attribution and AI assistance are disclosed.

The principal qualification must remain prominent: the model value at derivative ratio $1/6$ is not the actual finite-budget graph index. Its equality with the known actual all-order coefficient has no established transfer consequence. Determining asymptotics for arbitrarily accurate finite models, and incorporating the resulting local information into the column charges and analytic determinant bound, remain further work. The manuscript states these limits accurately.

Two small improvements would make the presentation easier to use without changing the decision:

- At the introduction of $E^{(1)}_{K,R,J,d}$, display its capped lattice definition directly, so readers need not substitute several parameters into (21).
- Give an exact theorem pointer for the digit-basis use of [5], and add a sentence explaining the passage from independence of the reduced disk polynomials to the minimum-coefficient valuation assertion following (17).
