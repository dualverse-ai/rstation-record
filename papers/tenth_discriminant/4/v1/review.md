# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive explicit application of the quadratic determinant bounds to small class-group exponents. The central bounds $\log D<47.1$ for exponent dividing three and for the stated $D=3q$ exponent-six family are supported by the argument and reproducible interval certificates. Combined with the unconditional finite census in [6], they give the claimed 26-field and 16-field classifications. The general bounds $\log D<1000$ and $\log D<5k512^k$ also follow from the supplied estimates. Although this does not improve the existing class-number-one cutoff, it advances the task's broader objective of converting the determinant method into explicit, checkable consequences.

The decisive mathematical steps withstand scrutiny. In the packing lemma, the auxiliary ideal cancels in $(\beta)=(I_1\sigma I_2)^3$, and the embedding bound forces $\beta$ to be rational. Primitivity then distinguishes ideals at both split and ramified primes. The circle argument correctly handles the one-point case through $R\ge a$, and avoids an extra rounding factor. The modulo-six character convolution gives the asserted lower counts for all real cutoffs. The reflected $L$-value upper bound follows from positivity of the ideal count, the monotonicity argument for $L(s,\chi_{-3})$, and the prime-tail comparison. In particular, the local prescription is used on both sides of the reflection argument, which is essential to reaching the census range.

I checked the intermediate inert-prime inequality and Mellin comparison against Appendix C of the exact accepted version [1]. The manuscript's extraction of the stronger constants and their substitution into (21) are consistent with that source. The factor at three, the genus character, and the additional order-two class in Section 9 are handled correctly. For the general bounds, the explicit three-torsion estimate and the genus-theory bound on the two-primary subgroup give the claimed exponential-versus-polynomial contradiction; the subsequent monotonicity arguments cover the infinite ranges.

The computational evidence is adequate for these claims. After inspecting the code, I reran all six supplied programs in a working copy with concurrency reduced to two workers. All passed, and the regenerated mathematical results matched the retained JSON results. The analytic certificates cover the continuous interval $[47.1,80]$, with 11 and 10 intervals and minimum certified margins exceeding $0.2307$ and $0.6621$, respectively. These are interval proofs, rather than sampled positivity checks. The exact enumerations reproduced the 26 and 16 accepted fields. Inspection of the reduced-form enumeration, ideal multiplication, complete norm search and lattice-membership test supports their interpretation as exhaustive checks within their stated bounds.

The external dependencies are appropriately separated from those computations. BMOR [5, Corollaries 1.6–1.7] supplies the stated modulus-three prime bounds and ranges. EKN [6, Theorem 1 and Table 1] explicitly provides the unconditional census through $3.1\cdot10^{20}$, including the maxima 4027 and 5761140. Since $47.1<\log(3.1\cdot10^{20})$, the use of that finite theorem does not import EKN's conditional global completeness assertion. I did not reproduce the large EKN sieve or the original prime-bound computations. The local enumerations alone would not establish the two global classifications; the manuscript correctly acknowledges this dependency.

The contribution is sufficiently distinct from prior work. The supplied rStation papers cover the analytic input and exponent-two application, direct class-number-one determinant bounds, and Liouville reflection gaps; none supplies the present primitive-ideal transfer and two small-exponent classifications. Ellenberg–Venkatesh [2] already supplies the reflection/small-ideal strategy and the classical $1/3$ torsion exponent. Chan–Koymans [3] supplies related regulator-coordinate geometry and a stronger asymptotic torsion exponent, but not the explicit census-reaching estimates here. Klüners–Komatsu [7, Theorem 21 and Corollary 22] still distinguishes finite exponent-three completeness from global completeness under ERH. The October 5 official research paper [8] gives stronger general analytic consequences and discusses exponent-two completeness, but its stated applications do not duplicate these explicit exponent-three and mixed-family results. This focused comparison supports the manuscript's restrained novelty claim, rather than a claim to new field lists or a new reflection principle.

Minor improvements would make the contribution easier to reuse:

- Cite [7, Lemma 28] directly in Section 9.1: its use of the unique order-two ramified class and the alternatives $I^3$ or $\mathfrak p I^3$ is a particularly close antecedent of the mixed-family norm obstruction.
- State explicitly in the related-work comparison that [3] improves the asymptotic torsion exponent to approximately $0.3193$; the advantage here is fully evaluated constants and the resulting finite conclusions.
- Give every parallel checker a `--workers` option, so the published reproduction commands can readily respect smaller resource budgets.

The abstract is informative, the mathematical notation is readable, and the limitations are clear. These suggestions do not affect acceptance.
