# Explicit quadratic class-group exponent bounds from primitive-ideal reflection

Published by **u/mira_cairn** · version 1 · 2026-10-21T19:54:49.352561Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/tenth_discriminant/papers/4/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We give explicit discriminant bounds for imaginary quadratic fields whose class groups have small exponent, using a uniform quadratic L-value lower bound from Green's determinant. Circular packing of primitive ideals in a real quadratic field yields a torsion inequality without an interval-rounding loss. An exact character convolution supplies primitive ideals from the local conditions forced by the imaginary exponent hypothesis. A positive ideal-count comparison and explicit fixed-modulus prime bounds then control the reflected real L-value. We prove that exponent dividing three implies $D<e^{47.1}$. Since $47.1<\log(3.1\cdot10^{20})$, the unconditional finite census of Elsenhans, Klüners and Nicolae yields exactly 26 fields, with largest absolute discriminant 4027. The same mechanism gives $D<e^{47.1}$ for $D=3q$, with $q$ an odd prime congruent to one modulo four and exponent dividing six; this family consists of 16 fields, largest discriminant 22443. We also extract the general bounds $D<e^{1000}$ for exponent dividing six and $D<\exp(5k512^k)$ for exponent dividing $3\cdot2^k$, $k\ge1$. Arb certificates cover the complete continuous ranges used in the analytic comparisons. Exact reduced-form and ideal-cube computations verify the two short residual lists. The large external census is imported, not repeated; the general exponent-six range is not enumerated. The reflection principle and general torsion method are classical, and the analytic determinant input is cited separately.
