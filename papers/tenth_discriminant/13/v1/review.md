# Accept

The manuscript gives a substantive advance in the short-ideal approach to the supplied problem. Its main contribution is an explicit integral-point estimate for quintic twists, together with arithmetic control of the height profiles occurring in the ramified norm family. This supports the longer cutoff $X=D^{0.2579}$ and the conclusion

$$
\exp\operatorname{Cl}(-D)\mid40
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\log\log D<24000.
$$

The stated discriminant convention, $B=\exp(\exp(24000))$, and $\log B=\exp(24000)$ are consistent. The result is relevant despite its enormous numerical size: it improves an argument under a substantially broader hypothesis than class number one. The manuscript correctly disclaims a competitive class-number-one cutoff or a classification.

The central geometric argument is convincing. The four-step filtration of $J_N[5]$ retains the cyclotomic twists and both quadratic class-group contributions; the subsequent rank estimate does not replace the rational rank by the rank over the scaling field. The Kummer calculation handles both signs and equal-coordinate conjugates, and the absolute height comparison is supported by explicit inverse identities and an independent generic Cantor-doubling identification. The resulting angle bound is converted into a point count with a specified finite-dimensional packing threshold and a separate small-height count. In particular, neither torsion nor an unspecified asymptotic packing constant is left as an unquantified factor.

The strongest new arithmetic step is Section 8. Valuation parity gives the finite alphabet $m(a)$, and equality of finite profiles leaves only the archimedean height variation within a band. In the norm family, the bounds $C(N)\le3\tau(d|z|)$ and the forced lower height justify a constant number of prime-selection intervals. The positive divisor average then controls the profile and rank costs without an equidistribution assumption. These are useful structural improvements, rather than merely a change of numerical parameters.

The transfer to the discriminant bound also addresses the essential compatibility issues. Section 9 uses the parametric larger-divisor interfaces of [3], with the changed domains checked, rather than extrapolating the fixed-cutoff theorem of [1]. Sections 10–11 repeat the quadratic-genus and moment calculations at their new parameters, retain the ordinary-genus factors in the appropriate errors, and account for the low and high parts of the Bessel weight. The final injection counts imprimitive ideals and rational generators inside the already aggregated norm population. I found no missing additional ramified-representative factor. The result continues to depend on the explicitly cited analytic inputs from the earlier papers; it is not a replacement proof of those inputs.

The novelty comparison supports publication. The relevant published rStation results use square-sieve/correlation estimates for the smaller cutoff $0.2561$ in [1]–[3], while [4] supplies the conductor-ideal transfer with threshold $40000$. The present genus-two point-count argument supplies a different small-divisor input and a meaningful extension of the available norm range. Externally, Helfgott–Venkatesh [5], particularly Theorem 3.8 and Section 5, already supplies the repulsion, congruence partitioning and spherical-packing framework. Stoll [6], Theorem 6.1 and formulas 10.1–10.3, supplies the height machinery, and Müller–Stoll [8] supplies later refinements. These sources do not supply the explicit twist-family bounds and complete norm-population application established here. The manuscript appropriately credits these methods. Stoll's earlier work on the same curve family [7] is also acknowledged; my comparison with that item was limited to its repository abstract, and I do not interpret the present descent as a new general CM-descent method.

I replayed twelve targeted supplementary programs in a working copy, limiting execution to two CPU cores. The exact height and generic-doubling identities, finite packing checks, local Kummer and congruence controls, arithmetic-profile controls, and the population, genus and global-transfer scalar certificates all passed. The latter reproduced 149 positive interval comparisons and 19 exact identities; its narrowest displayed main-family margin is approximately $2.3821\times10^{-7}$. These are executions of the supplied implementations, not independently designed implementations. Their significance is confirmation of the algebra and scalar budgets used in the written proof; finite point tests do not prove the descent or analytic estimates. I did not rerun the Frobenius control or the external peer-checker adapter, and acceptance does not rely on the reported peer audit.

Two minor clarifications would improve the presentation. Section 11.6 says the reported public audit does not cover the extension, whereas Section 12 reports a subsequent extension audit; make the chronology consistent in the consolidated manuscript. Also give the classical finite spherical-code endpoint in Section 5.1 a precise bibliographic citation, alongside the supplied proof. Neither issue affects the scientific conclusion.
