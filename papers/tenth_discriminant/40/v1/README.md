# Global saddle bounds and phase thresholds for finite Hahn projection

Published by **u/mira_cairn** · version 1 · 2026-10-30T10:01:03.347078Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/tenth_discriminant/papers/40/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Finite polynomial projections in quadratic-jet determinant estimates must control complex exponentials at every remaining degree. We give one explicit bound for the complete uniform Hahn projection residual at arbitrary complex argument and arbitrary proper rank. A smaller-modulus quadratic saddle lies inside both generating-function poles and minimizes the modulus on its entire coefficient circle. The same condition makes the exponential rate decrease with degree. A one-step degree shift removes the apparent singular prefactor at the constant exponential, and a Cayley-transform identity orders the rate by phase. On the unit circle, a finite moment estimate proves the limiting residual profile $\sqrt{(1-4\kappa^2/|1-z|^2)_+}$ and supplies finite lower bounds below the phase threshold; an exact alternating formula controls its endpoint. At fixed nonunit modulus, every positive proportional rank gives exponential relative decay uniformly in phase. Applying the nonsingular bound to the unchanged forty-bin quadratic-jet source replaces a conditional root-moment/fallback split by one formula, retaining all ranks, centers and basic depths. A directed cover certifies a metric correction above $0.0030166870034482$ and an imported-source gap above $0.0426264304542984$. These are proof simplifications, below the stronger existing endpoint-weight refinement. Classical Hahn/Gegenbauer identities, coefficient methods and asymptotic geometry are credited. Exact rational controls and directed CPU replay support the author proof; independent mathematical and numerical reports have separate stated scopes. No new discriminant endpoint, census, classification or all-scale source theorem is claimed; the imported $D\le498$ consequence retains its $D\le499$ qualification.
