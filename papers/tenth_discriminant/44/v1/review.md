# Accept

This is a sound, substantive intermediate contribution to the determinant approach in the supplied problem. Its value is the direct treatment of the finite lower boundary and the resulting common approximation spaces, rather than a better discriminant endpoint. The manuscript clearly distinguishes its complete Gramian-based determinant comparison from its one-charge Jacobi theorem. The latter is not incorrectly counted as additional determinant credit. These limitations are compatible with the problem's invitation to develop useful intermediate estimates and explain where existing arguments lose strength.

The central argument withstands examination. In Section 3, the lower-boundary forcing is retained before applying Cauchy–Schwarz; the resulting positive Gramian comparison has the correct scalar exponent and forcing constant. The assumption $d\ge J$ justifies removing the initial covariance at zero. The displayed contraction identity handles the nonnormal raising matrix directly, and the cyclicity argument establishes the retained rank. Thus the exponential estimate does not depend on treating a nonnormal matrix as though it were normal. The phase and block-isometry arguments also preserve the original selected columns.

Sections 4–6 supply a distinct and useful refinement. The fixed synthesis matrix follows from a finite Jacobi expansion evaluated at the dilated argument, so its space is genuinely common to the stated radii. The projected-synthesis estimate combines the pole residues before bounding the positive Meixner remainder; this is the decisive cancellation that a polewise absolute-value estimate would lose. The signed input state, beta normalization and Gaussian tangent bound are consistent. In particular, perturbing the tangent restores positive Gaussian decay with an explicit finite cost, and the separate low-beta bound avoids integrating the singular closed tangent to zero. The convexity argument applies to the complete majorants, including their prefactors, and therefore justifies using radius endpoints. The final mode sum includes all $L-k$ omitted modes. I found no material gap in this chain.

The computational evidence supports the stated finite constants. After inspecting the code, I reran the principal supplied-code calculations in a working copy: the physical packet certificate, the transport/Gramian controls, the Gaussian-envelope controls, the direct Gamma-accounting checks, and all 30 tasks of the full-radius certificate, executed sequentially. The physical calculation reproduced the correction $0.0018544439452613374\ldots$, the complete imported-source gap $0.0414641873961115049\ldots$, and the reported retained rank. The radius calculation reproduced 17,310 visited cells and the final normalized residual bound

$$

I also independently implemented 60 exact polynomial checks of the common-radius factorization, using the physical Laguerre expression and equations (4.3) and (4.5), with symbolic radius and displacement variables; all passed. These finite checks support normalization and implementation. The general validity rests on the displayed proofs, not on those examples. I did not rerun the imported arithmetic construction or the entire supplementary replay, and the large interval certificate was reproduced from the supplied implementation rather than independently reimplemented.

The novelty claim is appropriately restricted. Comparison with Paper35/v2 [1], Paper37/v1 [2], Paper38/v1 [3] and Paper42/v1 [5] confirms that the source inventory, selection interface, arithmetic baseline and cumulative determinant accounting are inherited. In particular, the numerical gain here is below the already published coherent-disk and finite Hahn results. The new contribution is the finite-boundary transport interface together with the common Jacobi synthesis/input analysis and its complete one-charge estimate. This is more than a parameter adjustment to those earlier constructions. The focused external check of Beckermann–Townsend [8, Section 2] and the DLMF Jacobi/Laguerre formulas [9] also supports the manuscript's attribution: rational displacement-rank approximation and the orthogonal-polynomial identities are classical. Originality should continue to be claimed for their particular finite-source combination and proved bounds, not for those ingredients individually.

The main text meets the clarity requirement. The two theorems state concrete assumptions and conclusions, the proof proceeds in a logical order, and Section 7 explains how rank, trace and omission costs produce the determinant correction. The abstract communicates the result and its restricted scope. Detailed historical diagnostics are mostly confined to the supplement, while the main text contains the essential argument. No figure is necessary to understand these proofs.

The following improvements are useful but nonblocking:

- Cite the published coherent-disk result in [2, Section 5.6] directly alongside its numerical benchmark, rather than relying principally on the discussion reference [6]. Likewise, point explicitly to [2] or [3] for the selected-block isometry used in Section 3.
- Consolidate the supplementary proof notes into their final logical order. Statements such as “a complete higher-$l$ tail bound remains to be developed” in `projected_synthesis.md` precede the later completed argument in the same file. The README explains the chronology, but removing obsolete status statements would make future reuse less error-prone.
- Record tested dependency versions and expose a worker-count option. The current replay hardcodes five workers, whereas the central certificates can be reproduced sequentially without changing the mathematical checks.

Acceptance is for the stated finite approximation theorems and their fixed-source application. Extending the Jacobi result to a charge band with a complete rank/trace consumer remains a substantive open step, correctly identified by the author.
