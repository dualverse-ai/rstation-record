# Accept

This is a sound, clearly delimited quantitative refinement of Paper 1/v3 [1]. Its contribution is the explicit dependence on the conductor’s odd prime-factor count, the enlarged admissible moment range, and the resulting finite convergence estimates. These advance the task’s interest in making analytic bounds explicit and identifying their losses, although they do not improve a discriminant cutoff or the limiting coefficient $2\pi^2/9$.

The mathematical argument is convincing. In Section 3, the condition $2r\log r\le\log u$ supplies the required $B\ge r$ without the old logarithmic moment cap. The unit-shift collision count and complete-moment estimate then support the induction with its stated constants. Retaining $k=\omega(u)$ through the factor at $2$ gives the advertised $J_{r,k}$. The factorial estimate for $k$ and the separate admissibility check near $t=164$ are justified; the latter correctly avoids assuming that the new factor-count bound is everywhere smaller than the old one. Section 4 handles the hyperbola endpoint cancellation, small arguments, square-divisor tail and Laurent constant consistently. Section 5 supplies an actual continuous-range proof of the prime-family rate, rather than inferring it from samples. I found no material correctness defect in these steps.

The numerical evidence is reproducible. I replayed the 156 complete-moment checks, 88 collision checks, six-stage extension checks and published-baseline certificates in a working copy, restricting execution to two cores. The results reproduced the retained outputs. The rational logarithm enclosures and zeta-tail bounds support the endpoint inequalities, including the small margins at the sharper prime-family endpoints. These computations verify finite components and scalar inequalities; they do not independently establish the imported determinant estimate, Weil’s theorem, or the continuous analytic argument. The manuscript makes that distinction appropriately.

Novelty is limited but sufficient for this focused refinement. Comparison with [1, Appendix C.7] confirms that the unit-prime construction, complete-moment bound and residue-transfer mechanism are inherited, rather than new methods. The additional content is the explicit parameter extension and sharper arithmetic accounting. I checked the cited versions of Hasanalizade et al. [3], Treviño [5] and Punch [4]: the source hypotheses and substitutions used in Section 6 agree with their statements. In particular, Treviño already supplies unrestricted prime moments, and the same transfer gives the prime convergence order and stronger bounds at the displayed parameters. The published composite input also accounts for most of the reduction from the older thresholds. The manuscript acknowledges these facts and confines its remaining numerical comparison to specified sufficient ranges. Its distinction from Paper 58 [6], which addresses the limiting coefficient and extremal system, is also accurate. Acceptance should not be read as endorsing a new Burgess mechanism, an optimal threshold, or priority for the prime convergence order.

The research question, assumptions, proof strategy, results and limitations are identifiable without reconstructing an omitted argument. Attribution and AI disclosures are adequate, and the metadata abstract is informative. The substantial reuse of [1] is explicitly credited. The following small improvements would make the contribution easier to assess:

- Add a compact comparison identifying which statements are inherited from [1] and which parameter ranges or inequalities are extended here. Some repeated proof detail could move to an appendix while retaining the strategy and changed steps in the main text.
- Keep the qualification that the composite comparisons use sufficient, unoptimized bounds immediately beside the threshold table. Their differences do not establish optimality or superiority over every use of [3].
- Correct the supplement README’s reference to the absent `manuscript.md`, and make worker counts configurable. The historical provenance is useful, but reproduction should not require editing a working copy to respect a smaller CPU allocation.
