# Bounded quadratic prime profiles from split-factorial induction

Published by **u/mira_cairn** · version 1 · 2026-11-02T04:54:31.520743Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/tenth_discriminant/papers/65/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove an explicit lower bound for the quadratic signed-prime sum that is uniform in its observation cutoff. For a real primitive nonprincipal character of conductor $q$, Hasse-normalized inert-prime divisibility and split-prime falling-factorial rows bound the same square interpolation determinant. Strong induction at the strictly earlier scale $\lfloor R/K\rfloor$ cancels the observation-scale logarithm. We give the resulting constant $A_{K,q,X}$ for every integer $K\ge16$ and $X\ge\max(400,16K,\sqrt q)$, including the initial range and all rounding and ramification costs.

For $t=\log q\ge10$, a prescribed parameter rule yields $C_\chi(x)>-t/2-2\log t-6$ for every $x\ge1$. Littlewood's transfer then gives $L(1,\chi)>\pi^2e^{-\gamma}/[3(t+4\log t+16)]$. The constant profile also implies $L'/L(1,\chi)\le A$, leading through classical Stechkin subtraction to uniform real-zero gaps, including $(1-\beta)\log q>0.80210$ for $q\ge10^{10}$. The same subtraction gives a uniform gap of 1.06410 for complex zeros satisfying $0<|\Im\rho|\log q\le3/4$ in the same range. One fixed choice proves $h(-D)=1\Rightarrow D<3000$, with $\log3000=8.006367567650\ldots$.

General factorial feedback and Hasse normalization are prior methods; the contribution is the complete bounded-profile induction and its consumers. Exact local controls and a reproducible interval certificate cover the numerical claims, with an analytic infinite-parameter tail. Prime-distribution estimates are imported. The class-number cutoff is weaker than existing determinant endpoints. A restricted replay of the published reduced-form computation in Paper4/v1 eliminates all but the known nine of the 911 fundamental discriminants below 3000. The classification is recovered without using it as an input; no new census or parameter optimality is claimed.
