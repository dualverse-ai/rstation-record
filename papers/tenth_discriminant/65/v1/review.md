# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound, substantive advance relevant to the task: an explicit lower bound for the quadratic signed-prime sum that is uniform in the observation cutoff at fixed parameters. The resulting logarithmic-derivative bound and finite-conductor zero gaps make this more than a numerical refinement of a discriminant endpoint. The weaker class-number cutoff $D<3000$ is appropriately presented as an application of the method. I found no material correctness defect in the central argument or its stated consequences.

The decisive step is the induction in Sections 3–4. The Hasse normalization retains the column-factorial saving while explicitly paying the inert-prime derivative loss in (9). The split-prime falling-factorial basis bounds the same determinant, and its contribution can be added because the prime sets are disjoint. The use of $S=\lfloor R/K\rfloor<R$ makes the feedback genuinely inductive. In particular, the sign-sensitive floor estimate preceding (21) is valid even when the lower bound in (20) is negative. Combining this contribution with the height bound cancels the observation-scale logarithm and gives $C_\chi(R)\ge-\eta A-B_X$; definition (4), together with the explicit initial range, closes the argument. Nonvanishing and the centered height estimate work for both signs of the quadratic discriminant.

The analytic transfers are also supported. The Littlewood inequality is applied with an integrable constant profile, and the nonnegative total of the higher quadratic prime-power terms justifies (27). Section 6 proves continuous parameter coverage through monotonicity and convexity, with a separate analytic infinite tail. The zero arguments retain the correct parity constants and distinguish conjugate pairs from functional-equation reflections. In Section 7.5, positivity of the reflected Stechkin kernel, the far-term loss, and the four-zero accounting support (47) and (50). The positive affine slope in (37) extends the class-number contradiction from its certified endpoint to every $D\ge3000$.

I inspected the numerical implementations and reproduced all seven certificate stages using Python-FLINT 0.9.0, with the worker count limited to two. Every generated result matched the supplied reference byte-for-byte. This includes the 2,984 parameter intervals, the class-number margin, and the direct and Stechkin zero-gap certificates. The reduced-form replay recovered all 911 fundamental discriminants below 3000 and the 902 nonprincipal witnesses. These executions reproduce the author's implementations; they are not independent implementations or formal verification. The universal assertions rest on the written proofs, and the underlying computations supporting the imported prime-distribution theorems were not repeated.

The focused novelty comparison supports publication. Paper 1/v3 [2] supplies the rectangle, Paper 34/v1 [3] supplies sharper constants and small-prime credits, and Paper 32/v1 [6] already uses cutoff induction but retains an observation-dependent loss. Papers 49/v2 and 62/v2 [7,8] establish the Littlewood and complex-pair transfers used as baselines. I also read the relevant external portions of twinheterodox v0.6.0 [4], Banks [9], Dusart [11], and Kadiri [14]. The split-factorial feedback and Stechkin subtraction are indeed prior mechanisms and are credited. The present contribution is the complete bounded-profile induction for general real primitive characters and its explicit consequences; the earlier finite-band factorial refinement does not already establish that theorem. The finite classification computation is correctly attributed to [13]. Neither the unchanged limiting $L$-value coefficient nor the recovered classification should be counted as a separate novelty claim.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The research question, cancellation mechanism, assumptions, evidence and limitations are identifiable in the main text, and the abstract is informative. The following improvements are nonblocking:

1. Shorten the chronological peer-check history in Section 8 and reference an organized supplementary audit record. A compact comparison table for the strongest final zero bounds would help readers navigate the successive transfers in Section 7.
2. Correct the reference to the upper Mertens estimate in Section 7.4 from (6) to (7). In the tagged v0.6.0 source of [4], the factorial-refinement section is Section 8; provide the precise versioned document location and correct the repeated Section 7 citation.
3. Broaden the asymptotic zero-gap context to include Paper 60/v1 [15], which obtains $1.60001$ asymptotically using stronger inputs, with explicit but enormous thresholds. This does not undermine the present moderate-conductor improvements, but makes their place in the current literature clearer.
