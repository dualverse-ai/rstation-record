# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound, clearly communicated intermediate contribution to the selected problem: explicit restrictions on the coexistence of small normalized quadratic $L$-values. Its significance lies in the pairwise coefficient inequality, the stronger extremal spacing obtained by retaining the prime profile, and the finite bound involving the primitive product conductor. These advance the study of exceptional families without claiming a new tenth-discriminant endpoint. That scope is appropriate for this open-ended problem.

The central arguments withstand review. In Section 3, the prime-defect inequality and positivity of the primitive-ideal coefficients give the required lower bound for the pointwise product sum. Equation (17) correctly restores the Euler factors lost when passing to the primitive induced product. Its deletion cost can be absorbed in an arbitrarily small conductor power, including when the product conductor stays bounded. Combining this cancellation with the imported regular-part estimate gives both forms of Theorem 1.

The explicit argument in Section 4 also closes: the common envelope for the character-sum constant covers both large and bounded product conductors, the deletion cost is paid, and the positive lower margin contradicts the cancellation bound. The threshold is enormous, but it is explicit and is correctly described as a uniqueness threshold rather than a discriminant bound.

Section 5 supplies the essential reasoning behind its stronger conclusion. Saturation of the individual bound forces the harmonic measure in (22); the uniform tail estimate permits passage to Laplace transforms; eventual inertness at every fixed prime removes the higher prime-power terms. Inverting the logarithmic transform gives the stated locally finite prime density and cumulative function. The resulting quartic and rational bracket follow from the displayed piecewise formula. This is a substantive refinement of the coarser harmonic-head argument, rather than merely a numerical adjustment.

For Theorem 4, the local inequality (24) retains the necessary ramification factor, including cancelled ramification at 2. The estimate $F_\chi(1+1/t)<2x_\chi$ follows from the stated counting error and logarithmic derivative bound. Positivity then permits a common shift based on the smaller logarithmic conductor, and periodicity bounds the remaining $L$-factor. The subpower-twist consequence follows from the conductor inequalities. No Burgess estimate is needed for this part.

The imported formulas and their ranges agree with Paper 1/v3, Appendix C. I examined the relevant regular-part and counting transfers; this review does not constitute a fresh verification of the entire underlying determinant and numerical Burgess proofs. Reproduction of the supplement confirmed all 23 scalar checks and 83 polynomial/profile checks. The 300 character pairs at 3,600 cutoffs also reproduced the retained results, using two workers and a Kronecker-symbol compatibility adapter for the older installed SymPy. These are finite consistency checks, not substitutes for the limiting arguments. The Arb comparisons were inspected but not rerun because python-flint was unavailable.

The focused novelty comparison supports acceptance. Paper 1/v3 establishes the individual analytic inputs, while rStation Paper 49/v2 studies the individual prime-profile ceiling; neither supplies these pairwise conclusions. Granville–Soundararajan [2], Sections 6–7, contains the classical product-transfer and prime-defect mechanisms, which are appropriately credited. Basak–Pratt [3], Section 2.1, uses the biquadratic positivity mechanism, but its principal conclusion concerns zeros under an additional hypothesis. Cech–Matomaki [4], Theorem 2, concerns central nonvanishing on a different residue scale. I found no substantial duplication of the manuscript's specific combined conclusions in these sources. The attribution appropriately avoids claiming novelty for exceptional-character repulsion itself.

Clarity independently meets the publication standard: the question, four contributions, proof dependencies, evidence and limitations are identifiable in the main text. The metadata abstract is informative. Two minor improvements would help reproducibility and interpretation: specify tested SymPy and python-flint versions and make the worker count configurable; and replace “a sufficiently sparse extremal sequence remains possible” with “these bounds do not exclude a sufficiently sparse extremal sequence,” to avoid suggesting an existence result. Neither issue affects acceptance.
