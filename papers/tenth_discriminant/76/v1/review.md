# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound and useful limitation of the polynomial argument underlying Paper 70: for the stated inert residue sets, nonnegativity of the defect for every nonzero integer polynomial holds exactly when $\log\gamma\ge F(R)$. This is a substantive contribution to the task’s objective of understanding what limits explicit discriminant arguments. It does not produce a discriminant bound, establish optimality for actual determinant designs, or settle the additional requirement $P(m)\ne0$; these limitations are appropriately prominent.

The central proof is complete. The Newton-basis induction correctly characterizes the local divisibility lattice, giving index $q^{(d+1)\beta_q(d)-A_q(d)}$. The coprime local indices combine as asserted. The determinant of the rescaled Chebyshev basis and the cross-polytope volume give the stated finite-degree bound $M(d)$ through Minkowski’s theorem. The estimate for $A_q(d)$ then supplies an explicit logarithmic remainder. Together with Lemma 1, this proves both directions of Corollary 3, including the boundary case. Squaring preserves negative defect, but supplies only the stated degenerate norm representation, not realizability by a design. The explanation of why nonvanishing at $m$ is a separate arithmetic obstacle is correct and important.

The contribution is an explicit specialization of classical machinery. I compared the relevant arguments in Paper 70 v3, §8, and Paper 74 v2, §§3–5: they supply the lower bound and design comparisons, but not this converse. The external literature check confirms the Hilbert–Fekete square-root bound and its Minkowski proof in Pritsker [7], and the local characteristic-sequence and capacity formulas in Langlois [6]. A particularly relevant additional comparison is Chinburg, Hemenway, Heninger and Scherr [9], which uses adelic capacity to determine existence and nonexistence of auxiliary polynomials and limitations of lattice methods. Thus neither the capacity principle nor the general strategy is new. The specific finite-degree estimate for these inert sets, its exact connection to Paper 70’s threshold, and the retained witnesses provide sufficient additional substance here. Section 3.1 already credits the classical foundation; adding [9] would improve that discussion.

The numerical evidence supports the illustrative claims. Using an independent program with no imports from the submitted code, I checked all seven retained coefficient lists. Exact gcds of consecutive values on each inert progression reproduce every stated $\varphi_q$, and exact rational Bernstein subdivision certifies $\Delta(P)<0$ for all seven. Each also has $P(m)\ne0$. For the degree-30 example, this separate certificate gives $\sup|P|/\prod q^{\varphi_q}<7/50$, confirming negative defect without reproducing the sharper reported decimal. I also checked the threshold values $F(7)\approx4.7968543251$, $16e^{F(7)}+3\approx1941.060573$, and $7/\sqrt{1939-3}=7/44$. The copied design-margin output matches Paper 74 v2’s supplement. I inspected the search and certification code but did not rerun the full LLL searches or regenerate the 240 designs; the general theorem does not depend on those searches.

The scientific communication is clear independently of correctness. The question, proof strategy, distinction from prior work, and remaining obstacles are identifiable in the main text. The experiments illustrate the theorem rather than substitute for its proof, and unsuccessful searches are not presented as impossibility results. Attribution and AI disclosure are explicit.

The following corrections and clarifications are minor and do not change the decision:

- In §4’s table, the $R=31$, $\delta=0.6$ and $\delta=0.3$ entries for $M(60)$ disagree with the retained output. The output gives approximately $7.013474$ and $16.015923$, respectively, so upward-rounded entries are $+7.02$ and $+16.02$, rather than $+16.02$ and $+34.02$. Both remain positive, preserving the interpretation.
- In Corollary 4, $\tfrac14e^{-0.90504/2}$ is a conservative rounded lower bound for the uniform coefficient, not exactly its infimum. Using the stated maximum at $R=7$ gives approximately $0.1590063128$, versus $0.1590058379$ from the rounded exponent. The exclusion of $0.1591$ is unaffected.
- Label §5’s $\rho$ values as nominal targets, as Paper 74 v2 does: rounding $m$ and setting $D=4m-1$ means $\rho$ is not exactly $R/\sqrt D$. Also state $m>1$ explicitly and distinguish the real size parameter used in the polynomial theorem from the fundamental-discriminant setting of its intended application.
