# Accept

The update gives a sound strengthening of the accepted norm-count argument. At the unchanged cutoff $X=D^{2561/10000}$, it improves the uniform saving from $1/8500$ to $1/2600$ and proves the additional aggregate estimate

$$
\sum_{\substack{d\mid\operatorname{rad}(D)\\d\le\sqrt D}}N_d(X)
<XD^{-1/2600},\qquad \log\log D\ge8000.
$$

These are useful advances in the quantitative short-ideal method, even though the manuscript retains its previous discriminant threshold $52000$.

The deletion of the discarded-frequency fallback is justified. For the selected primes, fifth powering permutes the residue field, so the raw zero-frequency character sum vanishes for every $y$. CRT consequently annihilates every full-distinct completion term with $(A,Qw)>1$, independently of the ramified congruence factor. The remaining centered frequencies fit the existing positive covariance enlargement: its summable Fejer envelope already controls all nonzero $A$, so removing the magnitude cutoff introduces no frequency-count loss. This argument concerns the $z$-completion frequency $A$; the separate nonunit Gaussian frequencies $B$ and other errors are correctly retained.

The replacement $m_0=q_1q_2$, $Q_0=q_3q_4$ uses actual coprime detector factors and satisfies the required differencing and completion domains. The three covariance savings, partial frequency coincidences, Gaussian errors, shared-prime patterns and equal-pair diagonal remain within their budgets at both parameter endpoints. Their affine dependence supplies the intervening range. I also checked BMOR [10, Corollary 1.6] directly: its threshold $50q^2$ gives $1250$ for modulus five, and the displayed interval subtraction and deletion of primes dividing $D$ support the claimed restricted-pool supply.

The aggregate argument preserves information genuinely lost in the old uniform majorization. For $d\le D^{1/100}$, retaining $5^{\omega(d)}$ and bounding the number of prime factors using the product constraint gives (RP4). The generating-function inequality has the correct direction, including replacement of $k$ by its upper bound. The entropy term decreases on the stated range, and the other three divisor ranges have sufficient savings to absorb their unrestricted subset sums. The resulting population bound needs no additional representative-count factor. The ideal injection agrees with [2, Section 10.9], including the treatment of the dyadic prime and recovery of imprimitive ideals. Equation (53) correctly incorporates this aggregate upper bound into the retained lower-count comparison.

I reproduced all three new scalar certificates with python-flint 0.9.0, obtaining byte-identical outputs: 473, 18 and 51 positive interval checks, respectively, together with the recorded identities and 420 shared-pattern endpoint comparisons. The smallest aggregate comparison margin at $\log\log D=8000$ is approximately $6.35688\times10^{-6}$. These computations verify the scalar comparisons; they do not reproduce the geometric theorem, the imported analytic estimates or BMOR's underlying computations. Acceptance also rests on the written arguments connecting those inputs.

The revision appropriately credits the pre-existing zero-frequency identity and retains the accepted orbit theorem. Its use of that identity with the full positive measure, and the resulting norm-population estimate, goes beyond [1] and the accepted version. The related weighted subset method in [12] serves a different conductor-family estimate; its stronger discriminant threshold $40000$ is disclosed without being claimed here.

Two minor editorial corrections would improve precision. In Section 1, distinguish the inherited local orbit theorem from the contributions new to this version, and mention the $40000$ comparison there as well as in Section 9. The zero-frequency identity cited as “[1, Section 3]” occurs at the end of Section 2 in the supplied published version of [1]. Neither issue affects acceptance.

Reference numbers follow the manuscript.
