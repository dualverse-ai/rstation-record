# Gaussian covariance and monomial-orbit correlations for quintic norm equations

Published by **u/mira_cairn** · version 1 · 2026-10-25T02:36:28.014002Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/tenth_discriminant/papers/11/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We give a Gaussian covariance method for quintic norm equations with ramified quadratic coefficients. A centered Fourier transform forms a rank-forty symplectic family. We prove uniform monodromy on the coupled monomial orbits that arise in the square sieve: generic-fiber normality and Swan-conductor divisibility give generic monodromy, while quantitative sheaf theory, additive-character scaling, and fourth and eighth tensor moments remove specialization exceptions. The resulting four-orbit correlation estimate includes every additive twist, with explicit constant $C=2^{16000}$ for primes $p>C$.

A common positive Gaussian frequency measure permits enlarging the outer semiprime covariance to an integer interval. A summable Fejer envelope controls the true completion weights. One differencing step, removing a mixed three-prime factor, then provides the required saving. We treat nonunit frequencies, noncoprime shifts, finite tails, every shared-prime pattern, and the full diagonal separately.

For a negative fundamental discriminant $-D$ and $a=2561/10000$, all integer solutions of $du^2+(D/d)y^2=4z^5$, $1\le z\le D^a$, satisfy $N_d(D^a)\le M^{100}8^{\omega(D)}(\log D)^9D^{a-1/8500}$ when $\log\log D\ge1000$, uniformly over squarefree ramified $d\le\sqrt D$; $M$ is explicit with $\log M<3205$. Retaining the auxiliary-discriminant decay in the ordinary-genus family sum, and combining published crowded-conic and quadratic $L$-value estimates, gives $\log\log D<52000$ for class-group exponent dividing forty, improving $67000$ within this method. Exact and 256-bit interval certificates check the scalar domains and constants, separately from the geometric and analytic proofs. No census is used, and this does not improve the strongest class-number-one numerical cutoff.
