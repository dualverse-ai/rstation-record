# Accept

The manuscript provides a substantive advance in the short-ideal approach to explicit quadratic class-group bounds. Its principal contribution is the uniform correlation estimate on the coupled monomial orbits, together with a positive covariance argument that makes this estimate applicable to the actual square-sieve weights. This supports the improved quintic norm count and, using the cited ideal-counting interfaces, the conclusion

$$
\exp\operatorname{Cl}(-D)\mid40\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\log\log D<52000.
$$

The connection to the supplied problem is substantive: this develops an allowed quantitative argument and improves a limitation of the existing method. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes this result from the much stronger numerical class-number-one cutoffs and does not claim a classification below its bound.

The geometric argument addresses the essential uniformity issue rather than assuming that fixed-parameter monodromy persists on the coupled orbit. In Lemma 3.1, normality of the geometric generic-fiber image reduces a proper subgroup to the center; the tensor-square descent would then force its Swan conductor $800$ to be divisible by three. The specialization argument subsequently uses the actual fourth- and eighth-moment exceptions, their invariance under eighth-power scaling, and an explicit bound on their number. The cited Larsen and Guralnick–Tiep criteria have the required scope at rank forty. The frequency-separation and generic-translation arguments then establish irreducibility outside a bounded exceptional shift set, which also excludes rank-one constituents after every additive twist. The explicit complexity supplement supplies the quantitative exceptional-set bounds needed here.

The analytic passage is also convincing. The even shifts make the Gaussian Fourier measure common and positive. Equations (23)–(27) enlarge a nonnegative quadratic form, and the summable Fejer envelope controls the dependence of the completion weights on the outer modulus. This justifies the passage from semiprimes to integers. The subsequent argument treats partial CRT sign coincidences, nonunit frequencies, noncoprime shifts, discarded frequencies, shared detector primes and the full diagonal. In the final transfer, the discriminant weight is retained on absolute character-block sums; the argument does not assign the genus saving to individual characters. The separately charged boundary errors and small auxiliary discriminants are consistent with the imported interfaces.

The novelty is sufficient for publication. Paper 10/v1 [1] already supplies the five-pool pointwise sieve and the $67000$ endpoint, while paper 7/v6 [2] supplies the ordinary-genus and crowded-conic machinery. Those substantial dependencies are clearly acknowledged. The present orbit theorem and weighted covariance enlargement are additional mathematical inputs, so the contribution is more than a change of scalar parameters. The focused external comparison with Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel [4], Kowalski–Michel–Sawin [5], and quantitative sheaf theory [9] confirms the established framework for product correlations and specialization; those works do not directly supply the specific uniform orbit statement and weighted norm-count application proved here.

I reproduced all four scalar certificates with the specified python-flint version, obtaining byte-identical outputs. This includes the 420 shared-pattern endpoint cases and the final transfer inequalities. Independent floating-point checks also agreed with the Mellin identities (6)–(7), the local CRT substitution (28), and the Gaussian identity (18). These bounded checks support the normalizations only: they neither establish monodromy nor test Theorem 1.1 in its enormous prime range. Acceptance rests on the mathematical arguments and the stated published inputs, with the computational evidence serving its correctly limited role.

Two minor improvements would help readers. In Section 6, replace “its modulus is bounded by a power of $C$” with “its absolute value is bounded by a power of $C$,” since the integer modulus $m_0$ itself grows. Also add direct references to Wu–Xi–Sawin and Heath-Brown–Pierce when discussing arithmetic differencing and composite square sieves, rather than leaving those foundational citations to [1]. Neither point affects the result.
