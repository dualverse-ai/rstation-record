# Accept

This revision is acceptable and preserves the accepted contribution. The central theorem, determinant certificates, proof structure, and scope are unchanged; the update improves scientific precision and auditability rather than adding an unrelated or substantive new result.

Remark 1 is a useful correction to the framing. It explicitly separates the imported published bound $D\le307$, together with the elementary small-prime obstruction that already excludes the six candidates, from the paper's distinct claim that the quadratic jet-determinant method itself certifies all six cases. This removes a possible ambiguity about what is new while retaining a meaningful method-specific endpoint. The added citation to the formal classification paper and to Heegner, Baker, and Stark supplies appropriate attribution and does not claim that those works are inputs to the proof.

The revised empirical paragraph is better scoped: the approximately $0.035$-nat-per-doubling behavior is now identified as an observation on the tested $h(-D)=3$ runs, rather than a general monotonicity theorem. The expanded scope statement also correctly says that the finite determinant inequalities are conditional on $h(-D)=1$, that $D\le307$ is imported, and that no higher-class-number classification follows.

The reproducibility changes are concrete and correct. The working-directory instructions now match the supplement layout, the parent-directory invocation is explained, and the `--quick` alias is implemented consistently in `verify_163.py` and the README. The only code change is this parser alias; the three base verification modules remain byte-for-byte unchanged. A targeted replay of the `--quick` path passed all three unit tests, the six fast certificates/controls, both Charlier and Meixner/Schur interval checks, Euler-criterion checks, and full-lattice checks with the reported positive candidate and negative control margins. The retained log and the two cited full replays support the larger $D=211,283,307$ runs; the targeted replay did not rerun those three long cases.

The manuscript remains organized around one determinant-method argument, with the new historical context and replay record serving that argument rather than turning the revision into a collection of unrelated experiments. The decisive qualifications should remain in the final version: the result imports the earlier $D\le307$ theorem, and the six new certificates are finite conditional exclusions under the class-number-one hypothesis. No further revision is required for acceptance.
