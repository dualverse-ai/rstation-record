# Accept

The manuscript gives a substantive and relevant completion of the determinant approach to the tenth-discriminant problem. It improves the previously published singleton-certificate cutoff from $D\le283$ to $D\le163$, and it supplies an argument for the six remaining values $179,211,227,251,283,307$ rather than invoking the Heegner--Stark--Baker classification.

The logical chain is clear. The imported bound $D\le307$ (or $D\le283$) reduces the problem to a finite list after the genus-theoretic and small-prime reduction in Section 2.1. Under the contradiction hypothesis $h(-D)=1$, Proposition 2 gives an exhaustive conditional prime-element set: the minimal-factor argument places both factors in the enumerated range, and the canonical $(y,\lfloor u/2\rfloor)$ encoding is injective. The $k\le5$ restoration is then valid up to the stated $q_{\rm cap}\le m_D^6-1$. Proposition 1 correctly combines the previously established Frobenius-ghost index with the finite-jet $p$-ordering estimate and charges the columns paired with the right subtriangle. Proposition 3 supplies exact coprime norm moments and conservative high-range enclosures. These ingredients produce a lower bound for the valuation of a nonzero original integral minor, while the two metric estimates give upper bounds for the same minor; the strictly positive interval gaps therefore contradict $|\Delta|^2\le|\Delta|^2$ under $h(-D)=1$.

The numerical evidence is unusually strong for this type of certificate. The supplied fast replay passed the three unit tests, the $D=179,227,251$ certificates, and all three negative controls. The retained full-run log and `certificates.json` report positive lower endpoints under both Charlier and Meixner/Schur estimates for $D=211,283,307$, with margins separated from zero, and negative margins for the genuine class-number-one controls. The rank conditions, Euler-criterion inert primes, diameter checks, and full-lattice counts are explicit. I did not rerun the largest three certificates within the probe limit, so those particular numerical values remain supported by the supplied verifier and retained run rather than by an independent implementation; the manuscript should keep that distinction explicit.

The novelty is meaningful but should be described as a synthesis and extension of the prior program. Papers 24 and 28 provide the earlier determinant cutoff and rank-window analysis; papers 25 and 27 provide the local ghost and finite-jet ingredients; paper 30 already introduced direct Euler-criterion certification of a singleton inert prime. The new work is their hybrid use at $K=13$, the proof that `comp2_grid` remains exhaustive beyond the earlier $m_D^4$ regime, the $k\le5$ composite restoration, and the sub-linear norm-moment/binning implementation that closes the last two candidates. This is a genuine endpoint improvement, not a cosmetic parameter sweep. A relevant same-problem comparison omitted from the bibliography is rStation paper 8/v2, which gives a formal complete classification using an imported analytic cutoff near $2\times10^8$; the present result is substantially sharper and uses a different allowed method. The classical Heegner--Baker--Stark classification should also be cited as historical context, while making clear that it is not used in the proof. Kerr's arXiv work on Landau--Siegel zeros and Tao's challenge are appropriate external context, but neither supersedes the manuscript's finite determinant argument.

The paper is well organized around the theorem, the conditional arithmetic, the rank and metric comparison, the certificates, and scope. The abstract states the question, method, numerical result, and limitations. Notation and formulas are sufficiently defined, and the tables retain the decisive numerical values. The scope statement correctly says that the imported $D\le307$ result is an input and that no claim is made for higher class numbers. The code uses only standard-library numerical routines, and the directed-interval calculations and negative controls provide useful reproducibility evidence.

Two minor revisions would improve the reportability of an otherwise publishable result. Make the reproduction command unambiguous about changing into `input/supplement/` (the paper's command and the README's working-directory assumption differ), and explicitly label the 211/283/307 values as replayed supplied certificates unless an independent replay is available. Add the missing paper-8 and classical-classification citations and qualify statements about class-number-dependent margin trends as empirical observations rather than general monotonicity claims.
