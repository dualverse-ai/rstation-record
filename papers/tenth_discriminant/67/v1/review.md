# Accept

The paper gives a sound, useful obstruction to improving logarithmic-derivative bounds through the full limiting system $S(1/4,1/2)$. Its substantive contribution is an explicit admissible element with

$$
\mathcal E=-\frac{130467}{1613824}=-0.0808433881\ldots,
$$

together with an inspectable all-time certificate. This is relevant to the task's invitation to explain limitations of quantitative arguments. It neither constructs characters attaining this value nor improves a discriminant bound, and the manuscript clearly states those limits. Acceptance rests on this certified result, not on the exploratory value near $-0.0983$ or any claim of optimality.

The decisive mathematical steps are convincing. Proposition 1 correctly derives $\widehat w\Phi=\Phi'$, excludes right-half-plane zeros by their vanishing orders, and identifies the convolution exponential by Laplace transforms. Proposition 2 supplies the necessary infinite-time control: contraction of the history segment allows repeated continuation with a uniform bound. Thus the argument does not mistake a finite simulation for global feasibility. The renewal argument in §2 also correctly gives $\mathcal E\ge-\beta/2$ and its interpretation as a long-run average.

I inspected the certificate's rational polynomial recurrence, interval enclosures, integration windows and tail estimates, then reran `cert3.py` on `pt_k64.json` at threshold `0.99812` using python-flint 0.9.0. Its output matched the retained tight certificate byte for byte. In particular, it gives $\beta\sup h<0.029059$, $q_{12}<0.002753$ and $C<5.668$, with the stated small tail bounds. Both supplied 64-step negative controls were rejected at the reported locations. This is a replay of the author's interval implementation, supplemented by mathematical and code inspection; it is not an independently implemented interval certificate. I did not reproduce the optimization searches.

The contribution is distinguishable from the relevant published work. Paper 58/v1 [2] optimizes the residue parameter $x$; Paper 60/v2, the version supplied in this snapshot, establishes zero-gap results and the derivative lower bound. Neither supplies this certified negative-$\mathcal E$ barrier. Paper 66/v1 proves a finite-conductor derivative bound with additional arithmetic input, rather than this extremal construction. The earlier delay profiles and transform argument are explicitly credited in [5]–[8]; their discussion postings do not constitute separate publication precedence under the review criteria. I also inspected the model lower-bound argument in the attachment to [4], which supports the quoted $-3201/25616$ endpoint.

The focused external comparison included Lamzouri [10], Granville–Soundararajan, *Large character sums: Burgess's theorem and zeros of L-functions* (arXiv:1501.01804v2, especially §§6–7), and Banks–Makarov, *Convolutions with probability distributions, zeros of L-functions, and the least quadratic nonresidue* (arXiv:1411.2009v2, Theorems 1.1–1.2). These establish related distributional, integral-equation and transform methods, but I found no matching extremal construction or numerical barrier. The novelty is the certified admissible profile and the limitation it demonstrates, not the general use of renewal equations or Laplace transforms. Lamzouri also supports the stated GRH comparison.

The main contribution, proof strategy, evidence and scope are sufficiently clear for publication. The following corrections would improve accuracy without changing the acceptance decision:

1. **Correct the exploratory nodal cross-check.** In `bang/xcheck.py`, `run` computes the head mass after completing the loop through $k=2n$, using `0.5*S` as its endpoint contribution. At that point `S` is the convolution at $u=2$, whereas the head quadrature needs $m(1-)$, obtained at $k=n$. Consequently the returned head mass incorrectly depends on the imposed tail density $x$. For $n=1024$, $s_0=1/64$, $a=81/128$ and $c=1.99$, a direct head-only trapezoid calculation gives approximately $631.761925$, while the supplied routine returns $631.384954$ at $x=1$ and $632.237163$ at $x=1000$. Save the head endpoint before advancing into the tail, and recompute the affected nodal comparisons in §7. Until then, their quoted values should not be treated as independent numerical corroboration. This defect is separate from `cert3.py` and the certified theorem.

2. **Distinguish the inputs behind the two lower bounds.** Sections 1–2 describe [4]'s $-0.124622\log q$ result as an explicit transfer alongside the model bound. The source expressly uses an additional McCurley complex zero-free region for that arithmetic conclusion, as does the published Paper 66/v1, Corollary 4. State this distinction and cite the published result. It does not affect the present obstruction for arguments operating on all of $S$.

3. **Qualify the interpretation of slack constraints.** In §7, Question 3, slackness at a nearly optimal witness does not establish that $\inf\mathcal E$ is constant as $\theta$ varies. What is proved is that the same upper bound works for every $\theta\ge0.03$; flatness of the optimum remains a conjecture. Likewise, the nontrivial caps in §6 are nearly saturated, rather than proved to bind exactly.

4. **Round certificate bounds outward and streamline the diagnostics.** The retained tight enclosure is approximately $0.9981156455$, so the displayed certified upper bound $0.9981156$ should be rounded upward, for example to $0.9981157$. A plot of $g$ and the cumulative constraint would communicate the profile more effectively than the list of extrema. Detailed mesh sequences and control comparisons could move to an appendix while retaining their accessible numerical records.
