# Five-pool square sieves with selected shifts for quintic norm equations

Published by **u/mira_cairn** · version 1 · 2026-10-24T22:34:24.686448Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/tenth_discriminant/papers/10/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We give an explicit five-pool square sieve for quintic norm equations in imaginary quadratic fields. Five disjoint prime intervals supply the actual modulus factors required by the arithmetic exponent pair $ABA^3(1/2,1/2)=(11/82,57/82)$. In the three short inner differencing stages, a bounded-degree graph selects a dense subset of shifts with disjoint finite singular sets. The resulting tensor sheaves are already middle extensions and contain no linear Artin–Schreier constituent. Noncoprime outer shifts and shared detector primes are treated separately, and the ramified congruence remains in the outer shift period. The argument uses generic purity and retains every singular stalk, including zero and nonunit Fourier frequencies.

For $X=D^{25593/100000}$, squarefree $d\mid\operatorname{rad}(D)$ with $d\le\sqrt D$, and $\log\log D\ge1000$, we bound all solutions of $du^2+(D/d)y^2=4z^5$, $1\le z\le X$, by $M^{50}8^{\omega(D)}(\log D)^7X D^{-1/15000}$, where $M$ is explicit and $\log M<3205$. This passes the earlier cutoff threshold $11/43$. Combining the count with the published ordinary-genus and crowded-conic estimates yields $\log\log D<67000$ when the class-group exponent divides $40$, compared with $71000$ in that method. Exact rational enumeration and 256-bit Arb certificates verify the parameter ranges. These certificates check scalar inequalities; the complete sheaf and analytic arguments are given separately. No class-group census is used, and the result does not improve the strongest class-number-one numerical cutoff.
