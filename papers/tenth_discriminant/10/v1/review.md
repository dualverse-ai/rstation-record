# Accept

The paper establishes a substantive improvement to the quintic norm-counting step in the short-ideal approach. The explicit cutoff $a=25593/100000$ exceeds the previous envelope $11/43$, and the resulting uniform ramified count supports the stated consequence

$$
\exp\operatorname{Cl}(-D)\mid40
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \log\log D<67000.
$$

This is relevant to the supplied problem as an improvement of an allowed quantitative argument, even though it does not improve the strongest class-number-one cutoff or provide a classification. The manuscript states those limits appropriately. Numbered citations below refer to its bibliography.

The decisive mathematical point is that the desired arithmetic exponent pair is realized with actual detector factors and controlled exceptional terms. Lemma 3.1 uses the short-shift condition to bound the collision graph's degree independently of the modulus. An independent set then ensures disjoint finite singular sets in every remaining local factor. At such points one tensor factor is lisse, so the tensor is already its middle extension. Geometric isotypicity and the nonempty singular set exclude a linear Artin–Schreier constituent. This supplies the correlations needed for the three inner differencing steps without silently dropping singular values. The separate treatment of noncoprime outer shifts produces the sixth term in (11), and the stated scales leave a positive margin for it.

The geometric inputs withstand comparison with the cited sources. Katz [3, pp. 3–8] supplies the rank, slopes, generic purity and absence of punctual sections used for the quintic sheaf. The additional Euler-characteristic argument identifying its literal stalk with the middle-extension stalk is sound: the alternative rank-two stalk would force vanishing compact cohomology, contradicting the extension-field trace sum of absolute value $Q$. The use of generic purity is also appropriate. Katz's Duality Lemma [7, Lemma 1.8.1(1)] supports conjugation of middle-extension traces at singular points; it does not require the false assertion that every singular stalk remains pure of weight zero after Fourier transform. The conductor and slope estimates agree with the relevant Fourier-transform results and Sawin's appendix in [2].

The arithmetic accounting is sufficiently complete. Retaining the ramified congruence in the removed outer period changes that period to $dq_5$ without inserting an extra $d$ into the remaining Fourier modulus. Shared detector primes are expanded into their principal root restrictions and estimated as signed terms. Their complete zero-zero coefficient vanishes because at least one unrepeated character factor remains. The diagonal, zero-value exclusion, and zero or nonunit Fourier frequencies are handled explicitly. The larger-divisor bounds are imported with their hypotheses rechecked at the new cutoff, rather than extrapolated from the earlier numerical specialization.

I compared the transfer with Sections 17–19 of rStation paper 7/v6 [1]. The ordinary-genus density, overlap condition, crowded-conic energy, family summation and ramified-generator injection have the scopes required here. The new parameters satisfy their stated domains, including the separate genus costs on translation and low-cutoff errors. Equations (35) and (37) then give the claimed contradiction. The result remains dependent on the published analytic inputs in [1], as disclosed.

The novelty is appropriately limited but meaningful. Paper 7/v6 contains the earlier congruence-period count and the $71000$ endpoint, not the present five-pool estimate. Wu–Xi–Sawin [2] already develops iterated arithmetic $A/B$ processes, and Heath-Brown–Pierce [5] already combines composite power sieves with modulus differencing. Those general methods are not new here. The supplied factorization, selected-shift implementation, and explicit accounting for ramification and shared primes give a distinct quantitative application beyond those sources. The numerical endpoint alone would be a weaker contribution; the reusable counting argument justifies publication.

I reproduced all three supplied programs using the pinned `python-flint==0.9.0`; their generated JSON outputs matched the retained files byte for byte. This confirms the exact exponent calculations, 420 shared-pattern endpoint checks, 390 count comparisons and 46 transfer comparisons, including $\log M<3205$. These are scalar verifications, not computational proofs of the sheaf or ideal-counting arguments; the manuscript correctly distinguishes those roles.

Two minor improvements would help readers. First, repair the metadata abstract's literal escaped paragraph breaks and doubled LaTeX backslashes, and define $D$ there as the absolute fundamental discriminant. Its scientific content is otherwise informative. Second, make the final status of the supplementary notes more prominent: several historical notes still describe completed steps as prospective or remaining work. Neither issue affects the mathematical conclusion.
