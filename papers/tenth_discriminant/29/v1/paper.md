Mira Cairn

## 1. The finite derivative question

Quadratic interpolation bounds for a hypothetical tenth class-number-one
discriminant compare an integral determinant's local divisibility with an
archimedean upper bound. Paper27/v1 [1] determines the universal weighted
jet index when every derivative is allowed and constructs stronger lower
bounds for a finite derivative budget. A finite cutoff leaves additional
local divisibility that the full-jet formula does not determine.

We isolate that additional divisibility as one universal fractional lattice.
The lattice is independent of the derivative budget after an explicit
cutoff, when the budget is a power of the residue characteristic. For
general sufficiently large budgets, the discrepancy has an explicit
logarithmic bound. This reduction leads to finite algorithms, a recursive
polynomial construction, and two-sided bounds for the leading quadratic
size of the universal index. It does not determine the exact asymptotic
constant or prove that the normalized index converges.

Let $p$ be a prime, let $O$ be the unramified quadratic discrete valuation
ring over $\mathbb Z_p$, and normalize $v_p(p)=1$. Put $q=p^2$ and
$c_j=\lfloor j/p\rfloor$. For $M,J\ge1$, let $L_{M,J}$ be the $O$-module
generated by the columns

$$
 \left(p^{c_j}\binom mj y^{m-j}\right)_{0\le m<M},
 \qquad y\in pO,\quad 0\le j<J.                         \tag{1}
$$

A term with $j>m$ is zero. Write $\iota(M,J)$ for the valuation of its
maximal-minor ideal. For $N\ge1$, define the fractional lattice

$$
 B_N=\left\langle
 \left(\frac{p^{a-\lceil a/p\rceil}x^{n+a}}
 {\binom{n+a}{a-1}}\right)_{0\le n<N}:
 a\ge1,\ x\in O\right\rangle_O,
 \qquad \beta_N=v_p(\det B_N).                           \tag{2}
$$

Here and below the determinant denotes the determinant ideal of a full
fractional lattice. Its valuation is signed; $\beta_N$ can be negative.
The finite-generation proof in Section 3 makes (2) an ordinary full lattice
after multiplication by a power of $p$.

For integers $m\ge0$ define

$$
 \alpha_q(m)=\sum_{\ell\ge1}\left\lfloor\frac m{q^\ell}\right\rfloor.
$$

Choose any integer $A_N\ge2$ satisfying

$$
 \left\lfloor\frac{(p-1)(A_N-1)}p\right\rfloor
 -\left\lceil\log_p(N+A_N-1)\right\rceil
 \ge\alpha_q(N).                                       \tag{3}
$$

Such an integer always exists, and the inequality is a finite check.

**Theorem 1 (exact universal tail).** If $J=p^k$ with $k\ge1$ and
$J\ge A_N-1$, then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\iota(J+N,J)={}&\sum_{m=0}^{J-1}\left\lfloor\frac mp\right\rfloor
 +N(J/p+k)+\frac{N(N-1)}2-v_p(N!)\\
 &+\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}v_p\binom{J+n}{J}+\beta_N.             \tag{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

If $N\le J$, the binomial-valuation sum is zero. The module $B_N$ is
generated by $1\le a<A_N$ and, for each such $a$, by the first $N+a$
nodes of a standard $q$-adic $p$-ordering. The zero node contributes a
zero column.

There is also a finite comparison for arbitrary $J\ge A_N-1$. Set

$$
\begin{aligned}
 H(N,J)={}&\sum_{m=0}^{J-1}\lfloor m/p\rfloor\\
 &+\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\left[
 \lfloor J/p\rfloor+n+v_p(J)-v_p(n+1)
 +v_p\binom{J+n}{J}\right],\\
 L_J={}&1+\lfloor\log_p\max(1,J-1)\rfloor.
\end{aligned}
$$

Then

$$
 H(N,J)+\beta_N\le\iota(J+N,J)
 \le H(N,J)+\beta_N+NL_J.                               \tag{5}
$$

The next theorem bounds $\beta_N$ without a growing matrix calculation.
Put

$$
 a=p(2p+1),\qquad b=p(p+1),\qquad
 \eta_* =\frac{\sqrt{(a-1)^2+4b}-(a-1)}{2b},
$$

$$
 d_N=\left\lceil\log_{(2p+1)^2}(N+1)\right\rceil,
 \qquad S_N=\sum_{n=1}^N\lceil\log_p(n+1)\rceil.
$$

**Theorem 2 (explicit lower bound).** For every $N\ge1$,

$$
 \beta_N\ge\frac{\eta_*}{2}N^2
 -[1+(2q+3)d_N]S_N-N.                                  \tag{6}
$$

The error in (6) is $O_p(N(\log N)^2)$, with the displayed constants
available for finite use. Section 6 supplies a finite upper bound by
explicit rational and polynomial columns. At $p=2$ it implies

$$
 \frac{\sqrt{105}-9}{24}
 \le\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\beta_N}{N^2}
 \le\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\beta_N}{N^2}
 \le\frac{307}{4320}.                                 \tag{7}
$$

Neither equality of the limits nor optimality of either constant is
asserted. The finite polynomial construction also transfers to the same
triangular supports and column prices as [1]. Section 7 gives exact floor
sums and arithmetic improvements at existing discriminant contracts.
Section 8 identifies the first finite saturation layer at every prime and
gives an explicit coupled row explaining a strict transfer example.
These comparisons do not rerun rank, metric, or composite-counting
certificates and do not exclude a new discriminant.

The strongest endpoint used for context is the imported $D\le331$ of
paper24/v2 [2], with $\log331=5.802118375377063\ldots$. No new census or
class-number-one classification is claimed. The separate imported
$D\le498$ consequence discussed in earlier work retains its $D\le499$
theorem qualification.

## 2. Classical ingredients and residue disks

Ordinary $p$-orderings and their normalized Newton bases are classical.
Bhargava [3, Theorems 7, 12, 31] treats respectively higher divided
differences, all Taylor coefficients at a fixed modulus, and their
combination. These statements do not directly determine a finite set of
weighted pure derivatives. We use the ordinary Newton basis, its factorial
valuations, and elementary analytic changes of coordinates. We do not
claim a new general theory of integer-valued polynomials.

For comparison, after adjoining $\pi$ with $\pi^p=p$, the full weighted
one-variable condition is the modulus-$\pi$ condition on the compact
subset $O$ of the ramified extension. For polynomials with coefficients
in $\operatorname{Frac}(O)$, $\pi^j\mathcal D^{(j)}f(x)$ integral is
equivalent to $p^{\lfloor j/p\rfloor}\mathcal D^{(j)}f(x)$ integral.
Restricted orderings in [3, Theorem 12], or their elementary product proof,
give the full one-variable index
$\sum_{m<R}\lfloor m/p^3\rfloor$. This explanatory specialization is
classical; our problem is the finite cutoff and its additional tail.

We first reduce the weighted one-variable problem on $O$ to (1). Let
$H(z)=z^q-z$. Every polynomial of degree below $R$ has a unique expansion

$$
 f(z)=\sum_{t=0}^{q-1}z^t F_t(H(z)),\qquad
 \deg F_t<M_t:=\max\left(0,1+\left\lfloor\frac{R-1-t}{q}\right\rfloor\right).
                                                               \tag{8}
$$

The polynomials $z^tH(z)^m$ form an integral monic triangular basis.

**Lemma 3 (residue-disk decomposition).** Let $c_0=0$ and let $c_j$ be
any nonnegative nondecreasing integers. The values
$p^{c_j}\mathcal D^{(j)}f(z)$ are integral for all $z\in O$, $j<J$, if
and only if $p^{c_j}\mathcal D^{(j)}F_t(y)$ is integral for all $y\in pO$,
$j<J$, and every $t$.

**Proof.** The $q$ roots $a$ of $H$ in $O$ have distinct residues and
$H'(a)$ is a unit. Integral formal inversion gives
$\psi_a(y)\in O[[y]]$ with $\psi_a(0)=a$ and
$H(\psi_a(y))=y$. On $pO$ these series converge to the inverse branches
of $H$. Their Hasse derivatives are integral there. Composition preserves
the weighted condition: an order-$j$ coefficient is an integral combination
of derivatives of orders $k\le j$, and $c_j\ge c_k$.

Thus the condition on $f$ is equivalent to the condition on all
$g_a(y)=f(\psi_a(y))$. The relation

$$
 g_a(y)=\sum_{t=0}^{q-1}\psi_a(y)^tF_t(y)
$$

has a Vandermonde matrix over $O[[y]]$ whose determinant is a unit.
Its inverse also has integral power-series entries and integral Hasse
derivatives on $pO$. Hasse--Leibniz and $c_j\ge c_k$ show that multiplying
by this matrix or its inverse preserves the weighted condition. This
proves both directions. $\square$

Duality and the unit triangular basis (8) show that the universal
one-variable column index on $O$ is exactly
$\sum_{t=0}^{q-1}\iota(M_t,J)$. The inverse-branch argument uses actual
one-variable points; it introduces no independent conjugate coordinate.

## 3. The universal tail and its finite presentation

Return to $c_j=\lfloor j/p\rfloor$ and $M=J+N$. Evaluation at $y=0$,
$j=m<J$, supplies the head pivots $p^{c_m}e_m$. Every other head entry
is divisible by its pivot, since
$m-j+c_j\ge c_m$ for $j\le m$. These pivots therefore clear the first
$J$ rows by integral column operations. Their tail entries are zero,
so the tail lattice is unaffected.

Write $a=J-j$ and $m=J+n$. At $y=px$ the tail entry is

$$
 p^{n+a+\lfloor(J-a)/p\rfloor}
 \binom{J+n}{J-a}x^{n+a}.                               \tag{9}
$$

The exact identity

$$
 \binom{J+n}{J-a}
 =\binom{J+n}{J}\frac J{n+1}
 \frac{\binom{J-1}{a-1}}{\binom{n+a}{a-1}}              \tag{10}
$$

separates a row factor and a column factor. If $J=p^k$, all
$\binom{J-1}{a-1}$ are $p$-adic units. Dividing by the explicit row
factors and rescaling columns by units gives (2) with $1\le a\le J$.
The row valuations are

$$
 J/p+n+k-v_p(n+1)+v_p\binom{J+n}{J}.                   \tag{11}
$$

For $0\le n<J$ the last binomial valuation is zero by Lucas's theorem.

It remains to prove that large $a$ add no generators. The $a=1$ columns
are $(x,x^2,\ldots,x^N)$. Evaluate them at the first $N$ nonzero nodes of
a standard $q$-adic $p$-ordering starting at zero. Adjoining the constant
row and zero node gives the ordinary Newton evaluation matrix, with
diagonal valuations $\alpha_q(0),\ldots,\alpha_q(N)$. Its normalized
evaluation matrix is integral triangular with unit diagonal, and the
monic Newton-to-monomial change is integral unimodular. Removing the
constant pivot therefore shows that the $a=1$ columns contain
$p^{\alpha_q(N)}O^N$.

The carry bound

$$
 v_p\binom{n+a}{a-1}\le\lfloor\log_p(n+a)\rfloor
$$

implies that the $a$th column has every entry in
$p^{\alpha_q(N)}O$ when
$a-\lceil a/p\rceil-\lfloor\log_p(N+a-1)\rfloor\ge\alpha_q(N)$.
Condition (3) implies this for every $a\ge A_N$: the continuous function

$$
 \frac{(p-1)(a-1)}p-\log_p(N+a-1)
$$

is nondecreasing for $N+a-1\ge3$, and (3) bounds it below by
$\alpha_q(N)$ at $A_N$. Condition (3) forces $N+A_N-1\ge3$. Hence only
$1\le a<A_N$ are needed. For each fixed $a$, the vector in (2) has degree at most
$N+a-1$ in $x$. Ordinary normalized Newton interpolation expresses every
value as an $O$-linear combination of the first $N+a$ values. This works
also for a vector polynomial with fractional coefficients, because the
interpolation identity is linear. We have proved finite generation and,
on summing (11), formula (4).

For general $J=pb+r_0$, $0\le r_0<p$, the same row extraction leaves
the column multiplier

$$
 d_a=p^{\lceil a/p\rceil-\lceil(a-r_0)/p\rceil}
 \binom{J-1}{a-1},\qquad 1\le a\le J.                  \tag{12}
$$

Its valuation lies between zero and $L_J$. If $J\ge A_N-1$, the
normalized tail $C_{N,J}$ consequently satisfies

$$
 p^{L_J}B_N\subseteq C_{N,J}\subseteq B_N.
$$

The row valuations sum to $H(N,J)$, which proves (5). The comparison
has an explicit cutoff; it is not a statement with an unspecified
large-budget threshold. $\square$

## 4. Antiderivatives, symmetry, and finite blocks

For a polynomial with zero constant coefficient define

$$
 I_\ell x^n=\frac{x^{n+\ell}}{\binom{n+\ell}{\ell}},
 \qquad \ell\ge0.                                     \tag{13}
$$

This is $\ell!$ times the repeated primitive with zero integration
constants. Pairing $(b_0,\ldots,b_{N-1})$ with (2), and writing
$A(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}b_nx^{n+1}$, identifies the dual of $B_N$ as

$$
 C_N=\{A:A(0)=0,\ \deg A\le N,\quad
 p^{\ell+1-\lceil(\ell+1)/p\rceil}I_\ell A(x)\in O
 \text{ for every }\ell\ge0,\ x\in O\}.                 \tag{14}
$$

There is useful translation symmetry, although this dual is not a ring.
For $t\in O$ put $T_tA(x)=A(x+t)-A(t)$. The exact identity

$$
 I_\ell(T_tA)(x)=I_\ell A(x+t)
 -\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\binom\ell j I_{\ell-j}A(t)x^j        \tag{15}
$$

follows by removing the Taylor polynomial of $I_\ell A$ at $t$ through
order $\ell$. Since the prices in (14) are nondecreasing, (15) proves
that $T_t$ preserves the dual and every uniform valuation level. Moreover
$T_sT_t=T_{s+t}$, so these maps are automorphisms. Unit dilations preserve
the same conditions by
$I_\ell(A(u\,\cdot))(x)=u^{-\ell}(I_\ell A)(ux)$.

If $e_n$ is the largest possible negative leading-coefficient valuation
in degree $n$ in this dual, triangular lattice elimination gives
$\beta_N=\sum_{n=1}^N e_n$. For $n\ge2$, $T_1A-A$ has leading
coefficient $n$ times that of $A$ and degree $n-1$, whence

$$
 e_n\le e_{n-1}+v_p(n).                                \tag{16}
$$

These observations do not prove convergence of $\beta_N/N^2$. At $p=2$,
$x$ and $x^2$ satisfy (14), but $x^3$ fails at $\ell=1,x=1$, where the
weighted value is $1/2$. For the first two polynomials the requisite
inequalities are
$\lfloor(\ell+1)/2\rfloor\ge v_2(\ell+1)$ and
$\lfloor(\ell+1)/2\rfloor\ge v_2\binom{\ell+2}{2}$.
The cases $\ell\le2$ are immediate; for $\ell\ge3$ the carry bound is
at most $\lfloor\log_2(\ell+2)\rfloor\le\lfloor(\ell+1)/2\rfloor$.
Thus even adjoining integral constants does not make the dual a ring,
and a multiplicative subadditivity argument is unavailable without
additional input.

For exact computation, tame characters split (2) into smaller blocks.
Put $h=q-1$. Replacing $x$ by a Teichmuller unit $\zeta x$ multiplies
row $n$ by $\zeta^n$, up to an invertible column scalar $\zeta^a$.
Since $h$ is a unit, integral Fourier idempotents give the direct sum
of row blocks $n\equiv r\pmod h$. Write $n=r+ht$ and $x=p^vu$ with
$u$ a unit. After a unit column rescaling, a block is generated by

$$
 \left(
 \frac{p^{a-\lceil a/p\rceil+v(n+a)}(1+py)^t}
 {\binom{n+a}{a-1}}
 \right)_t,
 \qquad a\ge1,\ v\ge0,\ y\in O.                        \tag{17}
$$

Here the map $u\mapsto u^{q-1}$ covers $1+pO$: raising to the prime-to-$p$
power $q-1$ is an automorphism of the principal units. If the block has
$T$ rows, its entries are polynomials in $y$ of degree below $T$.
Exactly $T$ ordinary $p$-ordering nodes therefore generate the values
for fixed $(a,v)$. Condition (3) truncates $a$, and any chosen modular
precision truncates shells whose normalized entries all vanish.
Uncapped local Smith pivots determine the index exactly; a capped pivot
gives only a lower bound and is never reported as an equality.

## 5. A recursive polynomial construction

Put $b(\ell)=\ell-\lceil\ell/p\rceil$ and
$\lambda(n)=\lceil\log_p(n+1)\rceil$. Say a monic zero-constant polynomial
$A$ has credit $\gamma$ if

$$
 v_p(p^{b(\ell)}I_\ell A(x))\ge\gamma
 \quad(\ell\ge0,\ x\in O).                             \tag{18}
$$

Say it has Gauss loss at most $g$ if all its coefficients have valuation
at least $-g$. The price in (14) is at least $b(\ell)$, so (18) gives
elements of the required dual after scaling.

Define

$$
 \eta_0=0,\qquad
 \eta_{d+1}=\frac{1+\eta_d}{p(2p+1)+p(p+1)\eta_d},
 \qquad a_d=1+d(2q+3),\quad b_d=3d.                    \tag{19}
$$

**Proposition 4 (uniform fixed-depth construction).** For every $d\ge0$
and $n\ge1$, there is a monic zero-constant polynomial $A_{d,n}$ of
degree $n$, Gauss loss at most $b_d\lambda(n)$, and credit at least
$\eta_dn-a_d\lambda(n)$.

**Base.** Take $A_{0,n}=x^n$. The carry bound for the denominator in
(13) gives at most $\lfloor\log_p(n+\ell)\rfloor$. For $\ell\ge1$,
$b(\ell)\ge(p-1)(\ell-1)/p$. Except when $p=2,n=1$,

$$
 \log_p(n+\ell)\le\log_p(n+1)+\frac{(p-1)(\ell-1)}p,
$$

because $(n+1)\log p\ge p/(p-1)$. The exceptional case follows from
$v_2(\ell+1)\le\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor+1$. The case $\ell=0$ is
immediate. Thus the base has credit at least $-\lambda(n)$ and Gauss
loss zero.

**First transformation.** Suppose $A(x)=\sum_{k\ge1}a_kx^k$ has degree
$n$, credit $\gamma$, and Gauss loss $g$. For an integer $C\ge0$ set

$$
 B(z)=\sum_{k\ge1}a_kp^{n-k}
       \frac{\binom{C+n}{C}}{\binom{C+k}{C}}
       (z^q-z)^{C+k}.                                  \tag{20}
$$

This polynomial is monic of degree $M=q(C+n)$ and vanishes at zero to
order greater than $C$. Its Gauss loss satisfies

$$
 g_B\le g+\lfloor\log_p(C+n)\rfloor.                  \tag{21}
$$

Indeed $p^{n-k}$ and $z^q-z$ are integral, the numerator binomial has
nonnegative valuation, and the denominator satisfies the carry bound.
Before composing with $z^q-z$, the $(C-\ell)$th weighted derivative at
$y=px$ is a multiple of $I_\ell A(x)$, with total factor

$$
 p^{n+\ell+\lfloor(C-\ell)/p\rfloor}
 \binom{C+n}{C}\binom C\ell,
 \qquad 0\le\ell\le C.
$$

The exponent in excess of $n+\lfloor C/p\rfloor$ is at least
$b(\ell)$. Consequently all weighted derivatives through order $C$
have credit

$$
 E=\lfloor C/p\rfloor+n+\gamma+v_p\binom{C+n}{C}.       \tag{22}
$$

Composition preserves this credit: the order-$j$ Hasse derivative of
a composition is an integral combination of derivatives of the outer
polynomial of orders $k\le j$, and $c_j\ge c_k$. This argument does
not require integral coefficients in $B$.

**Second transformation.** Conversely, suppose a monic polynomial $B$
of degree $M\ge q$ vanishes to order greater than $C$, has Gauss loss
$g_B$, and has weighted derivative credit $E$ through $C$. Put

$$
 A'=\frac{\mathcal D^{(C)}B}{\binom MC},\qquad t=M-C.
$$

It is monic and zero-constant, with Gauss loss at most
$g_B+v_p\binom MC$. Its antiderivative credit is at least

$$
 \min\!\left(E-\lfloor C/p\rfloor,
       \left\lfloor\frac{(p-1)C}{p}\right\rfloor-g_B\right)
 -\lambda(M)-v_p\binom MC.                             \tag{23}
$$

For $\ell\le C$ use the identity

$$
 I_\ell\mathcal D^{(C)}B
   =\mathcal D^{(C-\ell)}B/\binom C\ell.
$$

The zero integration constants follow from the order of vanishing.
The weighted derivative hypothesis, $c_{C-\ell}\le c_C$, $b(\ell)\ge0$,
and the carry bound prove the first branch of (23). For $\ell>C$ use

$$
 I_\ell\mathcal D^{(C)}B=\binom\ell C I_{\ell-C}B.
$$

The primitive has coefficient valuations at least
$-g_B-\lfloor\log_p(M+\ell-C)\rfloor$. The same logarithm estimate as
in the base, now with $M\ge q$, proves the second branch of (23).

**Degree realization and induction.** Abbreviate
$D_d=p(2p+1)+p(p+1)\eta_d$. Given a target degree $t$, choose the
largest integer $n\le t/D_d$ congruent to $t$ modulo $q-1$. If $n\ge1$,
set $C=(t-qn)/(q-1)$. Then

$$
 C\ge\frac{p(1+\eta_d)n}{p-1},\quad
 n>t/D_d-(q-1),\quad C+n\le t,\quad M=t+C<2t.           \tag{24}
$$

Apply (20) and (23) to $A_{d,n}$ and put $\lambda=\lambda(t)$. The first
Gauss loss is at most $(b_d+1)\lambda$. The two entries in the minimum
in (23) are bounded below by

$$
 (1+\eta_d)n-a_d\lambda,
 \qquad (1+\eta_d)n-1-(b_d+1)\lambda,
$$

respectively. The final two logarithmic losses total at most
$2\lambda+2$. Since $a_d\ge b_d+1$ and $0\le\eta_d<1$, the credit is
therefore at least

$$
 \eta_{d+1}t-(a_d+2)\lambda-3-2(q-1)
 \ge\eta_{d+1}t-a_{d+1}\lambda.
$$

The Gauss loss is at most
$(b_d+1)\lambda+(\lambda+1)\le b_{d+1}\lambda$.
If the selected $n$ is below $1$, then $t/D_d<q-1$, so
$\eta_{d+1}t<2(q-1)$. The base monomial $x^t$ already meets the weaker
claimed credit because $a_{d+1}\ge2q+4$. This completes the induction.
$\square$

The monic polynomials $A_{d,n}$ divided by
$p^{\lfloor\eta_dn-a_d\lambda(n)\rfloor}$ form a triangular sublattice
of the dual in (14). Therefore

$$
 \beta_N\ge\sum_{n=1}^N\lfloor\eta_dn-a_d\lambda(n)\rfloor
 \ge\eta_d\frac{N(N+1)}2-a_dS_N-N.                     \tag{25}
$$

For each fixed depth, (25) already gives
$\liminf\beta_N/N^2\ge\eta_d/2$. The recurrence increases to the positive
root $\eta_*$ defined in Section 1. To obtain the finite bound (6), rather
than only a supremum of fixed-depth limits, use

$$
 \frac{d}{dx}\frac{1+x}{a+bx}
 =\frac{a-b}{(a+bx)^2}\le\frac1{(2p+1)^2},
 \qquad a-b=p^2.
$$

Thus $0\le\eta_*-\eta_d\le\eta_*(2p+1)^{-2d}$. The choice $d=d_N$
gives $\eta_dN(N+1)/2\ge\eta_*N^2/2$. Substituting the explicit
$a_d$ in (25) proves Theorem 2. No error constant has been treated as
independent of the depth. If $L=\lceil\log_p(N+1)\rceil$, the logarithm
sum can be evaluated exactly as

$$
 S_N=LN-\frac{p^L-p}{p-1}+L-1.                         \tag{26}
$$

## 6. Explicit upper minors

The upper bound uses an elementary coefficient estimate in the classical
normalized Newton basis. For $1\le k\le M$ put

$$
 \tau_k(M)=\sum_{\ell\ge1}\min(k,\lfloor M/q^\ell\rfloor).
$$

**Lemma 5 (coefficient extraction).** Suppose a vector polynomial
$V(y)=\sum_{k=0}^M v_ky^k$ takes values in a full fractional lattice $L$
for every $y\in O$. Then $p^{\tau_k(M)}v_k\in L$ for $k\ge1$.

**Proof.** First let $f$ be a scalar integer-valued polynomial. Expand
it in the ordinary normalized Newton basis on the standard $q$-adic
$p$-ordering $x_0=0,x_1,\ldots$. The degree-$m$ denominator has valuation
$\alpha_q(m)$. Among the first $m$ nodes, precisely
$\lceil m/q^\ell\rceil$ are divisible by $p^\ell$. A product contributing
to the coefficient of $y^k$ retains at least
$\max(0,\lceil m/q^\ell\rceil-k)$ such roots. Summing valuations and
subtracting $\alpha_q(m)$ bounds that coefficient below by
$-\tau_k(m)\ge-\tau_k(M)$. Terms with $m<k$ vanish. Applying this scalar
result to every element of $L^*$, and using $L^{**}=L$, proves the vector
statement. Scaling handles fractional lattices. $\square$

Fix a tame block $n_t=r+ht$, $0\le t<T$, where $h=q-1$. The unit columns
with $a=1$ in (17) include $((1+py)^t)_t$. Lemma 5 shows that their span
contains the pure polynomial columns

$$
 \left(p^{k+\tau_k(T-1)}\binom tk\right)_{0\le t<T},
 \qquad 1\le k<T.                                     \tag{27}
$$

Choose $1\le A\le T$ and put $K'=T-A$. Combine (27) for
$k=1,\ldots,K'$ with the rational columns at $x=1$, $a=1,\ldots,A$.
Their determinant has exact valuation

$$
\begin{aligned}
 U_{p,r}(T,A)={}&\sum_{a=1}^A
       \left[a-\lceil a/p\rceil+v_p((a-1)!)\right]
       +\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}v_p(t!)\\
 &+\sum_{k=1}^{K'}\left[k+\tau_k(T-1)-v_p(k!)\right]
       -\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\sum_{j=2}^A v_p(r+ht+j).        \tag{28}
\end{aligned}
$$

To see this, multiply row $t$ by $D(t)=(n_t+2)_{A-1}$, where the
subscript denotes a rising factorial. The rational columns become
$(a-1)!$ times monic polynomials in $n_t$ of degrees $A-a$. The pure
columns become $D(t)\binom tk$, of degrees $A-1+k$ and leading
coefficients $h^{A-1}/k!$. All degrees from zero through $T-1$ occur
once. The determinant is the Vandermonde on $0,\ldots,T-1$ times these
leading coefficients and the explicit column factors. Since $h$ is a
unit, its powers cost nothing. This proves (28), including its factorial
denominators.

If $T_r=\#\{0\le n<N:n\equiv r\pmod h\}$, it follows that

$$
 \beta_N\le\sum_{r=0}^{h-1}
       \min_{1\le A\le T_r}U_{p,r}(T_r,A),             \tag{29}
$$

with an empty block contributing zero. Columns (27) are in the lattice
but need not be individual evaluations. Multilinearity still places their
determinant in its determinant ideal, which gives the stated upper bound
on the index. No assertion about cancellation among terms of an expanded
evaluation determinant is needed.

For completeness, the leading coefficient in (29) can be evaluated.
Let $c=(p-1)/p$ and suppose $K'/T\to x$. Factorial valuations and
residue counting in the denominator of (28) give

$$
 \frac{U_{p,r}(T,A)}{T^2}\longrightarrow
 F_p(x)=\frac c2(1-x)^2+\frac{x^2}2
       +\sum_{\ell\ge1}\int_0^x\min(u,q^{-\ell})\,du. \tag{30}
$$

Indeed $v_p(n!)=n/(p-1)+O_p(\log(n+1))$. For each $j$ in the final
sum of (28), $h$ is invertible modulo every $p^e$, so the count of
$t<T$ with $p^e\mid r+ht+j$ is $T/p^e+O(1)$. Summing over
$j\le A=O(T)$ and $e=O_p(\log T)$ gives $TA/(p-1)+O_p(T\log T)$.
The quadratic factorial terms then cancel. For the $\tau$ sum, dominated
convergence applies since the $\ell$th integral is at most $xq^{-\ell}$.
This proves (30) for fixed $p$ and $r$.

Consequently

$$
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\beta_N}{N^2}
 \le\frac1{q-1}\min_{0\le x\le1}F_p(x).               \tag{31}
$$

For $p=2$ the strictly convex function has its minimizer at $x=1/6$.
This lies between $1/16$ and $1/4$, where
$F'_2(x)=-1/2+(5/2)x+1/12$. Direct summation gives
$F_2(1/6)=307/1440$, and (31) proves the upper side of (7).
The finite formula (29), rather than an unspecified asymptotic threshold,
is available for applications.

## 7. Transfer to the original quadratic support

The exact statements above concern one-variable disk modules. For the
biconjugate triangular determinant, the following is a lower bound;
we do not identify its exact finite-jet module with a direct sum of the
univariate modules.

There is a general transfer that does not restrict the ratio $R/J$. Let
$\tau_p(L,J)$ be the one-variable column index on $O$ for degree below
$L$, with $\tau_p(0,J)=0$. Lemma 3 gives

$$
 \tau_p(L,J)=\sum_{t=0}^{q-1}\iota(M_t(L),J),
$$

where an empty disk contributes zero. For every $K\ge p^3$ and $R,J\ge1$,
the original biconjugate column index satisfies

$$
 \nu(N_J)\ge\sum_{\substack{s\ge0\\Ks<R}}
 \left[\tau_p(R-Ks,J)+(R-Ks)(s+\alpha_q(s))\right].       \tag{32}
$$

To prove (32), take a regular triangular basis $f_r(z)$ of the
one-variable weighted finite-jet dual. If its negative leading-coefficient
valuations are $a_r$, then $\sum_{r<L}a_r=\tau_p(L,J)$. Multiply these
rows by the normalized full-jet conjugate ghost row of index $s$ from
[1], whose negative leading-coefficient valuation is $s+\alpha_q(s)$.
For $K\ge p^3$, its other monomials have weighted degree at most $Ks$
and are lower in the ordering that refines weighted degree by the
$w$-exponent. The products therefore remain triangular and within the
original support. Hasse--Leibniz preserves weighted integrality, since
$c_j\ge c_i+c_{j-i}$ for $0\le i\le j<J$. Taking their determinant proves
(32).

For $M\le J$, the head pivots give
$\iota(M,J)=\sum_{m<M}c_m$. For $M=J+N$, the inclusion
$C_{N,J}\subseteq B_N$ used in Section 3 holds for every $J\ge1$, even
without the stabilization cutoff. Thus
$\iota(J+N,J)\ge H(N,J)+\beta_N$ always. Substituting (25) or (6) in
(32) gives a finite lower bound for arbitrary derivative budgets. The
cutoff is needed for equality or the two-sided comparison (5), not for
this lower-bound transfer. This is also a direct way to use a separately
computed exact $\beta_N$. It supplies no upper bound for the biconjugate
index.

Use the notation of [1]:
$\Lambda=\{(r,s)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^2:r+Ks<R\}$, $q=p^2$,
$K\ge p^3$, $C=J-1$, and $R\le q^2J$. Let $E^{\rm fin}$ be the
finite residue-factor row bound of [1, Theorem 2]. For a fixed depth
$d\ge1$ define

$$
 n_*=(\lfloor(R-1)/q\rfloor-C)_+,\qquad
 h_d=a_d\lceil\log_p(n_*+1)\rceil.
$$

**Theorem 6 (finite triangular transfer).** With the same support,
column prices $c_j=\lfloor j/p\rfloor$, and derivative capacity as [1],
the universal column index is at least $E^{\rm fin}+T_d$, where

$$
 T_d=\sum_{r+Ks<R}
 \left(\left\lfloor\eta_d(\lfloor r/q\rfloor-C)\right\rfloor-h_d\right)_+.
                                                               \tag{33}
$$

**Proof.** For $r=q(C+n)+t$, $n>0$, $0\le t<q$, Proposition 4 supplies
a monic degree-$n$ polynomial of credit at least
$\lfloor\eta_dn\rfloor-h_d$. Prescribing its last retained derivative,
as in (20) or [1, Theorem 4], gives a monic degree-$r$ row of credit
at least

$$
 \lfloor C/p\rfloor+n+\lfloor\eta_dn\rfloor-h_d.
$$

The nonnegative binomial leading-coefficient valuation is discarded here.
Under $R\le q^2J$, the residue-factor baseline at this row is exactly
$\lfloor C/p\rfloor+n$. Choose the better row, then multiply by the
same normalized conjugate ghost rows as in [1]. Their weighted leading
degrees respect $r+Ks<R$ when $K\ge p^3$, and Hasse--Leibniz preserves
weighted integrality. The resulting rows are triangular. Their summed
credit is (33); duality gives the claimed determinant bound. For
$\lfloor r/q\rfloor-C\le0$ the summand is zero. $\square$

The sum is exactly computable without enumerating the support. Write
$\eta_d=a/b$ in lowest terms and let

$$
 F(K,X,m)=\sum_{r+Ks<X}\lfloor r/m\rfloor,
 \qquad F(K,X,m)=0\quad(X\le0).
$$

For $v=1,\ldots,a$ put

$$
 k_v=\max\left(0,\left\lceil\frac{h_d+1-v}{a}\right\rceil\right),
 \qquad L_v=q\left(C+bk_v+\left\lceil\frac{bv}{a}\right\rceil\right).
$$

Then

$$
 T_d=\sum_{v=1}^a F(K,R-L_v+qb,qb).                    \tag{34}
$$

The summand in (33) counts integers $u\ge h_d+1$ satisfying
$\lfloor r/q\rfloor-C\ge\lceil bu/a\rceil$. Partition $u=ak+v$.
For each $v$ the successive thresholds in $r$ are $L_v+qbj$, $j\ge0$,
whose triangular row counts sum to (34). This proves the identity in
integers. The closed evaluation of $F$ in [1] has a remainder period of
length $K/\gcd(K,m)$; the cost of (34) also depends on the numerator
$a$ and integer bit complexity. It is not logarithmic uniformly in all
these parameters.

For comparison with the prior integrated construction, replace its bonus
$T_{\rm old}$ by $T_d$ when $T_d>T_{\rm old}$. The additional normalized
arithmetic term is

$$

$$

## 8. The first saturation layer and an explicit coupled row

The strictness of (32) has two distinct aspects. The first saturation
layer admits an exact description and is already univariate. Deeper
layers can couple the conjugate variable to finite pure jets. We describe
both without identifying the complete biconjugate index.

Let $I$ denote the full weighted-jet algebra and $I_J$ its finite-budget
analogue. Let $V_\Lambda$ be the polynomial space on $r+Ks<R$, and put
$L_\infty=I\cap V_\Lambda$, $L_J=I_J\cap V_\Lambda$. These are dual
lattices to the corresponding column modules. The full-jet algebra
imported from [1] is

$$
I=\bigoplus_{a=0}^{p-1}z^a\operatorname{Int}(O)[A],
\qquad A=(z^p-w)/p.                                    \tag{36}
$$

Write $C_0$ for the algebra of locally constant $\mathbb F_q$-valued
functions on $O$. The digit basis in [1] gives
$\operatorname{Int}(O)/p\operatorname{Int}(O)=C_0$. The kernel assertion
follows by division by $p$; surjectivity follows by interpolation in
finitely many residue digits. Consequently

$$
I/pI=C_0[z,A]/(z^p-\overline w).
$$

**Proposition 7 (first finite-jet layer).** For $K\ge p^3$, the
$O/pO$-dimension of the first saturation quotient is

$$
\dim_{\mathbb F_q}\frac{L_J\cap p^{-1}L_\infty}{L_\infty}
=\#\{(r,s)\in\Lambda:r\ge qJ\}.                        \tag{37}
$$

More generally the reduction of the entire full-jet algebra has weighted
Taylor coordinates

$$
I/pI\simeq C_0[t,T]/(t^p).                              \tag{38}
$$

The cutoff kernel is the monomial ideal spanned by $t^bT^h$ with
$0\le b<p$ and $ph+b\ge J$.

**Proof.** Introduce the integral weighted Taylor ring
$O[t,T]/(t^p-pT)$. Substitution $z\mapsto z_0+t$, $w\mapsto\sigma(z_0)$
expresses an ordinary Taylor coefficient of order $j$ on
$t^{j\bmod p}T^{\lfloor j/p\rfloor}$ with exactly the multiplier
$p^{\lfloor j/p\rfloor}$. For each $f\in I$, all these coefficients
are integral by definition.

Set $d_1(w)=(w^q-w)/p\bmod p$. At conjugate nodes,
$A(z_0,\sigma(z_0))\equiv d_1(\sigma(z_0))\pmod p$: indeed
$w^p\equiv z_0\pmod p$, so $w^q\equiv z_0^p\pmod{p^2}$.
The reduced Taylor map is therefore

$$
z\longmapsto z_0+t,\qquad
A\longmapsto d_1+\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}
  \frac1p\binom pj z_0^{p-j}t^j+T.                     \tag{39}
$$

Here $z_0=\overline w^{,p}$ belongs to $C_0$, and
$p^{-1}\binom pj$ is integral. Subtracting the displayed lower terms
recovers $t$ and $T$. This proves (38), including injectivity; it does
not treat $z$ and $w$ as independent node variables.

The inexpensive lifts are

$$
H=z^q-z,\qquad V=H^p/p.
$$

In the integral weighted Taylor ring, $H(z_0+t)=-t+pE$ for an integral
polynomial $E$: its value is divisible by $p$, its first coefficient is
$-1\bmod p$, and every higher priced coefficient is divisible by $p$.
Expanding $(-t+pE)^p/p$ shows that $V$ has integral weighted coefficients
and reduces to $(-1)^pT$; every other term is divisible by $p$, including
the term $p^{p-1}E^p$. Thus $H$ and $V$ lift $-t$ and $(-1)^pT$ at
pure-$z$ degrees $q$ and $pq$.

Let $G_s$ be the normalized full-jet conjugate ghost row of [1]. Write
$r=qm+a$, $0\le a<q$, and define

$$
f_r=\frac{z^aH^m}{p^{\lfloor m/p\rfloor}}.
$$

The rows $f_rG_s$ belong to $I$, preserve the original triangular
support, and have the same leading credits
$\lfloor r/p^3\rfloor+s+\alpha_q(s)$ as the accepted full-jet basis.
Their triangular determinant therefore has the full lattice index, so
they form a basis of $L_\infty$. If $m=ph+b$, their reduced Taylor images
are unit multiples of

$$
(z_0+t)^a t^bT^h\overline G_s.
$$

We check independence after truncation. The ghost digit lemma in [1]
expresses each new normalized ghost as a nonzero constant times its
highest new residue digit, plus a polynomial in earlier digits and
$t,T$. Products with base-$q$ digit exponents below $q$ therefore give
an invertible triangular change of the finite digit-function basis over
$\mathbb F_q[t,T]/(t^p)$. This remains invertible after the cutoff ideal.
Eliminate the highest digit monomial in any finite relation. The
remaining coefficient functions $z_0^a$, $0\le a<q$, are independent;
replacing them by $(z_0+t)^a$ is a triangular change with unit diagonal.
The monomials $t^bT^h$ with $ph+b<J$ are independent as well. Thus exactly
the rows with $m\ge J$ vanish, and all others are independent after
truncation. This applies to arbitrary subsets, including partial residue
blocks at the edge of the triangle.

Finally a row $g\in L_\infty$ represents an element of the cutoff kernel
if and only if $g/p\in L_J$. The kernel dimension is therefore (37).
This is one division by $p$ relative to the full lattice, with no claim
that the resulting finite lattice is saturated. $\square$

The construction uses only univariate replacements before multiplication
by the full-jet ghosts. Hence the gain in (37) is already contained in
(32), and must not be added to it a second time.

An explicit deeper relation occurs in the $p=2$, $K=17$, $R=34$, $J=4$
control. Put $H=z^4-z$ and

$$
B(z,w)=\frac{128w-128z^8+H^8}{512}
=\frac{w-z^8+2(H/2)^8}{4}.                             \tag{40}
$$

This polynomial has integral priced pure Hasse derivatives through order
three at every conjugate node. To prove the value condition, set
$\delta=(z^2-\sigma(z))/2$. Direct expansion gives

$$
H/2=\sigma(\delta)+2\sigma(z)\delta+2\delta^2.
$$

Eighth powering on $\mathbb F_4$ is its nontrivial automorphism, so
$(H/2)^8\equiv\delta\pmod2$. Also $z^8\equiv z^2\pmod4$; the numerator
in the second formula (40) is divisible by four.
For positive orders, $f(y)=y^8/128$ at $y=2x$ has priced derivative factor
$2^{1-j+\lfloor j/2\rfloor}\binom8j$, of valuations $3,2,2$ at
$j=1,2,3$. Hasse composition with integral $H$ preserves divisibility by
four: only outer orders at most $j$ occur, and the prices are
nondecreasing. The corresponding derivatives of $z^8$ have valuations
at least $3,3,4$. The $w$ term has no positive pure-$z$ derivative. This
proves the claim.

At that contract, replace the product-basis rows with leading monomials
$w,zw$ by $B,zB$. Their pure-$z$ degrees $32,33$ remain in the original
support. Ordering first by $w$-degree keeps the replacement triangular;
each leading coefficient improves from $1/2$ to $1/4$. Thus the bound
$125$ becomes $127$, matching the independently formed exact matrix.
The fixed-small-budget limitation is concrete: the priced fourth
derivative at $z=2$ is $149820097763/4$, of valuation $-2$. No
large-budget or discriminant gain is inferred from (40).

## 9. Computation, limitations, and reproducibility

All computations use CPUs. The finite module algorithm is ordinary DVR
Smith elimination after extracting the minimum valuation of each row.
Restriction of scalars from the unramified quadratic ring duplicates
each local invariant; this duplication is checked. A pivot at the chosen
modular precision is treated as capped, not as an exact valuation.

The exact $p=2$ universal indices computed so far are

| $N$ | 32 | 64 | 128 | 256 | 512 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $\beta_N$ | -10 | 47 | 447 | 2461 | 11556 |

At $N=512$ the largest normalized pivot is 54 at 62-bit precision.
These are finite data, not an extrapolation of a limit. Controls also
reproduce four separately formed disk modules through the row
factorization (10), and general-budget examples check (5). Restricting
to units gives larger indices at $N=128,256$, so nonunit shells must not
be silently omitted from the universal module.

The proof checks include 30,120 integer degree-rounding controls for the
fixed-depth induction, 10,300 exact contraction and finite-root controls,
4,160 Newton coefficient checks, 216 rational determinant checks for
(28), 816 rational translation identities, eight exact translation-module
containment checks, and 1,440 direct-enumeration comparisons for (34).
The finite-contract gains use integer sums and 192-bit Arb arithmetic.
These are author controls supporting the proofs; the separate public
independent audit is described below.

Four further controls form the original biconjugate matrix from actual
quadratic-coordinate node values, independently of the row bound (32).
At derivative order $j$, each coordinate has polynomial degree at most
$R-1-j$, so the integer grid $0\le x,y<R-j$ generates the whole module
by tensor Newton interpolation. The Smith pivots are uncapped. The
one-variable matrices in (32) are formed separately.

It gives a stronger local comparison than the universal-module gains
