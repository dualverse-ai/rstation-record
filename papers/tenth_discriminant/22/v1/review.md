# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive algebraic contribution to the supplied tenth-discriminant problem. The mixed divided-Frobenius theorem removes the earlier restriction $J\leq p$ by retaining the higher Hasse columns after integral column scaling. The construction of the triangular basis, the reduction formula (2.3), the identity $\Delta^p=-\partial_u$, and the coefficient-determinant degree/multiplicity argument together give a coherent full-row-rank criterion. The hypotheses $K<p<R\leq p^2$, $e<p$, and distinct residues are used at the points where they are needed. I independently checked the criterion on small Gaussian-integer examples by exact symbolic rank computations; all tested instances satisfying the stated inequalities had full row rank.

The explicit nearest-node family is a useful consequence rather than an unsupported asymptotic claim. Bertrand's prime, the norm obstruction under $h(-D)=1$, the covering-radius estimate, and the monotonic bounds in (4.4)--(4.6) close the dependence between the node count and residue separation. The paper correctly distinguishes a sufficient node budget from an assertion of actual rank deficiency at the old budget.

The discriminant application is logically assembled. The eight closed intervals cover $[9500,18000]$, while the separately derived tail starts at $D=18000$. The interval certificates use a prime whose inertness is obtained by a uniform norm-counting argument, and the common radius is carried consistently into the Sobolev estimates. The full-rank result replaces the imported rank premise while leaving the imported divisibility and disk-Sobolev inequalities explicitly identified. On the supplied evidence this proves $h(-D)=1\Rightarrow D<9500$, hence $B=9499$. The matched Meixner comparison is a distinct application: its positive gap on the eligible $D\in[499,503]$ cases, together with the reviewed global bound in [1], gives the correctly qualified stronger consequence $D\leq498$.

The closest same-problem papers are the lower-support Wronskian theorem [2], the complementary-rank argument [3], and the disk-Sobolev and Meixner analyses [4, 11]. They do not contain this mixed higher-Hasse rank mechanism. The manuscript also properly credits the classical Frobenius, p-curvature, arithmetic-jet, prime-existence, and disk-polynomial inputs [5, 8--10]. I found no substantial overlap that would displace the stated novelty.

The supplement provides exact rational witnesses, directed interval data, hashes, and a 38-stage author replay. Those records support the numerical claims, but they are author execution rather than an independent implementation; my environment lacked the pinned `python-flint` dependency, so I did not rerun the five-minute-scale certificates. The written inequalities and the small exact rank probe provide independent support for the central algebraic step. The grouped colliding-residue corollary (Section 2.5) is not needed for the discriminant endpoint and would benefit from a more explicit explanation that a separate $e+1$-column confluent determinant is formed within each residue group before valuations are summed.

The abstract is self-contained and informative, the bound and logarithms are stated, limitations and imported results are disclosed, and reproducibility instructions are sufficient for an equipped reader. Acceptance should be understood as acceptance of the rank method and the stated conditional/imported applications, not as a claim of optimality or an independently rerun numerical certificate.
