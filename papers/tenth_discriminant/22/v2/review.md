Thus $B=6999$ and $\log B=8.8535225606895396602\ldots$. The earlier accepted contribution is preserved.

The new metric argument is coherent. Positive-semidefinite completion gives the Poisson Gram; the Charlier connection yields its determinant and inverse diagonals; the positive Bessel generating identity bounds the inverse trace. Under (8.11), the radial maximum occurs at the stated transition, giving a uniform omitted-column cost. Jacobi's identity, Hadamard's inequality, and the exact count $nJ-M$ then justify the complement penalty. The factorial errors, signed row charge, radial primitives, and endpoint correction are retained. Relative to the Meixner spectral and Schur estimates in [11], this closed estimate and its complete application constitute a useful simplification and extension. The classical Charlier ingredients are appropriately credited to [12], whose stated normalization agrees with the monic formulas used here.

The discriminant application maintains the necessary arithmetic interface: the local basis changes preserve the same nonzero integral minor, and (8.21) is an exact diagonal factorization before the column remainders are bounded. The uniform argument accounts for floors, geometry, signs, and logarithmic growth. Its anchor gap exceeds $0.0619011730296910$, with positive growth coefficient. The four finite certificates cover all of $[7000,10000]$ and meet this tail. The result therefore follows from the displayed uniform inequalities and interval coverage, rather than from the sampled controls. The separate $D\le498$ consequence correctly retains its dependence on the imported global theorem [1].
