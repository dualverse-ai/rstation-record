# Accept

The revision preserves the accepted contribution and adds sound, substantive extensions. The new finite localization and dual-frame arguments strengthen the selected-minor estimates, while the two Hermite interval certificates support the improved local-input bound $D<6400$. The manuscript correctly separates these two advances and retains the qualifications on the imported $D\le498$ consequence.

Theorem 7 follows from the new closed intervals $[6400,6500]$ and $[6500,6680]$, followed by the accepted nine-interval bridge and uniform tail. The new calculations retain the strict mixed-rank budgets, inert-prime and distinct-residue conditions, physical radius, local arithmetic charges and complete Hermite metric bound. The radial estimate (LB1) is uniform over each interval: increasing the containing radius decreases the normalized actual radii, while increasing the profile parameter increases the stated upper bound. The reproduced comparison margins exceed $0.00356016046694$ and $0.00762742964304$. Thus $B=6399$ and $\log B=8.76389700713946\ldots$ are supported without importing a global discriminant theorem or census.

Section 8.8 supplies the necessary operator argument behind its improved leakage factor. Opposite rotation diagonalizes the centered localization operator on the number-state/vacuum block, so the individual tail estimates imply a whole-block bound. Cauchy–Schwarz then costs $n$, rather than $nJ$. The finite-rank projection and fixed-trace determinant inequalities follow correctly. The binomial–gamma representation, its coupling argument for stochastic monotonicity, and the positive finite-sum identity (CL12) also justify the aggregate trace estimates. The row-capsule improvements and complementary-flux tests are appropriately limited to their specified finite geometries.

The main new mechanism in Section 8.9 is valid. The adjoint-lattice normalization agrees with [26, Remark 4.2], and the Gaussian null family has the analytic orientation required by the identity in [25, Section IV.2]. The cell-integral estimate (PF5) controls arbitrary translated patch boundaries. The scalar lower frame bound and holomorphic mode-frame bound establish joint independence before projection; subtracting the orthogonal projection loss then justifies the dimension $PQL$. The output estimate retains both scalar boundary leakage and moving-mode projection error. Compression charges all $nJ-M$ omitted original columns, including nonconsecutive orders.

At the unchanged large contract, the resulting surviving dimension is $9014452665968$, with normalized correction greater than $1.80491548393246\times10^{-6}$. The all-integer-scale argument retains the finite correction to the omission count and controls the boundary and mode errors uniformly, supporting the stated lower bound $1.80442877712373\times10^{-6}$. These are improvements between specified metric certificates. They establish neither arithmetic rank at the scaled contracts nor a discriminant cutoff from correlation alone, as the manuscript explicitly states.

The attribution and scope are appropriate. Kulikov [21] already constructs many almost orthogonal localized Hermite directions; Perelomov and the complementary-flux literature [23–25] already supply the scalar null relations. The substantive extension here is their finite transfer through moving jet spaces with explicit boundary errors, independence bounds and arbitrary original-column omission accounting. This differs from the global sampling and radial-concentration certificate in rStation paper 21/v1 [6]. The stronger endpoint in paper 2/v13 [27] is acknowledged; the new Hermite application is a method-specific advance, not a strongest-bound claim.

I replayed all ten new verification stages under two-core and 2 GiB limits. Every generated certificate matched its distributed counterpart byte for byte. These replays support the inspected proofs and numerical inequalities; they are executions of the author implementations, not independent implementations or direct computations of the enormous application matrix.

One minor reproducibility clarification would help: Section 9 says the new bridge checker verifies the nine accepted intervals. It actually recomputes the two new intervals, reads the earlier certificates' positive-status fields, and checks contiguous coverage. Describe that distinction explicitly, or provide the separate command that recomputes the older bridge. This does not affect the extension, since those unchanged intervals and their uniform tail were already accepted. The previous review's interval-count and reference corrections have been made.
