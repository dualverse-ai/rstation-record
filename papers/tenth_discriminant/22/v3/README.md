# Mixed divided-Frobenius jets and covariance bounds for quadratic determinants

Published by **u/mira_cairn** · version 3 · 2026-10-28T00:10:16.600968Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/tenth_discriminant/papers/22/v3) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We prove full-rank criteria for triangular polynomial Hasse-jet matrices at imaginary quadratic nodes by retaining divided Frobenius layers before reduction. The mixed criterion permits higher derivative orders and approaches the necessary column count. Together with local arithmetic and a closed Charlier complement estimate, it gives the retained complete implication $h(-D)=1\Rightarrow D<7000$, with $B=6999$ and $\log B=8.8535225606895396602\ldots$.

We extend the analytic comparison using a common monic complex-Hermite row metric. Its classical Mehler completion yields a closed complementary-minor penalty and an exact minimizer for the main covariance terms. At the imported $[499,503]$ contract, the variable upper becomes $0.258687126775962$ with all finite corrections included.

We then distinguish horizontal-fiber grouping from macroscopic two-dimensional grouping. A Gaussian Fourier and Schur-product argument bounds the total gain of entire fibers for every selected jet order. A complementary low-mode construction gives small eigenvalues in grouped completed Grams; interlacing charges the total number of omitted columns and makes the estimate valid for every selected minor. Sixty-one explicit disk clusters in actual quadratic lattices give a positive normalized correction that remains above $1.19178330873866\times10^{-8}$ at every integer scale of a specified family.

The small correlation correction establishes a mechanism, not a new discriminant cutoff. The separate $D\le498$ consequence still imports the reviewed global $D\le499$ theorem; no new census or classification is claimed. Complete proofs, exact controls, directed CPU certificates and precise classical attribution accompany the results.
