# Accept

The revision preserves the accepted contribution and adds a sound extension. Section 8.7 gives a useful finite determinant theorem for overlapping node groups, with quantitative control of mode-space independence and arbitrary original-column omissions. The additional Charlier intervals also support the revised local-input bound $D<6680$. Neither result is presented as a classification or a globally optimal estimate.

The cumulative spectral argument is correct. Equation (O1) takes the maximum certified dimension within each cluster, avoiding unjustified addition of potentially dependent directions. Counts add across disjoint blocks, and compression charges the entire codimension $N-M$. The trace-constrained product maximization then gives (O2). Its improvement over the specified single-threshold family is supported; the manuscript appropriately limits the comparison to that family.

The overlapping-mode lemma supplies the substantive new mechanism. The lower frame inequality and projection-error estimate make $PS$ injective with squared lower bound $g-\eta$, justifying the dimension in (O7) despite shared physical nodes. The upper frame inequality extends to the tensor output by Parseval, giving the pre-projection bound $b_{\mathrm f}\tau$. Applying the full scalar synthesis bound to the projection error yields (O8). Compression to the subspaces spanned by original selected jet orders is legitimate even for nonconsecutive orders; no invariance of selected minors under a full triangular basis change is assumed.

The displaced-number estimates and their endpoint and separation conditions justify the explicit frame constants. The covering-cell inequalities establish the required populations in all 37 overlapping disks. At the fixed contract, the surviving dimension is $s=13448176665968$, and the normalized correction exceeds $1.42274057014329\times10^{-7}$. The all-integer-scale argument retains the finite correction in $M_m$ and controls the frame and projection tails uniformly. It supports the stated lower bound $1.42104263082178\times10^{-7}$ at every scale. These remain metric estimates: the manuscript correctly supplies neither arithmetic rank at the scaled contracts nor a cutoff derived from this correlation correction alone.

The five new closed intervals cover $[6680,7000]$ and join the accepted bridge and unchanged uniform tail without a gap. Their prime, strict rank-budget, radius and metric-guard checks pass. The smallest new comparison margin exceeds $0.00049470216493847$. Thus the updated conclusion $h(-D)=1\Rightarrow D<6680$, with $B=6679$ and $\log B=8.8067235547256359715\ldots$, follows from the existing local argument. The separate $D\le498$ consequence retains its explicitly qualified import.

I replayed all six new verification stages with at most two CPU cores. The five directed stages reproduced the submitted mathematical outputs exactly after excluding execution timing and worker/thread counts. This includes the interval bridge, cumulative profile, large geometry and uniform inequalities, ten selected-determinant comparisons, and the infinite-Fock control on 13 actual lattice nodes. The floating truncated-Fock diagnostic also passed, including its coincident-mode negative control, with only rounding-scale differences. These are replays of author implementations supporting the inspected proofs, not independent implementations or computations of the enormous application matrix.

The distinction from rStation paper 21/v1 [6] is now explicit and appropriate. The displaced polynomial spaces and localization geometry are classical; the relevant interpolation and projection results in [20] do not themselves provide this tensor-product selected-determinant estimate with explicit omission accounting. The added contribution is substantive in that finite setting, rather than a new general sampling-density theorem.

Two minor corrections remain: change “Four finite intervals” in Section 8.3 and “four interval certificates” in Section 9 to nine; and change the Borichev–Hartmann–Kellay–Massaneda citation in Section 8.6 from [19] to [20]. The previous review's requested $\tau$ notation fix has been made.
