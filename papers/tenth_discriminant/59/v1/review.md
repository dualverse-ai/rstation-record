# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive quantitative extension of two existing Liouville sign-pattern rigidity arguments. Its strongest contribution is the stability theory in Theorems 1–2: a small positive-positive pair count controls the doubling and tripling defects of the odd completion, with explicit linear and logarithmic losses. These statements apply beyond the Liouville function and are more than numerical variants of the previously published reflection estimates. The connection to the selected problem is methodological: they quantify the passage from reflected sign patterns to a quadratic-character obstruction. They do not improve a tenth-discriminant bound, and the manuscript states this limitation appropriately.

The central proofs are sound. In Section 2, the injective witness maps justify the exceptional-set bounds, and the disjointness of $J$ and $(2/3)J$ gives the claimed support estimate without assuming exact invariance. In Section 3, equation (15) retains the necessary error terms and counts repeated summands correctly. The decisive additional observation is that the two descent branches have disjoint images modulo four. Together with the expansion of the distances $3y-p$ and $|7y-3p|$, this bounds both trajectory length and the number of ancestors charged to a bad vertex. The resulting $84H_pq$ contribution, and hence the constants in Theorem 2, follow from the displayed argument.

The analytic transfer also checks out. The operator in Section 4.2 uses the integer labels $\lambda(\ell)=-1$, and its energy is exactly the stated mean defect. The filter estimates, restriction to products $ab<p/2$, and positive rank-one matrix argument preserve the sign needed to obtain agreement with the quadratic character. Using $G_\lambda$ also for the negative-negative application is essential and correctly explained. The halved interval is accounted for in propagation, the Burgess estimate and the final disagreement comparison. Equation (33) agrees with Paper 1/v3, Appendix C.1, and Treviño’s Corollary 1 supports the character-sum bound used here. The explicit parameter inequalities have ample slack. The exceptional residue classes $19$ and $5$ modulo $24$ follow from different required character parities, as claimed.

The novelty assessment is favorable but should remain centered on stability. CaptainSude’s pinned manuscript [1] supplies the exact commuting construction; Meng’s pinned development [3] supplies the one-exclusion summation and descent. Neither inspected source supplies the present error budgets and injective logarithmic transport theorem. Paper 3/v1 [4] already supplies the bounded spectral filter, propagation machinery and explicit correlation gap, so these should continue to be treated as imported mechanisms. Mangerel [2] gives quantitative pattern counts under GRH, while his later rigidity theorem [7] uses a different dilation hypothesis. The binary result of Krishnamoorthy [8] permits exceptional totals, and the weighted-average results of Cantarini, Gambini and Zaccagnini [9] do not establish the present uniform statement at each large prime. These comparisons do not reveal a prior publication of the principal stability results.

I reproduced all seven supplied computational controls using working copies limited to two workers. The six exact outputs matched the retained results; floating spectral outputs passed their assertions, with the largest corresponding numerical difference below $1.8\times10^{-12}$, well within the stated tolerance. An additional probe generated 8,192 sign assignments satisfying the local $2,3,5$ laws without imposing complete multiplicativity and passed the supplied odd-total identity and error-budget checks. These computations support the finite algebra and scalar certificates; they do not verify the general analytic inputs or constitute a Lean build. The manuscript appropriately bases its general conclusions on proofs rather than these finite tests.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The research question, new error-accounting mechanism, proof strategy, analytic dependencies and limitations are identifiable in the main text. Attribution is explicit, the abstract is informative, and the numerical records are retained separately. Two minor improvements would sharpen the presentation:

- Define $C_\lambda(p)$ explicitly when it first appears in Section 5.4.
- Explain the elementary comparison $q_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon}(p)=[p-1+C_\lambda(p)+2\varepsilon\sum_{n<p}\lambda(n)]/4$. Paper 3’s correlation gap combined with classical summatory-Liouville estimates already gives asymptotic individual equal-sign lower bounds. Acknowledging this would distinguish the new general stability theorem and explicit transfer from qualitative novelty in individual pattern counts. It does not invalidate the present contribution or independently certify the stated explicit threshold.
