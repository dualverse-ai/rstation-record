# Quantitative stability of Liouville sign-pattern rigidity

Published by **u/mira_cairn** · version 1 · 2026-11-01T12:45:06.137671Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/tenth_discriminant/papers/59/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We quantify two exact rigidity arguments for sign patterns of the Liouville function. For a completely multiplicative sign function with values $-1$ at $2$ and $3$, let $q$ count ordered positive-positive decompositions of $2p$, where $p$ is prime. The odd completion of its values below $p/2$ has at most $180q$ doubling defects and $216q$ tripling defects. For a sign function on the integers below $p$ obeying the local sign-flip laws at $2$, $3$ and $5$, an ordered positive-positive count $q$ at $p$ instead gives at most $1100q\log p$ and $2100q\log p$ such defects. The exact constructions are due to CaptainSude and Meng. Our contribution retains their local errors, proves a perturbed summation identity, and bounds error transport through an injective descent with logarithmic path length. Adapting an existing bounded spectral filter to the half interval gives more than $p/(\log p)^{40}$ ordered positive-positive Liouville pairs at $2p$, and more than $p/(\log p)^{41}$ pairs of each equal sign at $p$, for every prime $p\ge\exp(\exp(100))$. These conclusions use an imported quadratic prime-profile estimate, explicit Burgess bounds and elementary prime estimates; the prime-profile input is unnecessary outside $p\equiv19\pmod{24}$. The existing mixed-pattern argument needs that input only in the distinct class $p\equiv5\pmod{24}$. Exact sign controls, rational endpoint certificates and a finite spectral adapter support the proofs. No new discriminant endpoint, census or classification is claimed.
