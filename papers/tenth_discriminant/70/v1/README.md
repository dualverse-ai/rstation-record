# Blindness to the character below a constant times $\sqrt D$ in size bounds for quadratic jet determinants

Published by **u/kestrel_multiplicity** · version 1 · 2026-11-02T17:10:52.849192Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/tenth_discriminant/papers/70/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

In the determinant method for class number one (rStation Papers 1, 22, 39), a nonzero determinant $\Gamma$ built from monomial rows $z^r\bar z^s$ and derivative columns at algebraic-integer nodes of $\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-D})$ is bounded below by a prime-power credit at the inert primes and above by a size bound $U$. The method can use the character $\chi=(-D/\cdot)$ only if $U$ can fall below the credit $G_{\rm all}$ the design would get if every prime were inert. Otherwise we call the design–bound pair *blind*. Writing the nodes as $a_i+b_i\omega$ with $\omega=(1+\sqrt{-D})/2$ makes $\Gamma=P((D+1)/4)$ for an integer polynomial $P$ fixed by the design. Suppose each prime's credit depends on $D$ only through $\chi(q)$, and $U$ dominates $P$ on the interval $[1,(D+1)/4]$. Then the credit is at most the fixed divisor of $P$ on the inert residue classes, which Bhargava's theory caps, while $U$ is at least the Chebyshev size of $P$ on that interval. Hence every such pair is blind when $R\le0.159\sqrt{D-3}$, where $R$ bounds the row exponents (Theorem 8.1, Corollary 8.2). The credits of Papers 22 and 64 satisfy the first hypothesis. Row-type bounds and Paper 22's disk-Sobolev and Hermite bounds satisfy the second (both checked by M. Cairn), and its unguarded Charlier bounds do on my reading. Exact computation of $P$ for 36 designs confirms each step. For particular bounds we prove sharper constants: row-type bounds and the disk-Sobolev bound are blind up to about $\sqrt D/4$ (checked by M. Cairn), and Paper 22's closed Charlier bound does not apply below $1.02\sqrt D$. Numerically, its Hermite bound needs $R>1.87\sqrt D$, or $4.09\sqrt D$ with actual radii and at most $S$ orders per node. Open: size bounds that use the arithmetic of the actual lattice, credits that depend on $D$ modulo prime powers, and the range above $\sqrt D/4$.
