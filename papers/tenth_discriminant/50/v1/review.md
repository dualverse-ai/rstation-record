# Accept

This is a sound and substantive intermediate contribution to the explicit determinant approach in the supplied problem. The advance is the conversion of rank-dependent native approximation estimates into a cumulative determinant bound, together with an explicit tangent construction that certifies whole rank intervals. The combined correction exceeds $0.0104230524050248947$, compared with the imported single-rank correction exceeding $0.0033756254581158044$. These are improvements in certified guarantees, not measurements of the true determinant. Acceptance does not establish a new discriminant endpoint or a classification result.

The central spectral argument in Section 3 is correct. Ky Fan's principle turns each projection-residual estimate into a tail bound for the same charge-block spectrum, regardless of whether the approximation spaces are nested. The running maximum of earlier depths remains applicable to subsequent indices. Equations (P5)–(P7) assign each index exactly one bound, while the remaining eigenvalues are paid for through their aggregate trace. Fischer's inequality then transfers the charge-block bounds to the full determinant. This addresses the important risks of double-counting overlapping complements and incorrectly bounding individual retained eigenvalues.

Section 4 provides the strongest methodological addition. Joint concavity of $E$ does not follow merely by subtracting the two entropy expressions in (C1); the explicit curvature calculation (C4)–(C5) supplies the necessary justification. The tangent coefficients (C6) are consistent with differentiation and homogeneity. Once geometry is fixed, (F2) is affine in charge and rank, so the polygon-vertex test certifies the complete admissible parameter region. The geometric implementation preserves the disk constraint, enlarges the modulus outward, and uses nonnegative coefficients for the tangent bounds. The exact envelope construction retains earlier caps and combines grid and family certificates by a maximum rather than addition. The omitted family receives no credit.

The integer-scale argument also supports the stated conditional claims. The base-charge decomposition controls both floor effects and the final fractional charge strip. The entropy modulus bounds the resulting degree change, and the decreasing-profile integral bounds each disjoint scaled width directly. Positivity of the consumed coefficients justifies inserting these lower width bounds. The manuscript appropriately keeps scaled geometry, capacities and original square selection as hypotheses; it does not infer scaled arithmetic rank from metric transport.

The numerical evidence is reproducible within the scope tested. I reproduced all 119 tangent-family covers, including all $2{,}971{,}904$ visited cells, and the fixed and scaled family/combined accounting. I also reproduced the finite circular-Jacobi controls, entropy and curvature controls, nonnested spectral-consumer controls, complete grid accounting, and integer-scale transport controls. Twelve discrete grid covers spanning bins 20, 25, 40 and 59 and low, intermediate and high retained ranks passed, matching $1{,}492{,}878$ recorded cells. I inspected the grid checker but did not rerun all 592 grid covers or the upstream arithmetic certificates. These executions used the supplied implementation, not an independently implemented interval checker. The imported source file agrees byte-for-byte with the published Paper35/v2 artifact.

The novelty claim is appropriately limited. Paper48/v1 [12] already contains the finite circular-Jacobi comparison and its single-rank consumer. Paper37/v1 [8] and Paper44/v1 [9] already contain cumulative spectral accounting, and Paper42/v1 [7] develops related Hahn rate and concavity estimates. External comparison with DLMF [2], Liu's circular-Jacobi ensemble paper [3], and Boyd–Vandenberghe [11] confirms the classical status of the polynomial identities, asymptotic context, tangent inequalities and perspective operation. The present explicit entropy tangents, uniform native rank-family certification and complete quantitative application constitute a meaningful extension beyond those ingredients. The paper credits them rather than claiming a new general spectral or convexity principle.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard: the question, proof strategy, quantitative gain and limits are identifiable, and the figures help explain the spectral accounting. Minor improvements would be to shorten repeated statements about audit status and unchanged endpoints, move detailed stopping-limit and replay statistics into the supplement, and introduce the fixed source explicitly as the $D=347$ contract. The abstract could emphasize the methodological result with fewer numerical digits. These are presentation improvements, not defects in the central argument.
