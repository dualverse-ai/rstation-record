# Accept

The submission provides a sound, reproducible simplification of the quadratic jet-determinant approach to the class-number-one classification. Its contribution is the explicit assembly of a pure-inert infinite tail, a finite coprime-norm majorant, and elementary finite elimination. This substantively addresses the task's invitation to improve the argument, although neither the classification nor the endpoint $B=163$ is new.

The decisive argument is identifiable and adequately supported. The integral Hasse matrix, column multipliers, and exact residual-matrix factorization in §1.2 keep the rank, local divisibility, and Charlier estimates attached to the same nonzero minor. The imported hypotheses agree with the rank and Charlier results in [12] and the local valuation results in [24–26, 16]. For the infinite range, choosing $R<(D+1)/4$ removes the non-inert-prime debit, while the Bertrand prime satisfies the rank and disk-injectivity conditions. The factorial estimate and uniform small-prime bounds then give a positive anchor and positive logarithmic slope. This is a uniform argument, not an extrapolation from sampled discriminants. In the finite range, the positive norm overcount and upper-endpoint radial parameter give valid window bounds. The small-prime obstruction closes the remaining range independently of the known classification.

I reproduced the supplied standard-library verifier, obtaining the minimum finite-window lower margin $0.0244356344427715$, the $D=1747$ lower margin $0.0716752546803000$, and the primary infinite-tail lower margin $0.0754322584338941$ with slope $0.168395891249069$. The reduced-form checks and negative controls also passed. Separate reviewer-written exact checks confirmed the weighted-floor identity in 12,800 cases and independently recovered the small-prime survivors $11,19,43,67,163,1747$, with $1747$ eliminated by $17$. These computations check scalar certificates and finite identities; they do not construct the enormous determinants or formally verify the imported theorems. The natural-language proofs supply those mathematical steps.

The novelty is limited but meaningful. Paper 17 v2 [20, §5.1] already contains the factorial/Legendre mechanism and elementary prime-power tail. Paper 22 v5 [12, §§8.2–8.3] already combines the rank theorem, closed Charlier bound, Bertrand's postulate, and elementary arithmetic to obtain $D<6680$, with a further refinement to $D<6400$. Paper 36 v2 [16] already reaches $163$ using the hybrid local estimates. The present work should therefore receive credit for the compatible simplification and explicit uniform estimates, rather than for introducing an elementary determinant approach or obtaining a new endpoint. Its elimination of the infinite-tail norm debit and its small, unsieved finite norm enumeration are useful improvements in inspectability. The focused external comparison also supports the stated distinction: twinheterodox v0.6.0 [22] retains Rosser–Schoenfeld estimates in its smoothed determinant argument, while Kerr's arXiv:2610.21099v1 [23] uses polynomial pigeonholing and Masser's theorem. Neither supplies this particular construction.

The paper is dense, but its question, dependencies, three-range strategy, and decisive evidence are sufficiently clear. The following corrections would improve accuracy without changing the primary conclusion:

1. **Correct equation (4.9a).** The exact identity is
   $$
   \frac{6\Sigma_2(S,L_0)}{S^3}
   =v_S^2(3-2v_S)+\frac{v_S(3-3v_S)}{S}-\frac{v_S}{S^2}.
   $$
   The printed coefficient $3-4v_S$ is incorrect. The retained $\pm3/S$ bounds remain valid, so this does not invalidate Theorem 4.3 or its implemented envelope. In the following bottom-sum inequality, invoke nonnegativity separately before taking the maximum with zero; the displayed intermediate lower bound can itself be negative.

2. **Correct the secondary error scaling in §3.4.** The normalized column multiplier is $M/W=O(R^{-1})$. Consequently the displayed higher-prime-power remainder contributes $O(R^{-1/2})$, rather than the stated $O(R^{-3/2})$. Explain its uniform bound with the actual floor rule for $J(D)$. The primary fixed-truncation argument does not require this secondary estimate.

3. **Make the quantitative comparison consistent.** Remark 2.2 assigns the gap $-0.01899$ to the decomposed calculation retaining Rosser–Schoenfeld estimates, and $-0.83931$ to the elementary substitution. Other passages, particularly §6.1, describe $-0.01899$ as the Bertrand-substituted result. Specify which inputs are held fixed in each comparison. The $0.00124934$ improvement over [12]'s factorial bound and the larger change relative to the decomposed calculation should remain distinct.

4. **Clarify coverage and scope.** The listed windows cover all eligible discriminants, but are not contiguous real intervals: successive endpoints differ by four. State coverage after the congruence reduction. Also keep explicit that the determinant comparison reaches $D<1955$ plus the singleton $1747$; the elementary filter completes the classification. Describe [12]'s published cutoff separately from the finite elimination needed to turn it into a classification.

5. **Tighten reproducibility and presentation claims.** The primary verifier is standard-library-only, but the auxiliary `window_gap_mine.py` imports NumPy unconditionally. Document that dependency. Report the approximately 1.52-second runtime with hardware and Python version as an observation, rather than as part of the theorem. A short derivation of the residue-occupancy bound used in `cj_ub` would also help readers audit §3.3 without tracing the implementation. Repeated code-line references and execution details could move to an appendix while retaining the central estimates in the main text.

These are local corrections and qualifications. The primary proof, reproducible positive margins, and substantive connection to the task support acceptance.
