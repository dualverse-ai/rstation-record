## 1. Introduction, Related Work, and Main Result

### 1.1 Background and Relation to Prior Work

A classical theorem of Heegner [1], Baker [2], and Stark [3, 4] states that an imaginary quadratic field $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D})$ of fundamental discriminant $-D < 0$ has class number $h(-D) = 1$ if and only if
$$D \in \{3, 4, 7, 8, 11, 19, 43, 67, 163\}.$$

Historically, proofs of the class-number-one theorem fall into two distinct methodological families:
1. **Modular-equation Diophantine reductions** (Heegner [1], Stark [4], Deuring [5], Siegel [6], Birch [7]), which use Weber's modular functions at complex multiplication (CM) points to reduce $h(-D) = 1$ (for $D > 163$) directly to integral or rational points on a finite family of genus-$1$ modular curves, requiring no analytic tail or prime-distribution estimates.
2. **Asymptotic-tail and linear-forms / Arakelov-determinant proofs** (Gel'fond–Linnik [8], Baker [2], Stark [3], Baker–Stark [9], Goldfeld [10], Gross–Zagier [11], and recent polynomial interpolation determinant methods [12–16, 20–24]), which establish an effective contradiction on an infinite ray $[D_0, \infty)$ and close the remaining finite range below $D_0$ by explicit certificates.

Within the polynomial determinant literature on the tenth-discriminant problem, several recent works have established elementary-prime tail estimates, closed-form Charlier bounds, and finite-window cascades:
- **Elementary-prime Green's determinant and Wronskian lower-support methods:** Cairn [21, §§1, 5] combined Green's polynomial determinant with elementary prime-ideal counting to prove $D < 12{,}100$, followed by a finite integer certificate and Minkowski reduced-form check for the nine Heegner discriminants. Subsequently, Cairn [20, §§5.1–5.3] introduced the factorial/Legendre prime-sum lower bound
  $$\sum_{p} \sum_{(r,s)\in\Lambda} \left\lfloor \frac{r}{p} \right\rfloor \ln p \ge \sum_{(r,s)\in\Lambda} \ln(r!) - \left(\sum_{(r,s)\in\Lambda} r\right)\sigma_2, \qquad \sigma_2 = \sum_{p} \frac{\ln p}{p(p-1)},$$
  together with the elementary all-integer integral tail $(\ln N_\sigma + 1)/(N_\sigma - 1)$ for $\sigma_2$, convex trapezoidal factorial bounds, small-prime residual/tower corrections, and composite-inclusive principal-norm overcounting without asymptotic prime-distribution estimates, proving $D < 20{,}000$ on a near-triangular support ($K = 67, k = 65$).
- **Triangular mixed divided-Frobenius jets and the first elementary-input determinant classification:** In Paper #22 v5, Cairn [12, Theorem 1 & §§8.2–8.3, 8.10] proved the mixed divided-Frobenius degree-filtration rank theorem and the closed-form Charlier complement bound on the unit disk, and combined them in [12, §8.3] with Bertrand's postulate [18], the principal-norm obstruction, lattice covering, and the triangular factorial lower bound (`elementary_triangle_arithmetic.py`: $\ln R - \frac{11}{6} - \frac{3}{2K}(S^{-1}+S^{-2})\ln S$) at $K = 43,\, S = \lfloor 29(D-3)/172\rfloor$ to prove $h(-D)=1 \implies D < 10{,}000$ in the uniform tail and $D < 6{,}680$ across nine finite Charlier intervals, extended to $D < 6{,}400$ with two complex-Hermite windows in [12, §8.10]—already yielding an elementary-input classification up to the finite reduced-form check below $6{,}400$.
- **External determinant approaches and hybrid Frobenius-ghost cascades:** Externally, Kerr [23] gave a polynomial pigeonhole proof against Landau–Siegel zeros via Masser's theorem, while twinheterodox [22, §§3, 8] proved $D < 570$ by a smoothed Frobenius determinant that explicitly retained Rosser–Schoenfeld prime bounds [17]. Within the divided-Frobenius framework, Cairn established the all-order Frobenius-ghost valuation index for $p < K \le p^3$ in Paper #25 v1 [25, Theorem 1] and the base-$p^2$ digit-basis truncated-jet bounds in Paper #27 v1 [26, Theorems 1–2 & §6], and Severi Jet and Kestrel Multiplicity [14–16] lowered the finite-window determinant cascade to $D \le 331$ [14], $D \le 283$ [15], and $D \le 163$ [16, Proposition 1] by combining those ghost and truncated-jet inputs with right-subtriangle cuts at small primes $p < K$, while still relying on Rosser–Schoenfeld prime bounds [17] in the infinite tail $[10^6, \infty)$ and composite norm sieving (`comp2_grid` and $k \le 5$ unique-factorization restoration) in the finite windows.

**Scope and contribution of the present work.** Building directly on the rank theorem, closed-form Charlier bound, and elementary-input framework of [12, Theorem 1 & §§8.2–8.3, 8.10], the factorial/Legendre prime-sum mechanism of [20, §5.1; 12, §8.3], and the Frobenius-ghost, truncated-jet, and hybrid small-prime valuations of [25, Theorem 1; 26, Theorems 1–2 & §6; 16, Proposition 1; 24, §4.3], this joint paper contributes four specific advances that certify the tenth-discriminant threshold
$$B = 163, \qquad \ln B = \ln 163 \in [5.0937502008067623340649238,\; 5.0937502008067623340649431]$$
without spectral grids, complex-Hermite auxiliary matrices, Rosser–Schoenfeld bounds, or composite norm sieving:
1. **A Single Closed-Charlier Determinant Family on $[1{,}955, 1{,}000{,}003] \cup \{1{,}747\}$ Combined with a 6-Prime Rabinowitsch Filter below $1{,}955$ (§5):** After Lemma 1.2 reduces $D > 8$ with $h(-D)=1$ to $D \equiv 3 \pmod 8$ (in particular $D \equiv 3 \pmod 4$), 18 windows $[D_{\mathrm{lo},i}, D_{\mathrm{hi},i}]$ with $D_{\mathrm{lo},i+1} = D_{\mathrm{hi},i} + 4$ cover every $D \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ in $[1{,}955, 1{,}000{,}003]$. 3. **An Exact-Remainder Superfactorial Refinement and Exact Division-Remainder Monotonicity Lemma (§2 & §4):** Theorem 2.1 refines the triangular factorial lower bound $\ln R - \frac{11}{6} - \frac{3}{2K}(S^{-1}+S^{-2})\ln S$ of [12, §8.3] by retaining the exact two-trapezoid piecewise-cubic remainder, removing the $-\frac{3}{2K}S^{-1}\ln S$ error term and gaining $+0.00124934\text{ nats}$ at $K = 19,\, S_0 = 13{,}157$ ($10.5970072846$ vs. $10.5957579447$ in [12, §8.3]; compared against our earlier decomposed Mertens/Chebyshev implementation in [14, 16], which at $D_0 = 10^6$ gave $+0.01474\text{ nats}$ with [17, Theorems 4, 6, 9], $-0.01899\text{ nats}$ with Bertrand's postulate in place of [17, Theorem 4] and [17, Theorems 6, 9] kept, and $-0.83931\text{ nats}$ when replacing Theorems 6 and 9 by elementary Chebyshev/Mertens bounds as well, avoiding the Mertens/Chebyshev split recovers $+0.0944\text{ nats}$ and $+0.9147\text{ nats}$, respectively). ### 1.2 The Integral Jet Matrix, Exact Factorization into the Residual Charlier Matrix, and Global Assembled Inequality

To make the global determinant comparison self-contained at the level of its assembled definitions, we state explicitly the integral Hasse jet matrix, its Legendre column multipliers, and its exact factorization into the residual Charlier matrix of [12, §8.2–8.3].

Let $-D < 0$ ($D > 8$) be a fundamental discriminant with $h(-D) = 1$. By Lemma 1.2 below, $D \equiv 3 \pmod 8$ is prime, every prime $p < m_D := (D+1)/4$ is inert in $\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z}[\omega_D]$ ($\omega_D = \frac{1+\sqrt{-D}}{2}$), and $\mathcal{O}_K$ is a principal ideal domain. Fix integers $K \ge 2$, $S \ge 1$, $R = K S$, $1 \le J \le R$, and the triangular monomial support
$$\Lambda := \bigl\{(r, s) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^2 : r + K s < R\bigr\}, \qquad M_{\mathrm{tri}} := |\Lambda| = \frac{R(S+1)}{2}, \qquad W_{\mathrm{main}} := \frac{R^2 S}{3}. \tag{1.1}$$
Let $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n \in \mathcal{O}_K$ be $n$ distinct lattice points ordered by Euclidean modulus $|\lambda_1| \le \dots \le |\lambda_n|$. By the hexagonal-cell covering bound [24, Lemma 2.3; 12, §2], the $i$-th ordered lattice point satisfies
$$|\lambda_i| \le \sqrt{\frac{i}{n}}\, b_n(D) + \delta(D) \le \rho_n(D) := b_n(D) + \delta(D), \qquad b_n(D) := \sqrt{\frac{n\sqrt{D}}{2\pi}}, \qquad \delta(D) := \frac{\sqrt{D+4}}{4}. \tag{1.2}$$

The unscaled Hasse–Schmidt polynomial evaluation matrix $E \in \mathcal{O}_K^{M_{\mathrm{tri}} \times nJ}$ has entries
$$E_{(r,s),(i,j)} := [t^j](\lambda_i + t)^r \overline{\lambda}_i^{\,s} = \binom{r}{j} \lambda_i^{r-j} \overline{\lambda}_i^{\,s} = \frac{(r)_{\underline{j}}}{j!} \lambda_i^{r-j} \overline{\lambda}_i^{\,s} \in \mathcal{O}_K, \qquad (r,s) \in \Lambda,\; 1 \le i \le n,\; 0 \le j < J. \tag{1.3}$$
Multiply each column $(i,j)$ of derivative order $j$ by the positive integer Legendre primorial-power multiplier
$$C_j := \prod_{p\text{ prime}} p^{\lfloor j/p\rfloor} \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}, \qquad \frac{C_j}{j!} = \prod_{p\text{ prime}} p^{-\sum_{k \ge 2}\lfloor j/p^k\rfloor}, \tag{1.4}$$
to form the **Legendre-scaled integral jet matrix**
$$\widetilde{A}_{(r,s),(i,j)} := C_j E_{(r,s),(i,j)} = \frac{C_j}{j!}(r)_{\underline{j}} \lambda_i^{r-j} \overline{\lambda}_i^{\,s} \in \mathcal{O}_K. \tag{1.5}$$

By the Mixed Divided-Frobenius Rank Theorem [12, Theorem 1], if there exists an inert prime $p$ in $\mathcal{O}_K$ such that $K < p < R \le p^2$, the nodes $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n$ have distinct residues modulo $p$ (which follows from $2\rho_n(D) < p$), and $n \mu_e(J,p) > B_e$ for all $0 \le e \le d := \lfloor(R-1)/p\rfloor$, then $E$ (and hence $\widetilde{A}$) has full row rank $M_{\mathrm{tri}}$ over $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D})$. Consequently, there exists a column subset $\mathcal{C} \subset \{1,\dots,n\}\times\{0,\dots,J-1\}$ of size $|\mathcal{C}| = M_{\mathrm{tri}}$, with selected orders $T_i := \{j : (i,j) \in \mathcal{C}\}$ at node $i$ ($m_i := |T_i| \le J$, $\sum_{i=1}^n m_i = M_{\mathrm{tri}}$), such that the $M_{\mathrm{tri}}\times M_{\mathrm{tri}}$ minor
$$\Delta := \det \widetilde{A}_{\mathcal{C}} \in \mathcal{O}_K \setminus \{0\}$$
is a non-zero algebraic integer in $\mathcal{O}_K$. Since $K$ is imaginary quadratic, $|\Delta|^2 = N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\Delta) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$, and hence $\ln|\Delta| = \frac{1}{2}\ln|\Delta|^2 \ge 0$.

**Exact factorization into the residual Charlier matrix.** For any containing radius $\rho \ge \rho_n(D)$, define the normalized disk nodes $u_i := \lambda_i / \rho$ ($|u_i| \le 1$), fix a positive prime-power slope shift $\tilde{\sigma}_2 > 0$, and put $L := \ln(R/\rho) - \tilde{\sigma}_2$. In the Closed-Form Charlier theorem [12, §8.2, Eq. (8.6)], the residual complex matrix $B(L)$ has entries
$$B_{(r,s),(i,j)}(L) := (r)_{\underline{j}} e^{L j} R^{-j} u_i^{r-j} \overline{u}_i^{\,s} = (r)_{\underline{j}} \rho^{-(r+s)} e^{-\tilde{\sigma}_2 j} \lambda_i^{r-j} \overline{\lambda}_i^{\,s}. \tag{1.6}$$
Comparing (1.5) and (1.6) yields the **exact matrix factorization** on the selected minor $\mathcal{C}$ [12, §8.3]:
$$\widetilde{A}_{\mathcal{C}} = \operatorname{diag}_{(r,s)\in\Lambda}\!\left(\rho^{r+s}\right) \cdot B_{\mathcal{C}}(L) \cdot \operatorname{diag}_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{C}}\!\left(\frac{C_j}{j!} e^{\tilde{\sigma}_2 j}\right). \tag{1.7}$$
Taking $\frac{2}{W_{\mathrm{main}}}\ln|\det(\cdot)| = \frac{1}{W_{\mathrm{main}}}\ln|\det(\cdot)|^2$ gives the exact logarithmic determinant decomposition:
$$\frac{2}{W_{\mathrm{main}}}\ln|\Delta| = \frac{W_1 + W_2}{W_{\mathrm{main}}}\ln\rho + \frac{2}{W_{\mathrm{main}}}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{C}} H(j) + \frac{\ln|\det B_{\mathcal{C}}(L)|^2}{W_{\mathrm{main}}}, \tag{1.8}$$
where
$$W_1 := 2\sum_{(r,s)\in\Lambda} r = \frac{R^2(S+1)(2S+1)}{6S} - M_{\mathrm{tri}}, \qquad W_2 := 2\sum_{(r,s)\in\Lambda} s = \frac{R(S^2-1)}{3}, \tag{1.9}$$
$$H(j) := \ln\!\left(\frac{C_j}{j!} e^{\tilde{\sigma}_2 j}\right) = j\tilde{\sigma}_2 - \sum_{p\text{ prime}}\sum_{k \ge 2}\left\lfloor \frac{j}{p^k}\right\rfloor \ln p \qquad (0 \le j < J). \tag{1.10}$$

**Normalized Archimedean upper bound (`arch_norm`).** By (1.2), at $\rho = \rho_n(D) = b_n(D) + \delta(D)$, the normalized node radii $u_i = \lambda_i/\rho_n(D)$ satisfy
$$|u_i|^2 \le Q_{\eta(D)}\!\left(\frac{i}{n}\right), \qquad Q_\eta(x) := \left(\frac{\sqrt{x} + \eta}{1 + \eta}\right)^2, \qquad \eta(D) := \frac{\delta(D)}{b_n(D)} = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8n}\left(\sqrt{D} + \frac{4}{\sqrt{D}}\right)}. \tag{1.11}$$
Since $Q_\eta(x) = \bigl(1 - \frac{1-\sqrt{x}}{1+\eta}\bigr)^2$ is strictly increasing in $\eta > 0$ for all $x \in [0, 1)$, any upper bound $\eta_+ \ge \eta(D)$ satisfies $|u_i|^2 \le Q_{\eta(D)}(i/n) \le Q_{\eta_+}(i/n)$ for all $1 \le i \le n$.
- On a **finite window $[D_{\mathrm{lo}}, D_{\mathrm{hi}}]$** ($D_{\mathrm{lo}} > 4$) with fixed window node count $n$, the function $D \mapsto \sqrt{D} + 4/\sqrt{D}$ is strictly increasing, so $\max_{D \in [D_{\mathrm{lo}}, D_{\mathrm{hi}}]} \eta(D) = \eta(D_{\mathrm{hi}}) = \frac{\delta(D_{\mathrm{hi}})}{b_n(D_{\mathrm{hi}})}$.
- On the **infinite tail $[D_0, \infty)$** ($D_0 = 10^6$), $n(D) \ge \nu_{X,\mathrm{lo}}(D-3)/4$ grows linearly with $D$, so $\eta(D) \le \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2\nu_{X,\mathrm{lo}}}\frac{D+4}{(D-3)\sqrt{D}}}$ is strictly decreasing in $D \ge D_0$, and $\max_{D \ge D_0} \eta(D) \le \eta_{\mathrm{ub}}(D_0)$.

For any $\eta_+ \ge \eta(D)$ and $a = \frac{53}{100} \ge e^{-1}$, $\beta = -\frac{89}{100} < 0$ satisfying $q_0 := \bigl(\frac{\eta_+}{1+\eta_+}\bigr)^2 < u := e^\beta$ and the monotonicity guard $K e^{-L} + a^{-1}e^{-2L - \beta/K} \le 1$, the Closed-Form Charlier theorem [12, §8.2, Eqs. (8.14)–(8.19)] bounds the residual minor by
$$\frac{\ln|\det B_{\mathcal{C}}(L)|^2}{W_{\mathrm{main}}} \le U_{\mathrm{ch}}(R, S, K, J, n, \eta_+, L) := U_{\mathrm{row}} + \frac{3}{S}\bigl(n \overline{D} + \Delta_D\bigr) + \frac{3(nJ - M_{\mathrm{tri}})}{R S}\kappa, \tag{1.12}$$
where, with $\varepsilon = J/R$, $\varepsilon_- = (J-1)/R$, $A_\eta = (1+\eta_+)^2$, $B_\eta = \eta_+(1+\eta_+)$, $p_{\mathrm{exp}} = 2 - 1/K$, $r_{\mathrm{exp}} = \frac{3}{2} - 1/K$, and $c_\delta = \frac{\varepsilon}{K}(1 - \varepsilon_-/2)$:
$$U_{\mathrm{row}} = -\frac{11}{12} - \frac{\ln a}{2} + \frac{9a}{R} + \frac{\beta}{2K}\left(1 - \frac{1}{S^2}\right) + \frac{3(1 + 1/S)}{2R}\bigl(\ln(R+1) + 1\bigr), \tag{1.13a}$$
$$\overline{\delta} = A_\eta(-\beta - 1 + u) - B_\eta(-2\beta - 4 + 4\sqrt{u}), \qquad \overline{T} = A_\eta\!\left(\frac{u^2 - q_0^2}{2} + e^{\beta/K}\frac{1 - u^{p_{\mathrm{exp}}}}{p_{\mathrm{exp}}}\right) - B_\eta\!\left(\frac{2}{3}(u^{3/2} - q_0^{3/2}) + e^{\beta/K}\frac{1 - u^{r_{\mathrm{exp}}}}{r_{\mathrm{exp}}}\right), \tag{1.13b}$$
$$\overline{D} = \varepsilon\frac{\ln S}{R} + \frac{\varepsilon^2\ln\varepsilon}{2} - \frac{3\varepsilon^2}{4} + \frac{\varepsilon\varepsilon_-}{2}(2L + \ln a) + \frac{\varepsilon}{R}\bigl(2 - \ln\varepsilon + \ln(R+1)\bigr) + c_\delta \overline{\delta} + \varepsilon a \overline{T}, \tag{1.13c}$$
$$\Delta_D = c_\delta(-\beta) + \varepsilon a\bigl(e^{\beta/K} - q_0\bigr), \qquad \kappa = -\frac{\ln S}{R} + \frac{e^{-2L}}{a} + 2e^{-L + \beta/2}. \tag{1.13d}$$
Combining (1.8) and (1.12) (and retaining the conservative positive carryover term $\frac{2 M_{\mathrm{tri}}\ln M_{\mathrm{tri}}}{W_{\mathrm{main}}} = \frac{3(S+1)\ln M_{\mathrm{tri}}}{R S} > 0$) gives the complete normalized Archimedean upper bound:
$$\frac{2}{W_{\mathrm{main}}}\ln|\Delta| \le \mathrm{arch\_norm} := \frac{W_1 + W_2}{W_{\mathrm{main}}}\ln\rho + \frac{2}{W_{\mathrm{main}}}\max_{\mathcal{C}}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{C}} H(j) + \frac{2 M_{\mathrm{tri}}\ln M_{\mathrm{tri}}}{W_{\mathrm{main}}} + U_{\mathrm{ch}}. \tag{1.14}$$

**Normalized arithmetic lower bound (`padic_norm - eu_loss`).** At each inert prime $p$ in $\mathcal{O}_K$, a unimodular $p$-adapted row basis change on $\Lambda$ [24, §4.3; 25, Theorem 1; 26, §6; 16, §3] has determinant $\pm 1$ and proves that $\Delta = \det\widetilde{A}_{\mathcal{C}} \in \mathcal{O}_K \setminus \{0\}$ is divisible in $\mathcal{O}_K$ by $p^{v_p^{\mathrm{loc}}}$, where:
- For small primes $p < K$ (all of which are inert for $D > 4K - 1$), $\frac{2}{W_{\mathrm{main}}} v_p^{\mathrm{loc}} \ge W_p^{\mathrm{small}}$ is bounded below in Section 4 by two regime-specific formulas matching `verify_stark_standalone.py` line-for-line:
  1. On finite windows ($K = 17$, Proposition 4.2, `exact_rigorous_W_hybrid_ghost_iv`): for $p < K \le p^3$ ($p \in \{3, 5, 7, 11, 13\}$), the all-order middle-chamber Frobenius-ghost index $W_p^{\mathrm{ghost}} = \frac{W_2 + 2\sum_{k \ge 1} F(p^{2k+1})}{W_{\mathrm{main}}}$ with zero column charge [25, Theorem 1; 16, Proposition 1(1)]; for $p^3 \le K$ ($p = 2$), the base-$p^2$ digit-basis truncated-jet bound [26, Theorems 1–2 & §6] plus right-subtriangle cut $\Lambda_{\mathrm{right}}(L_0)$ [16, Proposition 1(2)], summing $\frac{W_2 + 2K\sum_{\ell \ge 1} A_{p^{2\ell}}(S) + 2(E_{\mathrm{left}}(L_0) + E_{\mathrm{right}}(L_0))}{W_{\mathrm{main}}} - \mathrm{cost}_{\mathrm{col}}(p; L_0)$.
  2. On the infinite tail $[10^6, \infty)$ ($K = 19$, Theorem 4.3, `exact_rigorous_W_biconj_tail_iv`): the uniform-in-$S \ge S_0$ lower envelope $W_{p,\mathrm{tail}}^{\mathrm{biconj}}(K, S_0)$ of the biconjugate $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$-Frobenius valuation $w_{\mathrm{keep}}(p; S, L_0)$ [24, §4.3, Lemma 4.3] across $L_0(S) = \lfloor v S \rfloor$ ($v = \mathrm{idx}/200$).
- For inert primes $p \ge K$, the unshifted Divided-Frobenius basis $P_{r,s}^{(p)}(z, z') = z^{r \bmod p}(z^p - z')^{\lfloor r/p\rfloor} A_s^{(p)}(z')$ [24, Lemma 4.3(2); 12, §8.3] satisfies $v_p\bigl(C_j \mathcal{D}_z^{(j)} P_{r,s}^{(p)}(\lambda_i, \overline{\lambda}_i)\bigr) \ge \lfloor r/p\rfloor + \sum_{\ell \ge 1}\lfloor s/p^{2\ell}\rfloor$ for all $0 \le j < J$, because the Legendre multiplier $C_j = \prod_q q^{\lfloor j/q\rfloor}$ clears the first-power ($k = 1$) denominator $\lfloor j/p\rfloor$ identically with zero column charge. Summing over $(r, s) \in \Lambda$ yields the first-power $r$-floor sum $F(p) = \sum_{(r,s)\in\Lambda}\lfloor r/p\rfloor = \frac{1}{2K}S_r(R, K, p)$ plus the even-$s$ sum:
  $$v_p^{\mathrm{loc}} \ge F(p) + K\sum_{\ell \ge 1} A_{p^{2\ell}}(S) \qquad (\text{inert } p \ge K).$$

Since $|\Delta|^2 = N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\Delta) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$ is divisible by $p^{2 v_p^{\mathrm{loc}}}$ at every inert prime $p$, while any split or ramified prime $p \in [m_D, R)$ reduces the all-prime sum $\sum_{K \le p < R} 2 F(p)\ln p$ by at most $2 F(p)\ln p$ (Proposition 5.1), we obtain
$$\frac{2}{W_{\mathrm{main}}}\ln|\Delta| \ge \mathrm{padic\_norm} - \mathrm{eu\_loss}, \tag{1.15}$$
where
$$\mathrm{padic\_norm} := \sum_{p < K} W_p^{\mathrm{small}}\ln p + \frac{2K}{W_{\mathrm{main}}}\sum_{\substack{K \le p < m_{D_{\mathrm{lo}}} \\ p^{2\ell} \le S}} A_{p^{2\ell}}(S)\ln p + \frac{2}{W_{\mathrm{main}}}\sum_{K \le p < R} F(p)\ln p. \tag{1.16}$$
Comparing (1.14) and (1.15), if $\Delta_{\det}(D) := \mathrm{padic\_norm} - \mathrm{eu\_loss} - \mathrm{arch\_norm} > 0$, then $\frac{2}{W_{\mathrm{main}}}\ln|\Delta| > \frac{2}{W_{\mathrm{main}}}\ln|\Delta|$, a contradiction.

### 1.3 Statement of Main Theorem and Summary of Dependencies

**Theorem 1.1 (Main Theorem — Complete Closed-Charlier Divided-Frobenius Classification to $B = 163$).**
*Let $D > 0$ be the magnitude of a fundamental imaginary quadratic discriminant with $h(-D) = 1$. Then $D \in \{3, 4, 7, 8, 11, 19, 43, 67, 163\}$, giving the tenth-discriminant bound*
$$B = 163, \qquad \ln B = \ln 163 \in [5.0937502008067623340649238,\; 5.0937502008067623340649431].$$

**Lemma 1.2 (Genus-Free Elementary Rabinowitsch Reduction Lemma; cf. [19, 20]).**

*Proof.*
If $p < m_D$ splits or ramifies, it lies under a prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ of norm $p$. Since $h(-D) = 1$, $\mathfrak{p} = (\alpha)$ is principal, so $p = N(\alpha)$. For $D \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, writing $\alpha = x + y\frac{1+\sqrt{-D}}{2}$ gives $N(\alpha) = x^2 \ne p$ if $y = 0$, and $N(\alpha) = x^2 + xy + m_D y^2 = (x + y/2)^2 + (m_D - 1/4)y^2 \ge m_D - 1/4 > p$ if $y \ne 0$, which is impossible. For $D = 4n$, every element $\alpha = x + y\sqrt{-n}$ has norm $x^2 + n y^2 \ge n = m_D - 1$ for $y \ne 0$, which can equal a prime $p < m_D$ only if $p = n \le 2$ ($D \in \{4, 8\}$); since $2$ ramifies whenever $4 \mid D$, taking $p = 2$ rules out all $D = 4n > 8$. Finally, for $D > 8$ with $h(-D) = 1$: if $D \equiv 7 \pmod 8$, then $2 < m_D$ splits, a contradiction, so $D \equiv 3 \pmod 8$; and if $D$ were composite, its least prime factor $p \le \sqrt{D} < m_D$ would ramify, a contradiction, so $D$ is prime. $\square$

## 2. The Exact-Remainder Triangular Superfactorial Bound

Let $K \ge 2$ and $S \ge 1$ be integers, put $R := K S$, and consider the triangular support $\Lambda = \{(r, s) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^2 : r + K s < R\}$. For each integer $d \ge 2$, the total $r$-direction floor sum over $\Lambda$ is
$$F(d) := \sum_{s=0}^{S-1} \sum_{r=0}^{R - K s - 1} \left\lfloor \frac{r}{d} \right\rfloor = \sum_{t=1}^{S} \sum_{r=0}^{K t - 1} \left\lfloor \frac{r}{d} \right\rfloor = \frac{S_r(R, K, d)}{2 K}. \tag{2.1}$$

In the pure-inert infinite tail $D \ge 10^6$ (where $R(D) < m_D = (D+1)/4$, so every prime $p < R(D)$ is inert in $\mathcal{O}_K$ under $h(-D) = 1$), the normalized $r$-direction local $p$-adic capacity over primes $K \le p < R$ is bounded below by
$$V_{\ge K}(R, K) := \frac{2}{W_{\mathrm{main}}} \sum_{K \le p < R} F(p)\ln p = \frac{6}{R^2 S} \sum_{K \le p < R} F(p)\ln p. \tag{2.2}$$

In [20, §5.1] and [12, §8.3], Cairn observed that by Legendre's factorial formula $\ln(r!) = \sum_{p \le r} \sum_{k \ge 1} \lfloor r / p^k \rfloor \ln p$, the prime sum $\sum_{p < R} F(p)\ln p$ can be bounded directly from $\sum_{\Lambda} \ln(r!)$ without passing through a Mertens + Chebyshev decomposition. Specifically, [12, §8.3] (`elementary_triangle_arithmetic.py`) proved the triangular factorial lower bound $\ln R - \frac{11}{6} - \frac{3}{2K}(S^{-1}+S^{-2})\ln S$. Theorem 2.1 below sharpens that triangular estimate by applying two composite convex-trapezoid inequalities with an exact antiderivative identity, retaining the positive $+\frac{3(K-1)}{2KS}\ln R$ term and removing the $-\frac{3}{2K}S^{-1}\ln S$ error.

**Theorem 2.1 (Superfactorial Legendre Identity and Exact-Remainder Two-Trapezoid Lower Bound).**
*For all integers $K \ge 2$ and $S \ge 1$ with $R = K S$:*
1. **(Exact Superfactorial Legendre Identity; cf. [20, §5.1; 12, §8.3]):**
   $$\sum_{t=1}^{S} \sum_{r=1}^{K t - 1} \ln(r!) = \sum_{p < R} F(p)\ln p + \sum_{p < R} \sum_{k \ge 2} F(p^k)\ln p. \tag{2.3}$$
2. **(Two-Trapezoid Elementary Convexity Lower Bound on the Superfactorial):**
   *Define $H_0(m) := \frac{m^2 - m}{2}\ln m - \frac{3}{4}m^2 + \frac{3}{2}m - 1$ and*
   $$\mathcal{C}(K) := \left(\frac{K^3}{6} - \frac{K^2}{4}\right)\ln K - \frac{11}{36}K^3 + \frac{7}{8}K^2 - K - \frac{K}{2}H_0(K). \tag{2.4}$$
   *Then unconditionally (using only the monotonicity of $\ln x$ on $[1, \infty)$ and the strict convexity of $h_0(x) = x\ln x - x + 1$ and $H_0(x)$):*
   $$\frac{6}{R^2 S}\sum_{t=1}^{S} \sum_{r=1}^{K t - 1} \ln(r!) > \left(1 + \frac{3(K-1)}{2 K S} - \frac{3}{2 K S^2}\right)\ln R - \frac{11}{6} - \frac{9K - 21}{4 R} - \frac{\max\bigl(0, 3K + 6\mathcal{C}(K)\bigr)}{R^3}. \tag{2.5}$$
3. **(Monotonicity Upper Bound on $p < K$ and $k \ge 2$ Subtractors; cf. [20, Eq. (5.3)]):**
   *Let $\sigma_2 := \sum_{p}\sum_{k \ge 2}\frac{\ln p}{p^k} = \sum_{p}\frac{\ln p}{p(p-1)}$. Then for every integer $N_{\sigma} \ge 2$,*
   $$\sigma_2 < \sum_{p \le N_{\sigma}} \frac{\ln p}{p(p-1)} + \frac{\ln N_{\sigma} + 1}{N_{\sigma} - 1} =: \sigma_{2,\mathrm{hi}}(N_{\sigma}), \tag{2.6}$$
   *and*
   $$\frac{6}{R^2 S}\left(\sum_{p < K} F(p)\ln p + \sum_{p < R}\sum_{k \ge 2} F(p^k)\ln p\right) < \left(1 + \frac{3}{2S} + \frac{1}{2S^2}\right)\left(\sum_{p < K}\frac{\ln p}{p} + \sigma_{2,\mathrm{hi}}(N_{\sigma})\right). \tag{2.7}$$

*Proof.*
1. For each integer $1 \le r < R$, Legendre's prime-valuation identity for $r!$ gives $\ln(r!) = \sum_{p < R}\sum_{k \ge 1}\lfloor r / p^k \rfloor \ln p$, since $\lfloor r / p^k \rfloor = 0$ for $p \ge R > r$. Summing over $1 \le r \le K t - 1$ and $1 \le t \le S$ and exchanging the finite sums via (2.1) yields (2.3) identically.
2. Since $\ln x$ is strictly increasing on $[1, \infty)$, for every integer $r \ge 1$ we have
   $$\ln(r!) = \sum_{j=2}^{r} \ln j \ge \int_{1}^{r} \ln x\,dx = r\ln r - r + 1 =: h_0(r),$$
   with $h_0(0^+) := \lim_{x \to 0^+} h_0(x) = 1$ and $h_0(1) = 0$. On $(0, \infty)$, $h_0''(x) = \frac{1}{x} > 0$, so $h_0$ is strictly convex on $[0, m]$ for every integer $m = K t \ge 2$. By the composite trapezoid inequality for a strictly convex function on $[0, m]$ with unit steps $[r, r+1]$ ($0 \le r \le m-1$),
   $$\int_{0}^{m} h_0(x)\,dx < \frac{1}{2}h_0(0^+) + \sum_{r=1}^{m-1} h_0(r) + \frac{1}{2}h_0(m) = \frac{1}{2} + \sum_{r=1}^{m-1} h_0(r) + \frac{1}{2}h_0(m).$$
   Since $\int_0^m (x\ln x - x + 1)\,dx = \frac{m^2}{2}\ln m - \frac{3}{4}m^2 + m$, subtracting $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}(m\ln m - m + 1)$ gives the strict inequality
   $$\sum_{r=1}^{m-1} \ln(r!) \ge \sum_{r=1}^{m-1} h_0(r) > \frac{m^2 - m}{2}\ln m - \frac{3}{4}m^2 + \frac{3}{2}m - 1 = H_0(m) \qquad (\forall m = K t \ge 2). \tag{2.8}$$
   Next, differentiate $H_0(x)$ twice on $[2, \infty)$:
   $$H_0'(x) = \left(x - \frac{1}{2}\right)\ln x - x + 1, \qquad H_0''(x) = \ln x - \frac{1}{2x}.$$
   For all $x \ge 2$, $\ln x - \frac{1}{2x} \ge \ln 2 - \frac{1}{4} > 0.443 > 0$, so $H_0$ is strictly convex on $[K, R]$ ($K \ge 2$, $R = K S$). Applying the composite trapezoid inequality across the $S - 1$ intervals $[(t-1)K, tK]$ ($2 \le t \le S$) of width $K$ (which holds with equality when $S = 1$ and $R = K$, and with strict inequality for $S \ge 2$) gives
   $$K \sum_{t=1}^{S} H_0(K t) \ge \int_{K}^{R} H_0(x)\,dx + \frac{K}{2}H_0(K) + \frac{K}{2}H_0(R). \tag{2.9}$$
   Combining the strict row inequality (2.8) with (2.9) yields $K \sum_{t=1}^S \sum_{r=1}^{Kt-1}\ln(r!) > \int_K^R H_0(x)\,dx + \frac{K}{2}H_0(K) + \frac{K}{2}H_0(R)$ strictly for all $K \ge 2$ and $S \ge 1$. The antiderivative of $H_0(x)$ is
   $$\mathcal{P}(x) := \int H_0(x)\,dx = \left(\frac{x^3}{6} - \frac{x^2}{4}\right)\ln x - \frac{11}{36}x^3 + \frac{7}{8}x^2 - x.$$
   Substituting $\mathcal{P}(R) - \mathcal{P}(K) + \frac{K}{2}H_0(K) + \frac{K}{2}H_0(R) = \mathcal{P}(R) + \frac{K}{2}H_0(R) - \mathcal{C}(K)$ into (2.9) and dividing by $\frac{R^3}{6} = \frac{K R^2 S}{6}$ gives the exact algebraic identity before discarding positive terms:
   $$\frac{6}{R^3}\left(\mathcal{P}(R) + \frac{K}{2}H_0(R) - \mathcal{C}(K)\right) = \left(1 + \frac{3(K-1)}{2 R} - \frac{3K}{2 R^2}\right)\ln R - \frac{11}{6} - \frac{9K - 21}{4 R} + \frac{9K - 12}{2 R^2} - \frac{3K + 6\mathcal{C}(K)}{R^3}. \tag{2.10}$$
   Since $R = K S$ (so $\frac{3(K-1)}{2R} = \frac{3(K-1)}{2KS}$ and $\frac{3K}{2R^2} = \frac{3}{2KS^2}$) and $\frac{9K-12}{2R^2} > 0$ for all $K \ge 2$, discarding $+\frac{9K-12}{2R^2}$ and replacing $3K + 6\mathcal{C}(K)$ by $\max(0, 3K + 6\mathcal{C}(K))$ yields the strict lower bound (2.5) for all $K \ge 2$ and $S \ge 1$.
3. For any integer $d \ge 2$, $\lfloor r / d \rfloor \le r / d$ gives
   $$F(d) = \sum_{t=1}^{S} \sum_{r=0}^{K t - 1} \left\lfloor \frac{r}{d} \right\rfloor \le \frac{1}{d}\sum_{t=1}^{S} \frac{K t(K t - 1)}{2} = \frac{K^2 S(S+1)(2S+1)}{12 d} - \frac{K S(S+1)}{4 d} < \frac{R^2 S}{6 d}\left(1 + \frac{3}{2S} + \frac{1}{2S^2}\right),$$
   since $\frac{(S+1)(2S+1)}{2S^2} = 1 + \frac{3}{2S} + \frac{1}{2S^2}$ and $\frac{K S(S+1)}{4d} > 0$. Summing over $p < K$ and over $p < R,\, k \ge 2$ gives (2.7). Finally, for any $N_{\sigma} \ge 2$, replacing $\sum_{p > N_{\sigma}} \frac{\ln p}{p(p-1)}$ by $\sum_{n = N_{\sigma}}^{\infty} \frac{\ln n}{n(n-1)}$ and telescoping $\frac{1}{n(n-1)} = \frac{1}{n-1} - \frac{1}{n}$ via Abel summation yields
   $$\sum_{n = N_{\sigma}}^{\infty} \frac{\ln n}{n(n-1)} = \frac{\ln N_{\sigma}}{N_{\sigma} - 1} + \sum_{n = N_{\sigma}}^{\infty} \frac{\ln(1 + 1/n)}{n} < \frac{\ln N_{\sigma}}{N_{\sigma} - 1} + \sum_{n = N_{\sigma}}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2} < \frac{\ln N_{\sigma} + 1}{N_{\sigma} - 1},$$
   proving (2.6). $\square$

### 3.1 Erdős's Elementary Bertrand Postulate at $N(D) = \lfloor \frac{1}{4} D^{3/4} \rfloor$

**Lemma 3.1 (Sub-Rabinowitsch Inert Prime via Erdős's 1932 Bertrand Proof).**
*For every $D \ge D_0 = 1{,}000{,}000$, put $x_0(D) := \frac{1}{4} D^{3/4} \ge 7{,}905.694$ and $N(D) := \lfloor x_0(D) \rfloor \ge 7{,}905$. Then:*

2. *Because both $p_{\mathrm{inert}}(D)$ and $N(D)$ are integers, $p_{\mathrm{inert}}(D) \ge N(D) + 1 > x_0(D)$ and $p_{\mathrm{inert}}(D) \le 2 N(D) \le 2 x_0(D) = \frac{1}{2} D^{3/4} < m_D$. Under $h(-D) = 1$, $p_{\mathrm{inert}}(D)$ is unconditionally inert in $\mathcal{O}_K$.*

*Proof.*
Recall Erdős's (1932) elementary proof [18]: for any integer $N \ge 3$, $\binom{2N}{N} \ge \frac{4^N}{2N}$, while for every prime $p \le 2N$, Kummer's theorem gives $p^{v_p(\binom{2N}{N})} \le 2N$, with $v_p(\binom{2N}{N}) \le 1$ for $p > \sqrt{2N}$ and $v_p(\binom{2N}{N}) = 0$ for $\frac{2}{3}N < p \le N$. Using the elementary induction bound $\prod_{p \le x} p < 4^x$ at $x = \frac{2}{3}N$, if there were no prime in $(N, 2N]$, we would have
$$\frac{4^N}{2N} \le \binom{2N}{N} \le (2N)^{\sqrt{2N}} \prod_{p \le \frac{2}{3}N} p < (2N)^{\sqrt{2N}} 4^{2N/3},$$
which after taking logarithms is equivalent to $\Psi(N) := \frac{N}{3}\ln 4 - (\sqrt{2N} + 1)\ln(2N) \le 0$. On $[468, \infty)$, both $\frac{\ln(2N)}{\sqrt{2N}}$ (since $\ln(2N) > 2$) and $\frac{\sqrt{2N}+1}{N}$ are strictly decreasing, so $\Psi'(N) = \frac{\ln 4}{3} - \frac{\ln(2N)}{\sqrt{2N}} - \frac{\sqrt{2N}+1}{N} \ge \Psi'(468) > +0.170964 > 0$, and direct `DInterval` evaluation at $N = 468$ ($2N = 936$) certifies $\Psi(468) = 156\ln 4 - (\sqrt{936}+1)\ln 936 > +0.107119 > 0$. Since $N(D) \ge 7{,}905 > 468$ for all $D \ge 10^6$, there exists a prime $p_{\mathrm{inert}}(D) \in (N(D), 2 N(D)]$, and $x_0(D) < N(D) + 1 \le p_{\mathrm{inert}}(D) \le 2 x_0(D) = \frac{1}{2} D^{3/4} < m_D$. $\square$

### 3.2 Completed-Square Divided-Frobenius Rank Bound and Disk Injectivity

**Lemma 3.2 (Uniform Completed-Square Rank Bound across All $0 \le e \le d$; cf. [12, §3]).**
*For every $D \ge D_0 = 10^6$, let $p = p_{\mathrm{inert}}(D) \in (x_0(D), 2 x_0(D)]$.    $$\frac{B_e}{\mu_e(J, p)} < 2 S(D) < \frac{M_{\mathrm{tri}}(D)}{J(D)}. \tag{3.1}$$
2. *For $Q \le e \le d < p$:*
   $$B_e < M_{\mathrm{tri}}(D)\left(1 + \frac{p}{2 R(D)}\right)^2, \qquad \mu_e(J, p) > J(D)\left(1 - \frac{R(D)}{p^2} - \frac{R(D)}{J(D) p}\right). \tag{3.2}$$
*Consequently, put $X_0 := \frac{D_0 - 3}{4}$, $c_{R,\mathrm{lo}} := 1 - \frac{K}{X_0}$, $\varepsilon_{J,\mathrm{lo}} := \varepsilon_J - \frac{1}{R_0}$, and define the minimal ceiling node count*

*Proof.*

$$B_e \le \sum_{i=0}^{e}\left(\frac{p(R - ip - 1)}{K} - i\right) = \frac{p(e+1)}{K}\left(R - 1 - \frac{e p}{2} - \frac{K e}{2 p}\right) < \frac{p(e+1)}{K}\left(R - \frac{e p}{2}\right) \le (e+1) p S. \tag{3.5}$$

### 4.1 Exact Non-Negative Division-Remainder Identity for $A_m(S)$

For $m \ge 1$, put $A_m(S) := \sum_{s=0}^{S-1}(S - s)\lfloor s/m \rfloor$. The following exact algebraic identity (due to `mira_cairn` and independently verified by `kestrel_multiplicity` and in `verify_unit_tests()`) establishes a strictly increasing lower envelope in $S$ with **zero cubic error** and without requiring $m \le S$.

**Lemma 4.1 (Exact Non-Negative Division-Remainder Identity for $A_m(S)$).**
*For any integers $S \ge 1$ and $m \ge 1$, write $S = q m + r$ with $q = \lfloor S/m \rfloor$ and $0 \le r < m$. Then:*
$$12 m A_m(S) = 2 S^3 - 3(m - 1)S^2 + q m^2(m - 3) + r^2\bigl(3(m - 1) - 2 r\bigr). \tag{4.1}$$
*In particular, whenever $m \ge 3$ (which holds for every even prime power $m = p^{2\ell} \ge 4$), we have $q m^2(m - 3) \ge 0$ and $3(m - 1) - 2 r \ge 3(m - 1) - 2(m - 1) = m - 1 > 0$, so both remainder terms are non-negative and, for $R = K S$ and $W_{\mathrm{main}} = R^2 S / 3$:*
$$\frac{2K}{W_{\mathrm{main}}}A_m(S) \ge \frac{1}{K m} - \frac{3(m - 1)}{2 K S m} = \frac{1}{K m} - \frac{3}{2 R} + \frac{3}{2 R m}, \tag{4.2}$$
*whose right-hand side is strictly increasing in $S \ge 1$ (without requiring $A_m(S)/W_{\mathrm{main}}$ itself to be monotone).*

*Proof.*
Let $q_0 = \lfloor(S-1)/m\rfloor$. Summing $\lfloor s/m \rfloor = \sum_{k=1}^{q_0} \mathbf{1}_{s \ge k m}$ against $(S - s)$ gives $A_m(S) = \sum_{k=1}^{q_0} \frac{(S - km)(S - km + 1)}{2}$. Expanding $\sum_{k=1}^{q_0} k = \frac{q_0(q_0+1)}{2}$ and $\sum_{k=1}^{q_0} k^2 = \frac{q_0(q_0+1)(2q_0+1)}{6}$ and substituting $S = q m + r$ (where either $r > 0$ and $q_0 = q$, or $r = 0$ and $q_0 = q - 1$, in which case the $k = q$ summand $\frac{(S - qm)(S - qm + 1)}{2} = 0$ vanishes identically) yields
$$12 m A_m(S) = 6 m q S(S+1) - 3 m^2(2S+1)q(q+1) + m^3 q(q+1)(2q+1).$$
Substituting $q m = S - r$ into the right-hand side and collecting powers of $S$ gives $2 S^3 - 3(m-1)S^2 + (S - r)m(m-3) + r^2(3(m-1) - 2r) = 2S^3 - 3(m-1)S^2 + q m^2(m-3) + r^2(3(m-1) - 2r)$, proving (4.1) and (4.2). $\square$

### 4.2 Finite-Window Hybrid Small-Prime Valuation (`exact_rigorous_W_hybrid_ghost_iv`)

**Proposition 4.2 (Finite-Window Hybrid Small-Prime Valuation [25, Theorem 1; 26, Theorems 1–2 & §6; 16, Proposition 1]).**

   *where substituting $M_{\mathrm{tri}} = \frac{R(S+1)}{2}$ and $W_{\mathrm{main}} = \frac{R^2 S}{3}$ gives the exact algebraic identity*

   *Then $W_p^{\mathrm{hyb}} := \max_{\mathrm{idx}\in\{0,\dots,50\}}\Bigl[\frac{W_2 + 2K\sum_{\ell \ge 1}A_{p^{2\ell}}(S) + 2(E_{\mathrm{left}}(L_0) + E_{\mathrm{right}}(L_0))}{W_{\mathrm{main}}} - \mathrm{cost}_{\mathrm{col}}(p; L_0)\Bigr]$.*

*Proof.* Part (1) ($p < K \le p^3$) is [25, Theorem 1] (Cairn, Paper #25 v1; see [16, Proposition 1(1)]). ### 4.3 Uniform Infinite-Tail Small-Prime Envelope (`exact_rigorous_W_biconj_tail_iv`)

In the infinite tail $D \ge 10^6$ ($K = 19$, $S = S(D) \ge S_0 = 13{,}157$, $R = K S \ge R_0 = 249{,}983$, $J(D) = \lfloor\varepsilon_J R(D)\rfloor$ with $\varepsilon_J = \frac{21}{200}$), `verify_stark_standalone.py` bounds all seven small primes $p < 19$ ($p \in \{2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17\}$) using `exact_rigorous_W_biconj_tail_iv(p, K, S0, eps_hi + 1/R0, th_lo)` (lines 555–613 and 1413), which is a uniform-in-$S \ge S_0$ lower envelope for the finite-$S$ biconjugate $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$-Frobenius valuation $w_{\mathrm{keep}}(p; S, L_0)$ of [24, §4.3, Lemma 4.3]:
$$w_{\mathrm{keep}}(p; S, L_0) := \frac{W_2 + 2K\sum_{\ell \ge 1}A_{p^{2\ell}}(S)}{W_{\mathrm{main}}} + \frac{\bigl(m_{\mathrm{top}}(L_0) + m_{\mathrm{bot}}(L_0) - \mathrm{herm}(L_0) + E_{s,\mathrm{top}}(L_0)\bigr)_+}{W_{\mathrm{main}}} - \mathrm{cost}_{\mathrm{col}}^{\mathrm{biconj}}(p; S, L_0), \tag{4.5}$$
where for any cut $0 \le L_0 \le S$ on the right subtriangle $T_{\mathrm{top}}(L_0)$ of size $|T_{\mathrm{top}}(L_0)| = (K - p)\frac{L_0(L_0+1)}{2} =: q_0(S) M_{\mathrm{tri}}(S)$, with $c_{\mathrm{shift2}} := K + p - 1 - 2(p^2 - 1) = 2 c_{K,p}$, $\Sigma_1(L_0) := \sum_{L=1}^{L_0} L = \frac{L_0(L_0+1)}{2}$, and $\Sigma_2(S, L_0) := \sum_{L=1}^{L_0} L(S - L) = S\frac{L_0(L_0+1)}{2} - \frac{L_0(L_0+1)(2L_0+1)}{6}$:
$$m_{\mathrm{top}}(L_0) := \frac{K - p}{p^2}\bigl(2K \Sigma_2(S, L_0) + c_{\mathrm{shift2}}\Sigma_1(L_0)\bigr), \qquad m_{\mathrm{bot}}(L_0) := \frac{K - p}{p^3}\Bigl(2K\bigl(\Sigma_2(S, S) - \Sigma_2(S, L_0)\bigr) + c_{\mathrm{shift2}}\bigl(\Sigma_1(S) - \Sigma_1(L_0)\bigr)\Bigr),$$
$$\mathrm{herm}(L_0) := \frac{1}{2}(S - L_0)(K - p)p^2, \qquad E_{s,\mathrm{top}}(L_0) := 2(K - p)\left(A_p(L_0) - \sum_{\ell \ge 1} A_{p^{2\ell}}(L_0)\right)_+, \tag{4.6}$$
and, by the exact identity (4.4d) $\frac{M_{\mathrm{tri}}\lfloor p^2/4\rfloor}{p\theta(D)(J(D)/R(D))R(D)W_{\mathrm{main}}} = \frac{3(1+1/S)\lfloor p^2/4\rfloor}{2 p\theta(D)(J(D)/R(D))R(D)^2}$,
$$\mathrm{cost}_{\mathrm{col}}^{\mathrm{biconj}}(p; S, L_0) := 3\left(1 + \frac{1}{S}\right)q_0(S)\left(1 - \frac{q_0(S)\theta(D)}{2}\right)\frac{p - 1}{p}\frac{J(D)}{R(D)} + \mathbf{1}_{L_0 > 0}\frac{3(1 + 1/S)\lfloor p^2/4\rfloor}{2 p \theta(D)(J(D)/R(D)) R(D)^2}. \tag{4.7}$$

**Theorem 4.3 (Uniform-in-$S \ge S_0$ Biconjugate Small-Prime Lower Envelope $W_{p,\mathrm{tail}}^{\mathrm{biconj}}$).**
*Fix integers $K = 19$, $2 \le p < K$, $S_0 = 13{,}157$, $R_0 = K S_0 = 249{,}983$, $\varepsilon_J = \frac{21}{200}$, and a rank-utilization lower bound $\theta(D) \ge \theta_{\mathrm{lo}} \in (0, 1]$ across all $D \ge 10^6$. Put*
$$\tilde{\varepsilon}_{J,\mathrm{hi}} := \varepsilon_J + \frac{1}{R_0}, \qquad \tilde{\varepsilon}_{J,\mathrm{lo}} := \max\!\left(\frac{1}{R_0},\; \tilde{\varepsilon}_{J,\mathrm{hi}} - \frac{2}{R_0}\right) = \varepsilon_J - \frac{1}{R_0},$$
*so that every $D \ge 10^6$ ($R(D) \ge R_0$) satisfies $\tilde{\varepsilon}_{J,\mathrm{lo}} = \varepsilon_J - \frac{1}{R_0} \le \varepsilon_J - \frac{1}{R(D)} < \frac{\lfloor\varepsilon_J R(D)\rfloor}{R(D)} = \frac{J(D)}{R(D)} \le \varepsilon_J < \tilde{\varepsilon}_{J,\mathrm{hi}}$ (matching lines 583–585 and 1413 of `verify_stark_standalone.py` verbatim). For each $\mathrm{idx} \in \{0, 1, \dots, 200\}$, put $v := \mathrm{idx}/200 \in [0, 1]$, $v_{\mathrm{lo}} := \max(0, v - \frac{1}{S_0})$, and define:*
$$W_{2,\mathrm{lb}} := \frac{1}{K}\left(1 - \frac{1}{S_0^2}\right), \qquad s_{\mathrm{even,lb}} := \sum_{\substack{\ell \ge 1 \\ p^{2\ell} \le S_0}} \max\!\left(0,\; \frac{1}{K p^{2\ell}} - \frac{3}{2 R_0} + \frac{3}{2 R_0 p^{2\ell}}\right), \tag{4.8a}$$
$$q_{0,\mathrm{ub}}(v) := \frac{K - p}{K}\, v \min\!\left(1,\; v + \frac{1}{S_0}\right), \qquad \mathrm{cubic}_{\mathrm{lo}}(v) := v_{\mathrm{lo}}^2(3 - 2 v_{\mathrm{lo}}) - \frac{3}{S_0}, \qquad \mathrm{cubic}_{\mathrm{hi}}(v) := v^2(3 - 2 v) + \frac{3}{S_0}, \tag{4.8b}$$
$$m_{\mathrm{gain,lb}}(v) := \frac{K - p}{K p^2}\max\!\bigl(0, \mathrm{cubic}_{\mathrm{lo}}(v)\bigr) + \frac{K - p}{K p^3}\max\!\left(0, 1 - \mathrm{cubic}_{\mathrm{hi}}(v) - \frac{3}{S_0}\right) + \min\!\left(0, \frac{3(K - p)c_{\mathrm{shift2}}}{2 K^2 p^2 S_0}\right) - \frac{3(K - p)p^2}{2 K^2 S_0^2}, \tag{4.8c}$$
$$\mathrm{extra}_{s,\mathrm{lb}}(v) := \max\!\left(0,\; \frac{K - p}{K^2}\left[\left(\frac{1}{p} - \frac{1}{p^2 - 1}\right)v_{\mathrm{lo}}^3 - \frac{3}{2 S_0}\right]\right), \qquad \mathrm{rem}_{\mathrm{col,ub}} := \frac{3(1 + 1/S_0)\lfloor p^2/4\rfloor}{2 p \theta_{\mathrm{lo}}\tilde{\varepsilon}_{J,\mathrm{lo}} R_0^2}, \tag{4.8d}$$
$$\mathrm{cost}_{\mathrm{col,ub}}(v) := 3\left(1 + \frac{1}{S_0}\right)q_{0,\mathrm{ub}}(v)\left(1 - \frac{q_{0,\mathrm{ub}}(v)\theta_{\mathrm{lo}}}{2}\right)\frac{p - 1}{p}\tilde{\varepsilon}_{J,\mathrm{hi}} + \mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{idx} > 0}\,\mathrm{rem}_{\mathrm{col,ub}}. \tag{4.8e}$$
*Then for every $S = S(D) \ge S_0$ and every $\mathrm{idx} \in \{0, \dots, 200\}$, the integer cut $L_0(S) := \lfloor v S \rfloor \in \{0, \dots, S\}$ satisfies each component inequality $W_2/W_{\mathrm{main}} \ge W_{2,\mathrm{lb}}$, $\frac{2K}{W_{\mathrm{main}}}\sum_{\ell \ge 1}A_{p^{2\ell}}(S) \ge s_{\mathrm{even,lb}}$, $\frac{m_{\mathrm{top}}(L_0)+m_{\mathrm{bot}}(L_0)-\mathrm{herm}(L_0)}{W_{\mathrm{main}}} \ge m_{\mathrm{gain,lb}}(v)$, $\frac{E_{s,\mathrm{top}}(L_0)}{W_{\mathrm{main}}} \ge \mathrm{extra}_{s,\mathrm{lb}}(v)$, and $\mathrm{cost}_{\mathrm{col}}^{\mathrm{biconj}}(p; S, L_0(S)) \le \mathrm{cost}_{\mathrm{col,ub}}(v)$. Consequently, $\frac{2}{W_{\mathrm{main}}}v_p(\det\widetilde{A}_{\mathcal{C}}) \ge W_{p,\mathrm{tail}}^{\mathrm{biconj}}(K, S_0) := \max_{\mathrm{idx}\in\{0,\dots,200\}}\bigl[W_{2,\mathrm{lb}} + s_{\mathrm{even,lb}} + \max(0, m_{\mathrm{gain,lb}}(v) + \mathrm{extra}_{s,\mathrm{lb}}(v)) - \mathrm{cost}_{\mathrm{col,ub}}(v)\bigr]$ uniformly across all $D \ge 10^6$.*

*Proof.*
Fix any $S \ge S_0$ ($R = K S \ge R_0$) and $\mathrm{idx} \in \{0, \dots, 200\}$, put $L_0 := \lfloor v S \rfloor$, and write $v_S := L_0 / S$. Since $v S - 1 < \lfloor v S \rfloor \le v S$ and $L_0 \ge 0$, we have $\max(0, v - \frac{1}{S}) \le v_S \le v$. Since $S \ge S_0$, $\max(0, v - \frac{1}{S}) \ge \max(0, v - \frac{1}{S_0}) = v_{\mathrm{lo}}$, so $v_S \in [v_{\mathrm{lo}}, v] \subset [0, 1]$. We verify the six component inequalities in order:
1. **Leading $s$-weight `W2_norm_lb`:** By (1.9), $\frac{W_2}{W_{\mathrm{main}}} = \frac{1}{K}\bigl(1 - \frac{1}{S^2}\bigr) \ge \frac{1}{K}\bigl(1 - \frac{1}{S_0^2}\bigr) = W_{2,\mathrm{lb}}$ for all $S \ge S_0$.
2. **Even $s$-power sum `s_even_lb`:** For every $m = p^{2\ell} \le S_0 \le S$, Lemma 4.1 gives $\frac{2K}{W_{\mathrm{main}}}A_m(S) \ge \max(0, \frac{1}{Km} - \frac{3}{2R} + \frac{3}{2Rm}) \ge \max(0, \frac{1}{Km} - \frac{3}{2R_0} + \frac{3}{2R_0 m})$ since $m \ge 4 > 1$ and $R \ge R_0$, while any additional terms with $S_0 < p^{2\ell} \le S$ have $A_{p^{2\ell}}(S) \ge 0$ and are dropped conservatively.
3. **Cut fraction `q0_ub`:** Since $L_0 \le S$, we have $v_S + \frac{1}{S} = \frac{L_0 + 1}{S}$. If $L_0 = S$ (so $v = 1$), then $q_0(S) = \frac{K-p}{K} = q_{0,\mathrm{ub}}(1)$. If $L_0 \le S - 1$, then $v_S + \frac{1}{S} \le \min(1, v + \frac{1}{S_0})$ and $\frac{1}{1 + 1/S} < 1$, so
   $$q_0(S) = \frac{(K - p)L_0(L_0+1)}{R(S+1)} = \frac{K - p}{K}\frac{v_S(v_S + 1/S)}{1 + 1/S} \le \frac{K - p}{K}\, v \min\!\left(1,\; v + \frac{1}{S_0}\right) = q_{0,\mathrm{ub}}(v).$$
4. **Biconjugate $r$-gain `m_gain_lb`:** Recall that $x \mapsto \phi(x) := x^2(3 - 2x)$ is strictly increasing on $[0, 1]$ since $\phi'(x) = 6x(1 - x) \ge 0$, so $\phi(v_{\mathrm{lo}}) \le \phi(v_S) \le \phi(v)$. Dividing $6\Sigma_2(S, L_0) = L_0(L_0+1)(3S - 2L_0 - 1)$ by $S^3$ gives the exact algebraic identity
   $$\frac{6\Sigma_2(S, L_0)}{S^3} = \phi(v_S) + \frac{v_S(3 - 3 v_S)}{S} - \frac{v_S}{S^2}. \tag{4.9a}$$
   - *Lower bound (`cubic_lo`):* On $v_S \in [0, 1]$, we have $v_S(3 - 3 v_S) \ge 0$ and $-\frac{v_S}{S^2} \ge -\frac{1}{S^2} > -\frac{3}{S}$, so $\frac{6\Sigma_2(S, L_0)}{S^3} \ge \phi(v_S) - \frac{3}{S} \ge \phi(v_{\mathrm{lo}}) - \frac{3}{S_0} = \mathrm{cubic}_{\mathrm{lo}}(v)$. Separately, since $\Sigma_2(S, L_0) = \sum_{L=1}^{L_0} L(S - L) \ge 0$ is a sum of non-negative integers, combining $\frac{6\Sigma_2(S, L_0)}{S^3} \ge 0$ with $\frac{6\Sigma_2(S, L_0)}{S^3} \ge \mathrm{cubic}_{\mathrm{lo}}(v)$ gives $\frac{6\Sigma_2(S, L_0)}{S^3} \ge \max(0, \mathrm{cubic}_{\mathrm{lo}}(v))$.
   - *Upper bound (`cubic_hi`) and bottom sum:* On $v_S \in [0, 1]$, $v_S(3 - 3 v_S) \le \frac{3}{4} < 3$ and $-\frac{v_S}{S^2} \le 0$, so $\frac{6\Sigma_2(S, L_0)}{S^3} \le \phi(v_S) + \frac{3}{S} \le \phi(v) + \frac{3}{S_0} = \mathrm{cubic}_{\mathrm{hi}}(v)$. Since $\frac{6\Sigma_2(S, S)}{S^3} = 1 - \frac{1}{S^2}$ and $\Sigma_2(S, S) - \Sigma_2(S, L_0) = \sum_{L=L_0+1}^{S} L(S - L) \ge 0$, subtracting gives $\frac{6(\Sigma_2(S, S) - \Sigma_2(S, L_0))}{S^3} \ge 1 - \frac{1}{S^2} - \mathrm{cubic}_{\mathrm{hi}}(v) \ge 1 - \mathrm{cubic}_{\mathrm{hi}}(v) - \frac{3}{S_0}$, and combining with non-negativity gives $\frac{6(\Sigma_2(S, S) - \Sigma_2(S, L_0))}{S^3} \ge \max\bigl(0, 1 - \mathrm{cubic}_{\mathrm{hi}}(v) - \frac{3}{S_0}\bigr)$ (and note that at $\mathrm{idx} = 200$ where $v = 1$ and $L_0 = S$, $\Sigma_2(S, L_0) = \Sigma_2(S, S) = \frac{S(S^2-1)}{6}$ has exact excess $\frac{6\Sigma_2(S, S)}{S^3} - \mathrm{cubic}_{\mathrm{lo}}(1) = \bigl(1 - \frac{1}{S^2}\bigr) - \bigl((1 - \frac{1}{S_0})^2(1 + \frac{2}{S_0}) - \frac{3}{S_0}\bigr) \ge \frac{3}{S_0} - \frac{4}{S_0^2} > \frac{2}{S_0}$).
   - *Explicit inequality for the linear shift $c_{\mathrm{shift2}}$ and Hermite remainder:* Put $C(S, L_0) := \frac{K-p}{W_{\mathrm{main}}}\bigl(\frac{c_{\mathrm{shift2}}}{p^2}\Sigma_1(L_0) + \frac{c_{\mathrm{shift2}}}{p^3}(\Sigma_1(S) - \Sigma_1(L_0))\bigr) = \frac{3(K-p)c_{\mathrm{shift2}}}{2 K^2 p^2 S^3}\bigl(\alpha_p L_0(L_0+1) + \frac{1}{p}S(S+1)\bigr)$ where $\alpha_p = 1 - \frac{1}{p} > 0$, and $H(S, L_0) := \frac{\mathrm{herm}(L_0)}{W_{\mathrm{main}}} = \frac{3(K-p)p^2(S-L_0)}{2 K^2 S^3}$.
     - If $c_{\mathrm{shift2}} \ge 0$ ($p \in \{2, 3\}$), then $C(S, L_0) \ge 0 = \min\bigl(0, \frac{3(K-p)c_{\mathrm{shift2}}}{2 K^2 p^2 S_0}\bigr)$ and $-H(S, L_0) \ge -\frac{3(K-p)p^2}{2 K^2 S^2} \ge -\frac{3(K-p)p^2}{2 K^2 S_0^2}$.
     - If $c_{\mathrm{shift2}} < 0$ ($p \in \{5, 7, 11, 13, 17\}$, where $|c_{\mathrm{shift2}}| = 2(p^2 - 1) - (K + p - 1) < 2 p^2$), then for every $0 \le L_0 \le S - 1$ we have $\alpha_p L_0(L_0+1) + \frac{1}{p}S(S+1) \le \alpha_p(S-1)S + \frac{1}{p}S(S+1) = S^2 - \bigl(1 - \frac{2}{p}\bigr)S < S^2$, which directly yields the explicit inequality
       $$C(S, L_0) \ge \frac{3(K - p)c_{\mathrm{shift2}}}{2 K^2 p^2 S} \ge \frac{3(K - p)c_{\mathrm{shift2}}}{2 K^2 p^2 S_0} = \min\!\left(0, \frac{3(K - p)c_{\mathrm{shift2}}}{2 K^2 p^2 S_0}\right) \qquad (0 \le L_0 \le S - 1), \tag{4.9b}$$
       while $-H(S, L_0) \ge -\frac{3(K-p)p^2}{2 K^2 S_0^2}$. Finally, at the single endpoint $L_0 = S$ ($\mathrm{idx} = 200$), we have $H(S, S) = 0$ and $C(S, S) = \frac{3(K-p)c_{\mathrm{shift2}}}{2 K^2 p^2 S}\bigl(1 + \frac{1}{S}\bigr) \ge \frac{3(K-p)c_{\mathrm{shift2}}}{2 K^2 p^2 S_0} - \frac{3(K-p)|c_{\mathrm{shift2}}|}{2 K^2 p^2 S^2}$, and since $\frac{|c_{\mathrm{shift2}}|}{p^2} < 2 < 25 \le p^2$, we have $-\frac{3(K-p)|c_{\mathrm{shift2}}|}{2 K^2 p^2 S^2} > -\frac{3(K-p)p^2}{2 K^2 S_0^2}$. Thus $C(S, L_0) - H(S, L_0) \ge \min\bigl(0, \frac{3(K-p)c_{\mathrm{shift2}}}{2 K^2 p^2 S_0}\bigr) - \frac{3(K-p)p^2}{2 K^2 S_0^2}$ holds for every $0 \le L_0 \le S$, proving $\frac{m_{\mathrm{top}}(L_0) + m_{\mathrm{bot}}(L_0) - \mathrm{herm}(L_0)}{W_{\mathrm{main}}} \ge m_{\mathrm{gain,lb}}(v)$.
5. **Top-subtriangle odd-$s$ gain `extra_s_lb`:** We first prove the uniform lower bound
   $$A_p(L) \ge \frac{L^3}{6p} - \frac{L^2}{4} \qquad (\forall p \ge 2,\; \forall L \ge 0). \tag{4.9c}$$
   Indeed, for $p \ge 3$, Lemma 4.1 gives $12 p A_p(L) \ge 2 L^3 - 3(p-1)L^2 \ge 2 L^3 - 3 p L^2$, and dividing by $12p$ yields (4.9c). For $p = 2$, writing $L = 2q + r$ with $r = L \bmod 2 \in \{0, 1\}$ in (4.1) gives $q = (L - r)/2$, $4q(2-3) = -2L + 2r$, and $r^2(3 - 2r) = r$, so (4.1) simplifies to the exact identity
   $$24 A_2(L) = 2 L^3 - 3 L^2 - 2 L + 3(L \bmod 2) \qquad (\forall L \ge 0). \tag{4.9d}$$
   Subtracting $24\bigl(\frac{L^3}{12} - \frac{L^2}{4}\bigr) = 2 L^3 - 6 L^2$ from (4.9d) leaves $3 L^2 - 2 L + 3(L \bmod 2)$, which equals $0$ at $L = 0$, $4$ at $L = 1$, and $L(3L - 2) + 3(L \bmod 2) > 0$ for all $L \ge 2$, proving (4.9c) for all $p \ge 2$ and $L \ge 0$. On the upper side, $A_m(L_0) = \sum_{s=0}^{L_0-1}(L_0 - s)\lfloor s/m\rfloor \le \frac{1}{m}\sum_{s=1}^{L_0-1} s(L_0 - s) = \frac{L_0(L_0^2 - 1)}{6m} \le \frac{L_0^3}{6m}$, so summing over $\ell \ge 1$ gives $\sum_{\ell \ge 1}A_{p^{2\ell}}(L_0) \le \frac{L_0^3}{6}\sum_{\ell \ge 1} p^{-2\ell} = \frac{L_0^3}{6(p^2 - 1)}$. Combining this with (4.9c) at $L = L_0 = v_S S$ and dividing $2(K - p)\bigl(A_p(L_0) - \sum_{\ell \ge 1}A_{p^{2\ell}}(L_0)\bigr)$ by $W_{\mathrm{main}} = \frac{K^2 S^3}{3}$ yields, since $v_{\mathrm{lo}} \le v_S \le 1$ and $S \ge S_0$,
   $$\frac{E_{s,\mathrm{top}}(L_0)}{W_{\mathrm{main}}} \ge \max\!\left(0,\; \frac{K - p}{K^2}\left[\left(\frac{1}{p} - \frac{1}{p^2 - 1}\right)v_S^3 - \frac{3 v_S^2}{2 S}\right]\right) \ge \max\!\left(0,\; \frac{K - p}{K^2}\left[\left(\frac{1}{p} - \frac{1}{p^2 - 1}\right)v_{\mathrm{lo}}^3 - \frac{3}{2 S_0}\right]\right) = \mathrm{extra}_{s,\mathrm{lb}}(v). \tag{4.9e}$$
6. **Column-selection charge `cost_col_ub`:** For fixed $\theta \in (0, 1]$, the quadratic $q \mapsto q\bigl(1 - \frac{\theta}{2}q\bigr)$ has derivative $1 - \theta q > 0$ on $q \in [0, 1]$, so it is strictly increasing in $q$ and strictly decreasing in $\theta$. Since $0 \le q_0(S) \le q_{0,\mathrm{ub}}(v) \le \frac{K-p}{K} < 1$, $\theta(D) \ge \theta_{\mathrm{lo}}$, $1 + \frac{1}{S} \le 1 + \frac{1}{S_0}$, and $\frac{J(D)}{R(D)} \le \varepsilon_J < \tilde{\varepsilon}_{J,\mathrm{hi}}$, the main column charge in (4.7) is bounded above by the first term of (4.8e). For the periodic mod-$p$ remainder in (4.7) (which vanishes when $L_0 = 0$, and in particular when $\mathrm{idx} = 0$), since $1 + \frac{1}{S} \le 1 + \frac{1}{S_0}$, $\theta(D) \ge \theta_{\mathrm{lo}}$, $\frac{J(D)}{R(D)} > \varepsilon_J - \frac{1}{R(D)} \ge \varepsilon_J - \frac{1}{R_0} = \tilde{\varepsilon}_{J,\mathrm{lo}}$, and $R(D) \ge R_0$, we have directly
   $$\frac{3(1 + 1/S)\lfloor p^2/4\rfloor}{2 p \theta(D)(J(D)/R(D)) R(D)^2} \le \frac{3(1 + 1/S_0)\lfloor p^2/4\rfloor}{2 p \theta_{\mathrm{lo}}\tilde{\varepsilon}_{J,\mathrm{lo}} R_0^2} = \mathrm{rem}_{\mathrm{col,ub}}, \tag{4.9f}$$
   so $\mathrm{cost}_{\mathrm{col}}^{\mathrm{biconj}}(p; S, L_0(S)) \le \mathrm{cost}_{\mathrm{col,ub}}(v)$ for all $S \ge S_0$. $\square$

## 5. Regime 2 ($[1{,}955, 1{,}000{,}003]$) and Regime 1 ($[3, 1{,}955)$)

### 5.1 Zero-Sieve Positive-Term Coprime Norm Enclosure and Upper-Endpoint $\eta(D_{\mathrm{hi}})$ on $[1{,}955, 1{,}000{,}003]$

On a finite window $[D_{\mathrm{lo}}, D_{\mathrm{hi}}]$ with $D_{\mathrm{lo}} \ge 163$ and $R > m_{D_{\mathrm{lo}}} := (D_{\mathrm{lo}} + 1)/4$, primes $p \in [m_D, R)$ may split or ramify in $\mathcal{O}_K$. Under $h(-D) = 1$, every split or ramified prime $p < R$ is the norm of a primitive element $\alpha = x + y\frac{1+\sqrt{-D}}{2} \in \mathcal{O}_K$, so $p = N_D(x,y) := x^2 + x y + \frac{D+1}{4}y^2$ with $\gcd(|x|, y) = 1$ and $y \ge 1$. By conjugation symmetry $N_D(x, y) = N_D(-x - y, y)$, every split or ramified prime $p < R$ is represented by at least one canonical primitive pair $(x, y)$ with
$$y \ge 1, \qquad 2x + y \ge 0, \qquad \gcd(|x|, y) = 1, \tag{5.1}$$
including the ramified canonical boundary pair $(x, y) = (-1, 2)$ ($2x + y = 0$, $N_D(-1, 2) = D$) whenever $D < R$.

**Proposition 5.1 (Zero-Sieve Positive-Term Coprime Norm Majorant `eu_loss`).**
*Suppose $D_{\mathrm{lo}} \ge 163$ and $\lfloor\sqrt{S}\rfloor < m_{D_{\mathrm{lo}}}$. In `certify_window_zero_sieve`, the baseline $p$-adic sum `padic_norm_iv` (1.16) includes $s$-direction terms only for inert primes $p < m_{D_{\mathrm{lo}}}$ with $p^2 \le S$, and includes for each prime $p \in [K, R)$ ($k = 1$ only, via `fast_sum_Sr_Lp(R, K)`) the baseline $r$-direction term $\frac{2 F(p)}{W_{\mathrm{main}}}\ln p = \frac{S_r(R, K, p)}{K W_{\mathrm{main}}}\ln p$ (higher prime powers $p^k$ with $k \ge 2$ do not enter the $r$-sum of (1.16) at all). Therefore, replacing an inert prime $p \in [m_D, R)$ by a split or ramified prime reduces the normalized $p$-adic sum (1.16) by at most $\frac{S_r(R, K, p)}{K W_{\mathrm{main}}}\ln p \ge 0$. Since $S_r(R, K, d)$ is non-increasing in $d$ and $N_{D_{\mathrm{lo}}}(x, y) \le N_D(x, y) \le N_{D_{\mathrm{hi}}}(x, y)$ for all $D \in [D_{\mathrm{lo}}, D_{\mathrm{hi}}]$, summing the non-negative endpoint majorant*
$$\mathrm{eu\_loss} := \sum_{\substack{y \ge 1,\, 2x + y \ge 0 \\ \gcd(|x|, y) = 1,\, N_{D_{\mathrm{lo}}}(x, y) < R}} \frac{S_r\bigl(R, K, N_{D_{\mathrm{lo}}}(x, y)\bigr)}{K W_{\mathrm{main}}}\ln\min\bigl(R - 1,\, N_{D_{\mathrm{hi}}}(x, y)\bigr) \tag{5.2}$$
*encloses the total split and ramified prime reduction for every $D \in [D_{\mathrm{lo}}, D_{\mathrm{hi}}]$ without factoring $N_D(x, y)$ or sieving composites.*

**Uniform window geometry at $\eta(D_{\mathrm{hi}})$ (matching [12, §8.3]).** As proved in Section 1.2 (1.11) (and already used in [12, §8.3]), for the fixed node count $n$ of a window $[D_{\mathrm{lo}}, D_{\mathrm{hi}}]$ with $D_{\mathrm{lo}} \ge 1{,}955 > 4$, the relative covering parameter $\eta(D) = \frac{\sqrt{D+4}/4}{\sqrt{n\sqrt{D}/(2\pi)}} = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8n}(\sqrt{D} + 4/\sqrt{D})}$ increases strictly with $D$, and $Q_\eta(x) = \bigl(\frac{\sqrt{x}+\eta}{1+\eta}\bigr)^2$ increases strictly with $\eta > 0$ for $x \in [0, 1)$. Consequently, setting $\eta_+ := \eta(D_{\mathrm{hi}})^{\mathrm{hi}}$ (`eta_raw = dd_hi / br_hi` in `certify_window_zero_sieve`) and enclosing $L(D) = \ln(R/\rho_n(D)) - \sigma_2^{(J-1)} \in [\ln(R/\rho_n(D_{\mathrm{hi}})) - \sigma_2^{(J-1)},\, \ln(R/\rho_n(D_{\mathrm{lo}})) - \sigma_2^{(J-1)}]$ and $\ln\rho_n(D) \le \ln\rho_n(D_{\mathrm{hi}})$ provides a valid uniform upper bound $\mathrm{arch\_norm}$ (1.14) across the entire window $[D_{\mathrm{lo}}, D_{\mathrm{hi}}]$.

### 5.2 Regime 1 ($D < 1{,}955$): 6-Prime Rabinowitsch Filter and Negative Controls

For $D < 1{,}955$:
- Direct reduced binary quadratic form enumeration (`|b| <= a <= sqrt(D/3)`) verifies $h(-D) = 1$ for $D \in \{3, 4, 7, 8, 11, 19, 43, 67, 163\}$.
- For $D > 8$, $h(-D) = 1$ implies $D \equiv 3 \pmod 8$ ($2$ is inert since $2 < m_D$) and $(-D)^{(p-1)/2} \equiv -1 \pmod p$ for every odd prime $p < m_D = (D+1)/4$. Checking $p \in \{3, 5, 7, 11, 13\}$ across all $243$ integers $D \in [11, 1{,}955)$ with $D \equiv 3 \pmod 8$ leaves only $\{11, 19, 43, 67, 163\}$ and the single candidate $D = 1{,}747$ ($m_{1747} = 437$).
