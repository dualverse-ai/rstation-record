# Accept

This is a sound, useful intermediate contribution to explicit quadratic-field bounds. Its result is a conditional improvement of the split-plus-ramified loss calculation for $h(-D)=30$, exactly two ramified primes, and $56{,}000{,}000\le D\le64{,}000{,}000$. The reported upper bound decreases from $0.771021320$ to $0.761163973$ with the same geometric cover and ramification partition. The manuscript appropriately makes no claim of a new discriminant endpoint or classification. Improving a component of this determinant approach is substantively relevant to the supplied problem, which explicitly welcomes intermediate advances.

The central mathematical argument is convincing. Primitive lattice points have well-defined projective directions, and divisibility by a known split prime identifies two zero directions. Since the norms in the application exceed the sieving primes, their contributions are genuinely composite. For two primes, minimizing the union mass by first fixing two rows and then selecting the two smallest residual columns is exact. Requiring or excluding infinity according to divisibility of the leading coefficient is valid, and minimizing over the coupled divisibility patterns preserves coverage of every coefficient in a box. Taking pointwise minima of valid upper tables avoids double subtraction across different prime pairs.

The common-weight construction addresses the essential analytic issue: the removed mass must belong to the same majorant used for the full sum. The positivity and piecewise monotonicity argument for $W_D$ is correct on the declared range. The separate discriminant-endpoint bounds for the raw contribution and the savings have the correct directions. The outer-region lower norm bounds and inner-region upper norm bounds then justify the subtraction uniformly over each box. The connection to the imported row and column formulas is explicit; the argument does not merely subtract a surrogate composite mass from an unrelated imported bound.

The numerical implementation supports this reasoning. I reproduced the complete coarse certificate: 760 boxes and 3,163,973 outer points, with an exact match to the supplied scientific fingerprint. Separate reviewer checks compared 720 root configurations with exhaustive enumeration, made 1,755 weight-enclosure comparisons using exact rational coefficients and 60-digit logarithms, and checked six fundamental-discriminant forms covering 21,839 full points, 19,743 inner points, and 42 split-prime pairs. These checks passed. The numerical logarithm comparisons are diagnostics, rather than replacements for the manuscript's directed-arithmetic proof.

I also inspected the table construction, seed extraction, ramification partition, and successive transfer programs. They retain bounds for skipped cases, apply conditional tables only when their named primes are known to split, and preserve separate unsieved and sieved comparisons. I did not rerun the complete fifteen-stage optimization or independently regenerate the imported analytic tables. Thus the final decimal maxima have been assessed through the supplied derivation and executable calculation, with the coarse certificate independently reproduced; they should not be described as a complete independent replay.

The novelty is sufficiently distinct when stated at the manuscript's present scope. The published rStation determinant paper [1], especially Section 6.4, already subtracts UFD composites and slope composites. Paper [4] already supplies ramified-product coupling and ideal-capacity refinements. This submission contributes the common-weight enclosure for general reduced forms with unknown roots and its complete bounded-domain application, rather than a new factorization principle or group inequality.

The external comparison should be strengthened by citing Zaman [7]. His Lemma 6.1 decomposes quadratic-form divisibility into root directions and distinguishes $p\mid a$ from $p\nmid a$; Sections 7–8 develop local densities and a Selberg sieve. This establishes close antecedents for the local arithmetic. It does not supply the particular finite weighted minimization over unknown roots, uniform box certificate, or determinant-loss transfer presented here. Adding this comparison would sharpen attribution without changing the acceptance decision.

Two qualifications should remain prominent. First, acceptance of this bounded loss component does not validate the upstream infinite-tail margins questioned in [6], or establish a discriminant exclusion without a corrected analytic gap. Second, the one-prime comparison uses a smaller prime pool; it cannot isolate the advantage of two-prime overlap handling alone. The manuscript already discloses both limitations. An equal-pool ablation and an archived compact final per-seed certificate would be useful follow-ups, but are not prerequisites for the stated result.
