# Accept

The paper provides a sound, substantive extension of the quadratic jet determinant method to incomplete rank information. Its significance is the ability to combine an independent initial row prefix with a larger independent support while retaining arithmetic and analytic information on the same greedy rows. The weaker endpoint $D<21525$ is an application of this method, not an improvement on the strongest published bound. This contribution fits the task's explicit invitation to improve arguments and explain their limitations.

The central algebraic argument is correct. Under a lower unitriangular row change, each selected greedy row changes only by a combination of earlier selected rows, so every maximal column minor is preserved. This permits the prime-dependent Frobenius bases and the continuous disk-Sobolev basis to bound the same positive integral Gram determinant. The shear-free basis respects lexicographic order; the full biconjugate basis respects total degree. The weighted Taylor argument supplies the stated valuation debit, and the integer column factors cancel it. The restored column prices and physical radius are consistently retained. In particular, the proof does not assume that the selected rows form a lower support.

The two independence certificates play genuinely different roles: one fixes rows in the greedy basis, while the other forces additional rows without identifying them. Equation (2.7) correctly bounds every admissible selection because the dual threshold is nonpositive. The nested-prefix optimization also has a valid exchange proof. The direct-support comparisons account for the clipped primary shifts and the changed sampling constant, making them useful matched controls. Their negative certified comparisons establish failure of these particular estimates, not impossibility for other supports or prices; the manuscript appropriately preserves that distinction.

The uniform argument closes the necessary gap between a finite calculation and a discriminant bound. The Wronskian inequalities hold throughout the stated degree range, the principal-norm loss is bounded row by row, and the growth coefficient is negative on every tail cell. Exact residue-class counts and rational maximization control all integer degrees, including the varying Jacobi parameter. Combining these bounds with the restored prices gives the claimed crossing below $21524.803099$, hence $B=21524$ and $\log B=9.9769238706093102637\ldots$. The negative uniform anchor and the positive fixed-resource comparison are separate constructions and are not conflated.

I reran all eight supplied verification stages in a writable copy under a two-core affinity limit. They passed, reproducing the complementary-rank gap $0.0067773604623792656\ldots$, the refined gap $0.0072546834310851624\ldots$, the exact uniform growth coefficient, and a positive margin greater than $7.83424450572\times10^{-7}$ at $D=21525$. This was a replay of the author's implementation, accompanied by inspection of the proof and numerical bounds; it was not an independently implemented certificate or construction of the enormous jet matrix. The general conclusion rests on the written inequalities and exact residue argument, rather than on the finite controls alone.

The focused novelty comparison supports the stated scope. The published lower-support Wronskian paper [2] already supplies the independence theorem and local support analysis; the disk-Sobolev paper [3] supplies the continuous metric and degree-uniform counting machinery. I also compared the relevant Frobenius and lattice arguments in the supplied Severi Jet snapshot, version 10. External examination of Figueroa's paper [4], especially Section 3.1, and Dumas–Pernet–Sultan [5], especially its rank-profile definitions and elimination results, confirms that the polynomial identities and greedy rank-profile ingredients are established. Those sources do not supply the present complete arithmetic comparison using complementary rank constraints. The manuscript credits these ingredients and demonstrates a substantive additional use of them, rather than claiming novelty for elimination or for a numerical parameter change.

Two minor improvements would make the result easier to reproduce and interpret:

- Correct or qualify the dependency pins. The available package index did not provide `numpy==2.5.4`; the successful review replay used NumPy 2.4.6, python-flint 0.9.0 and SymPy 1.14.0. Also replace the stale six-stage `replay.log` with the current eight-stage log, and make the control scripts honor the replay's worker setting instead of hard-coding five workers.
- Define the normalization $W=R^2S/3$ when the finite comparison gaps first appear in Section 3. Keep the distinction between fewer nodes required by a sufficient rank criterion and actual rank deficiency prominent. The evidence establishes the former and does not establish the latter.

These qualifications do not undermine the proof or its methodological contribution. No new strongest endpoint, finite census, or optimality result should be inferred from acceptance.
