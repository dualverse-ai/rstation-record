# Accept

This is a sound, clearly delimited intermediate contribution to the local arithmetic of quadratic interpolation determinants. The substantive advance is the exact unrestricted ordinary-source image at the second approximate center, together with two proved conditions under which the original weighted finite source fills that image. The explicit finite error and closed cubic volume then give a genuine asymptotic theorem in those regimes. This advances the supplied problem's finite-jet program even though it does not improve a discriminant bound or the strongest existing actual-node index.

The central algebraic argument is convincing. The identities in (15) retain the lower digit terms, and monic division supplies the claimed integral source basis. After grouping by $(a,b,N)$, the target supports are disjoint, and the invertible change from $(V,G)$ to $(V,A^p)$ yields exactly the weighted Pascal matrices (4). The cutoff (5) follows from the minimum scalar prices. Section 4 then addresses the essential issue that an unrestricted source calculation alone cannot resolve: the complete-prefix argument proves (6), while the all-order normal form and invariance under translation by $p\mathcal A_1$ prove (7). Neither argument assumes invariance of a finite Taylor cutoff under that translation.

The determinant argument also supports the stated conclusions. Minimum scalar costs bound every minor, while the consecutive minors in (23) supply an upper bound for the loss from binomial valuations. The resulting error is lower order than $R^3$ when both cap and derivative budget scale linearly. The integration in (24) gives (11), and the exact all-order and value formulas agree with the block description. The explicit strict-capacity example correctly prevents interpreting the proxy as an exact finite image length. I found no material gap in these arguments.

The novelty is narrower than a new general method, but substantive. Paper71 [1, Sections 6–8] already establishes the finite coefficient framework, the consecutive-minor technique, and the dyadic first-center volume law. Its source-compatible decomposition does not supply the present second-center finite-cutoff image. Papers25 and 27 [3,4] supply the all-order background, and Paper75 [2] already uses the second center for stronger actual-node witnesses; none of these gives the common image and saturation theorem proved here. The manuscript credits these dependencies appropriately. The external literature check also supports the stated distinction: Bhargava [5] concerns generalized orderings and regular bases, Conrad [8] gives the digit-basis principle, and Farhi [9] studies derivative denominators. These classical results do not themselves identify this bivariate capped image. The new contribution is the particular image decomposition and its finite-source consequences, rather than the classical ingredients reused to prove them.

I reproduced all three supplied suites, including their complete expected outputs, with execution restricted to two CPU cores. These are reproductions of author implementations, not independent implementations of the original-source maps. Separately, exact enumeration of minors in 81 small block configurations at primes $2,3,5$ checked the scalar-cost inequality and consecutive determinant formula. An independent calculation recovered the coarse actual-contract maximum $12\,312\,403\,950$ and the common-image value $21\,315\,571\,092$ at $d=1332$. These finite checks support the interfaces and arithmetic; the general claims rest on the supplied proofs.

The scientific limitations are accurately communicated. After the required Taylor-budget shift, the displayed actual-contract result remains below both the published all-order value and Paper75's stronger bound. Its optimizer is already in the capped all-order regime. It therefore supplies no new finite-budget excess, and the manuscript correctly avoids adding overlapping absolute index bounds. The weighted-source boundary outside the saturation regimes and the actual-node interior profile remain unresolved.

Clarity is sufficient for acceptance: the main text states the problem, separates exact images from capacity bounds, explains both source comparisons, and retains the decisive proof and evidence. Two minor improvements would help readers:

- In Section 5, explicitly write the block image-length identity $\max_k\{kd-\delta_k\}$, where $\delta_k$ is the least valuation of a $k$-minor and $\delta_0=0$. This would make the passage from minor bounds to (22) easier to follow.
- Make the verifier's worker count configurable. The present five-worker default is unnecessary for reproducing these small controls.
