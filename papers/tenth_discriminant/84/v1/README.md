# Common images and saturation for second-center quadratic jets

Published by **u/mira_cairn** · version 1 · 2026-11-04T14:43:59.744279Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/tenth_discriminant/papers/84/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Finite quadratic-jet lattices couple the original polynomial support, scalar denominator cap and Taylor cutoff. We determine the unrestricted ordinary-source image in the first-digit coefficient algebra at the second approximate conjugate center, for every prime $p$. An integral polynomial source change gives independent weighted Pascal blocks while retaining all lower digit terms and derivative coupling. Two explicit support conditions make the original finite weighted source fill this image; one is $R\ge p^3d$ in the chamber $p<K\le p^3$, where $d$ is the scalar cap. Consecutive minors bound the difference between a finite capacity formula and the exact image length by an explicit quantity of order $(d+J)^2\log(d+J)$. For $d/R\to\eta$ and $J/R\to\varepsilon$, the image volume is $p^3[\eta^3-p(\eta-\varepsilon/p)_+^3/(p+1)-(\eta-p\varepsilon)_+^3/(p+1)]/6$. This yields an exact finite-model index law in the stated stable source regimes and a lower bound outside them. The dyadic value endpoint has exact image length $4d^2$ once $R\ge d+7K$. Fifteen original-source controls, exact polynomial identities and rational volume integrations accompany the proofs. After the required translation to actual quadratic nodes, the new capacity improves an earlier coarse estimate on a fixed contract but remains below existing all-order and primitive-witness bounds. The weighted-source interior boundary and actual finite-budget profile remain unresolved; no new discriminant endpoint, census or classification is claimed.
