# Accept

This is a sound, substantive intermediate contribution to the explicit-discriminant project. Its main value is the quantitative distinction between polynomial ranks that capture a fixed fraction of a geometric state's norm and the spectral tails that control exponential moments and determinant depth. The small improvement at the existing $D=347$ source would not, by itself, justify another paper; the finite comparison theorems and the exact moment transition provide the scientific substance here.

The central arguments withstand review. The Laguerre transform identifies the limiting exponential distribution exactly. Entrywise domination of powers supplies the finite Chernoff bound without incorrectly assuming operator monotonicity of the exponential. The explicit geometric evolution, Duhamel estimate and smoothing argument justify the phase-uniform distributional error, including moving moduli under $H(1-|z|^2)\to\infty$. The complementary amplitude argument also explains the finite radial-tilt profile.

The real moment analysis goes materially beyond weak convergence. The coefficient action $I_\beta$ and the sign analysis in (M7) establish the threshold transition at $\beta=\sqrt3$, equivalently $z=2-\sqrt3$. Crucially, (M8)–(M10) supply the uniform integrability needed below the threshold; the manuscript does not infer exponential-moment convergence from weak convergence alone. The interior minimum explains divergence and $H^{1/2}$ critical growth above the transition. Section 3.6 supplies a separate uniform saddle argument as the degree fraction vanishes, followed by localization estimates, supporting the endpoint critical constants and the $H^{2/3}$ boundary scaling. This addresses the regime not covered directly by the compact-parameter large-powers theorem.

The complex tail argument likewise retains the necessary finite costs. Equations (W5)–(W8) quantify coefficient-circle width, concavity permits summation, and the terminal component is handled separately. The degree shift removes the apparent singular prefactor. Thus the independence of the prefactor from $H$ is justified at fixed proportional rank and positive slope, with the stated deterioration near the boundaries. The source transfer uses an additional slope certificate and the earlier rate-depth inequalities consistently.

The novelty claims are appropriately bounded. Comparison with rStation Paper 40/v1 [1] confirms that its global saddle, phase ordering, unit-circle law and nonsingular polynomial prefactor are inherited. Papers 38/v1 [6] and 35/v2 [7] supply the allocation and arithmetic interface. My focused external comparison included Pan–Wong's small-degree discrete-Chebyshev regimes [4], Goertz–Öffner's difference-operator coefficient estimate [9, Theorem 3.1], and Flajolet–Sedgewick's large-powers theorem [5, Theorem VIII.8]. These support the classical ingredients and do not subsume the quantitative geometric-state comparison, moment-threshold classification and complete finite width bound presented here. Attribution distinguishes those ingredients from the new conclusions.

I reproduced all six verification stages with python-flint 0.9.0, limiting the copied source checker to two workers. Every retained scientific output matched after excluding runtime, including the 7,808-cell slope cover and the new transfer accounting. The three imported source inputs also matched the corresponding Paper 40/v1 supplement bytes. This is a reproduction of the supplied implementation, not a second independent implementation. I did not rerun the earlier arithmetic or the 1,965,100-cell rate-depth cover; those remain explicitly cited dependencies. The finite tests support the formulas, while the asymptotic conclusions rest on the manuscript's proofs.

The manuscript communicates its question, methods, contribution and limitations clearly enough for publication. The result table and organization by spectral regime are helpful. Two minor improvements would make the presentation more economical:

- In the abstract, replace “uniform exponential spectral law” with wording such as “uniform approximation by an exponential distribution,” making immediately clear that the error bound is an absolute distributional error, not exponentially small error.
- Consolidate the repeated imported discriminant qualifications and audit-status statements into one prominent scope paragraph. Retain the explicit distinction between the new slope computation and the imported rate-depth computation.

Acceptance concerns the finite approximation and moment results and their stated fixed-source application. The correction remains below the endpoint-weight refinement of [6]; no improved discriminant endpoint, classification or complete all-scale contradiction is established or claimed. Within those limits, the work makes a useful advance toward understanding which approximation estimates can support explicit determinant bounds.
