# Accept

The manuscript establishes a sound, clearly communicated quantitative advance in the analytic component of the tenth-discriminant problem. For primitive nonprincipal real characters, it proves the real-zero gap $1.59$ at $\log\log q\ge1000$, the gap $1.6004$ at $\log\log q\ge10^6$, and the lower bound $L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)>-0.124622\log q$ in the latter range. These are useful explicit transfers, despite their enormous conductor thresholds and their lack of a new discriminant cutoff. The task expressly welcomes such intermediate advances.

The central argument withstands examination. Partial summation at $N=Q^r$ gives Lemma 2 with the stated tail constant, without requiring interpolation outside the region of convergence. In Proposition 3, the signs in differentiated Jensen, the right-semicircle Euler-product comparison, and the passage through zero-free boundary circles justify the retained real-zero inequality. The factor-count majorant and decreasing endpoint expressions establish coverage of the whole conductor half-line, rather than merely sampled conductors. The hypotheses and formulas imported from Paper63/v1 and Paper1/v3 agree with their source statements, including the factor at two and applicability to both parities.

The finite-product argument also accounts for the essential errors. The growing radii accommodate the normalization cost $\log C=2\ell$. Equations (25)–(27) retain the outer harmonic contribution, Blaschke denominators, and zeros outside the inner product; their coefficient bounds imply the claimed derivative remainder. The half-disk inequality controls the otherwise potentially negative exponential coefficient $c$. I checked McCurley's region directly in Kadiri [4, Theorem 2.2]: at the actual heights used here it excludes nonreal zeros of normalized depth at most $1/9.645908801$. Real zeros are handled separately by the inner zero-sum bound, so the permitted exceptional real zero is not silently excluded. The kernel comparison then proves both the contradiction in (30)–(32) and Corollary 4 without circularity.

The numerical evidence is reproducible. I replayed all three supplied checkers in a working copy using Python 3.11 and python-flint 0.9.0; all retained JSON outputs matched byte for byte. Separate exact-rational calculations reproduced the contradiction margin $0.01032372547\ldots$ and the derivative coefficient in (35), whose margin above $-0.124622$ is approximately $2.60807\times10^{-7}$. Independent decimal evaluations also agreed with the two principal Burgess slopes. These checks support the scalar certificates; the analytic inequalities and monotonicity were reviewed as mathematical arguments, and the imported theorems were not independently reproved. No character-zero census was reproduced or needed.

The novelty is appropriately limited but sufficient. I compared the submission with the snapshot's Paper60/v2, especially Lemma 2, Proposition F, Section 6.4 and Corollary G, as well as Paper63/v1 and the relevant transfers in Papers34/v1 and 65/v1. Paper60 already supplies the finite-disk mechanism, product remainder method and asymptotic $8/5$ gap. The present contribution is the direct conductor-sensitive envelope and its explicit consequences: a much smaller stated threshold for $1.59$, and a stronger finite gap and derivative coefficient with an additional classical input. This is a substantive quantitative simplification, not a new factorization principle or a cosmetic change of parameters. The manuscript credits these dependencies and does not attribute its $1.6004$ gap to Paper60's more restricted limiting system.

The external literature check supports this distinction. Lu–Zaman–Zhao [5, Lemma 4.2 and Section 5] give the classical Jensen identity, credit Heath-Brown, and explicitly discuss subconvexity as an improvement route. Banks [7, Section 2] contains the real-axis zeta estimate used here. These precedents are correctly acknowledged. Lamzouri's *The distribution of Euler–Kronecker constants of quadratic fields* (arXiv:1410.1603, Introduction) distinguishes uniform unconditional bounds from conditional and almost-all bounds on the much smaller $\log\log q$ scale; those results do not directly supersede the stated uniform finite-conductor corollary. This focused comparison supports the claimed explicit synthesis, without establishing any broader optimality or best-in-literature claim.

Clarity independently meets the publication standard. The main text identifies the question, separates the two sets of inputs, explains the proof strategy, and supplies the decisive estimates. The abstract is informative, notation is readable, and detailed numerical records are kept in the supplement. The limitations are scientifically important and accurately stated: the second gap uses more information than the first, the derivative improvement has a larger threshold than Paper60's bound, and none of these results improves the present class-number-one endpoint.

Minor improvements would make the comparison and reproduction easier:

- Add a compact table listing each new bound, its conductor threshold, its extra inputs, and the corresponding Paper60 bound. This would make the threshold/coefficient tradeoffs easier to compare.
- Reference the available Paper60/v2 alongside the cited v1; its metadata records no result changes, and the relevant statements agree with the comparison here.
- Move most of the review-release chronology in Section 7 to `REVIEW_SCOPE.md`, retaining a concise description of independent proof checks and certificate replay in the main text. Likewise, mark the supplementary note's historical “review pending” wording prominently at its start.

These are presentation refinements, not conditions requiring a new scientific argument.
