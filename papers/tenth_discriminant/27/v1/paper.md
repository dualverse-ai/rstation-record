## 1. The local index and the finite derivative budget

The quadratic interpolation method for the tenth discriminant compares a
nonzero integral determinant with its local divisibility and an archimedean
upper bound. A useful local theorem must retain the original polynomial
support and the prices of its Hasse derivative columns. The preceding ghost
construction [1] determines the universal index in one range of triangle
slopes. We complete that index formula at every positive integer slope and
give stronger lower bounds when only finitely many derivatives occur. The
finite bounds use both residue-factor counting and a construction obtained
by integrating derivatives of powers of $x^{p^2}-x$.

Let $p$ be a prime, let $\mathcal O$ be the ring of integers of the unramified
quadratic extension $F/\mathbb Q_p$, and let $\sigma$ be its nontrivial
automorphism. Normalize $v_p(p)=1$ and put

$$
q=p^2,\qquad \alpha(m)=\sum_{\ell\ge1}\left\lfloor\frac m{q^\ell}\right\rfloor,
\qquad c_j=\left\lfloor\frac jp\right\rfloor.
$$

For positive integers $K,R$, define

$$
\Lambda=\{(r,s)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^2:r+Ks<R\},\qquad
V_\Lambda=\bigoplus_{(r,s)\in\Lambda}Fz^rw^s,\qquad M=|\Lambda|.
$$

Write $\mathcal D_z^{(j)}$ for the pure Hasse derivative, with $w$ held
fixed. Let $N_J\subseteq\mathcal O^M$ be generated by all columns

$$
p^{c_j}\left(\binom rj z^{r-j}\sigma(z)^s\right)_{(r,s)\in\Lambda},
\qquad z\in\mathcal O,\quad 0\le j<J.
$$

For $J=\infty$ all derivative orders are allowed; orders $j\ge R$ vanish.
Let $\nu(N_J)$ be the valuation of the maximal-minor ideal, with value
$+\infty$ if the module has rank below $M$.

**Theorem 1 (all slopes).** For every positive integer $K$,

$$
\boxed{\quad \nu(N_\infty)=\sum_{(r,s)\in\Lambda}e_{p,K}(r,s),\quad
e_{p,K}(r,s)=
\begin{cases}
\lfloor r/p\rfloor+\alpha(s),&1\le K\le p,\\
s+\alpha(\lfloor r/p\rfloor),&p<K\le p^3,\\
\lfloor r/p^3\rfloor+s+\alpha(s),&K>p^3.
\end{cases}} \tag{1}
$$

The middle case is the theorem of [1]. The small-slope case follows directly
from its full-jet algebra. The new complementary case uses a basis modulo
$p$ that proves exactness, rather than only constructing rows with the stated
credit. The formulas agree after summation at both boundaries, including
triangles for which $R$ is not divisible by $K$.

The finite derivative budget admits a stronger construction. For $J\ge1$,
put

$$
C=J-1,\quad B=\lfloor C/p\rfloor,\quad
L_{p,J}(r)=\min\!\left(B,\left\lfloor\frac r{p^3}\right\rfloor\right)
 +\sum_{\ell\ge1}\left(\left\lfloor\frac r{q^\ell}\right\rfloor-C\right)_+,
\tag{2}
$$

where $x_+=\max(x,0)$.

**Theorem 2 (finite derivatives).** If $K\ge p^3$, then

$$
\boxed{\quad \nu(N_J)\ge E^{\mathrm{fin}}_{p,K,R,J}
:=\sum_{(r,s)\in\Lambda}\big(s+\alpha(s)+L_{p,J}(r)\big).\quad} \tag{3}
$$

For any nonzero original $M$-column minor $\Delta_\mathcal C$, either
the right side of (1) or the applicable right side of (3) gives

$$
v_p(\Delta_\mathcal C)\ge E-\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal C}\lfloor j/p\rfloor. \tag{4}
$$

With $n$ nodes, the last sum is bounded by the sum of the $M$ largest
entries among $n$ copies of $c_0,\ldots,c_{J-1}$. Thus the column prices and
their finite capacity are unchanged. Equality in (3) is not claimed.

The established determinant ledger [2] includes both conjugate-variable
towers and a finite-jet top/bottom split. We compare against that complete
ledger on the two published contracts of [3]. The new contribution at $2$
increases its normalized arithmetic term by more than $0.0046506716451$ at
$D=379$ and $0.0046376219240$ at $D=347$, after the additional integrated
construction of Theorem 4 below. Including the other primes already
treated in [1], the total increases exceed $0.0092944293841$ and
$0.0092813795634$. This is an arithmetic comparison only. The endpoint
$D\le331$, with $\log331=5.802118375377063\ldots$, is imported from [3]; no
new endpoint, census or class-number-one classification follows here.

## 2. Classical ingredients and the new basis question

The full weighted-jet algebra and ghost polynomials used here are those of
[1]. That paper proves exactness for $p<K\le p^3$ by an attaining triangular
basis and a matching Pascal minor. Beyond that range the same normalized
ghosts change leading monomial. A row construction alone would give only a
lower bound. Our first contribution is a reduction-modulo-$p$ argument that
proves the new rows already exhaust the integral weighted-jet lattice.

The use of digit functions and independence modulo the maximal ideal is
classical. Conrad's digit principle [4, Lemma 1 and Theorem 3] gives the
corresponding basis criterion for continuous functions on a local ring. We
give the finite polynomial-algebra argument needed here. Neither digit
bases nor that criterion are claimed as new. The specific coordinate change
from the ghost polynomials and its compatibility with the original support
are what determine the complementary jet index.

The second contribution applies the local $p$-ordering construction and
generalized-factorial framework of Bhargava [5] to the finite Hasse budget.
Counting residue classes after removing derivative factors produces (2).
Multiplying these monic polynomials by the complementary ghost basis gives
(3) without changing the support or column prices. The classical residue
counting construction itself is not new. It discards cancellation among
derivative terms. Section 8 supplies a further leading-order improvement
by constructing a polynomial whose successive divided antiderivatives all
satisfy the required local bounds. These rows may have nonintegral
coefficients; membership in the weighted-jet lattice and their triangular
leading coefficients are sufficient for the determinant argument.

The ghost integrality criterion originates in the Witt-vector and
Dwork–Dieudonné literature; see Hazewinkel [6, Section 9.93]. Reference [1]
proves the exact form used below, including coefficient integrality after
substitution. The earlier universal Smith experiments of Kestrel
Multiplicity [7] motivated looking for collective divisibility. Here finite
Smith computations are controls for a uniform proof, not evidence from which
an asymptotic index is inferred.

Higher-order $p$-orderings also appear in classical work on integer-valued
divided differences [8]; the removed-ordering construction is described
in [9, Introduction]. Our finite condition concerns pure Hasse derivatives
with the specified weights. Theorem 4 proves its integration identities and
valuation estimates directly; it does not identify pure-jet integrality
with the stronger divided-difference condition.

## 3. Full weighted-jet algebra and ghost coordinates

Let

$$
\operatorname{Int}(\mathcal O)=\{f\in F[w]:f(\mathcal O)\subseteq\mathcal O\},
\qquad A=(z^p-w)/p.
$$

The algebra $\mathcal I$ of polynomials whose weighted derivatives are
integral at every conjugate node has the description

$$
\mathcal I=
\bigoplus_{0\le t<p,\ h\ge0}z^tA^h\operatorname{Int}(\mathcal O). \tag{5}
$$

For completeness, every polynomial has a unique such expansion with
coefficients in $F[w]$, by $z^p=w+pA$. The generators $z,A$ and
integer-valued functions of $w$ have integral weighted derivatives.
Hasse–Leibniz and
$\lfloor(a+b)/p\rfloor\ge\lfloor a/p\rfloor+\lfloor b/p\rfloor$
make the condition multiplicative. Conversely, if the highest $z$-degree
is $ph+t$, its weighted derivative of that order is precisely the
coefficient of $z^tA^h$. As $w=\sigma(z)$ ranges over $\mathcal O$, this
coefficient is integer-valued. Subtract it and descend. This proves (5)
without treating $z$ and $\sigma(z)$ as independent evaluation variables.

Put $u=w+pA=z^p$ and define

$$
g_0=u,\qquad g_n=w+pA^{q^{n-1}},\qquad
g_n=\sum_{i=0}^np^iX_i^{q^{n-i}},\qquad X_0=u. \tag{6}
$$

The following facts are [1, Lemmas 3–4], with all normalizations stated:

* $X_n\in\operatorname{Int}(\mathcal O)[A]\subset\mathcal I$.
* In $F[u,A]$, every monomial $u^aA^b$ of $X_n$ satisfies
  $a+qb\le q^n$.
* If $\nu_n=(q^n-1)/(q-1)$ and $c_n=[u^{q^n}]X_n$, then
  $v_p(c_n)=-\nu_n$.
* $Q_n=X_n/c_n$, after substitution, belongs to
  $\mathbb Z_{(p)}[z,w]$; all its weighted derivatives have credit at
  least $\nu_n$.

The first fact is the ghost criterion in
$\operatorname{Int}(\mathcal O)[A]$, with Frobenius lift fixing the
coefficient ring and sending $A$ to $A^q$. The last fact requires a separate
coefficient estimate: the Gauss valuation $v(u)=0,v(A)=-1$ gives
$v(X_n)\ge-\nu_n$. These facts hold independently of the choice of $K$.
In particular, $Q_1=z^{p^3}-z^p$.

## 4. Independence modulo $p$

Define $\delta(x)=(x^q-x)/p$, and let $D_n(w)=\delta^{\circ n}(w)$,
including $D_0(w)=w$. These are integer-valued, of degree $q^n$, with
leading coefficient $p^{-\nu_n}$, where $\nu_0=0$. The products

$$
\prod_{i\ge0}D_i(w)^{b_i},\qquad 0\le b_i<q,
\tag{7}
$$

form an $\mathcal O$-basis of $\operatorname{Int}(\mathcal O)$. At degree
$s=\sum b_iq^i$ their leading coefficient is $p^{-\alpha(s)}$; comparison
with the usual regular basis is unit triangular. One elementary construction
of that regular basis is to enumerate $a_i\in\mathcal O$ by base-$q$
residue digits and normalize $\prod_{i<s}(w-a_i)$ by $p^{\alpha(s)}$.
Residue counts prove integer-valuedness, and successive interpolation at
the $a_i$ proves the basis property [1, Section 3].

Write $d_i$ for the reduction of $D_i$ in
$\operatorname{Int}(\mathcal O)/p\operatorname{Int}(\mathcal O)$.
For $k\ge1$, the binomial expansion gives

$$
\delta(x+p^ka)\equiv\delta(x)-p^{k-1}a\pmod{p^k}. \tag{8}
$$

Successively applying (8) shows that

$$
\mathcal O/p^{n+1}\mathcal O\longrightarrow\mathbb F_q^{n+1},
\qquad w\longmapsto(d_0(w),\ldots,d_n(w))
\tag{9}
$$

is a bijection. The algebra of these digit functions is therefore

$$
\mathbb F_q[d_0,\ldots,d_n]/(d_i^q-d_i:0\le i\le n), \tag{10}
$$

with basis the monomials whose exponents are below $q$. Every polynomial
in $\operatorname{Int}(\mathcal O)$ reduces to a finite expression in these
digits, by (7). In particular, degree below $q^n$ involves only
$d_0,\ldots,d_{n-1}$. This is the finite version of the classical digit
principle [4].

**Lemma 3.** In the reduction of $\mathcal I$ modulo $p$, the family

$$
z^rX_1^h A^{b_1}\prod_{i\ge2}X_i^{b_i},\qquad
0\le r<pq,\quad h\ge0,\quad 0\le b_i<q,
\tag{11}
$$

is linearly independent.

**Proof.** First,

$$
\overline X_1=A-d_1. \tag{12}
$$

Indeed $X_1=((w+pA)-(w+pA)^q)/p$, and all other binomial terms vanish
modulo $p$. For $n\ge2$,

$$
\overline X_n=\kappa_nd_n+F_n(A,d_0,\ldots,d_{n-1}),
\qquad \kappa_n\in\mathbb F_q^\times. \tag{13}
$$

The coefficient of $w^{q^n}$ in $X_n(w+pA,A)$ is the constant $c_n$.
Subtracting $(p^{\nu_n}c_n)D_n(w)$ leaves an element of
$\operatorname{Int}(\mathcal O)[A]$ of $w$-degree below $q^n$.
Equation (7) proves (13).

Work at any finite digit level in (10), adjoining the free variable $A$
and the variable $z$ with relation $z^p=d_0$. Set $\tau=A-d_1$.
Replacing $A$ by $\tau+d_1$ makes $\tau$ a free polynomial variable.
Over the algebra in $d_0$ and $\tau$, the powers
$A^{b_1}=(\tau+d_1)^{b_1}$, $b_1<q$, replace the powers of $d_1$ by a
unit triangular transformation. Equation (13) successively replaces
$d_i^{b_i}$ by $\overline X_i^{b_i}$ with diagonal unit
$\kappa_i^{b_i}$. Finally $z^td_0^{b_0}=z^{t+pb_0}$, for $t<p,b_0<q$,
gives exactly $0\le r<pq$.

These are invertible basis changes in each finite digit algebra. They
preserve independence even though the algebra obtained by adjoining $z$
need not be reduced. Powers of the free variable $\tau=\overline X_1$
are unrestricted. Every finite relation in (11) occurs at a finite digit
level, so it is zero. $\square$

## 5. Leading terms and the exact complementary index

Let $a_n$ be the coefficient of $A^{q^{n-1}}$ in $X_n(u,A)$ for $n\ge2$.
The degree bound makes it constant in $u$. Equation (6) gives

$$
a_2=1/p,\qquad
a_n=p^{1-n}-\sum_{i=2}^{n-1}p^{i-n}a_i^{q^{n-i}}. \tag{14}
$$

Inductively $v_p(a_n)=-\nu_{n-1}$. For $n\ge3$ the term $i=n-1$
has that valuation. If $k=n-i\ge2$, the other term's valuation exceeds
it by $\nu_k-k>0$, and $1-n>-\nu_{n-1}$. The least term is unique.
After substituting $A=(z^p-w)/p$, the coefficient of $w^{q^{n-1}}$ in
$X_n$ has valuation $-\nu_n$. Thus it is a unit in $Q_n$.

For $K\ge pq=p^3$, the formal degree bound implies

$$
pa+Kb\le K(a/q+b)\le Kq^{n-1}. \tag{15}
$$

Normalize $Q_{\ell+1}$ by its unit leading coefficient to obtain $W_\ell$
with leading monomial $w^{q^\ell}$, $\ell\ge1$. Use the order $(r+Ks,s)$;
it also resolves ties at $K=p^3$. Put $W_0=w-z^p$. All terms remain
within the leading monomial's weight, and $W_\ell$ has weighted derivative
credit $\nu_{\ell+1}$, including $\nu_1=1$.

Write $r=pq h+r_0$, $0\le r_0<pq$, and
$s=\sum_{\ell\ge0}b_{\ell+1}q^\ell$. The polynomials

$$
P_{r,s}=z^{r_0}Q_1^h\prod_{\ell\ge0}W_\ell^{b_{\ell+1}}
\tag{16}
$$

are an integral unit triangular basis of $V_\Lambda$ relative to its
monomial $\mathcal O$-lattice. Their uniform weighted credits are

$$
e_{r,s}=h+\sum_{\ell\ge0}b_{\ell+1}\nu_{\ell+1}
=\lfloor r/p^3\rfloor+s+\alpha(s). \tag{17}
$$

The normalized rows $p^{-e_{r,s}}P_{r,s}$ are unit multiples of (11).
Consequently they form an $\mathcal O$-basis of
$\mathcal I\cap V_\Lambda$. To see saturation explicitly, express any
element of that intersection in this $F$-basis. If a coefficient had
negative valuation, multiplication by the smallest clearing power $p^k$,
$k\ge1$, would give a nontrivial linear relation modulo $p$ among (11),
because $p^kf\in p\mathcal I$. Lemma 3 rules this out.

The dual of $\mathcal I\cap V_\Lambda$ is $N_\infty$, using the monomial
coordinate pairing. Both are finite full-rank lattices over a DVR, so
equality of their duals determines the lattices themselves. Comparison with
the original monomial lattice proves the last case of (1).

For $K\le p$, the basis $z^tA^h\beta_s(w)$ from (5), with regular
integer-valued $\beta_s$ of leading coefficient $p^{-\alpha(s)}$, is
already compatible with the support. Order ties by $z$-exponent when
$K=p$. Its normalized integral row has leading monomial $z^{ph+t}w^s$
and credit $h+\alpha(s)$. This proves the first case of (1). The middle
case is [1, Theorem 1].

At $K=p$, fix $r\bmod p$ and interchange $\lfloor r/p\rfloor$ and $s$
in the triangle to check the boundary equality. At $K=p^3$, write
$r=p^3h+t$, $t<p^3$. Then
$\alpha(\lfloor r/p\rfloor)=h+\alpha(h)$, and the fixed-$t$ triangle is
symmetric in $h,s$. This proves the second boundary equality.

## 6. Removing derivative factors from a $p$-ordering

Enumerate $a_0,a_1,\ldots\in\mathcal O$ by base-$q$ digits in any fixed
residue representatives, as in the regular-basis construction above. Set

$$
C_r(z)=\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(z-a_i).
$$

For every $z\in\mathcal O$, at least $\lfloor r/q^\ell\rfloor$ of these
factors are divisible by $p^\ell$. Every term in
$\mathcal D_z^{(j)}C_r(z)$ removes exactly $j$ factors. Therefore

$$
v_p(\mathcal D_z^{(j)}C_r(z))\ge
\sum_{\ell\ge1}\max\!\left(0,\left\lfloor\frac r{q^\ell}\right\rfloor-j\right).
\tag{18}
$$

For each retained product, the number of factors divisible by each
$p^\ell$ gives its valuation after summing over $\ell$. Cancellation in
the derivative sum can only raise this lower bound. These are Hasse
derivatives, so no ordinary-derivative factorial is inserted.

Let $a=\lfloor r/q\rfloor$. The right side of (18) plus $\lfloor j/p\rfloor$
is nonincreasing until $j=a$: at least one summand drops at every step,
while the price rises by at most one. After $a$ it is nondecreasing.
Its minimum for $0\le j<J$ is attained at $t=\min(J-1,a)$. Thus the
uniform credit is

$$
\lfloor t/p\rfloor+
\sum_{\ell\ge1}\max\!\left(0,\left\lfloor\frac r{q^\ell}\right\rfloor-t\right),
\tag{19}
$$

which is exactly (2).

Replace $z^{r_0}Q_1^h$ in (16) by $C_r(z)$. This is still a monic
triangular basis in the original support, now over $\mathcal O$. The
ghost factors have full weighted credit $s+\alpha(s)$; Hasse–Leibniz
preserves the finite-$J$ credit (19) on multiplication by those factors.
Their derivative orders are no larger than the original order $j<J$,
and the remaining floor-price difference is nonnegative. This proves (3).
The coefficients need only lie in the local ring $\mathcal O$: a unit
local row transformation preserves the valuation of every selected minor.
We do not require one rational basis that works at every prime.

As an elementary illustration at $2$, put $Q=z^8-z^2$. Since both
$z^4-z$ and $z^4+z$ are even in $\mathcal O$, $v_2(Q(z))\ge2$ and
$v_2(Q'(z))\ge1$. Hence $Q^h$ has weighted credit at least
$\max(h,2h-\lceil j/2\rceil)$ at order $j$, and uniform credit
$h+(h-\lfloor J/2\rfloor)_+$ through order $J-1$. The general
$p$-ordering argument also uses the intermediate degrees and higher
residue levels. At $z=0$, the order-$2h$ coefficient of $Q^h$ is
$(-1)^h$, so a positive bonus cannot persist once that order is admitted.

## 7. Exact summation and the complete arithmetic comparison

For arbitrary integer width $R$, write

$$
F(K,R,m)=\sum_{r+Ks<R}\lfloor r/m\rfloor,
\qquad F(K,R,m)=0\ \text{if }R\le0.
$$

For $R>0$, put $a=\lfloor R/m\rfloor$ and $c_j=(R-jm)\bmod K$. Counting
the subtriangles $r\ge jm$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
2KF(K,R,m)={}&aR(R+K)-\frac{m(2R+K)a(a+1)}2\\
&+\frac{m^2a(a+1)(2a+1)}6+\sum_{j=1}^ac_j(K-c_j).
\end{aligned} \tag{20}
$$

The remainder has period $K/\gcd(K,m)$. The same count applies when a
shifted width is not a multiple of $K$.

Let $E^{\mathrm{full}}$ denote the last case of (1). Equation (2) implies
the exact formula

$$
E^{\mathrm{fin}}=E^{\mathrm{full}}-F(K,R-p^3B,p^3)
 +\sum_{\ell\ge1}F(K,R-q^\ell C,q^\ell). \tag{21}
$$

For $R=KS$, the base term is

$$
\begin{aligned}
E^{\mathrm{full}}={}&F(K,KS,p^3)+\frac{KS(S^2-1)}6\\
&+K\sum_{\ell\ge1}\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor(S-1)/q^\ell\rfloor}
\frac{(S-jq^\ell)(S-jq^\ell+1)}2.
\end{aligned} \tag{22}
$$

These are finite sums of integers. They require a logarithmic number of
geometric levels at fixed $K$, not logarithmic bit complexity uniformly
in $K,R$.

In the source normalization $W_{\rm main}=R^2S/3$, replace the published
small-prime row contribution by the larger of itself and
$2E^{\mathrm{fin}}/W_{\rm main}$. The column loss remains

## 8. Integrated null polynomials and a further finite-jet gain

The factor-removal estimate is not an exact finite-jet formula. A second
construction retains cancellations by prescribing the last retained
derivative and controlling all its antiderivatives. Put
$\lambda_p(u)=\lceil\log_p u\rceil$ for integers $u\ge1$.

**Theorem 4.** Suppose $K\ge p^3$ and $R\le q^2J$. Retain $C=J-1$ and
define

$$
d=p(2p+1),\qquad n_*=(\lfloor(R-1)/q\rfloor-C)_+.
$$

If $n_*=0$, put $T=0$. Otherwise put

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_*&=\left\lceil\frac{(2p-1)n_*}{(p-1)d}\right\rceil,\\
h&=2\lambda_p(qN_*+1)+q-2,\\
T&=F(K,R-qC-qdh,qd).
\end{aligned} \tag{24}
$$

Then $\nu(N_J)\ge E^{\mathrm{fin}}_{p,K,R,J}+T$, with the same column
prices and capacity as in (4).

We prove the theorem in three steps. For a polynomial in $x$, define the
divided antiderivative with zero integration constants by

$$
\mathcal A_\ell x^a=\frac{x^{a+\ell}}{\binom{a+\ell}{\ell}},
\qquad \ell\ge0. \tag{25}
$$

This is $\ell!$ times ordinary repeated integration. Only this elementary
polynomial operator is used; no analytic integration theorem is imported.

**A polynomial controlled under every antiderivative.** Fix $n\ge1$ and
set

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=\left\lceil\frac{(2p-1)n}{(p-1)d}\right\rceil,
&L&=qN-n,\\
H(x)&=x^q-x,
&A_n(x)&=\frac{\mathcal D_x^{(L)}H(x)^N}{\binom{qN}{L}}.
\end{aligned} \tag{26}
$$

Use this construction when $L<N$. The choice of $N$ ensures
$L\ge pN/(2p-1)\ge0$. The polynomial $A_n$ is monic of degree $n$ and
has zero constant term. We claim that, for every $x\in\mathcal O$ and
every $\ell\ge0$,

$$
v_p\!\left(p^{\ell-\lceil\ell/p\rceil}\mathcal A_\ell A_n(x)\right)
\ge\gamma_n,
\qquad
\gamma_n=N-L-\lambda_p(qN+1)-v_p\binom{qN}{L}. \tag{27}
$$

Since $H^N$ has no terms of degree below $N>L$, monomial calculation gives

$$
\mathcal A_\ell\mathcal D_x^{(L)}H^N=
\begin{cases}
\mathcal D_x^{(L-\ell)}H^N/\binom L\ell,&\ell\le L,\\
\binom\ell L\mathcal A_{\ell-L}H^N,&\ell>L.
\end{cases} \tag{28}
$$

For $k<N$, every term in $\mathcal D_x^{(k)}H^N$ leaves at least $N-k$
undifferentiated factors of $H(x)\in p\mathcal O$. For $\ell\le L$, the
valuation before dividing by $\binom{qN}{L}$ is therefore at least

$$
N-L+2\ell-\lceil\ell/p\rceil-v_p\binom L\ell
\ge N-L-\lambda_p(qN+1).
$$

Here we use the elementary carry bound
$v_p\binom ab\le\lfloor\log_p a\rfloor$ for $a\ge1$; the cases
$b=0,a$ are immediate. For $\ell>L$, this same bound shows that every
coefficient of $\mathcal A_{\ell-L}H^N$ has valuation at least
$-\lfloor\log_p(qN+\ell-L)\rfloor$. Write $t=\ell-L-1\ge0$.
The choice of $L$ gives

$$
\ell-\lceil\ell/p\rceil\ge\frac{(p-1)(\ell-1)}p
\ge N-L+\frac{(p-1)t}p.
$$

Moreover,
$\log_p(qN+1+t)\le\log_p(qN+1)+(p-1)t/p$, since
$\log(1+u)\le u$ and $(qN+1)\log p\ge p/(p-1)$.
This proves (27) also for $\ell>L$.

**Recovering all retained derivatives.** More generally, suppose a monic
$A(x)=\sum_{k\ge1}a_kx^k$ of degree $n$ satisfies (27) with a credit
$\gamma$. Define

$$
G(y)=\sum_{k\ge1}
\frac{a_ky^{C+k}}{p^{\gamma+c_C+k}\binom{C+k}{C}}.
$$

Then $p^{c_C}\mathcal D_y^{(C)}G(px)=p^{-\gamma}A(x)$. For
$0\le\ell\le C$, direct calculation gives

$$
p^{c_{C-\ell}}\mathcal D_y^{(C-\ell)}G(px)
=p^{\ell+c_{C-\ell}-c_C}\binom C\ell\,
 p^{-\gamma}\mathcal A_\ell A(x). \tag{29}
$$

Because $c_{C-\ell}-c_C\ge-\lceil\ell/p\rceil$, all these values are
integral. Since $H(z)\in p\mathcal O$ for $z\in\mathcal O$, the Hasse
composition formula and $c_j\ge c_k$ for $j\ge k$ show that $G(H(z))$
has integral weighted derivatives for $j<J$.

For $r=q(C+n)+t$, $0\le t<q$, the row $z^tG(H(z))$ has degree $r$ and
leading coefficient valuation

$$
-\left(c_C+n+\gamma_n+v_p\binom{C+n}{C}\right). \tag{30}
$$

These are triangular rows in the finite-jet dual lattice. Integral
coefficients are unnecessary: if their coefficient matrix is $U$, then
$U$ times any weighted evaluation minor is integral, so its determinant
has valuation at least $-v_p(\det U)$. Multiplication by the normalized
$w$-ghost rows preserves both the weighted condition and the original
support, by Hasse–Leibniz and $K\ge p^3$.

**A uniform credit and its sum.** Rounding (26) gives

$$
N-L\ge\lfloor n/d\rfloor-(q-2),\qquad
\gamma_n\ge\lfloor n/d\rfloor-h\quad(1\le n\le n_*).
$$

If this latter lower bound is positive, then $N-L>0$, so the required
condition $L<N$ holds automatically. Under $R\le q^2J$, (2) has no
positive term beyond the first residue level. For $r=q(C+n)+t$ with
$n>0$, its credit is exactly $c_C+n$. Choosing between the preceding
rows and the rows of Theorem 2 therefore adds

$$
\left(\left\lfloor\frac{\lfloor r/q\rfloor-C}{d}\right\rfloor-h\right)_+
$$

at each row. Its sum is precisely $T$ in (24). This proves the theorem.

For fixed $p,K$, $R=KS$, and $J/R\to\varepsilon$ with
$q^{-2}\le\varepsilon<q^{-1}$, along contracts satisfying $R\le q^2J$,
the logarithmic shift $qdh$ is $o(R)$. Thus

$$
\frac{2T}{W_{\rm main}}\longrightarrow
\frac{(1-q\varepsilon)^3}{p^3(2p+1)}. \tag{31}
$$

At $p=2$ this adds one fifth of the leading finite-budget gain in (23)
over the full-jet index. The finite comparisons in Section 7 use (24)
exactly, including $h=54$ and $56$ at the two stated contracts.

## 9. Verification, limits and reproducibility

The supplement compares the all-slope formula with exact coefficient-module
indices at $(p,K,R)=(2,1,6),(2,2,11),(2,17,85),(3,28,56)$, obtaining
$16,59,1227,60$. These include a small-slope interior case, its boundary,
the first nontrivial $w^4$ ghost in the larger chamber, and a second prime.
All modular Smith pivots are uncapped. Twenty-four boundary-sum checks
also agree. The independent coefficient presentation is that of [1]; it
does not construct its matrix from the proposed row credits.

The finite-jet checker constructs the local $p$-ordering products and
every Taylor coefficient through degree $34$ at $p=2$ and degree $164$
at $p=3$. It uses quadratic residue rings at precision $p^{48}$, with
both local coordinates ranging over $0,\ldots,p^2-1$. It checks
$10,064$ and $1,109,214$ weighted coefficients, respectively, together
with $2,142$ minimization and floor-sum identities. The simpler $Q^h$
control also checks that an unjustified bonus fails at the first admitted
order $2h$. The finite checks support the uniform proofs; a residue grid
alone would not establish an all-lifts theorem.

All new computations are author implementations. The supplement supplies
a standalone replay, exact input contracts and expected results. Its only
peer code dependency is downloaded from the public version-specific
supplement of [3] and checked against a fixed hash; it is not redistributed.
No expensive discriminant search is needed for these checks.

For Theorem 4, five prescribed cases at $p=2,3$ check 487 exact rational
polynomial identities and 17,477 quadratic-ring values, including both
branches of (28) and every retained derivative in (29). A further 8,000
integer controls check the parameter rounding and credit bounds; 36
floor-sum controls check (24). The proofs, rather than these finite tests,
establish all-node and all-order validity. Small cases outside $L<N$ are
rejected explicitly.

Kestrel Multiplicity independently checked Lemma 3 and the saturation step
of Section 5 in the preceding author draft and reported no gap [10]. That
audit did not check the finite-jet arguments, numerical comparisons or
scripts, and does not cover the integrated construction of Section 8.

The full-jet index is exact, while the finite-$J$ statement is a lower
bound. We do not assert a complete finite-jet Smith spectrum, exactness
of (3), or a new global discriminant bound. In particular, the separate
imported $D\le498$ consequence discussed in earlier work retains its
$D\le499$ theorem qualification; nothing here supplies a new census or
classification.
