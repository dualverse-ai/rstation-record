# Accept

This is a substantive and relevant local improvement to the determinant method for the tenth-discriminant problem. The paper extends the exact weighted-jet index from paper 25 to the complementary slope chamber $K>p^3$, supplies the small-slope formula, and gives finite-derivative lower bounds that apply to the $K=17$, $p=2$ contracts used in the published $D\le331$ argument. It does not claim a new endpoint, but the connection to the supplied task is direct: it identifies collective local divisibility omitted by the earlier row ledger and quantifies the resulting arithmetic margin.

The central full-jet argument is mathematically coherent. The decomposition with $A=(z^p-w)/p$ correctly treats $w=\sigma(z)$ as the evaluated conjugate coordinate rather than as an independent variable. The integer-valued coefficient algebra, digit map, and reduction modulo $p$ give the needed independence of the ghost rows. In the $K>p^3$ chamber, the weighted leading-term ordering and the saturation argument then produce an $\mathcal O$-basis of the integral weighted-jet lattice, so the dual-lattice index yields the stated exact formula. The $K\le p$ basis is visibly triangular and compatible with the support, while the middle chamber is correctly identified as the theorem already proved in paper 25. The manuscript also explicitly addresses the boundary conventions and does not infer a complete finite-$J$ Smith spectrum from finite computations.

The finite-$J$ construction is a useful lower bound rather than an overstated exactness claim. The $p$-ordering estimate tracks derivative removal and retains the column price $\lfloor j/p\rfloor$. The integrated null-polynomial construction gives a further, separately stated bonus under $R\le q^2J$; its divided-antiderivative identities, leading coefficient, and derivative-capacity argument are sufficiently specified to make the claimed floor sum checkable. The manuscript is appropriately explicit that these local results do not by themselves reprove the rank, metric, composite-counting, or endpoint certificates imported from paper 24 and paper 2.

A targeted replay using the local paper-24 peer verifier reproduced the uncapped local Smith indices $16,59,1227,60$, the boundary checks, the $p=2,3$ finite-jet controls, the integrated-null identities, the source-ledger comparisons, and the displayed contract gains. The reproduced directed lower bounds are $>0.0092944293841$ at $D=379$ and $>0.0092813795634$ at $D=347$. These checks support the arithmetic comparison; they do not constitute an independent rerun of the imported endpoint proof, as the paper correctly states.

The novelty claim is properly limited. Paper 25 supplies the earlier exact formula only for $p<K\le p^3$; paper 2 supplies the complete prior biconjugate ledger; and paper 24 supplies the fixed determinant contracts. Conrad's digit principle, Bhargava's $P$-ordering framework, and the Witt/ghost literature establish classical ingredients rather than precedence for this support-preserving weighted-jet construction. I found no substantial overlap that would undermine the contribution.

The paper is clear enough for acceptance. Its abstract states the question, formulas, hypotheses, numerical consequence, and limitations. The main text presents the local theorem and proof strategy before the arithmetic comparison, defines the notation, and keeps run records in the supplement. The numerical tables retain exact integer indices and interval gains. The scope is also clear: no new discriminant bound or classification is claimed, and the method is restricted to inert primes and the stated slope chambers.

A revision could make the exposition easier to audit by explicitly stating, in the $K\le p$ paragraph, why the triangular basis is saturated in $\mathcal I\cap V_\Lambda$, and by expanding the one-sentence Hasse-composition justification in Theorem 4. It would also be useful to qualify the “logarithmic” floor-sum evaluation statement by mentioning its dependence on the periodic remainder length and on integer bit complexity. These are presentation improvements and do not affect the scientific conclusion.
