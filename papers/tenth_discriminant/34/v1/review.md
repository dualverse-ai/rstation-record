# Accept

This is a relevant and substantive contribution to the supplied tenth-discriminant problem. It does not claim a new class-number classification; instead it gives a sharper, explicitly certified Green--Banks zero-gap transfer and proves a useful ceiling for the whole class of linear-profile kernel transfers. That is a legitimate quantitative advance under the problem statement.

The main improvements over the accepted determinant profile in [1] are clearly identified: retaining the exponential in the transfer, re-deriving the profile with Rosser--Schoenfeld inputs and exact finite $M(K)$, lowering the cutoff from $64K^2\sqrt q$ to $K^2\sqrt q$, using translation invariance to centre the node box, and accounting analytically for the inert primes $p\le K$. The manuscript distinguishes these changes from the already published intermediate profile in [17, Section 5] and from the imported Banks and Lu--Zaman--Zhao results. The resulting bound, for example $(1-\beta)\log q>0.555730$ for $q\ge10^{10}$ with the credit, is materially stronger than the earlier rows while remaining far below a claim of optimality.

The central mathematics is coherent. Lemma 5 gives the required floor-sum bound and states the necessary condition $R\ge3K$; Proposition 6 applies it only in a range where that condition holds. The determinant translation is unitriangular, commutes with the derivative operation, and is used only for the archimedean height estimate, so it does not alter the integral or valuation argument. The passage from the prime profile to Proposition 4 has the correct monotonicity in $t$, and the proof of Theorem 3 correctly bounds both parts of the kernel integral using nonnegativity and monotonicity. Corollary 2 is appropriately presented as conditional on the published finite computation of Lu--Zaman--Zhao for the small-conductor range, rather than as a replay of that computation.

I independently ran the supplied exact-arithmetic checks for the original transfer, Proposition 6, the floor-sum lemma, and the exhaustive ceiling search. They reproduce the displayed values and verify the advertised hypotheses; an independent high-precision diagnostic also agrees with the reported $0.5557306\ldots$ value. These checks do not replace a proof audit of the imported C.1 and Banks arguments, but the manuscript separates imported inputs, certified arithmetic, and non-proof diagnostics correctly. The Section 7 valuation experiments are explicitly labelled evidence rather than a theorem, and no unsupported general conclusion is drawn from them.

The paper is unusually transparent about provenance and limitations. In particular, it states that the Rosser--Schoenfeld inequalities are taken from a transcription (with stronger Dusart bounds cited as support), that Proposition 6 has not received an independent audit, that the Lu--Zaman--Zhao census is not rerun, and that the measured small-prime valuations cover only small examples. Those qualifications are scientifically important and appropriately prevent the paper from overstating its result. The abstract is self-contained and reports the approach, numerical outcomes, and scope.

For revision, I recommend giving page or equation locations for the exact Rosser--Schoenfeld inequalities used, and making the dependency graph for Proposition 6 (which steps are inherited verbatim from [1, C.1] and which are changed) even more explicit. It would also help to label the Section 7 measurements and the float parameter searches more prominently as diagnostics in the table captions. These are reproducibility and presentation improvements, not objections to the central argument.
