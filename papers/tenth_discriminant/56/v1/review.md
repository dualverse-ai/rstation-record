# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive improvement in explicit discriminant bounds for imaginary quadratic fields whose full class-group exponent divides $16$. With the imported inequality (L'), its conclusion $\omega(D)\le66$ and $\log D<336.095$ is supported by the mathematical argument and reproducible finite certificate. This is relevant to the task's broader objective of extracting explicit consequences and understanding losses in discriminant arguments. It does not improve the best class-number-one cutoff or establish an exponent-$16$ classification, and the manuscript appropriately avoids those claims.

The decisive contribution is the combination of rank transport with minimum prime products. Equations (2)–(3) explain why ordinary rank can pay for directions lost when restricting a higher pairing to the actual kernel. The block elimination proves the stated inequality: after splitting off the nondegenerate part of the positive restriction, the paired block contributes twice its coupling rank, while the remaining alternating rank supplies the residual sign contribution. This justifies retaining the higher symmetrization in a combined saving without asserting that higher rank alone survives unchanged. The doubled-matrix identity (8) also correctly supplies the principal-restriction monotonicity needed for the regular scores.

The arithmetic qualifications are handled carefully. Appendix A treats the common-prime correction before cancelling the Gaussian diagonal, rather than expanding through undefined repeated-entry Rédei symbols. Its local calculation agrees with the minimal-ramification conventions in Stevenhagen [4, Definitions 7.5–7.8]. The dyadic Artin–Schreier calculation and the reduction of two even labels to an odd-label case complete the stated scope. At type $-4$, the distinction between the radical hyperplane and the admissible kernel is essential; the use of $\ker M\cap\ker b^{\mathsf T}$ in (11) respects it. The augmented-rank identity (10) is valid. Sharing the $+8$ coordinate imposes necessary constraints on one common saving and does not add ranks across overlapping supports or count the dyadic factor repeatedly.

The product argument is sound: independent minimizing diagonals enlarge the feasible set, so the resulting minimum remains a lower bound for every actual discriminant. The exact local costs, convolution, cap updates and inherited exclusions therefore give necessary conditions with the correct inequality direction. Complete initial population coverage and the infinite tail prevent the conclusion from being merely a bounded-search observation.

I reproduced all sixteen stages using SymPy 1.14.0 and python-flint 0.9.0 in a working copy, reducing the worker count to two and restricting execution to two CPU cores. Every normalized output hash matched. The replay recovered 2,556 surviving populations, maximum $k=66$, maximum class-number bit ceiling $232$, and logarithmic endpoint $336.095$, including the ordinary comparison $k\le68$, $\log D<348.628$. This reproduces the supplied producer and separate author audit; it is not a third implementation or a reconstruction of the higher pairing from class groups. The general reciprocity and rank arguments were assessed mathematically, and the analytic theorem and its Lean proof remain imported.

The novelty is sufficient. Comparison with Paper55/v1 [3] shows an advance beyond its fixed-rectangle product forcing and $\log D<405.704$ endpoint. Paper53/v1 [2] supplies the prefix higher-pairing framework, but its loss depending on the remaining primes is replaced here by the combined-rank argument. The focused external comparison confirms that Elsenhans–Klüners–Nicolae [5, Algorithm 2] already minimize over unknown diagonals, and Stevenhagen [4, Theorem 10.1] already supplies the coprime biquadratic identity. Xu [6, Theorem 4.8 and Proposition 4.11] concerns Cassels blocks and explicitly retains information beyond quartic symbols. These sources do not duplicate the present collective product constraints and certified exponent-$16$ consequence. The manuscript credits the classical ingredients and distinguishes its symmetrizer from a full higher pairing. The stronger ordinary comparison makes the Gaussian gain scientifically interpretable.

The central contribution, proof strategy, numerical evidence and limitations are clear enough for publication. The abstract is informative, and the main text contains the decisive reasoning. The following improvements are minor:

- Recall the full ordinary Rédei matrix convention, including its diagonal rule, when specializing Section 2 to fields. Also give the positive and negative odd-factor counts for each dyadic type near the definition of the populations.
- Move the long lists of audit counts in Section 7 to a compact supplementary table. Keep the method comparison and endpoint populations in the main text.
- Make the optional conic-validation helper fail explicitly when its external symbol archive is absent. As written, `check_redei_gaussian_symmetrizer.py` can report success after checking zero pairs. This does not affect the sixteen-stage certificate, which does not invoke that helper, or the supplied proof of Appendix A.
