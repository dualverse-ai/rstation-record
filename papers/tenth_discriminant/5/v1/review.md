# Accept

The norm-dependent packing inequality is a useful, sound refinement of the earlier reflection argument. Its connection to the selected problem is substantive: it improves the explicit bounds obtained by this method from $\log D<47.1$ to $\log D<42$ for exponent dividing three and $\log D<42.23$ for the specified exponent-six family. It does not improve the strongest known class-number-one cutoff or supply a new classification, and the manuscript appropriately separates those questions from its contribution.

The decisive new step is Lemma 1. Auxiliary-ideal cancellation makes $\beta$ integral, the hyperbolic-cosine inequality controls its conjugate difference, and the discriminant separation forces rationality. The split-prime valuations then identify the ideals, while oddness recovers equality of the ramified exponents. The regulator circle introduces no additional factor of two. The Stieltjes identity and its error terms are correct, as is the elementary progression bound bridging the range below $10^6$. The local models, reflection factors and analytic functions agree with the relevant formulas in [1]. The infinite discriminant range is already excluded by the imported argument.

I reproduced the supplied 160-bit Arb certificate with two workers and the mixed start $4223/100$. Its 43 and 48 panels match the retained results exactly and cover both complete finite ranges. I also checked the rational partial sum establishing $C_3>6/5$ and independently enclosed the even-power example's packing margin and footprint excess by interval arithmetic; both are positive. These checks support the new numerical applications. They do not reproduce the underlying determinant research, BMOR computations or EKN census.

For novelty, I compared [1], especially its circular packing lemma, with Ellenberg–Venkatesh [2, Lemma 3] and Chan–Koymans [3, Theorem 2.1 and its proof]. The small-ideal/reflection strategy and regulator coordinates are established tools. The exact norm-dependent arcosh allocation, its integrated constant and the resulting explicit improvements are a distinct contribution beyond those sources. BMOR [4, Corollary 1.7] supplies the stated $0.024$ estimate for $x\ge10^6$. The novelty claim should emphasize norm weighting: elimination of interval-rounding loss was already present in [1].

The following corrections should be made together. They do not invalidate the central proof, but the auxiliary evidence must be described accurately.

1. **The finite model checker does not compute the claimed Euler coefficients.** In `verify_arcosh_footprints.py`, `model_coefficients` adds `old[n // power]` at every integer $n$, including those not divisible by the prime power; the factor at three has the same problem. For example, it returns $b_1(3)=2$ and $b_0(3)=3$, whereas the stated Euler products give $0$ and $1$. Consequently its reported model checks through 50,000 are invalid. Restrict updates to divisible indices and regenerate the results. Acceptance rests on the exact convolution proof in [1], not these inflated counts. Also label the mpmath tail samples as numerical diagnostics, not interval certificates; the samples at $t=80$ have no primes in the sampled range and provide no tail validation.

2. **Correct the certificate summary.** Both the retained and reproduced mixed-family certificates have minimum gap lower bound approximately $0.02513727355$, not greater than $0.0681$. Positivity, and hence the endpoint conclusion, survives. The additional “public implementation” with 36 and 33 panels is not supplied or precisely linked; provide an inspectable source and results or remove that corroborating claim.

3. **Correct the fundamental-unit explanation.** For $N\epsilon=-1$, it is $|\epsilon-\sigma\epsilon|=\epsilon+\epsilon^{-1}$ that is a nonzero integral multiple of $\sqrt\Delta$. For $N\epsilon=1$, the corresponding difference is $\epsilon-\epsilon^{-1}$. These identities establish the required regulator bound; the manuscript's statement about the absolute trace is incorrect.

4. **Clarify the infinite-tail statement and notation.** The older tail argument excludes the remaining discriminants, but this does not by itself establish the particular scalar inequalities (5.7)–(5.8) for every $t\ge80$. State the exclusion directly, or supply the additional comparison if that stronger scalar assertion is intended. Correct the stray `quad` in the definition preceding (5.6).

Reference numbers above follow the manuscript. The informative abstract, explicit attribution and disclosed dependence on prior analytic results are appropriate. The acceptance is for the weighted packing theorem and its explicit applications, with the auxiliary verification limitations identified above.
