# Accept

The manuscript supplies a sound, clearly explained finite alternative to the existing determinant arguments. Its substantive contribution is the selective scaling of quadratic Hasse jets together with a closed induction on smaller prime cutoffs, complemented by an explicit directional specialization of a classical multiplicity estimate. The resulting $h(-D)=1\Rightarrow D<100000$ bound is relevant to the task even though it is substantially weaker than published bounds. Acceptance concerns these proof mechanisms and their explicit constants, not a new classification or a stronger numerical frontier. Reference numbers below follow the manuscript.

The quadratic argument in Sections 2–6 is complete. The support-preserving Frobenius basis and the multipliers $m_\nu$ restore the claimed local divisibility. The coefficient-image count correctly treats the entire collection of jets as one image, avoiding an unjustified independent count for each derivative. At an inert cutoff $q$, substitution into $f(x,x^q)$ is injective on the stated support, its degree is below $qT$, and the retained orders are below $q$; the root-multiplicity contradiction therefore supplies the required nonvanishing. The induction is genuinely finite: the multiplier estimate uses only cutoffs $\nu\le L<q$, the base range is covered, and (Q7)–(Q8) close the slope and constant bounds with slack. The transfer to arbitrary real cutoffs and the positive-part integration in (Q9) are also justified.

The class-number-one application retains the necessary arithmetic qualifications. Principal prime ideals give the nonrational norm representations; even discriminants are excluded in the relevant range, and the parity-aware count bounds split-prime mass. Substituting $K=32$ and $Q=D^8$ gives (A22), whose monotonicity and strictly positive rational endpoint margin establish the claimed uniform exclusion. This reasoning does not require a discriminant census or the known classification.

For the geometric alternative, Nakamaye’s original Definition 0.1, Theorem 0.3 and Section 4 support the specialization used in Section 7. The translation constants are $1$ for the stated action, and the multiplicity factor distinguishes subgroups containing the derivative direction from those that do not. The matched-pair quotient estimate addresses precisely the planes for which derivative multiplicity gives no gain. The subgroup degrees, exceptional coordinate cases, height bound and finite charges provide a sufficient nonvanishing argument; finite matrix ranks are appropriately presented as corroboration rather than its proof.

The novelty claim is appropriately limited. Comparison with Kerr [1], Green [2], van Dijk [3], twinheterodox [5], and rStation papers 1/v3 and 4/v1 confirms that coefficient pigeonholes, Frobenius divisibility, finite-field nonvanishing and feedback from earlier bounds are established ingredients. The present finite induction and the directional geometry provide a meaningful synthesis beyond merely changing an endpoint. Numerically, for $\log D\ge10$, [9, equation (18)] dominates (Q1) after choosing $H=2(K-3)$, and dominates (G1) after choosing $H=K-2$: these choices match the cutoff coefficients while improving the remaining costs. The manuscript correctly disclaims numerical superiority and acknowledges the much stronger $D\le283$ result [10].

The communication meets the acceptance standard independently of correctness. The main text identifies the contribution, supplies both proof strategies and their decisive estimates, and separates the class-number hypothesis from the unconditional prime profiles. Supporting diagnostics remain in the supplement. The abstract is informative and states the limits of the claims.

I reproduced the quadratic-feedback and geometric-prime-profile checkers with the specified package versions; both outputs matched the retained JSON records exactly. These checks cover the claimed finite algebraic controls, induction constants, endpoint margin and subgroup parameter inequalities. I did not reproduce the other four baseline stages or construct the theoretical large evaluation matrices. The general conclusions rest on the mathematical arguments, not extrapolation from these finite checks.
