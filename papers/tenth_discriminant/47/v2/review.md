# Accept

This revision preserves the accepted classifications and improves their computational support and presentation. The changes are limited to checks of existing results, clearer descriptions of the computations, dependency documentation, and attribution. They do not introduce a separate substantive contribution. The resulting manuscript remains organized around the common analytic bound, finite reductions, and classifications; the additions do not obscure that argument.

The clarification in the proof of Theorem A is important and correct: the original form counter returns $\min(h,OB+1)$, so it determines the exact class number only when $h\le OB$. The new `h_dirichlet.c` independently evaluates Dirichlet's finite character-sum formula on every retained open field, including the exceptional unit factors for discriminants $-3$ and $-4$. I inspected and reran this program on all 14,584 records. Its output matched the retained output exactly: 2,272 fields have $h\le OB$, 12,312 have $h>OB$, and there are no mismatches with the capped form counts. The revised aggregation script also passed on the retained search outputs. This is useful corroboration of the existing classification, although it does not independently establish that the large searches omitted no open fields. I did not rerun those searches or rebuild the Lean development in this revision review.

I also reran `table1_primes.py`, obtaining output identical to the supplement. All 25 listed primes congruent to $7\pmod8$ satisfy $\mathrm{Ono}=h$; exactly the stated 14 satisfy $M_{odd}\le2$, and the other 11 have explicit witnesses with three distinct prime factors. This resolves the numerical issue within the manuscript's own table. It does not establish what Louboutin's original Table 1 printed, so the historical discrepancy with Gica's reported count remains appropriately qualified. That unresolved attribution detail does not undermine the classifications.

The added reference to Paper 49 [23] agrees with its published version 2, particularly Corollary 2 and §6. The stronger Littlewood bound is acknowledged as existing work and is not incorporated as a new result or needed for the present proofs. The prominent Lean version and repository requirements, and the explicit distinction between author-computed full-window outputs and the earlier review's bounded reruns, address the corresponding documentation concerns.

Two minor editorial improvements remain advisable. First, align the abstract and the general statements that a second program “repeats each search” with §4: for Theorem A, the independent full-window implementation repeats the pruning, while the new class-number computation checks the retained open fields. Likewise, call the original counter's output a capped count wherever §4 currently calls it the “$h$ found” by that program. Second, condense §6's version-by-version rerun history into a description of the current reproducible checks, leaving the chronology in the supplement. Neither issue warrants withholding acceptance.
