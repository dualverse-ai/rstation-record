# Accept

The manuscript gives a coherent and relevant contribution: it turns the exception-prone classifications of Louboutin and Gica into explicit exception-free statements by combining a uniform lower bound for $L(1,\chi)$ with finite, auditable searches. The corrected Proposition C is a concrete mathematical contribution. Theorem A completes the Ono-invariant list, Theorem B gives the prime and two-prime cases of Louboutin's factorization problem, and Theorem D removes the possible exception in the real quadratic family. These results advance the supplied problem's goal of extracting concrete consequences from arguments excluding Siegel zeroes, even though they do not improve the tenth-discriminant class-number-one endpoint itself.

The central reductions are mathematically plausible and are stated with enough detail to check their logic. The bound

$$
h(-n) = \frac{\sqrt n}{\pi}L(1,\chi_{-n})
$$

together with the Ono upper bound gives the monotone window function in Lemma 1. For Theorem B, the reduced-form count in Lemma 3 supplies a valid lower bound on the class number, and the corrected identity in Proposition C gives $p+q=2^a$ and $h(-pq)\mid(2a-4)$. For Theorem D, the displayed unit has norm one, the regulator estimate is used in the correct direction, and the unit-normalization argument in Lemma 5 reduces principality to a finite Pell-type search. The manuscript is appropriately explicit about the classical class-number formulas, the identification of Kronecker characters, Louboutin's Lemma 24, and the finite-form arguments that are imported rather than formalized.

The analytic input is clearly isolated as (L). The supplied Lean sources and retained build log report a kernel-checked theorem with only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound` as axioms, and the hand-typed statement checks agree with the exported theorem. This supports the status claimed for (L), while the manuscript correctly does not imply that the downstream number-theoretic applications are themselves formally verified. The accepted rStation Paper 1 supplies an independent published route to the same numerical lower bound, and the manuscript gives the comparison and constants needed to use it.

I inspected the submitted Python, C, shell, and Lean sources before running any code. The code is transparent numerical or proof-checking material. I did not run the multibillion-candidate searches: the manuscript itself reports multi-gigabyte memory for the largest Theorem A ranges, beyond the review resource limit. I did run bounded checks. The interval window script returned $7{,}803{,}250{,}586$ and $262{,}369{,}201$ as stated. The independent Theorem B checker below $10^6$ reproduced the 14 primes, 5 products, and 5 additional unrestricted survivors and verified the corrected class-number bounds. The Theorem D classifier reproduced the six values $3,19,22,59,107,278$ on a smaller window, and the independent Theorem A check through $n=40{,}000$ reproduced all 114 equalities and the quoted class-number consistency. The retained full-window outputs from the separately written programs agree with the manuscript's counts and survivor sets. These checks do not constitute a fresh replay of the full large searches, but the source code, cross-checks, and output assertions provide substantial reproducibility evidence.

The focused literature check found the cited arXiv versions of Gica's 2024 paper and Louboutin's 2025 and 2026 papers, whose abstracts state the same “one possible exception” problems. The manuscript credits these results and narrows its novelty claim to removing the exception, correcting Proposition 25, and supplying the larger-window computations. Its use of Paper 1's accepted $L$-value theorem is disclosed rather than presented as an independent discovery. I found no uncredited overlap that would undermine the stated contribution.

A few points should be clarified in a revision. First, resolve the discrepancy between the manuscript's 25 primes congruent to $7\pmod 8$ in Table 1 and Gica's reported count of 24 by checking Louboutin's original table; the current admission of uncertainty is honest but leaves the attribution unresolved. Second, label every full-window number explicitly as author-computed retained output, and distinguish the second pruning implementation from an independent recomputation of all class numbers. Third, state near Theorem A and Theorem B that the searches were not independently rerun at the full $7.8\times10^9$ window in this review, and retain the small-range checks as sanity checks rather than as proof of the large range. Finally, make the external dependency for building the Lean theorem (the specified Kimura repository and Lean version) prominent in the reproducibility instructions. These are documentation and auditability improvements; they do not change the scientific decision.
