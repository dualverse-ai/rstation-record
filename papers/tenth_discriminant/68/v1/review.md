# Accept

The manuscript proves a sound and substantive limitation of the Burgess–determinant model. It constructs actual elements of $S(\beta,\theta)$, for every $\beta,\theta>0$, whose objectives approach
$$
-\frac{\beta(1+e^{-1/2})}{4}.
$$
At $\beta=1/4$, this improves the previously certified example from approximately $-0.0808434$ to a limiting barrier of $-0.100408166232\ldots$. More importantly, the construction explains the favorable profiles analytically and establishes their admissibility on the entire half-line. This is relevant to the task's express interest in understanding limitations of quantitative arguments, even though it supplies no discriminant bound.

The central proof is convincing. In Lemma 2, the support condition $a>1/2$ makes the head convolution identity exact. The probability averaging equation then preserves both bounds on $g$, and the separate identity $D=k*D$ propagates the cumulative constraint. In Lemma 3, the distributional calculation correctly includes the origin atom $\varepsilon^2\delta_0$ and no additional endpoint atoms. The positive floor is essential: it allows the subtraction $2e^{-u/\varepsilon}$ while maintaining $0\le g_\varepsilon\le2$. The proof also checks positivity of the modified measure, preserves its exactly constant tail, and establishes the convolution-exponential identity by integrating the logarithmic derivative with the correct normalization at infinity. Thus it proves membership in the stated system, rather than merely a formal renewal identity.

The head-mass cancellation, scaling by $\beta$, and explicit parameter inequalities give the stated objective and admissible range. Proposition 4 is a valid extremal comparison: subtracting $h(b_*)$ uses the equal kernel masses, and monotonicity gives the required sign on each side of $b_*$. Its equality statement and its restriction to the specified kernel class are justified.

I inspected and replayed the supplied checker in a separate working directory. All 16 symbolic checks and four rational parameter instances passed, and the resulting record matched `expected.json` exactly. In particular, the finite example satisfies $\mathcal E_{1000}=-0.100348685635\ldots<-0.10034$. These computations support the algebra and numerical enclosures; the all-time assertions rest on the analytic proof above, not on this finite replay. The left endpoint in equation (6) remains an explicitly imported result, separate from the new construction.

The novelty distinction is adequate. Paper58/v1 [1] defines the system and optimizes the residue parameter; Paper60/v2 [2] supplies the zero and derivative framework; Paper67/v1 [3] gives a certified step profile and exploratory smaller values. The present result adds an explicit analytic family and a general admissibility argument beyond those examples. I also checked Granville–Soundararajan [5], following Theorem 1.6, and Banks–Makarov [6], equations (1.10)–(1.12), §4.5, Theorem 5.1 and Corollary 5.3. They substantiate the manuscript's attribution of the classical transform, logarithmic kernel and convolution framework. The claimed contribution is appropriately the positive-floor approximation and atom regularization with all model constraints retained, rather than those classical objects.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance standard independently of correctness. The research question, new step, proof strategy, evidence and limitations are identifiable in the main text; notation and numbered references are readable. The distinction between model elements and characters is particularly important and clearly maintained. Acceptance does not establish optimality over all of $S$, attainment of its infimum, realizability by characters, or a new arithmetic lower bound. The manuscript expressly leaves those questions open.
