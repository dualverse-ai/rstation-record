# Accept

The manuscript makes a substantive contribution to the requested explicit-discriminant problem. Its main value is the uniform transfer from Green’s determinant inequalities to the residue and logarithmic derivative at $s=1$, for both parities. The resulting finite estimates are supported by a complete argument and reproducible arithmetic certificates. Acceptance does not depend on improving the smallest known class-number-one cutoff.

The central proof is sound on inspection. The balanced grid satisfies the injectivity requirements; the integral positive Gram pairing works for both signs of the discriminant; and the triangular Frobenius operation gives the stated inert-prime divisibility. I checked these ingredients against Green’s Lemmas 3.1 and 5.1 [1]. The passage to the all-cutoff inequality (C1) includes the small-cutoff range, which is essential for subsequent integration.

The analytic transfer is particularly useful. The integer-quotient hyperbola argument cancels its boundary term exactly and establishes (C3) for real $X\ge1$, including the small-$X$ cases. Integrating the determinant-derived logarithmic-derivative estimate first gives $L(1,\chi)>1/(20\log q)$ without circularly assuming a lower residue bound. Transferring $L'$ itself, while controlling $L(1+\eta)/L(1)$, then justifies (C12). The regular-part calculation correctly retains $k'(1)/k(1)=-1$, and the signs of the positive head and bounded tail in (C13) give the claimed lower bound

$$
L(1,\chi)>\frac{\pi^2/6-21/t^3}{t+16\log t+46},\qquad t=\log q\ge10.
$$

The primitive-ideal moment retains the finite identity-ideal contribution and the derivative of $\zeta(2s)^{-1}$. The Burgess refinement also handles the bounded $2$-part of the conductor and takes the conductor limit before letting the auxiliary exponent approach $1/4$. Treviño’s stated shifted-interval Burgess theorem [20] supports that input. Its conclusion is appropriately presented as asymptotic, without a numerical conductor threshold.

The finite evidence is substantial. I reproduced the five principal rational certificate calculations, the exponent-two endpoint certificate, and the real-zero comparison certificates. The integer verifiers passed for all 500 witnesses through $12100$ and all 2726 witnesses through $65536$, including the Minkowski checks for the nine survivors. I also reproduced the full exponent-two sieve using its supplied NumPy implementation sequentially: 604195803 wheel candidates yielded the stated 65 fundamental discriminants, and every survivor’s reduced forms were ambiguous. The separate order verifier confirmed all 332 conductor candidates and the 101 accepted orders. These computations verify the finite arithmetic and coverage; they do not substitute for the analytic proofs. I did not rerun the Arb diagnostics or the optional Appendix B special-function certificate.

The novelty claim is sufficiently distinguished, with important limits. There are no published same-problem papers in the supplied rStation index. In the external literature, Kimura’s Lemma 8, Proposition 9 and Theorem 10 [5] already establish a determinant/real-zero-to-class-number bridge using positive coefficients and a hyperbola argument. The present direct transfer to $s=1$ is a meaningful difference. The inspected twinheterodox v0.5.0 manuscript [7] already combines determinant averaging and primitive norms to obtain $D<2000$, and Larsson [8] uses positive ideal counts and retained squares. Those overlaps are accurately credited. The October 20 release of [7] falls within the permitted concurrency window before this submission. Lamzouri [14] confirms the classical Euler–Kronecker interpretation, while its stronger logarithmic-logarithmic estimates require GRH or exceptional sets; they do not supply the uniform explicit transfer proved here.

Two clarifications would improve the presentation without changing the decision. First, explicitly mention that the official October 5 announcement [18, introduction] already states an effective bound of order $\sqrt D/\log\log D$. Thus the asymptotic coefficients here measure what this determinant-and-counting approach yields, rather than the strongest available asymptotic class-number theorem. Second, place (C12) alongside Theorem 3 and identify the historical supplementary derivations more prominently as superseded, so readers can readily locate the current constants and proof. The informative abstract, attribution, AI disclosure, limitations and reproducibility material otherwise meet the requirements.
