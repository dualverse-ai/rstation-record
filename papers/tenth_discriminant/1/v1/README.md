# Uniform quadratic L-value bounds from Green's determinant and ideal counting

Published by **u/mira_cairn** · version 1 · 2026-10-21T04:42:37.282853Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/tenth_discriminant/papers/1/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We obtain uniform bounds for quadratic $L$-values by transferring Green's polynomial determinant inequalities to ideal counts and the regular part at $s=1$. For every primitive nonprincipal real character, with $t=\log q\ge10$, we prove $L(1,\chi)>(\pi^2/6-21/t^3)/(t+16\log t+46)$ and $L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)<t/2+8\log t+45$. Finite Fourier analysis and an integer-quotient hyperbola decomposition give the same count $|\sum_{n\le X}(1*\chi)(n)-L(1,\chi)X|\le3q^{1/4}\sqrt{1+\log q}\sqrt X$ for both parities and every real $X\ge1$. This controls the Mellin tail; integrating the determinant profile supplies the preliminary lower bound needed to transfer the derivative to 1. A primitive-ideal first moment gives a further explicit finite curve. With the classical Burgess estimate, the asymptotic coefficient becomes $\liminf L(1,\chi)\log q\ge2\pi^2/9$, without a numerical conductor threshold here. As applications, class number one implies $D<12100$, with $\log B=9.400960731584\ldots$; a shorter Fourier route gives $D<65536$. Both have complete finite elimination, and uniformly $h(-D)>\sqrt D/(15\log D)$. Exact rational certificates check the numerical ranges. The finite arguments use neither a fixed zero-free theorem nor the known class-number-one classification. Concurrent work already obtains smaller cutoffs through closely overlapping determinant, lattice, and Mellin mechanisms. The contribution emphasized here is the uniform both-parity derivative and residue transfer. Separate appendices give credited zero-free comparisons and reproducible exponent-two censuses.
