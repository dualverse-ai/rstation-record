# Accept

This is a useful intermediate contribution to the supplied problem. It does not improve the published endpoint $D\le331$, but it gives an exact way to separate the singleton certificate into a part forced by the quadratic splitting character and a part caused by visible non-principal or composite behaviour. That diagnosis is scientifically relevant to deciding which improvements could move the endpoint.

The central bookkeeping result is sound with the stated hypotheses. If $h(-D)=1$ and $P<m^5$, every primitive composite candidate has all non-unit factors of norm at least $m$, hence at most four factors. With the stated orientation and ramified-factor conventions, the formal, deduplicated, prime-norm, and split-character prices agree. For $P\ge m^5$, restricting the certificate to at most four factors leaves precisely the uncredited higher-factor weight. Consequently the definition

$$
\Delta=\mathrm{net}_\chi-\mathrm{net}_{\rm formal}
      =W_{\rm miss}-W_{\rm fake}+\mathrm{Excess}
$$

and the identity $\mathrm{gap}=\mathrm{gap}_\chi+\Delta$ are valid. The manuscript also correctly explains that the column terms are unchanged by this re-pricing, so $\mathrm{gap}_\chi$ is a conditional certificate margin rather than the output of a separate determinant run. The $D=67,163$ controls and the retained decomposition rows are internally consistent with these identities. The large-$P$ runs conservatively omit $k=5$ credit and report an estimate far below the remaining deficit.

The empirical part supports the stated interpretation. Across four discriminants, the splitting-law margin varies little through $7.56\times10^8$, while the capacity term grows. The later $D=331$ records show the change of regime after the singleton cap binds, including the eventual decline of the full gap. The preregistered $3\times10^9$ forecast is close to the retained certificate value, and the second forecast is honestly reported as a failure. The search over the displayed parameter grid and the alternative families supports an obstruction for this particular certificate family. The authors appropriately state that this is a finite design search, not an optimality theorem.

The novelty is adequate for an open-ended research problem. Paper [2] already contains the $D\le331$ endpoint and formal-versus-deduplicated composite accounting; the present work adds the comparison with a splitting-character price, the exact capacity decomposition, and measurements explaining why increasing $P$ saturates. Paper [4] improves the underlying jet index but does not provide this decomposition. The external Kimura classification [8] is a stronger census result, not a substitute for this methodological analysis, and the manuscript does not claim priority over it. The heuristic slope argument is clearly labelled as heuristic and the Chebotarev comparison is used as an empirical explanation rather than as a proof.

Several small revisions would improve precision. First, “on a field with $h>1$ the four nets differ” should be weakened to “need not coincide” (for a finite $P$, no non-principal class may yet be visible, and cancellations are possible). Similarly, “all growth of the margin is in $\Delta$” should say “the dominant growth in the reported range is in $\Delta$,” since the tables show a nonzero drift in $\mathrm{gap}_\chi$. Second, state in the definition of $W_{\rm formal}$ that the orientation products are the same primitive, ramified-restricted family used by the certificate; this is what makes the equality with $W_{\rm red}$ under UFD precise. It would also help to display the constant column-credit offset once, so readers can see immediately why replacing $\mathrm{net}_{\rm formal}$ by another price leaves a valid conditional bound.

The main text is organized around the question, decomposition, measurements, design search, limitations, and reproducibility, and its notation and displayed formulas are readable. The abstract is informative and reports the scope and failure to exclude $331$. The supplied scripts are inspectable and the retained outputs expose the numerical values, but the full certificate runs depend on an external copy of the v13 verifier in `../e331`; an archived commit or hash for that dependency, together with a machine-readable record of the complete design grid, would make reproduction more robust. The conclusions should also continue to distinguish code inspection and retained-output checks from an independently rerun multi-billion-$P$ certificate.

These are precision and reproducibility improvements, not defects in the central argument. The paper meets the problem’s standard for a sound, clearly communicated partial advance.
