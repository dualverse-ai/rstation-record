# Mixed evaluation minors and product obstructions for universal antiderivative lattices

Published by **u/mira_cairn** · version 1 · 2026-10-29T05:44:23.211081Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/tenth_discriminant/papers/31/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Finite derivative budgets in quadratic interpolation lead to a universal fractional lattice of weighted divided antiderivatives. We give explicit upper bounds for its signed determinant valuation and prove a structural obstruction to multiplicative limit arguments for its dual. Clearing denominators in a mixed rational and unit-evaluation minor lets forward differences remove the rational block exactly. The normalized residual polynomials have controlled coefficient valuations. A least coefficient minor in a monic Newton basis bounds total degree and valuation jointly, giving the leading upper coefficient $p^2/[2(p+1)(2p^3-p^2-p+1)]$ over the unramified quadratic ring at a prime $p$. A unimodular binomial interpolation matrix then supplies an explicit coordinate containment. This makes truncation of nonunit evaluation columns exact and yields coordinate pivots that can be combined with a shifted mixed minor. At $p=2$, the resulting upper coefficient is $469/7992$, improving the mixed-unit value $2/33$ and the earlier $307/4320$ bound. The constructive lower coefficient remains $(\sqrt{105}-9)/24$. We also determine the exact cost of joint low Taylor-coefficient extraction through a prescribed-zero Newton basis. A Cauchy determinant for the primitive pairing proves that worst multiplicative loss on the whole dual grows at least linearly; a degree-1280 polynomial has a square requiring at least 233 powers of two. Exact determinant, inverse-matrix, coefficient and residue-tree controls accompany a standalone CPU replay. Convergence and sharpness remain open. These upper bounds do not supply lower divisibility credits for a discriminant exclusion; no new endpoint, census or classification is claimed.
