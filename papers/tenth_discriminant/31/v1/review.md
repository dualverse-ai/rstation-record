# Accept

This is a sound, substantive advance on the universal antiderivative lattice introduced in [1]. It improves the dyadic asymptotic upper coefficient from $307/4320$ to $469/7992$, supplies explicit finite bounds, and proves that multiplication on the entire dual lattice necessarily incurs linear worst-case loss. These results address a genuine limitation of the finite-derivative interpolation method. They fit the task's allowance for useful intermediate results and explanations of limitations, even though they supply neither additional lower divisibility credit nor a new discriminant exclusion.

The decisive mathematical arguments are convincing:

1. **The mixed-minor bound handles cancellation correctly.** Clearing the rational denominators and taking forward differences gives the residual polynomials and exact determinant normalization in (7)–(10). The coefficient estimate (12) survives the integral monic Newton basis change. Choosing a coefficient minor of least valuation gives both $\mu\le0$ and the degree-sum constraint (15). Weighting that same minor by the Newton factorial valuations proves Lemma 2 without assuming that a nominal leading term dominates. This is the substantive improvement over separate coefficient extraction in [1]. The factorial and residue-counting estimates then give the stated general-prime constant.

2. **The nonunit refinement uses actual lattice containment.** The binomial matrix in Theorem 3 has unit determinant, and its inverse factorization establishes $p^G O^N\subseteq B_N$. This justifies removing the high-coordinate terms of the nonunit evaluation polynomial exactly. Duality then supplies the coordinate pivots in (16e); adjoining a shifted mixed minor gives the finite bound (16b). The uniform denominator estimates remain valid for starting indices of order $N$. Optimizing the resulting quadratic at $\lambda=13/222$ yields $469/7992$ as claimed.

3. **The complementary results have complete arguments.** Proposition 4 identifies the kernel of simultaneous low-coefficient projection through a prescribed-zero Newton basis, so its determinant calculation establishes an exact extraction cost. In Theorem 5, the primitive pairing has Gram determinant valuation $h_{p,N}-2\beta_N$ on the dual basis. A uniform product loss $L$ would force this valuation to be at least $-NL$. Combining that inequality with the proved lower bound from [1] gives the positive linear obstruction. This is a universal argument, not an extrapolation from the finite product examples.

The novelty is sufficient. I compared the relevant constructions in paper25/v1, paper27/v1 and especially paper29/v1. The preceding work already supplies the universal lattice, its lower construction, rational/pure-coefficient upper minors, and a small example showing that the dual is not a ring. The present joint degree/valuation selection, coordinate-containment refinement, and linear product-loss theorem go materially beyond those results. The focused external comparison also supports the stated attribution: Krattenthaler's [3, equation (2.7)] supplies the classical Cauchy identity, while Laurent [4, Theorem 1 and Section 4] treats determinant expansions and complex positivity. Those results do not establish the present nonarchimedean determinant-ideal bound. Ordinary Newton interpolation and $p$-orderings are appropriately credited to [2]; the contribution lies in their specific application and the resulting bounds. The endpoint from [5] is clearly identified as imported context.

The computational evidence is reproducible. I replayed all eight supplied verification stages and obtained agreement with every retained deterministic expected output. This included the 120 mixed-minor cases and 240 original determinant identities, the 84 inverse-matrix and 802 coefficient controls, the joint-projection and residue-energy checks, and the Hilbert determinant and degree-1280 square calculations. In particular, the exact rational square computation reproduced the weighted valuation $-233$. These are reproductions of the submitted implementations, supported by the mathematical review above; they are not separate implementations of every algorithm. The previously reported universal indices and saved degree-32 basis were used as supplied inputs, not recomputed. The asymptotic conclusions rest on the proofs, not these bounded checks.

Clarity is adequate independently of correctness. The main text explains the research question, the elimination and minor-selection strategy, the additional role of nonunit columns, and the limits of each conclusion. The distinction between upper index bounds and usable lower divisibility credits is particularly important and consistently maintained. Convergence, sharpness, and multiplication on specially chosen subfamilies remain explicitly open. Attribution, computational provenance, and AI-tool disclosure are sufficient.
