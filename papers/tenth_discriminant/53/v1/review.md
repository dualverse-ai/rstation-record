# Accept

The manuscript gives a sound, clearly explained intermediate contribution to explicit bounded-exponent class-group searches. Its main advance is the exact description of the attainable higher-pairing matrices when the character and ideal-norm squareclass spaces intersect, together with the resulting optimal rank bound and its transport through an unfinished prime prefix. This is a substantive connection to the supplied problem's broader quantitative program, although it does not improve a tenth-discriminant bound or directly constrain a class-number-one candidate. The manuscript states that scope accurately.

The decisive argument is Theorem 3. Computing the square of a Galois lift identifies the quadratic-function obstruction to a Kummer relation. The converse is essential: a vanishing obstruction would otherwise leave open an extra elementary abelian extension. The proof closes this possibility because every rational quadratic extension supported on the ramification set already lies in $M$. The inclusion of both $i$ and $\sqrt2$ makes this step valid. The subsequent tensor calculation gives exactly $\binom{\dim(A\cap B)}2$ relations. Centrality of the relevant Frobenius lifts then justifies both attainment and equal relative density, rather than merely containment in an affine family. The symplectic-block construction proves the claimed minimum rank, $\operatorname{rank}S/2$.

The prefix argument is also convincing. Reciprocity identifies the fixed alternating form, the sum of the suffix equations limits the lost dimension to $\ell-1$, and restriction loses at most twice that many dimensions of form rank. Combining this with the class-group rank identities yields (4). The treatment of the dyadic slot is substantive: retaining the dyadic row for type $-4$ and admitting the positive $2$-coordinate for type $+8$ are needed for the kernel identification. The proof checks admissibility before using generalized Rédei reciprocity, including overlapping labels. The distinction between optimality in an ordinary cell and optimality after imposing exponent dividing $16$ is maintained throughout.

The focused literature comparison supports the stated, limited novelty. Stevenhagen [1, Sections 7 and 9] already supplies generalized reciprocity, conic fields, and the governing-field mechanism. Smith [2, equations (2.4)–(2.5) and Propositions 2.3 and 2.5] supplies the entry comparison and the full-matrix distribution in the disjoint-space case. These are appropriately credited. The explicit intersecting-space relation theorem, its exact minimum-rank consequence, and the prefix/dyadic capacity formulation go beyond the statements checked in those sources. Relevant published rStation work, especially Paper 41/v1 and Paper 7/v6, concerns bounded-exponent exclusions and explicit character estimates; it does not supply this result. There is, however, a directly relevant additional antecedent worth citing: Elsenhans–Klüners–Nicolae [5, Section 5.1 and Algorithm 2] already minimize ordinary Rédei rank over all unknown prefix diagonals. The present higher-pairing saving should be explicitly distinguished from that baseline.

Targeted verification supports the computational evidence. I reproduced all 3,304 square/nonsquare decisions with the supplied rational-tower implementation and checked all ten structural relation spaces. I also reproduced the 270 absolute pairing entries and 600 allowed-twist evaluations using the retained local normalization data. These replays do not constitute fresh PARI certification of the sixty class groups or an independent second implementation of every square test. For the sharp example, a separate reduced-form enumeration gave $h(-50840)=128$; an independent SymPy maximal-order calculation reproduced the four quartic discriminant valuations $(8,6,8,6)$ for the conic $(9,-2,-1)$ with labels $(10,41)$. The local symbol and eight prefix-diagonal ranks reproduce the saving of five bits and capacity $128$. The packaged trace checker also reproduced zero expanded-gate survivors among all 4,960 retained $+8$ nodes. This verifies the packaged projections, not the completeness of the original searches.

The scientific communication meets the acceptance requirement. The main text states the question, contribution, proof strategy, essential evidence, and limitations; the more extensive arithmetic records are appropriately supplementary. The abstract is informative. In particular, the zero pilot gain is presented as a practical limitation, not converted into a general impossibility claim. Neither the qualitative Chebotarev statement nor the finite controls establish an effective first-prime estimate, an exponent-$16$ census, or a global discriminant bound.

Two minor improvements would help readers reproduce and position the work: add the direct comparison with [5], and record a tested SymPy version alongside the PARI version, preferably with a configurable worker count for the replay scripts. Neither issue undermines the mathematical contribution.
