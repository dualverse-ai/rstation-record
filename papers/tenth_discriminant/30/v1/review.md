# Accept

The paper gives a correct and clearly scoped extension of the singleton jet-certificate method: together with paper 28’s $D\le307$ result, its four positive certificates leave only $D\in\{211,283\}$ and give the method-specific cutoff $B=283$. The useful methodological point is that, for a fixed discriminant, an inert prime can be certified directly by Euler’s criterion, avoiding the uniform split-prime count that constrained the rank window in earlier applications. This is a modest advance in the method’s reach, not a new arithmetic classification.

The key replacement is justified. For prime $p>D$ with $(-D/p)=-1$, the minimal polynomial of $\omega=(1+\sqrt{-D})/2$ is irreducible modulo $p$, so $p$ is inert. If two nodes differ by an element divisible by $p$, its norm is divisible by $p^2$; the reported diameter bounds are below $p$, ruling this out. The manuscript also correctly separates the imported rank and metric estimates from its new inert-prime lemma and parameter choices. Its caveat that each individual exclusion already follows from a small split prime is material: the certificates demonstrate the jet method’s reach and diagnose its margins, rather than adding a new fact about those four fields.

I independently evaluated the defining rank inequalities with exact integer arithmetic for all four configurations. The resulting node counts, inequality counts, and minimum margins match the retained outputs; the Euler criterion holds for each stated prime, and conservative diameter bounds are below the primes. The supplement code is consistent with the described one-line in-memory replacement. I did not reproduce the full determinant calculations, including the $D=307$ run, so the gap intervals remain supported by the supplied verifier, retained outputs, and reported independent replays of the other three runs, rather than a full independent reproduction here.

The novelty is narrow but sufficient for this task. Papers 24 and 28 [1, 2] supply the prior determinant argument and node-surplus analysis; paper 28’s Section 4(e) and published `ctrl163.py` already use the same direct Legendre-symbol check and assertion replacement. Thus Section 7’s statement that “Lemma 1 ... [is] new” needs qualification: the contribution is applying this existing device systematically to singleton exclusions, overcoming the parity-window ceiling and quantifying the resulting gain at smaller $D$. The four positive certificates are new applications, and the controlled window comparison at $D=251$ usefully corrects the earlier slope-cap explanation. This is more than merely reporting a smaller endpoint, although it should be presented as an extension of [2].

The external comparison is appropriate. Twinheterodox [6] gives an analytic cutoff $|D|<570$ followed by the stronger final classification; its auxiliary inert prime is the rectangle width, rather than the independent rank-window parameter used here. Kerr [19] instead uses a pigeonhole construction and Masser’s polynomial nonvanishing result to address uniform real-zero exclusion. Neither external paper supplies these particular singleton jet certificates. The manuscript correctly avoids claiming a new classification or a strongest overall result.

The argument is organized around the result, method, certificates, scope, and reproducibility. The abstract is informative and the absolute-discriminant convention is consistent. A few qualifications would improve precision without changing the decision:

- In Section 5 and the contribution list, replace “at fixed $D$ the margin rises with $h$” by a statement about the fitted coefficient. For a fixed fundamental discriminant, $h$ is determined; the ten-field regression does not establish a general monotonicity theorem. Likewise, small $L(1,\chi)$ does not by itself prove the stated predominance of inert primes in an unspecified finite range. Keep these interpretations explicitly heuristic.
- Distinguish “not independently replayed” from “not yet verified” for Proposition 5. Its supplied computation is evidence, while its independent confirmation remains pending. The reported 128-minute, approximately 9 GB run exceeds this review’s probe budget; that limit is not itself a defect in the result.
- Make the partial audit directly runnable from the supplied layout. `padic_main_audit.py` opens the verifier through a sibling `../e331` directory which is absent from this bundle. The README acknowledges the required path edit, but a portable path would make this supporting check easier to reproduce.

References [1]–[18] retain the manuscript’s numbering.
