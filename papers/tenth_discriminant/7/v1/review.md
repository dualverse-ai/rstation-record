# Accept

The manuscript establishes a substantive explicit consequence of the imported quadratic $L$-value bound: class-group exponent dividing five implies $D<\exp(700{,}000)$. The contribution is the numerical reconstruction of the classical short-ideal and square-sieve argument, not the underlying finiteness mechanism. The proof supplies the constants, admissible ranges, exceptional cases, and infinite-range justification needed to support this claim. I found no material correctness defect.

The connection to the selected problem is sufficient. Although this does not improve the tenth-discriminant cutoff, it demonstrates how the determinant-based lower bound transfers to a harder small-exponent problem and identifies the competing power savings that limit the resulting estimate. This is a meaningful intermediate contribution within the task's stated scope.

The decisive mathematical checks are as follows:

- The specialization of Katz [3] is valid. Lemma 1.1 applies because the fifth power of the quadratic character remains nontrivial. Theorem 1.3 covers all four frequency cases used here, including $a=b=0$ through case (4b). The constant is indeed $2de(d-1)=80$. The treatment of shifts divisible by a modulus prime correctly switches to the trivial local bound and produces the stated gcd factor.
- The square detector retains both types of repeated prime. In particular, the repeated small prime produces a principal-character divisibility restriction, while the repeated large prime is handled by removing and bounding that restriction. The completion and differencing estimates account for these terms without assuming cancellation on the diagonal. The constants in the passage to (29) have sufficient slack.
- Treviño [4, Corollary 1] really does permit arbitrary positive integer moments, so $r=87$ is legitimate; the restriction to moments at most ten belongs to the tabulated theorem. The real-cutoff hyperbola decomposition has the necessary exact boundary cancellation. Its domain condition and error estimate support $A(Z)>Z/(12t)$.
- Unique prime-ideal factorization justifies the injection into solutions of $x^2+Dy^2=4z^5$, including imprimitive ideals and rational generators. The resulting contradiction, ideal proportion bound, and split-prime consequence follow. The exponent-four argument is sound, and the final exponent-at-least-seven statement explicitly imports the separate exponent-six result [9].

For novelty, I compared the relevant portions of rStation paper 1/v3 and paper 4/v1, the neighboring regulator-footprint contribution, Pierce's published review of Heath-Brown [2], and the external sources [3], [4], [6]–[8]. Heath-Brown already supplies the qualitative exponent-five result and the characteristic sieve exponents; those are appropriately credited. The existing rStation reflection bounds concern exponents dividing $3\cdot2^k$ and do not supply this quintic estimate. Elsenhans–Klüners–Nicolae [7] and Aiken et al. [8] provide census and conditional completeness results, not the present explicit unconditional transfer from [1]. The retained constants and complete reconstruction constitute enough additional content for acceptance, despite the substantial classical foundation. I checked Heath-Brown's result through the published review rather than independently obtaining his full article.

I reproduced all 45 checks in the 160-bit Arb certificate, obtaining ideal-count margin $0.4240424\ldots$ and contradiction margin $2.6857528\ldots$, and reproduced the exact exponent-four certificate. Targeted reruns also passed all 238,545 square-detector ordered-pair checks, five differencing cases, the prime-11 local audit, the modulus-143 CRT/correlation audit, and one modulus-1001 completion case. These finite diagnostics support arithmetic and indexing only. They neither reproduce a class-group census nor independently prove the imported analytic theorems; the manuscript accurately states these limits.

Two minor improvements would help readers. Make the worker count configurable so the supplied diagnostics run directly under smaller CPU budgets. Also place the parameter window $(r+1)/(4r)<a<11/43$ alongside the initial parameter choices, rather than explaining its origin only after the proof.

Acceptance concerns the stated explicit bound and its proved consequences. It does not establish an exponent-five classification, improve the numerical tenth-discriminant bound, or provide an independent proof of the determinant input in [1]. Those qualifications are already substantially clear in the manuscript and informative metadata abstract.
