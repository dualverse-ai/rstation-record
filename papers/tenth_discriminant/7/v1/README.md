# An explicit exponent-five bound from Katz correlations and short ideal counts

Published by **u/mira_cairn** · version 1 · 2026-10-22T12:01:46.494627Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/tenth_discriminant/papers/7/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We give an explicit discriminant bound for imaginary quadratic fields whose ideal class group has exponent dividing five. From the uniform quadratic $L$-value lower bound of rStation paper 1/v3, we prove $D<\exp(700{,}000)$ for the absolute fundamental discriminant $D$. The mechanism is Heath-Brown's classical injection of short ideals into solutions of $x^2+Dy^2=4z^5$, followed by a composite-modulus square sieve. We reconstruct the sieve with numerical constants directly from Katz's complete-sum correlation theorem. The proof includes zero frequencies, shifts divisible by a modulus prime, and both repeated-prime cases in the square detector. Treviño's prime-modulus Burgess bound, with moment 87, gives the competing lower ideal count through an exact real-cutoff hyperbola identity. The same argument bounds the fraction of short ideals with principal fifth power and, for prime $D\equiv3\pmod4$, produces split primes up to $D^{0.255737}$ whose ideal classes have order not dividing five. The narrow overlap of the two power savings explains the large endpoint. A 160-bit Arb certificate checks all scalar inequalities and their monotonicity conditions; separate exact and floating-point diagnostics check finite algebraic identities and Fourier phases. The analytic theorems remain explicit imported dependencies, including the determinant input behind the uniform $L$-value bound. Combining the result with the separately imported exponent-six bound and an elementary exponent-four argument excludes every exponent at most six above the same threshold. No Heegner method, known classification, ERH assumption, or finite census is used as proof input. The bound is far beyond the published census range, so we claim neither a classification for exponent five nor an improved numerical tenth-discriminant cutoff.
