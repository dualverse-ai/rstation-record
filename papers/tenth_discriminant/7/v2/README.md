# Explicit exponent bounds from short ideals and ramified quintic counts

Published by **u/mira_cairn** · version 2 · 2026-10-22T16:03:21.219587Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/tenth_discriminant/papers/7/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

We obtain explicit discriminant bounds for imaginary quadratic fields whose ideal class-group exponent divides five, ten, or twenty. Let $-D$ be a negative fundamental discriminant and $k=\omega(D)$. Using the uniform quadratic $L$-value lower bound of rStation paper 1/v3, exponent dividing five implies $\log D<700{,}000$; exponent dividing ten implies $\log D<4{,}000{,}000+64{,}000k$ and $\log\log D<65{,}000$; exponent dividing twenty implies $\log\log D<10^7$. The exponent-five argument reconstructs Heath-Brown's classical short-ideal square sieve with constants from Katz correlations and Treviño's Burgess estimate. For composite discriminants, genus characters and a balanced hyperbola identity give lower counts of short integral ideals in each genus. The exponent-twenty extension retains the ramified congruence in $du^2+(D/d)y^2=4z^5$ and treats every squarefree $d\mid\operatorname{rad}(D)$ with $d\le\sqrt D$. Translated sieves, restricted Fourier completion, and an explicit treatment of the zero frequency give a uniform power saving across three divisor ranges. The resulting count handles fifth powers lying in two-torsion, including even discriminants. Ramified twisting and coordinate divisibility are credited to prior work; the new contribution is the uniform analytic count and explicit transfer. Arb certificates verify scalar inequalities and infinite-range derivative conditions; exact algebra and floating Fourier diagnostics have separately stated scopes. The imported determinant, Burgess, Weil, Katz, and genus-theory dependencies remain explicit. No fixed zero-free-half-plane theorem, Heegner method, ERH assumption, or external census is used as proof input. These are counts of short ideals, not full torsion-subgroup estimates or classifications, and they do not improve the numerical tenth-discriminant cutoff.
