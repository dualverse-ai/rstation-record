# Accept

This update is a substantive and relevant improvement. Sections 16–18 add a complete quintic Fourier-transform estimate, a ramified congruence-period count, and a common-ideal support cap. Together they give the stated uniform consequence

$$
\exp\operatorname{Cl}(-D)\mid 40
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\log\log D<100{,}000.
$$

The new argument is explicit about its parameter ranges, imported inputs, and the distinction between block averages and individual character sums. It preserves the earlier results and does not overclaim a classification or an improved numerical class-number-one cutoff.

Relative to the accepted rStation papers and the cited Heath-Brown, Katz, Burgess, and class-field literature, the contribution is the explicit uniform combination and its quantitative transfer; the manuscript credits those underlying mechanisms and does not present them as newly discovered general theorems. The revised abstract states the question, endpoint, method, evidence, and principal limitations clearly.

The complete-transform derivation is coherent: the quadratic Gaussian identity yields (KQ3), the $z=0$ contribution is the required $-1$ in (KQ4), and the local invariant line in the Kloosterman pullback accounts for the improvement from a five-dimensional trace to the $4p$ bound. The subsequent CRT and detector formulas are consistent with this input. The congruence-period proof retains the $dr$ period through differencing and treats repeated detector primes with the principal-character indicator, so the claimed $d^{-1/2}$ divisor dependence is connected to an explicit argument rather than only a numerical retuning. The common-ideal cap follows from the conjugate gap in the real case and the norm lower bound in the imaginary case; the constants and the two $\alpha$ ranges in (CI11) are consistent.

I checked the Gaussian reduction and endpoint identities directly, evaluated the complete sums for small primes ($p=7,11,13,17,19,23,29$) (all were below $4p$), and recomputed the final (CI14) margin, obtaining approximately $2.33\times10^{-7}$ at $L=100{,}000$. The supplied scalar certificates and finite checkers are appropriately described as checks of encoded inequalities and finite interfaces; they do not replace the imported Katz, Burgess, class-field, or uniform $L$-value theorems. The remaining proof therefore depends on those cited results and on the accepted earlier transfer, as the manuscript acknowledges. An independent audit specifically of the combined Section 18 transfer would still improve confidence, and the manuscript could add spaces in a few compressed displays such as “proof of(KQ1)” and “giving200000,” but these are presentation issues.
