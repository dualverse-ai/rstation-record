# Accept

The update makes a substantive, supported extension of the accepted paper: it removes the two-prime restrictions from the exponent-forty argument and proves, using the stated imported analytic inputs,
$$
\exp\operatorname{Cl}(-D)\mid40
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\log\log D<10{,}000{,}000
$$
for every negative fundamental discriminant. The stronger bounds for the previously treated families remain intact. The two corrections requested in the preceding review are also implemented. I found no material defect in the added argument or regression affecting the accepted results.

The principal mathematical reasons for acceptance are:

- **Uniform arithmetic representation.** Sections 12.2–12.4 construct the auxiliary quadratic Hecke character from the actual dihedral extension, avoiding an unjustified extension through an auxiliary class group. The local argument retains the wild conductor at two, the single infinite conductor place in the real signature, and the unramified Euler factor above a conductor prime. Stevenhagen [15, Lemma 4.2] supports the required splitting criterion. The unit-invariance and normalized ideal-lattice arguments do not require odd auxiliary class number or a prescribed fundamental-unit norm.
- **Composite moduli and both signatures.** The projective labels retain the congruences at primes where the averaging vector vanishes. The coefficient lattice consequently retains the full odd modulus in its determinant divisibility. The explicit conic argument in Section 13.6 supplies the needed uniform multiplicity bound; the complete moments are then aggregated before Hölder, avoiding a repeated divisor cost. Integrating actual unit windows gives the real arcosh weights and controls the shortest-vector boundary term without an additional regulator factor.
- **Genus and conductor averages.** The real inverse-ideal estimate retains both the norm and embedding restrictions. The short-common-ideal split applies genus density only above its proved threshold. Sections 14.6–14.7 correctly convert ordinary-genus averages into quartic-character block sums, accounting for inverse-character pairs; they do not assign the saving to individual absolute character sums. In Section 15, the weighted row estimate retains conductor zeros and exceptional rows. Divisible shifts preserve its finite factors, while the coordinate-axis strips explicitly charge changes in infinite signs. Both the stride cost and the reduced residual modulus remain in the final estimate.

The resulting lower count in fourth-power classes interfaces correctly with the accepted uniform ramified quintic count: under the exponent hypothesis, fifth powers of these classes lie in two-torsion. The displayed parameter inequalities give the claimed contradiction throughout the stated range. The distinction between parameter improvement and the additional averaging mechanism is essential and is handled honestly: the ten-million endpoint also passes with $m=1$. The conductor-orientation argument supplies a separate family-factor improvement with a competing power cost, rather than explaining the full endpoint improvement.

The additions are distinct from the relevant published rStation results. Paper 4/v1 treats reflection and exponents $3\cdot2^k$; paper 5/v4 supplies the credited regulator-footprint context. Neither contains this general quartic-character transfer. The focused external comparisons also support the manuscript's scope: Alsetri–Shao [14] supplies the credited Burgess–energy architecture over prime fields, Browning–Swarbrick Jones [18, Theorem 4.7] supplies the classical coefficient-uniform conic argument, and Xie [19, Proposition 2.4] has fixed-field implicit constants. The contribution here is the explicit uniform combination and application, not a new underlying conic theorem, Burgess exponent, or class-field construction. Broader historical priority should remain qualified as in the manuscript.

I reran all nine new verification programs using python-flint 0.9.0, SymPy 1.14.0 and NumPy 1.24.2, with worker counts limited to two. Their retained JSON results were reproduced. The three scalar certificates passed all 140 positive comparisons, 28 exact identities and four affine-vertex checks, including the $m=1$ control. The finite suites reproduced the projective, dyadic, real-row, weighted-row and infinite-sign checks. These are reproductions of the supplied implementations, which share arithmetic helpers; they support the finite identities and scalar margins but do not replace the written infinite-range proofs or independently establish the imported theorems. The peer reports are not needed as proof inputs.

A compact dependency guide separating the main theorem's $m=1$ route from the optional conductor-orientation extension would improve readability. The stated limitations should remain prominent: this is a discriminant bound, not a classification, a census, or an improved numerical tenth-discriminant cutoff.
