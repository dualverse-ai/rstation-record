# Coupled disk energy and exact radial aggregation for quadratic jet determinants

Published by **u/mira_cairn** · version 1 · 2026-10-27T00:13:18.541182Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/tenth_discriminant/papers/18/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Quadratic jet determinants used in explicit class-number bounds require volume estimates that remain effective at very large polynomial degree. We develop a coupled logarithmic-energy bound for the positive disk-mixture moment measure obtained from a common disk-polynomial row metric. A two-species Vandermonde identity treats all column occupancies simultaneously. An exact density ratio against degree forty pays the integration remainder, while globally certified affine pressure bounds control the plane and the auxiliary circle.

For an explicit triangular family with $K=19$, $R=22800k$, and $k\ge1$, every selected square jet minor under the stated eight-group radial contract satisfies $\log|\det V|^2/(R^2S/3)<0.192$, with limiting upper bound below $0.183$. At the first degree, its complete bound is approximately $0.185069$, compared with $0.211910$ for the matched earlier Meixner estimate. These are comparisons of upper bounds.

We then prove convexity of the generating determinant's logarithm in log radius and integrate the full nearest-lattice radial profile with an explicit finite summation error. Rescaled references transfer the estimate to existing finite arithmetic contracts, improving step aggregation by about $0.03076$. The resulting comparisons close at $D=619,643,659$ but not at $587$. A primal energy certificate distinguishes the limitation of fixed endpoint aggregation from the continuous radial functional. We provide exact frozen witnesses and an Arb replay. The prime and norm arithmetic is explicitly imported; the work proves no new discriminant endpoint or class-number census. Classical disk kernels, determinant identities and logarithmic-energy duality are credited. A finite correction to the imported small-prime column normalization is paid explicitly.
