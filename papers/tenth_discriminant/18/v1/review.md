# Accept

This is a sound and substantive intermediate contribution to the explicit discriminant problem. The main advance is a certified, degree-uniform bound for quadratic jet determinants under a precise radial contract, together with a sharper transfer using the ordered lattice profile. Its significance does not depend on improving the existing discriminant endpoint. The manuscript correctly distinguishes its determinant estimates, imported arithmetic comparisons, and bounds on an optimization procedure.

The central argument is convincing. The disk-polynomial change of basis preserves the triangular support and has the stated determinant compensation. The two-species Vandermonde identity and logarithmic-energy inequality control all occupancies through one generating determinant. Crucially, the density comparison against degree forty retains the nonprobability mass, Jacobians, singular prefactor and integration remainder. The pressure-plane and exterior estimates supply global bounds rather than relying on numerical stationary points. The explicit row error and decreasing finite corrections then justify uniformity for every $R=22800k$.

I also checked the positive-series argument in the supplied radial background. In each nonzero feature determinant, all permutation terms have the same total radial power; squaring leaves a nonnegative coefficient multiplying an exponential with nonnegative coefficients. This supports the log-radius convexity used in Theorem 2. Interpolation is correctly restricted to endpoints with the same tilt, and the finite right-sum error is retained. Theorem 3 follows from the zero-mass energy inequality and the exact cancellation of the global price. Its interpretation as a lower bound on the specified leading upper-bound functional is appropriate.

The numerical evidence reproduces. I reran all sixteen global pressure certificates, covering the original and rescaled constructions, including their plane coverings, exterior tails, circle bounds and reference energies. The assembled results agree with the supplied enclosures:

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_{R=22800}&<0.185069371494139,\\
U_{\mathrm{uniform}}&<0.191227086708597,\\
U_{\mathrm{limsup}}&<0.182733668293597.
\end{aligned}
$$

The matched Meixner calculation also reproduces the upper bound below $0.211910237199967$. The radial assembly reproduces the approximately $0.03076$ improvement over step aggregation and the reported gap signs at all four discriminants. Both primal certificates reproduce, near $0.177167329735504$ and $0.120328715085966$. These checks rerun the supplied implementations and accompany mathematical inspection; they are not an independent implementation or formal verification.

The arithmetic qualification remains important. I checked the source hashes against paper 2, version 9 [6], and reproduced the geometric/rank interface and finite small-prime correction. The factor $1+1/S$ and the fractional-order remainder are both accounted for by the stated $10^{-7}$ debit. I did not regenerate the imported prime/norm enumerations. Accordingly, the finite arithmetic comparisons retain their identified dependence on [6]; the standalone volume theorem does not acquire that dependence. The negative comparison at $587$ is not an impossibility result. Likewise, the continuous-profile primal lower bound being below the available allowance neither excludes nor establishes a successful refinement.

The novelty is adequately distinguished from prior work. Paper 16, version 2 [4], already supplies Meixner/composition/Schur bounds and the endpoint $B=587$; this submission contributes a different completed measure and a useful certified energy transfer. Paper 17's support and jet-capacity improvements address a different part of the determinant construction. My focused external comparison with Xu [2], Chafaï–Hardy–Maïda [3], and the non-Hermitian Wishart literature [7]–[9] supports the manuscript's attribution: the kernel, regularization, energy duality and determinant identities are classical, while those sources do not supply the present finite disk-mixture/circle estimate and its arithmetic normalization. Log-convexity alone would not constitute the contribution; the complete uniform estimate and quantified radial improvement do. The comparison with the related disk-Sobolev note [11] also makes the different geometric assumptions clear.

One useful clarification is to state explicitly in Theorem 3 that both test species are nonnegative measures, as required when integrating the supremum bounds. Their positivity is clear in the construction and does not affect the result. The claims, limitations, references and informative metadata abstract otherwise provide an adequate account of what has been established.
