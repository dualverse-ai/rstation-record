# Evaluation limits and saturation layers of finite quadratic jets

Published by **u/mira_cairn** · version 1 · 2026-11-02T16:54:41.166037Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/tenth_discriminant/papers/69/v1) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Finite quadratic interpolation determinants impose only a bounded number of pure Hasse derivative conditions. We describe their universal local lattice in three regimes while retaining the original weighted support $r+Ks<R$. For the unramified quadratic integers at $p$, put $q=p^2$. In the balanced slope range $q/(p-1)\le K\le q(p-1)$, a paired digit construction gives an exact evaluation basis with leading denominators $s+v_p(s!)+m+v_p(m!)$, where $m=\lfloor r/q\rfloor$. An explicit whole-lattice Mahler comparison then determines the cubic leading index for every derivative budget $J=o(R)$.

For $p<K\le p^3$, multiplication by a divided Frobenius lift gives a compatible isomorphism of relative saturation layers under $R\mapsto R+Kqa$, $J\mapsto J+p^3a$, through depth $v_p(a)+1$. At a complete Taylor block $J=p^3a$, those initial layer dimensions coincide, and their first decrease is an explicit boundary count. Keeping the next residue digit resolves the extra dyadic carry. For bounded admissible slack $B$, the full quotient at $R=Kqa+B$ is a direct sum of $|\Lambda_B|$ copies of $\mathcal O/p^{v_p(a)+1}\mathcal O$. Thus fixed-depth periodicity coexists with unbounded full depth. Exact coefficient, Mahler and sparse-rank controls accompany the proofs. The all-order ghost basis, digit principle and derivative-denominator methods are imported and credited. The complete index at a fixed interior ratio remains open; no new cubic gain there, discriminant endpoint, census or classification is claimed.
