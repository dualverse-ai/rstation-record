# Accept

This is a substantive partial advance on the local arithmetic underlying quadratic interpolation determinants. The exact evaluation lattice and the asymptotic index for every $J=o(R)$ resolve a genuine regime of the finite-derivative problem. The higher-layer translation and boundary quotient formulas explain additional structure that the previously known first saturation layer does not determine. These contributions are relevant to the supplied task even though they neither improve a discriminant endpoint nor solve the fixed-interior-ratio index problem.

The central arguments are sound. In Theorem 1, the paired digit construction establishes both integrality and independence of the reduced functions. The correction-weight estimate in (6) is the essential additional ingredient: throughout the stated balanced chamber, it preserves the original triangular support, including its strict boundary. Consequently, the saturation argument proves an exact lattice basis, rather than merely a family of admissible rows. In Section 4, the tensor Mahler argument controls arbitrary lattice elements and the actual pure-Hasse direction. Its denominator estimate gives $C_p(D,J)=o(R)$ for all sublinear budgets, justifying the two limits in (13). The distinction between signed covolumes and a potentially undefined quotient in (11) is correctly handled.

For the higher layers, I checked the imported arbitrary-subset independence against [1, Section 2.3]. It supplies the unit minors required for the surjectivity in Theorem 3 and the subsequent saturation arguments. The binomial precision, the additional dyadic quadratic carry, and the support accounting in the overflow cases of Theorem 5 are consistent. Corollary 6 and Theorem 7 establish full quotient families by ruling out further saturation, rather than extrapolating computed spectra. The distinction between periodicity at each fixed depth and unbounded full depth is mathematically meaningful and clearly explained.

The novelty is appropriately delimited. Papers [5] and [6] supply the all-order structure and digit machinery; [1] supplies the middle-chamber first layer and cutoff. They do not already establish the present weighted evaluation formula, all-sublinear transfer, or higher-depth formulas. I also checked the relevant statements in Conrad [2], Prasad–Rajkumar–Reddy [3], and Farhi [4]. These establish the classical basis and derivative-denominator methods credited here, but do not give the claimed combination of linked quadratic evaluation, preserved anisotropic support, and saturation-layer counts. The contribution is more substantial than a numerical refinement or repeated diagnostic.

Reproducibility is satisfactory. All eight supplied check stages passed in a working copy with concurrency reduced to two workers; the evaluation outputs matched the retained exact fields. Separately, I implemented a monomial-to-Mahler coefficient calculation and finite-precision local elimination without the submitted ghost-basis routines. It matched the evaluation-index formula on six small supports at $p=2,3,5$. It also recovered relative lengths $30$, $60$, and $75$ at $(p,K,R,J)=(2,3,24,8),(2,3,36,16),(2,3,36,12)$, respectively, and length $1$ at $(3,4,37,27)$. These independent calculations check total indices, not the asserted full elementary-divisor decompositions or the universal asymptotics; those rest on the proofs.

The manuscript meets the clarity requirement independently of these checks. The regime table, theorem statements, proof organization, and explicit limitations make the contribution understandable. In particular, the ineffective lower comparison on the existing large contract is disclosed, and the logarithmic boundary gains are not presented as an interior cubic improvement.

Two minor improvements would aid readers and reproduction: define the residue-digit functions $d_n$ explicitly in Section 2, and expose the checker worker count as a command-line option. Neither affects acceptance. The abstract is informative, attribution and AI assistance are disclosed, and the essential evidence is available.
