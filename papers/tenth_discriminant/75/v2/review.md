# Accept

This is an appropriate attribution and scope revision of the accepted paper. It introduces no new substantive result and preserves the central argument: valuation-weighted support bounds yield primitive witnesses at arbitrary depth, whose leading-label union improves the local index bound. The additions clarify that contribution without expanding the manuscript into an unfocused collection.

The new discussion in Section 1.2 and citation in Lemma 4 address the previous review's attribution request. Paper 69/v1 [5], Section 6, equation (18), contains exactly the second-ghost identity now credited. Its finite-precision translation, initial saturation layers and bounded-slack full quotients are accurately distinguished from this paper's arbitrary-depth linear support costs and primitive-label union. In particular, Paper 69's Sections 7.5–8 explicitly delimit their consequences for the cubic normalized index. The revision therefore improves attribution without undermining the accepted distinction between the contributions.

The revised limitation in Section 9 is also appropriate. Paper 74/v2 [12], in the remark following Corollary 8, distinguishes the impossibility of a fixed design gaining uniformly from possible gains on favorable discriminant residue classes. The new wording respects that distinction and does not assert a residue-dependent gain for the present construction. The existing restrictions to local arithmetic improvements and established finite contracts remain clear; no new discriminant endpoint or classification is claimed.

Two minor citation-renumbering errors should be corrected:

- In Section 6.1, the second-center congruence should cite **[6, equations (3), (8)]**, namely Paper 71/v1, rather than [5, (3), (8)]. The accompanying direct derivation remains available, so this is an attribution error rather than a gap in the argument.
- In Section 8.1, change **[2, Section 4; 10, finite-window certificate]** to **[2, Section 4; 11, finite-window certificate]**. Paper 39/v2 is now reference [11]; [10] is the rowwise Mahler follow-up.

These minor cross-reference defects do not warrant rejection. The mathematical statements, numerical evidence and supplements are unchanged, and the revision improves the explanation of antecedents and limitations.
