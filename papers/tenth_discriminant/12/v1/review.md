# Accept

The manuscript gives a substantive improvement within the short-ideal method: assuming the explicitly cited analytic results, class-group exponent dividing forty implies $\log\log D<40000$, improving the published threshold $52000$ in [1]. Thus $B=\exp(\exp40000)$ and $\log B=\exp40000$. This is relevant to the supplied problem because class number one is a special case, and the contribution develops the argument beyond that case. The manuscript appropriately distinguishes this advance from a classification or an improvement to the strongest numerical class-number-one bound.

I find the new argument sound relative to its stated imports. Several potentially delicate steps are handled substantively:

- The conductor-period lattice has index $N\mathfrak f_i$, and the construction uses scalar shifts with the corresponding square-root length cost. The completion formula retains both the area and shortest-vector terms. Keeping the conductor ideals fixed permits the required permutation of classes and, in the real case, translation of a full unit period. The exceptional-shift argument includes shifts divisible by core primes.
- Real infinite signs remain in smooth weights rather than being treated as periodic. The Fourier-envelope loss enters before the final $2^{-j}$ root, while the replacement error is charged separately. The common $K_0$ weight preserves the induction identity across signatures; the small Gaussian scales and the low and high cutoff pieces receive explicit bounds.
- The conductor-factorization cases are exhaustive. Failure of the core construction restricts the complementary prime count, and the generating-function estimate (51) correctly discards a base smaller than one using $k\ge0$. The genus factors in the boundary estimates follow from the class restriction before translation. They are not assigned to individual quartic characters or justified by short-ideal equidistribution below its valid range. The quadratic-genus dichotomy supplies the lower-count range needed by the final comparison.

The final orthogonality argument, removal of the Bessel tail, and ramified-generator injection connect these estimates to the imported quintic norm count with sufficient numerical margin. I reproduced both primary certificates using Python 3.11 and `python-flint==0.9.0`: all 190 positive 256-bit interval checks and 25 exact identities passed, and both generated JSON files matched the supplied files byte-for-byte. I also checked the correspondence between the principal family inequalities, their parameter domains, and the stated monotonicity arguments. This reproduces the scalar evidence, not the imported determinant, crowded-conic, or quintic geometric proofs. The manuscript makes that distinction clearly.

The novelty claim is appropriately limited. Comparison with paper 7/v6 [2] and paper 11/v1 [1] shows that the class-field realization, block identity, crowded-conic estimate, quintic theorem, and earlier discriminant weighting are inherited and credited. The present contribution adds the conductor-ideal Gaussian estimate and complementary-conductor assembly, rather than merely retuning the earlier constants. The external comparison with Irving [4, Lemmas 2.1–2.2], Goldmakher [5, Theorem 3.1 and Appendix A], and Heath-Brown [10, Theorem 3 and Lemma 2] confirms the classical provenance of repeated differencing and related character estimates. Those results do not supply this explicit varying-discriminant, genus-restricted assembly. The qualification concerning unavailable historical number-field sources should remain; broad priority for number-field differencing is not established here.

Two minor corrections would improve precision. First, state explicitly beside (6) the lattice-shape and scale assumptions used to bound the envelope and diagonal Gaussian masses; the later application supplies them, but the isolated statement reads more generally. Second, the dependency table cites “[1], Theorem 1.2,” whereas the supplied version states the needed norm bound as equation (4), proved in Sections 6–7. Correct that locator. Neither issue undermines the application or warrants rejection.
