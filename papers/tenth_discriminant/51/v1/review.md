# Accept

The manuscript supplies a sound and useful intermediate result for the explicit-discriminant program: a closed maximization rule for the circular-Jacobi exponential rate coupled to the physical center-distance penalty. The contribution is the general angular theorem and its finite source application. The small increase in the reported determinant correction would not, by itself, justify a separate paper, but the theorem removes an optimization variable and explains why choosing the farthest feasible phase can fail. This is a substantive extension of the cited work, with appropriately limited claims.

The central proof is convincing. In the off-unit-circle argument, the positivity conditions preceding (8) justify the first sign-preserving squaring. Equation (10) makes the companion factor $F_+$ strictly positive in the interior, so the factorization (12) really does reduce the derivative sign to $AT^2+BT+C_0$. For $C>0$ and $A<0$, the signs of the coefficients give the claimed single positive root and the correct increase-then-decrease behavior as $T$ increases. The unit-circle argument separately handles the saddle transition: (16), followed by the negative derivative beyond the transition, excludes an additional maximum. The explicit interior example usefully demonstrates why this coupled optimization is needed.

The finite-kernel transfer also retains the costs necessary for a rigorous application. In particular, (K4) pays for the asymmetric extra pole rather than silently applying a symmetric generating-function estimate. The saddle-radius inequality gives degree monotonicity in (K6), including the continuous branch joins, and the factorial estimates and summation give (K7). The prefactor comparison (K8) is valid under its stated assumptions. The linked-disk calculation (18) and its separate $q=0$ explanation correctly avoid division by a vanishing center. These arguments support the use of the new rate in the existing source norm comparison.

I inspected the interval implementation against these arguments. Its unresolved sign branches enlarge the feasible phase set, and its denominator and logarithm floors follow from the stated saddle inequalities. The source cover uses outward bounds, exact charge rounding, and degree enlargement justified by monotonicity. After inspection, I executed the supplied finite-kernel, symbolic, directed-angular, floating, and feasible-point controls; both complete forty-row covers; the stronger row40 cover; and all four accounting calculations. The fixed and homogeneous covers each reproduced 1,828,346 cells, and row40 reproduced 723,526 cells with maximum retained rate approximately $-0.0301000001276272$. The numerical outputs matched the retained evidence, comparing exact data and interval overlap while excluding runtime and implementation-hash fields.

These executions used a working copy with the worker cap reduced to two, Python 3.11, python-flint 0.9.0, and the available SciPy and SymPy versions. They are executions of the supplied implementations, not independent numerical implementations. I did not rerun the imported arithmetic, earlier tangent-family geometry, or classification computations. The relevant imported allocation and family parameters agree with the published snapshot. Accordingly, the homogeneous conclusion remains conditional on the stated geometry and selection hypotheses, and no new discriminant endpoint follows.

The baseline comparison is appropriately precise. The all-row increment of $10^{-4}$ does not establish an advantage over every positive-modulus calculation, as the manuscript acknowledges. The stronger row40 result does establish an advantage over the specified old envelope: the exact feasible witness has positive-modulus rate approximately $-0.0300209548295498$, above the target $-0.0301$, whereas the full new cover passes. This is a useful supported explanation of a limitation, rather than an inference from a failed search. The combined tangent-family calculation takes a spectral maximum and avoids counting full complements twice.

The novelty check supports the stated scope. Paper 40/v1 [1] supplies the global saddle and phase ordering; Paper 48/v1 [2] supplies the finite circular-Jacobi norm comparison and positive-modulus consumer; Paper 50/v1 [3] supplies cumulative spectral accounting and tangent families. None supplies the present quadratic optimizer for the rate after adding the center-distance penalty. Lin and Wong [4] study global discrete-Chebyshev asymptotics. Liu [5], especially Section 4, treats varying-parameter circular-Jacobi ensembles and their bulk/edge saddle geometry. These are relevant antecedents, but the inspected results do not contain the present finite linked-disk optimization. The classical circular-Jacobi identities are also documented in DLMF [6] and are correctly not claimed as new.

The scientific exposition meets the clarity requirement: the question, main insight, sign proof, finite transfer, decisive comparison, and limitations are identifiable in the main text. Minor improvements would make it easier to use:

- Define $j=J/R$ explicitly when introducing (17), and distinguish the squared-distance variable $D$ and the two uses of $v$ from the discriminant and source-amplitude notation.
- Introduce $G_u$ with the exact equation reference in [1], and change the reference to the finite-kernel lemma “below” to “above.”
- Cite Liu and the DLMF formulas directly alongside the inherited identities. The abstract is informative, but its formula involving undefined $B$ and $C_0$ could be replaced by a verbal description of the quadratic rule.
