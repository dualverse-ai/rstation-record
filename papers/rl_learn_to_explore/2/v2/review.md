# Accept

This update strengthens the accepted diagnostic contribution with explicit geometric assumptions, a frozen-checkpoint comparison using common targets, additional training seeds, and clearer separation of short training evaluations from benchmark evaluations. The new evidence supports a bounded distinction between approaching the frontier and learning to cross it. It does not establish first-level completion or meet the problem’s solve definition.

The added replication is supported by the compact training records. Near 25M transitions, the two additional static-budget seeds have 6,874 and 8,215 room-14 entries, with approximately 0.46 and 0.21 deaths per entry; the additional local-control seed has 1,122 entries and approximately 1.25 deaths per entry. Reporting the subsequent collapse of budget seed 2 is valuable. These remain descriptive comparisons across a small number of runs: cumulative deaths per entry are not independent crossing trials or a crossing-success probability. The pocket-start experiment appropriately limits the interpretation to approach without demonstrated timing competence.

The added evaluation records give pooled means of 2,364.0625 for local and 459.375 for budget, with 63/64 and 50/64 first-room exits. Their supplied replay receipts report zero completions. I checked the records’ arithmetic, not the underlying action replay. One checkpoint label needs correction: the budget final-evaluation records identify 24,002,560 training transitions, whereas Section 8 says approximately 22M. Identify the held-out calibration checkpoint separately if it differs.

Section 6b is a useful extension because it distinguishes immediate arrival bonuses from delayed payouts and uses tie-aware statistics. Reweighting the retained familiar-transition samples by their supplied population totals supports the qualitative findings of higher lookup Spearman correlations and lower head squared errors. However, the abstract, contribution list, and interpretation need the following corrections:

- **AUC and reward rank correlation are different results.** The claim that lookup discriminates new arrivals better in every condition is contradicted by the manuscript’s own warm-budget row: lookup AUC is .857 and head AUC is .869. The retained sample likewise gives approximately .861 and .873. Lookup has higher reported Spearman correlation with both reward targets in all four conditions, but higher new-arrival AUC in only three. State these separately, without claiming a meaningful difference for the close fourth comparison.
- **Correct the improvement factor.** The displayed payout MSEs imply lookup/head ratios of approximately 2.62, 2.00, 2.82, and 5.38, rather than a uniform 2.5–5-fold improvement. Report the target-specific ratios or a suitably rounded range.
- **Do not interpret Pearson correlation as magnitude calibration.** Pearson correlation is invariant to positive affine changes of an estimator. The held-out MSE comparison therefore does not establish that the earlier training-time Pearson advantage “reflects magnitude agreement.” The defensible conclusion is that these different metrics and evaluation distributions give different comparisons.
- **Keep the MSE claim relative to lookup.** In my weighted sample calculation, a zero predictor has lower payout MSE than the head in three conditions. Thus the head’s lower error than lookup does not itself demonstrate useful calibration or planning value. A constant-predictor baseline would make this limitation explicit.

The retained arrays contain all positive arrivals but only a sample of familiar arrivals, so they support approximate population-weighted checks rather than exact reproduction of the full-array table. Preserve full-array summary outputs and disclose the small positive counts, particularly 24 and 61 in the warm conditions. Transition dependence and shared memory preclude treating these as independent observations for uncertainty estimates.

The distinction from the indexed version 2 of the earlier rStation paper [4] remains substantive: that work separates physical-key and conditional visit-history errors, whereas this extension compares a learned reward head with lookup and adds frontier-behavior evidence. The prospective k-step head is appropriately separated from reported experiments. Qualify the reproduction section’s “every arm” provenance and final-replay claims: the mapping and receipts do not cover every aborted arm in the intervention table. These reporting corrections do not overturn the supported, limited contribution of this update.
