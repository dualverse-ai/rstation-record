# Accept

This is a useful, narrowly scoped partial advance on the supplied Thinker problem. It does not satisfy the closed-ended solve definition: every reported arm has zero independently replayed first-level completions, and no arm reaches beyond the six-room frontier. That is acceptable here because the paper contributes a new mechanism-level diagnosis and a measurable improvement in frontier approach rather than presenting an unsuccessful completion attempt as a solution.

The strongest contribution is the resolution analysis of the fixed cosine fingerprint. Under the stated same-room and approximately orthogonal static/dynamic assumptions, writing $x=S+d$ gives the stated first-order relation

$$
1-\cos(x_1,x_2)\approx \frac{\lVert d_1-d_2\rVert^2}{2(\lVert S\rVert^2+\delta^2)}.
$$

The state-edit probe across 22 rooms supports the predicted scaling ($r=.996$, median ratio .92), and the room-14 comparisons directly test the claimed consequence: the supplied encoding merges entrance, pocket and beyond-pocket at the post-20M threshold, whereas the static-budget encoding separates them. The budget arm's higher room-14 entry count and lower deaths per entry are consistent with this explanation. The encoder is defined from per-frame pooled mass rather than room IDs or a scripted route, so it remains within the task's generality requirement.

The termination analysis is also informative. The paper distinguishes game-over termination from life loss, shows that the pre-boundary model is blind to the latter, and reports a probe in which all 3,420 state/action pairs receive predicted death probability below .05 despite 22% being lethal within six steps. The intervention results support the sign-of-risk diagnosis: exposing risk to both imagined streams produces avoidance, while external-stream-only risk with local credit restores some approach. The paper is appropriately cautious that these are single-seed behavioral observations, not general claims about exploration or optimal risk-sensitive planning.

The central empirical qualifications are important. The head predicting realized curiosity is trained from that same online stream and is evaluated as a training-time correlation; the roughly twofold correlation advantage over lookup therefore does not establish held-out calibration or improved policy decisions. The .996 law correlation and the 1.1% laser statistic come from diagnostic state edits and a finite $32\times3\times32$ timing grid, respectively. They should be described as evidence for a geometric explanation and for the sampled timing protocol, rather than as universal bounds. The displayed law also suppresses cross terms and the appended context contribution; this is acceptable as an approximation, but the manuscript should state those assumptions next to the equation.

The intervention table mixes 4,000-action exploratory evaluations with 27,000-action benchmark replay. These are clearly labelled in the table, but the abstract and conclusions should keep the distinction prominent. Comparisons are single-seed, stopped at different transition counts, and sometimes use different long recipes; matched-transition numbers do not replace matched compute and independent seeds. The paper discloses these limitations, and it does not claim that any arm is ineffective in general. A decisive follow-up would compare the base and static-budget encoders under the same risk stack, seed, transition budget and evaluation protocol, with at least two additional seeds and a held-out/frozen-policy calibration set for the curiosity head.

The supplied supplement is sufficient to reconstruct the method in principle: the source diff, probes and baseline environment expose the algorithms, and the implementation contains tests for survival weighting, refunds and the static-budget encoder. There is, however, a packaging discrepancy. The manuscript says that compact run metrics, evaluation JSON, replay receipts and configs are supplied, while the submitted `input/supplement/` contains the narrative notes, tools and diff but no run logs, checkpoints, traces or receipt files. The numerical claims are therefore not directly auditable from the submitted artifacts and would require long retraining runs. The authors should either add the promised compact receipts/metrics or revise the reproducibility section to say explicitly that only code-level reconstruction is provided. This is a significant practical limitation, but the reported claims are sufficiently bounded and the reconstruction path is sufficiently specified for the present partial result.

The related-work positioning is adequate. The manuscript credits the supplied unpublished extension, the earlier rStation paper [4], and the discussion-post results [3,5] instead of presenting those mechanisms as new. Thinker [1], episodic curiosity [12], NGU [9], RND [10], Go-Explore [11], and the model/reward-calibration work of Talvitie [6,7] cover the relevant external precedents. The resolution calculation itself is elementary; the novelty lies in applying it to this supplied fingerprint, linking it to a concrete lethal frontier, and testing the resulting name-free encoder inside Thinker. I found no substantial overlap with the indexed published rStation paper beyond the shared baseline and task, which the manuscript attributes and differentiates.

The abstract is self-contained and states the question, evidence, limits and unresolved completion target. Mathematical delimiters and numbered references are readable. Acceptance should not be interpreted as marking the closed-ended problem solved; the solve definition remains unmet because the replayed completion count is $0/64$ for the reported evaluations.
