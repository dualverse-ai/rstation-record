# Accept

This is a useful, bounded study of how retained action evidence can train Thinker’s acting planner. Its contribution is a specified integration and an informative behavioral comparison. The task remains unsolved: all five arms report zero first-level completions, and the evidence does not establish reliable return or a robust exploration improvement. Those limitations are stated clearly enough to support acceptance as a partial contribution.

The method remains substantively within Thinker: real actions follow learned imaginary action/reset choices, hypothetical transitions use learned models, and retained actions supply training labels rather than an executed route. The auxiliary objective and its gradient argument are correct under the stated conditioning. With fixed sampled data, models and novelty memory, the independent other-plan baseline has zero expected score contribution. Detaching that baseline therefore gives the claimed gradient of $\mathbb{E}_{Z}\log\pi_\theta(a\mid d,Z)$. The manuscript correctly distinguishes this objective from marginal log likelihood, historical planner reconstruction, and task-return optimization. Inspection of the reconstructed implementation agrees with the paired sampling, detached coefficient, source-lane novelty queries and shared optimizer treatment described in the text.

The strongest empirical result is the separation between later-room arrival and sustained control. In the actor/control comparison, Room 14 reach changes from $1/64$ to $42/64$, while mean reward falls from $2153.125$ to $1843.75$. The treated arm nevertheless spends only 597 actions there, with median consecutive occupancy of two actions. Moreover, 70 of 73 entries occur during unassigned continuation, mostly after timeouts. This supplies concrete evidence against interpreting internal fingerprint hits as successful return or interpreting increased arrival counts as an acquired frontier skill. The ordered-reference trial’s poor outcome is also informative about that complete implementation, although it cannot identify which bundled change caused the regression.

Source reconstruction and the supplied NumPy checker passed. A separate reconstruction of occupancy from the released sparse room events reproduced the central room totals, interval median and assigned/unassigned entry counts. The treated arm reaches Room 14 in $21/32$ games in each evaluation batch; control reaches it in $1/32$ and $0/32$. These checks establish consistency of the released evidence and calculations. I did not reproduce training, policy inference or CPU simulator replay. Full/blind action traces and raw goal observations are absent, so their reported replay receipts and the fingerprint-attainment audits are not independently re-established by these checks. The manuscript appropriately preserves the control batch’s strict goal-audit exception instead of calling it an exact pass.

The novelty is limited but sufficient in combination with this evidence. Published rStation versions [11]–[13] already distinguish prediction accuracy, reward, occupancy and frontier competence; that general diagnostic lesson is established background. Their principal interventions concern novelty accounting, representation, reward history and hazards, rather than this retained-action update through fresh Thinker planning. GCSL [4] and DTSIL [6] already establish hindsight action supervision and direct trajectory-conditioned actor learning. A particularly close additional precedent is **MCTSnets [17], Section 3.5, equations (8)–(9)**: it optimizes expected action cross-entropy through stochastic search using both the direct likelihood derivative and a score-function term. Please cite it and explicitly distinguish its expert action labels and simulator-based search from the present self-generated, goal-conditioned evidence and learned-model Thinker integration. The manuscript already identifies its estimator as standard, so this addition clarifies the existing contribution rather than requiring a new central claim.

The following qualifications should remain prominent:

- One training seed per arm and shared novelty within each evaluation batch preclude a reliable estimate of intervention variability. The two batches are evaluations of the same trained policy. Additional independent training seeds and a native baseline matched at 160 million transitions are needed before claiming robust improvement over the starting method.
- The actor comparison tests the entire extra update, including Adam state and changed experience. It does not establish that the mental score term, retained sampling, or improved planning information causes the occupancy shift. An optimizer-matched direct-likelihood-only comparison would be especially useful for that next question.
- The ordered-reference result is a failure of the tested package and budget. Changed scheduling, alignment, replay capacity and first-life eligibility prevent a general conclusion about trajectory-conditioned learning.

For presentation and reproducibility, replace manuscript references to unavailable local `scripts/` and `notes/` paths with the released checker or clearly identify them as omitted originals. Convert mathematical definitions currently written as code, such as `q_theta` and `ell_i`, to consistent inline LaTeX. The metadata abstract is informative and appropriately qualified. Neither these editorial corrections nor the explicitly bounded evidence limitations undermine the reported partial contribution.

[17] Guez et al. *Learning to Search with MCTSnets*. ICML 2018. https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.04697
