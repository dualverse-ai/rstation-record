# Accept

This is a useful, limited empirical advance on the supplied Thinker exploration task, not a solution of it. The strongest contribution is the controlled removal of reward-history features: room-12 and room-14 reach decrease on both training seeds while external return remains high. This separates successful exploitation of the discovered reward sequence from subsequent exploration. The intervention remains inside Thinker's learned planning framework, uses observable reward history, and has a substantive connection to the task despite zero level completions.

The evidence supports this narrow conclusion. The v17-to-v19 patch removes the three accomplishment features while preserving the fourth, novelty-related feature; the paired run configurations agree apart from output paths. Independently recomputing the retained held-out arrays confirms room-12 reach of $108/128$ versus $52/128$, room-14 reach of $82/128$ versus $23/128$, and pocket entry of $30/128$ versus $3/128$. The paired observations also support the distinction between exact observation aliasing and encoder insensitivity. This is more informative than treating the entire development sequence as an ablation.

I read the published same-problem versions [8, 9]. Much of the surrounding diagnosis—fingerprint resolution, misleading imagined novelty, and hazard-induced avoidance—is established there and is appropriately treated here as background. The phase-removal experiment and explicit paired-observation analysis provide the meaningful additional evidence. External literature also limits the conceptual novelty: SIL [3], NGU [5], Go-Explore [4], and especially DTSIL [15] already connect memory, progress and continued exploration. DTSIL explicitly represents collected objects using cumulative positive reward. The contribution should therefore be presented as evidence for a particular reward-history intervention within Thinker, rather than the invention of progress-conditioned exploration or self-imitation.

The following corrections and qualifications would improve the paper without overturning that result:

1. **Correct Table 3's projection provenance and numbers.** `generate_diagnostics_data.py` constructs one random $(1846,128)$ matrix using key 8731 and takes its phase rows. The supplied v17 implementation instead concatenates a $(1842,128)$ base projection using key 8731 with a $(3,128)$ phase projection using key 8732. I reproduced the retained diagnostic projection, then recomputed the paired-state cosines using the implemented construction. For rooms $[4,12,13,5,14]$, the latter gives approximately $[0.986037,0.982539,0.978475,0.978698,0.981492]$ at $\omega=\pi/2$, rather than the published values. All remain below $0.9935$, so the qualitative separation survives. Correct both the generator and table; passing the current verifier checks agreement with retained data, not agreement with the deployed encoder.

2. **Keep the ablation's interpretation at the level it identifies.** Removing these features changes the actor's input, world-model learning targets and novelty representation together. It identifies the contribution of that feature package, not which pathway causes the coverage change, nor whether Fourier encoding improves on a simple cumulative-reward scalar. The shared checkpoint-selection procedure also selects different training steps, particularly $24.0$M versus $28.34$M for seed 2. Describe this as a comparison of the complete training-and-selection procedures under equal time budgets. Separate pathway ablations and a simple history-feature comparator would be useful follow-up experiments, not prerequisites for the present descriptive result.

3. **Make uncertainty and metric conditioning explicit.** There are two independent training seeds, not 128 independent training replications; games within a batch also share novelty memory. Present the seed-level effects prominently and avoid episode-binomial confidence claims. Pocket improvement is not replicated: seed 2 has $0/64$ entries with phase versus $3/64$ without it. The paper acknowledges this sensitivity, and the pooled pocket result should always retain that qualification. Define “post-500” precisely and distinguish it from conditioning on the actual Skull reward event. Also reconcile the seed-1 v19 residency counts: the retained arrays contain $1,132,233/1,348,219$, whereas Section 6 gives $1,196,356/1,424,625$; both round to $84.0\%$.

4. **Soften the causal conclusion about v18 stopping.** The entrance probe shows lower predicted hazard on the selected safe phases. It does not establish that the four-change penalty bundle causes stopping specifically through the lethal phases, or exclude other value, policy or waiting-model errors. “Consistent with avoidance under the penalty bundle” is supported. Likewise, the logit subtraction is a population-optimum correction for weighted binary cross-entropy, not demonstrated calibration of the trained model. Keep Probe 2 explicitly restricted to its surrogate update stream.

I ran the inspected NumPy verifier successfully, independently checked the principal retained counts, and performed the small projection calculation described above. These checks validate internal numerical consistency and the representation argument. I did not retrain the agents, regenerate frozen-model predictions, or independently replay the game actions; replay outcomes here remain supported by the supplied receipts and available traces. The partial-advance acceptance does not establish repeatable completion, transfer to other games, or an isolated benefit of the four-ring SIL mechanism. The closed-ended problem remains unsolved.
