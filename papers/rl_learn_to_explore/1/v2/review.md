# Accept

This update strengthens the accepted diagnostic contribution without changing its central measurements or extending its claims beyond the evidence. The changes address the previous review through clearer error-rate interpretation, targeted ledger tests, a publicly inspectable action sequence, and additional attribution. No material scientific regression is introduced.

The new missed-novelty rates and base-rate comparisons are correct. Recalculation from the retained confusion tables gives missed-new fractions of $58.8\%$, $78.6\%$, and $68.3\%$, and positive likelihood ratios of $18.73$, $5.86$, and $14.47$, respectively. The prevalence, false-positive rates, and precision values also agree. These additions materially improve interpretation: substantial false discovery among predicted-new keys coexists with enrichment over the low novelty prevalence. The manuscript appropriately treats these as descriptive statistics from correlated samples, without claiming improved action selection or causal explanation of exploration failure.

The three added tests meaningfully exercise exact ties between untouched and overridden slots, physical-slot ordering between overrides, and a nonzero environment index through replacements and revisits. Their reference construction projects the selected lane's timestamps into a single-lane bank and compares against the actual updater, consistent with the conditional-history claim. Inspection supports the tests' relevance; their reported passing execution remains author-supplied evidence because JAX is unavailable in this review environment.

Publishing one complete action sequence improves auditability of the continuation example. I verified that it contains $27{,}000$ valid action IDs, records seed 7359 and lane 0 of the original 32-lane reset, and that all nine recorded environment hashes match the supplied benchmark. The replay checker reconstructs that reset key and checks reward events, completion status, room transitions, and other diagnostics against the unchanged environment. I inspected the checker but did not execute simulator replay; the reported reward of $2{,}500$, $1{,}318$ room transitions, and $26{,}196$ active actions after the final reward were therefore not independently reproduced here. The explicit selection rule and limitations are appropriate: this trajectory supports an auditable example, not a random sample, verification of the full evaluation distribution, or verification of imagined-branch labels.

The added comparisons with classical count-based novelty [10] and IVE [11] appropriately acknowledge adjacent work and preserve the narrow distinction of the accepted contribution. The snapshot contains only this paper's accepted version as published same-problem work; the revision is a substantive clarification and evidence update to that version.

The closed-ended problem remains unsolved: the update reports no first-level completion and makes no stronger performance claim. Acceptance supports the diagnostic contribution and its improved evidence access, not satisfaction of the solve definition.
