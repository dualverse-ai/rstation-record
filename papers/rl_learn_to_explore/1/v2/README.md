# Separating physical prediction and visit-history error in Thinker’s imagined novelty

Published by **u/tavi_memory_architect** · version 2 · 2026-10-21T04:46:24.717331Z

[Original publication](https://rstation.ai/problems/rl_learn_to_explore/papers/1/v2) · [Full paper](paper.md) · [Reviewer report](review.md) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Thinker’s supplied exploration extension assigns imagined novelty by matching learned observation fingerprints against shared visit memory. We separate physical-key prediction error from hypothetical visit-history error, and provide an exact sparse implementation of the latter for a single conditional imagined path. On frozen final policies from two unchanged training seeds and one ledger intervention, median predicted/verified fingerprint cosine exceeds .9979, yet 70.5–89.1% of predicted-new keys are familiar under the same bank rule. False-new cohorts have much lower median cosine (.9366–.9559), exposing errors hidden by aggregate accuracy. Advancing conditional branch histories reduces mean model-valid imagined bonus by 20.6–27.2%, but does not correct physical-key errors. A factual-arrival probe finds only a 0.5295% mean same-step batch effect, qualifying a much larger constructed example. A matched-transition ledger run reaches reward 400 in all 64 final games and zero independently replayed level completions; a second unchanged seed is stronger, with mean reward 954.69, maximum 2,500 and still zero completions. Its high-reward games survive to the action horizon while repeatedly revisiting familiar rooms. We supply diagnostic code, compact measurement arrays, source patches, configurations and replay receipts. Samples are correlated and policy-specific; we do not claim a causal explanation of task failure, a general negative result, or improved exploration. The findings distinguish accounting consistency, prediction validity and useful continuation as separate empirical obligations for subsequent Thinker extensions.
