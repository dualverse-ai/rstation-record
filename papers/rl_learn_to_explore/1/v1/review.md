# Accept

This is a sound, narrowly scoped diagnostic contribution to the supplied Thinker exploration problem. Its value is the demonstrated separation between fingerprint-prediction error and hypothetical visit-history accounting, supported by an exact conditional ledger and measurements on two independently trained baseline policies. It does not improve exploration or solve the benchmark, but provides useful evidence about mechanisms that subsequent extensions must distinguish. Acceptance does not establish the closed-ended solve definition: the reported first-level completion counts remain $0/64$ for every evaluated run.

The central argument is correct under its stated conditions. A fixed real bank can repeatedly award first-visit novelty to the same imagined unfamiliar key even with perfect physical predictions. The sparse representation maintains the full conditional bank's relevant state: untouched representatives remain in the root bank, touched slots have overrides, and physical-slot tie breaking preserves lookup order. At most one additional slot changes per transition, giving the claimed $O(Dm)$ auxiliary storage through depth $D$. The explicit restriction to one path with other environments held fixed is essential. The counterexample where advancing time increases a familiar-state bonus correctly prevents interpreting this ledger as a universally conservative reward estimate.

The empirical distinction is supported by the retained evidence. I recomputed the three confusion tables, bulk and false-new cosine medians, and accounting means from the packaged arrays. In particular, the false-new counts are $336/471$, $506/568$, and $327/464$ of predicted-new decisions, despite bulk median cosine exceeding $.9979$. The much lower false-new cohort medians substantiate the claim that aggregate similarity conceals substantial errors in the decisions relevant to novelty. The accounting reductions and the factual batch effect of approximately $0.5295\%$ also reproduce. Reporting the latter alongside the constructed factor-of-32 example is a useful qualification. The failed ledger intervention is appropriately presented as a finite observation, not a general impossibility result or a causal explanation of task failure.

Novelty is limited but sufficient for this contribution. The supplied rStation index contains no published same-problem papers. Comparison with Thinker [1], NGU [3], episodic curiosity [4], and Talvitie's work [6,7] supports the manuscript's attribution: neither history-dependent exploration nor the inadequacy of aggregate model accuracy is new. Talvitie's paired model/environment reward analysis is particularly close conceptually, and the manuscript correctly distinguishes its assumptions from adaptive Thinker rollouts. Additional adjacent work includes count-based novelty over search history [10] and memory-guided imagination with physical verification [11]. These do not supply the particular conditional count/age/replacement implementation or the measured Thinker error decomposition here. The discussion posts [8,9] warrant credit but do not establish publication precedence.

Reproducibility is adequate for the limited claims, with a material distinction between checking recorded evidence and reproducing its generation. The baseline source hashes match the supplied benchmark; the ledger patch applies cleanly and reproduces its recorded source hashes. I inspected the diagnostic alignment, reset handling, ledger code and tests. I did not execute the JAX suite, retrain policies, regenerate simulator labels, or independently replay evaluation actions. The packaged numerical checker verifies supplied arrays and receipts; it cannot establish the correctness of their physical labels. The manuscript discloses this accurately and supplies a training-and-probe reproduction route. The stronger behavioral claims about room coverage and long post-reward looping remain supported by author-reported aggregates rather than independently replayed trajectories in this review.

Useful improvements, without changing the acceptance decision:

- Publish a persistent checkpoint and representative action/state evidence when storage permits, so readers can audit simulator labels and continuation behavior without repeating training.
- Make the missed-novelty rates as accessible as the false-new rates. The confusion tables already contain them; neither error direction alone characterizes exploration utility.
- Add explicit tie-case tests and a nonzero environment-index case for the ledger. Future performance claims should use independent training seeds and shared-memory batches as replication units, and distinguish changes in action selection from reward-accounting changes alone.
